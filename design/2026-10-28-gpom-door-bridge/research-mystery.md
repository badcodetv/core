# Research: withholding information to create mystery (GPOM door bridge)

## Techniques

1. **Information gap (Loewenstein, 1994).** Curiosity is the wish to close a specific, known unknown. It needs some prior knowledge (total ignorance triggers nothing), the gap must feel closeable, and curiosity peaks as the answer gets near. Explaining at the door closes the gap early, so the pull ends. Film example: Jaws, where we know a shark exists but see it late.
2. **Suspense vs surprise (Hitchcock/Truffaut, 1962).** Show the audience the bomb and they get fifteen minutes of suspense, where surprise gives fifteen seconds of shock. Suspense needs the audience to hold the key facts, not the characters. Example: Rear Window, where we watch Jeff watch. Here the audience already knows the killbots, the cost and "consciousness is missing". Let them hold the bomb, so the cost lands as dread when the humans walk.
3. **The 2+2 principle (Stanton).** "Don't give them 4, give them 2+2." "Make the audience put things together." The audience wants to work for its meal, within limits (too hard means confusion). Example: WALL-E's first act, a whole story told with no dialogue. Give two facts and omit the sum.
4. **Mystery box (Abrams, TED 2007).** An unopened box holds more potential than its contents. Examples: Jaws (the mechanical shark failed and the film improved), Alien, Lost. The box must be promised and it must matter. The risk is a tease with no payoff, so the later reveal has to deliver.
5. **Ellipsis and the Kuleshov/editor's effect.** The audience builds meaning from two juxtaposed shots and bridges the cut itself. Examples: the 2001 bone-to-satellite match cut (bridges millennia with no explanation), and match-on-action cuts that skip the walk to the car. The cut asks the question and the viewer answers it.

## Why explanation kills tension
Once the gap is closed there is no deprivation to resolve. The viewer shifts from inferring to receiving, which is passive. Stated stakes also read as sermon (this is a Story-over-sermon rule for BadCode).

## Three ideas for the door problem
1. **Let the audience hold the sum (2+2).** Before the retreat, plant two facts (Maddie's line "consciousness is the missing part", the AI "misses" humanity). Then cut from the killbots lowering weapons to a held beat of Maddie turning and simply walking uphill. No one explains. The viewer concludes "she's offering them something" and the unease supplies the mystery.
2. **Cut on the question, not the answer.** End the retreat on the old woman looking at Maddie, and one humans-side choice (she picks up her bag, or hands the door key to a child). Smash cut to the hundred walking in a line over the hill. The ellipsis does the persuading, and the missing talk is the box.
3. **Image-rhyme bridge (match cut).** Match the killbot's lowered weapon or dimming red eye to something in the humans' procession (a lantern, a lowering hood, a row of figures with the same silhouette). The graphic match implies "the threat has become the escort" without words. Optionally keep one unexplained detail (the building's door already open, humming) as the box for the next scene.
