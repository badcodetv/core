# Shape 02: the picture carries it

I treated every line as a cost and kept only what the camera cannot say. The crew's desperation is shown by the picture (the monitor tight, the masks, the hands, the siren), and the stop at the red light by the outrider's glove and the ambulance rocking to a halt, so no one needs to narrate either. The words that survive are the ones that give the viewer a number and an order (BP, "move") plus the minister's line. Where a line would repeat a shot, the shot wins and the line goes.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane round 3, the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |

| **Total spoken** | | **5.9** |

**Why it works.** Three spoken lines in 14 seconds, and every one earns its place. The first gives the viewer the number and the verdict in two seconds, so "dying" is settled before the light changes. The driver's swear is the one human reaction the picture cannot show, and it makes the next beat hurt. Then we take the speech away. "What's he doing?!" is cut because the audience is already asking it, looking at the glove (relied-on shot: the OUT1-c outrider with the raised hand, beat B). Taking the siren out at the stop gives the held beat a silence of its own, so the minister's line arrives into a quiet room and sounds more absurd, and it needs no lip-sync because it comes through the glass (the bolder through-the-windscreen shot, beat C, with his car inches from the bonnet). The flatline lands on what the viewer has just watched: a man on the monitor, a minister outside, and nothing said that could tell them what to feel. Silence is the only edit that cannot be argued with. It also keeps us the sarcastic observer Kai asked for, with no one explaining the joke.

**Cuts from current.** PARAMEDIC 2: "What's he doing?!" (the glove and the stop say it). The longer "sats dropping" and "oxygen dropping" expansions from Kai's wider note (the monitor tight in shot A carries oxygen and sats as numbers on screen). Any second paramedic speech at all: the second paramedic acts, never speaks. The "look, we're gonna lose him, hurry up" repeat (the first line already says it). About 3 s of speech overall against the 7-9 s allowed, which is deliberate: the spare seconds go to the held beat and the flatline.

**Risk.** The picture has to be doing the work, and if shot A is a calm wide it fails. The monitor must be tight, the crew visibly frantic, and the BP numbers legible on the screen (60/30, falling). If the Suno siren will not drop out cleanly at the stop, beat B loses its silence and the minister's line has to fight it. Fewer lines also leave less room for Kai's "ER-level drama": he may feel the crew are under-voiced, and one more shout (Option: PARAMEDIC 2 "Oxygen dropping!" in A) is the obvious thing to add back. The driver's swear and the minister's silent entrance both rely on Suno speech mode landing in a gap, so the take needs checking for crosstalk. Medical plausibility is safe: BP readings and "lose him" are real paramedic talk and nothing is invented.

**Protected lines.** "BP 60 over 30" survives verbatim inside the first line. "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" survives verbatim and unchanged; it is the only joke and it is Jack's. The only altered line is the driver's, trimmed to Kai's own words ("get the f*** out of the way"), and PARAMEDIC 1's "we're gonna lose him" is kept as in the current lines.
