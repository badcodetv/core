# Shape 05: The other side wins

I flipped who sets the terms. The crew are the persuaders (they want through); the outrider and the minister are the resisters. Normally the crew would win or rage. Here the crew concede: the glove goes up and the heroes, who were shouting seconds ago, obey, and then they are polite to the man who is killing their patient. The outrage lands because the people we most want to see fight back do not. Pushed to the extreme: the driver ends by apologising to the minister's car.

- **Why it works.** In five seconds the crew are heroes at full volume. Then a single raised glove makes the loudest man in the vehicle say "Yes, sir," and that submission is the outrage: the viewer wants him to floor it and he cannot, because the other side holds the terms. The minister never even sees the crew; he is on the phone, so the resisting side wins without noticing it was a contest. The driver's last line is the only joke: it is sarcastic observation, not a moral, and it sits one second before the monitor goes flat, so the politeness curdles. "What, he's going to die" comes from the viewer, not from us.
- **Cuts from current.** "Come on, get out of the way!" becomes Kai's harder "Get the fuck out of the way!". "What's he doing?!" is removed (the crew no longer question the stop; they accept it, which is the shape). The outrider gets a two-word line. New: "Yes, sir." and "Take your time, sir." Both are proposals for Jack, who writes the jokes.
- **Risk.** A crew that obeys can read as the real-life crew being cowardly, and it works against Kai's wish for desperate heroes in beat B. A Suno speech track may make "Take your time, sir." sound sincere rather than bitter, so the delivery tag matters. The outrider voice has to be audible over the siren in 0.7 s. The driver's politeness could blunt the flatline if it lands as a gag rather than a wound. Fallback: keep "Yes, sir" and cut "Take your time, sir."
- **Protected lines.** "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" is verbatim and fixed. "BP 60 over 30" is verbatim inside Paramedic 1's line, with Kai's "fucking hurry up, we're gonna lose him" added back from the meeting. Medical terms (BP, sats, oxygen) are real UK paramedic language; nothing medical is invented.
