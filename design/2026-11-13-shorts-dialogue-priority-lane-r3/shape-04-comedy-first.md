# Shape 04: Comedy first, bluntness and bad timing

I let the jokes carry the argument. The crew say the hard fact flatly and at the worst moment, so the horror comes from how plain it is. The ambulance half stays dead serious so the crew are heroes. The comedy lives only in the collision: the minister's trivia lands exactly as the crew say something terminal, and one paramedic reacts with a stunned, blunt echo of the trivia. Nobody states a moral. Any new joke below is a proposal for Jack.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane round 3, the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | [shouting over siren, 0-3 s] BP 60 over 30, f***ing hurry up, we're gonna lose him! | 3.0 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [overlapping, flat and fast] Sats seventy-eight and dropping. He's peri-arrest. | 2.0 |
| DRIVER | [5 s, at the glove] Get the f*** out of the way! | 1.5 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, crossing in front] I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres! | 2.2 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [stunned, quiet, under the minister's last word] ...Raspberry? | 0.8 |
| MONITOR | [11-14 s: one long flatline tone, then silence, then the logo] | 0 |
| **Total spoken** | (P2's sats line overlaps P1, so about 2.3 s of it is on the clock) | **about 8.0** |

**Why it works.** The first five seconds are pure panic with real numbers, so the viewer believes he is dying. The driver's line makes the red light outrageous. Then the joke is a collision: a man dies on one side of the glass and on the other a man is furious about fruit-shaped canapes. Paramedic 2's quiet "...Raspberry?" is the bluntness beat: the crew, who have just said "peri-arrest", repeat the trivia back like it was the only thing they could not process. The audience supplies the rest. The flatline is bare and silent, so the laugh dies in their throat and they say "what, he's going to die". No moral, no narrator.

**Cuts from current.** "What's he doing?!" is gone, replaced by the sharper echo "...Raspberry?" because reacting to the line is funnier than asking an open question. "Come on, get out of the way!" is now Kai's own sweary line. "We're gonna lose him" moves into the BP line. A clinical sats/peri-arrest line is added.

**Risk.** The echo can read as mockery of the crew if it is played arch. It must be hushed and stunned, not a gag line. Overlap of P1 and P2 in a Suno speech track may turn to mush, and the minister's line must stay audible over the siren. If Jack finds the echo too cute, delete it and the short still works. Medical terms are real UK paramedic language (BP, sats, peri-arrest); none are invented.

**Protected lines.** "BP 60 over 30" survives verbatim inside P1's line. "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" is verbatim and untouched.
