# Shape 06: Reversal, the listener gets there first

Every crew line is cut off at the exact moment the viewer has already finished the sentence. The crew never says "he's dying because of the minister"; they start a question, the minister's trivia lands on top of it, and the audience supplies the answer. The speaker is always one step behind the viewer, so the outrage is the viewer's own and no moral is ever stated. Lines are short, shoutable and clinically real (BP, sats, "peri-arrest"); the hero lines come first and fast, then the sentences start breaking.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane round 3, the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | [shouting over the siren, hands on the patient] BP 60 over 30 — we're gonna lose him! | 2.4 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [overlapping, eyes on the monitor] Sats dropping — seventy-eight — he's peri-arrest! | 1.6 |
| DRIVER | [A: pulling up at the red, outrider's glove raised] Oh you are fucking joking — get the fuck out of the way! | 2.0 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [B: staring through the windscreen] They're not seriously gonna — he's got ten minutes, he's — | 1.6 |
| MINISTER | [C: on the phone, crossing slowly in front] I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres! | 2.0 |
| MONITOR | [D: flatline tone, no voices, siren cuts out; hold, black, logo] | 0.0 spoken |
| **Total spoken** | (overlap of lines 1-2 not counted twice; flatline and silence 3 s follow) | **8.0** |

- **Why it works.** The viewer is told he is dying in five seconds by two people who do not stop talking, and then the crew run into a wall: the driver's swear is the only complete thought, and Paramedic 2 starts two sentences ("they're not seriously gonna", "he's got ten minutes, he's") that the audience finishes in their own head before the minister's line arrives. The minister's trivia then lands as the answer nobody asked for, in the one gap the crew have left open. Cut-off sentences also read as real panic, since people under pressure abandon grammar, and they keep every crew line under three seconds for Suno. The flatline is silent so the audience, not the speaker, says "he's going to die".
- **Cuts from current.** "What's he doing?!" becomes the longer broken sentence "They're not seriously gonna — he's got ten minutes, he's —". "Come on, get out of the way!" becomes "Oh you are fucking joking — get the fuck out of the way!" (Kai's own driver words). Added: a short overlapping oxygen line from Paramedic 2 ("sats dropping", Kai's "oxygen dropping" made clinical). Dropped: Kai's "hurry up, we're gonna lose him guys" second pass; the single "we're gonna lose him" carries it.
- **Risk.** The cut-off sentences must be performed as interrupted by the scene, not as a TTS glitch: Suno may finish a trailing dash or read it as a flat stop, and the dash line at 1.6 s is the likeliest to be mangled. "Ten minutes" is a medical claim; it is plausible for a critical patient but is an invention, so Jack or Kai should confirm it or swap it for "he's got no time". The overlap in A needs the Suno track to layer, or the two lines will play in series and push the total to about 9.5 s. Swearing and shouting over a siren may reduce intelligibility of "BP 60 over 30"; keep it first, clear and slowest.
- **Protected lines.** "BP 60 over 30" survives verbatim (written "BP sixty over thirty" for voicing only). "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" survives verbatim, untouched, as the last spoken words in the short; nothing is allowed to speak after it but the monitor.
