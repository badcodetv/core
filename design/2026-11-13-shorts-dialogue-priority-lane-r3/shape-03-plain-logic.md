# Shape 03: The logic said plainly, a character reasons it out loud

I let the crew do the arithmetic aloud. A paramedic and the driver state the premises as shouted sums (the minutes he has, the minutes to hospital, so what the road owes us), so that when the ambulance is stopped, the viewer already holds the syllogism and the conclusion is outrage. Nobody states a moral; they state numbers, and the numbers are the argument. Every premise is a clinical or practical fact a crew would really shout, and the minister's line is left to land as the unspoken minor premise.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane round 3, the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | [over the patient, shouting over the siren] BP 60 over 30, he's got four minutes! | 2.4 |
| DRIVER | [overlapping, eyes on the road] Six minutes out! Get the f*** out of the way! | 2.2 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [as the ambulance jolts to a halt, red light, outrider's glove] We've stopped! Why have we stopped?! | 1.6 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, crossing in front of the ambulance] I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres! | 2.5 |
| MONITOR | [flatline tone, silence, black, logo] | 0 (3 s silent hold) |
| **Total spoken** | | **8.7** |

- **Why it works.** In five seconds the viewer gets a sum they can do without thinking: four minutes of life, six minutes of road, so every second matters and the crew are plainly heroes racing a clock they are already losing. That makes the red light land as outrageous because the maths has just been put in the viewer's head, and "We've stopped!" is the answer to the sum. Then the minister's trivia arrives as the only other number in the scene: nothing. No moral is stated and nobody names the decision-maker; the viewer completes the logic themselves. Four-minutes-versus-six also gives Suno clear, shoutable beats, and the shortfall is built in before the stop, so the flatline feels earned rather than arbitrary.
- **Cuts from current.** "We're gonna lose him" is replaced by the countdown (it says the same thing with a number). "Come on" is dropped from the driver for the swear in Kai's own words. "What's he doing?!" becomes "Why have we stopped?!", which asks about the stop rather than the outrider, so it reads as outrage at the delay and not confusion.
- **Risk.** The sum is the whole device: "four minutes" and "six minutes" are invented numbers, and "he's got four minutes" is a bit blunt for a real paramedic (they would more likely say "he's peri-arrest"), so it may read as scripted. Overlapped lines two and three may smear in Suno and lose the numbers. The minutes are never confirmed, so a viewer could read the flatline as bad luck rather than the delay; if the red light is longer than the gap, it is arithmetically fine, but only if the picture holds the stop for a visible beat. A cleaner clinical swap is "he's peri-arrest" for "he's got four minutes", at the cost of the countdown.
- **Protected lines.** "BP 60 over 30" survives verbatim. "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" survives verbatim, unaltered. Kai's "get the f*** out of the way" survives, with "Six minutes out!" added in front of it. Any new joke here is a proposal for Jack; none is proposed.
