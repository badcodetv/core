# Shape 09: Borrowed craft, numbers as a countdown

I stole four devices from things that already compress persuasion. From ER-style medical drama (the Crichton-era overlapping, jargon-and-numbers pace, which Casualty and Holby also run on) I took the idea that the viewer need not understand the words, only the urgency, so every crew line is a number or an order. From Hitchcock's bomb-under-the-table (suspense is the audience knowing what the characters do not) I made the minister the oblivious party while the viewer holds both rooms. From the Godfather baptism cross-cut (a sacrament set against killings, joined by sound) I let the minister's trivial line land over the dying monitor's beeping. From the Kuleshov effect (the cut gives the meaning) I put the flatline straight after his line with no word to explain it. The extreme: the crew's numbers fall each time they speak, so the dialogue is a countdown, and the swearing is rhythm in the way The Thick of It uses it, not decoration.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane round 3, the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | [shouting over the siren, bagging the patient] BP 60 over 30! | 1.1 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [eyes on the monitor, overlapping] Sats seventy-six and dropping, oxygen dropping! | 1.5 |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | Stay with me, mate. [to the driver] We're gonna lose him, hurry up! | 1.5 |
| DRIVER | [leaning on the horn, up front] Get the fuck out of the way! | 1.0 |
| PARAMEDIC 2 | [the ambulance lurches and stops, red light, outrider's glove] What's he doing?! | 0.8 |
| PARAMEDIC 1 | [low, to the patient, pressure is no longer reading] Come on, come on, hurry up. | 0.9 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, heard through the windscreen as his car crosses] I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres! | 2.3 |
| MONITOR | [flatline tone, then nothing, then black, then the logo] | 0 (3 s silent) |
| **Total** | | **9.1 spoken, 3 s silent** |

Note: lines overlap by design (ER style), so the speech runs about 8 s of clock time. If Suno needs it tighter, drop the second Paramedic 1 line at 5.5 s first.

- **Why it works.** The viewer cannot check the medicine, so numbers and shouting do the persuasion: ER's trick is that urgency reads before comprehension. Each crew line is smaller and more desperate than the last (60 over 30, then sats, then "pressure's gone" in the last whisper), so the dialogue itself counts down before any screen does. The red-light stop is made outrageous by contrast: Paramedic 2's "What's he doing?!" is a question the audience answers for themselves as the minister's car crosses. The minister is Hitchcock's oblivious table, laughing over the bomb: he never knows, the audience knows everything. His line is delivered on top of the monitor's beeps, the baptism cross-cut done by ear: the sacred and the trivial in one soundscape. Then silence and black (Kuleshov): with nothing said, the audience writes the moral themselves, which is Kai's "what, he's going to die".
- **Cuts from current.** "Come on, get out of the way!" becomes the Driver's sweary version (Kai's own words); Paramedic 1 gains a quiet "come on" after the stop so the crew stays heroic while held; no line explains the red light, and nothing is said after the minister.
- **Risk.** Overlap can turn to mush in a single Suno speech track, so the numbers may not be intelligible; "76" is the first thing to go wrong, and it is fine if it does as long as the rhythm survives. The quiet "pressure is no longer reading" beat needs the monitor tone under it, or it vanishes. "Hurry up" appears twice, deliberately, but could read as filler. Clinical plausibility: sats and BP are real readouts and "oxygen dropping" is Kai's own; nothing invented beyond the numbers, which are for Kai or a clinician to confirm are plausible together.
- **Protected lines.** "BP 60 over 30" survives verbatim as the opening line. "I said raspberry-shaped hors d'oeuvres!" survives verbatim and is Jack's; only the stage direction (heard through the windscreen) is added. Neither has a new joke attached; the only new material is crew language, which is a proposal for Jack.
