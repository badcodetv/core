# Shape 03: The logic said plainly

The fat cat is the only one who reasons, and he does it out loud, flat and unembarrassed, like a man reading out a rule of the universe. He states the whole argument (you were useful, you are not now, so forget it) in three short clauses, then ends on the protected "Forget it." In beat B the same syllogism comes back trimmed, because he has said it so often he no longer needs all the steps. The veteran and the nurse never speak: their silence keeps the target on him. The button is the same logic applied to the audience, a single deadpan sentence to camera. No moral is stated; the argument is the villain's, and we only let him finish it.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FAT CAT (1919, to the veteran) | You were useful. Now you're not. Forget it. | 3.0 |
| FAT CAT (today, to the nurse) | [same pose, bored, a glance at his watch] Useful then. Not now. Forget it. | 2.6 |
| FAT CAT (button, to camera, unaged, sipping) | CAPTION over the bench: "1919 / TODAY". [beat] Works every time. | 2.0 |
| **Total** | spoken, in a 14 s picture (5 + 5 + 4) | **7.6** |

Button options for Jack, same logic, each under 4 s:
- Spoken: "Works every time." (above)
- Spoken: "Gratitude. Never lasts. Cheaper that way."
- Silent action: he does not speak; he pats the empty bench space beside him, the next person already sitting down. Sound: a single polite clap that stops dead.
- CAPTION only: "He hasn't aged. The logic hasn't either."

**Why it works.** The first line is a complete three-step proof, so the audience learns the rule in beat A. In beat B the proof arrives shortened with the same rhythm (useful, not, forget it), so the echo registers instantly and the shortening is the joke: he has done this so many times the argument has worn smooth. The reasoning is entirely his, so he is the decision-maker and the target, and the soldier and nurse are only ever the ones being reasoned at. "Thanked then forgotten" is carried by "useful" (they were wanted, which the picture shows with a medal and a faded clap) and by the dead-calm cruelty of "Now you're not". The button turns the logic on its own pattern: a rule that works every time is a system, not a mood.

**Cuts from current.** The bare "Forget it." in both beats is padded with the two reasoning clauses; nothing is removed, only added to, and the button gets a spoken line instead of an undefined gap. All stage business (no ageing) stays visual.

**Risk.** Plain logic can sound like a lecture if it runs long: three clauses is the ceiling. "Useful" is blunt and could read as contempt for the veteran and nurse if the drawing makes them pitiful; they must look dignified and the cat must look absurd. "Works every time" borders on a stated moral and may be too on the nose; the silent bench-pat button is safer. The shortened echo in B depends on the voice delivering the same rhythm in a faster, bored read.

**Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, as the closing words of both beats, spoken twice.
