# Shape 08: The place is the argument (the memorial plaque on the bench)

Every word of dialogue is cut to the protected "Forget it." and the argument moves onto one object: a brass plaque screwed to the bench's backrest, "LEST WE FORGET. Presented by the Whaleby Trust." He tells a man who served to forget, sitting on a bench that begs the park to remember. The plaque is held in close-up in every beat, so the echo needs no words, and the button is the plaque getting one line longer.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAPTION (A, over establishing shot, 1919 ink) | 1919. [push in to the plaque: LEST WE FORGET. PRESENTED BY THE WHALEBY TRUST] | 2.0 |
| FAT CAT (to the veteran, not looking up from his paper) | [pats the plaque like a pet] Forget it. | 1.5 |
| CAPTION (B, same frame, nurse in the veteran's place) | Now. [plaque identical, a second, smaller plate screwed under it: THANK YOU HEROES] | 2.0 |
| FAT CAT (to the nurse, same pat, same paper) | [pats the plaque] Forget it. | 1.5 |
| BUTTON (silent action + sound) | [Hold on the plaque. A small screwdriver-less hand-lettered third plate appears in ink under the others: PRESENTED BY THE WHALEBY TRUST. Sound: one dry brass "tink", then nothing.] | 3.5 |
| **Total** | | **10.5** (leaves ~3.5 s of air for held beats; max 14) |

- **Why it works.** The joke is the geometry: the plaque is a permanent instruction to remember, the man sitting under it is a permanent instruction to forget, and the plaque carries his own name, so he is the decision-maker AND the donor, thanked for the gift of the very bench the thanks is screwed to. "Forget it." reads as an echo instantly because the frame, the pat and the plaque do not change, only the person beside him does. The "THANK YOU HEROES" plate is the thanked-then-forgotten beat in two words with no clapping needed. The button needs no moral: the name on the plaque is the same name both times, and the camera lets you read it. He never ages, because the brass never tarnishes.
- **Button alternates.** (1) Silent action, as above. (2) CAPTION over the held plaque: "Bench maintained by the Whaleby Trust. Since 1919." (3) FAT CAT, under his breath, to the camera, after the nurse leaves: "Lest we forget what I paid for this." (4) Sound only: a pigeon lands on the plaque, he flaps it off with the paper, same flap as 1919 (a repeated gesture, no words). (5) CAPTION: "Next to him: you." then the bench has a third seat free (an invite, risks preaching, listed for edges).
- **Cuts from current.** Nothing spoken is lost; "Forget it." is kept twice. Cut: any speech from the veteran or nurse, any explanation of the war or the pay freeze, and the vague "he hasn't aged" button, which becomes the plaque doing the work.
- **Risk.** A plaque is small, and at 9:16 on a phone the text must be readable: it needs a hard close-up and a bold serif, held at least 1.5 s each time. Reading the plaque costs eyes, not ears, so TTS has little to do and Jack may feel it is not funny enough aloud. "Whaleby Trust" must stay invented (check it names no real foundation). The nurse beat risks reading as pay-freeze politics if the second plate is too topical, so keep THANK YOU HEROES generic and undated. The button may read as an in-joke about benches, not about him, unless his face is also visible in the last frame.
- **Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, twice, with only a stage direction (the pat on the plaque) added in square brackets.
