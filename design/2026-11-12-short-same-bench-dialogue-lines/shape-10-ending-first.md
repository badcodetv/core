# Shape 10: Ending first, written backwards

I wrote the last ten seconds first (the second scene's setup and the button) and asked what the button needs the audience to already know. It needs one rule: he says "Forget it" before the other person finishes asking. Once that is the rule, the button is the rule firing with nobody in the chair yet, and A and B are built only to teach it. Taken to the extreme: the victims get a half-sentence each and the fat cat gets the whole joke.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| VETERAN (1919) | Sir, about the pension, I only [cut off] | 1.5 |
| FAT CAT (1919) | [not looking up, newspaper raised] Forget it. | 1.0 |
| CAPTION (card, ink) | TODAY. SAME BENCH. | 1.0 |
| NURSE (today) | Sir, about the pay, I only [cut off] | 1.5 |
| FAT CAT (today, identical, unaged) | [not looking up, newspaper raised] Forget it. | 1.0 |
| FAT CAT (beat C, a third shadow falls across his paper, nobody speaks) | [lowers paper an inch, before a word is said] Forget it. | 1.5 |
| CAPTION (button, silent hold, pen-ink stamp) | NEXT. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **~9.0 spoken/caption + ~5 s of holds and pen-drawn action = about 14 s** |

Alternative buttons to swap into beat C (director's pick):
- C2 (sound): the fat cat says nothing; the bench-slat creak and a pocket-watch tick, then a third shadow falls and his paper rises. Silent action, no line.
- C3 (caption only): CAPTION: "THANKED. THEN FORGOTTEN. SAME MAN." Warning: states the moral, the one thing this shape avoids.
- C4 (line): FAT CAT, glancing at a watch: "Is it that time already? Forget it." Funnier but breaks the pre-emption rule.

**Why it works.** The joke is the interruption: he never hears the ask, so the 'thanks' they were owed is shown by its absence rather than stated. A and B are identical beats with the nouns swapped (pension, pay), so the echo lands without a word of explanation. The button does the "same man, every time" job by proving he no longer needs the other person present: he has said it so often it fires on a shadow. The soldier and nurse are cut off, never mocked; the contempt stays on the man on the bench and the habit, not the technology or the victims. No moral is stated.

**Cuts from current.** Both lines stay; the soldier and nurse get a cut-off half-sentence to make him the interrupter. The open-ended button becomes the pre-emptive "Forget it" plus a stamped caption. No gratitude speech: the thanking happens in the picture (a flag, a clap, bunting), not the dialogue.

**Risk.** Three "Forget it"s may feel like a one-note triple; if the third lands flat, use C2 (silent). "Pension" and "pay" are specific enough to read as the policy fights of their eras, and "NEXT." may read as a queue joke rather than the same-man joke; test both. The cut-off lines are hard for TTS to clip cleanly, so these may need to be sound-designed, not voiced. Nothing here shows him unaged except the drawing, so the art must make "identical" unmistakable (same hat, same watch-chain, same bench slat chipped).

**Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim three times, each on the fat cat.
