# Shape 06: Reversal, the listener gets there first

The point is never spoken by the fat cat. Each time he opens his mouth, the person he is about to dismiss already knows the sentence. In 1919 the veteran is cut off mid-ask; in the present the nurse says the line before he can, and the fat cat is merely pleased at how well-trained she is. The audience works out the echo a half-beat before the speaker does, and the button is the same reversal turned on him: he is the only one who never learns anything.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VETERAN (1919) | [hopeful, hat in hand] "They said the country would see me right, sir, I only..." | 2.5 |
| FAT CAT (1919) | [not looking up from his paper] "Forget it." | 1.2 |
| NURSE (today) | [sits, tired, before he speaks; mouths it flat] "Forget it." | 1.2 |
| FAT CAT (today) | [delighted, same paper, same top hat] "Forget it. Quick learner." | 2.2 |
| CAPTION (button, silent action) | [he checks his pocket watch, it is a 1919 watch; he has not aged; the nurse and the ghost of the veteran's empty seat share a look] CAPTION: "Same bench." | 4.0 |
| Total | | 11.1 (+ ~2.9 s of held pictures and the bench-swap transition = 14 s) |

- **Why it works.** The echo does not need explaining because the nurse performs it: she says his line for him, so the audience realises "this has happened before" at the same moment she does, and the cut to 1919 in beat A has already planted the phrase. "Quick learner" is the dark joke: he treats her weary pre-emption as good manners, which makes him the whole problem without a word of moral. The button is silent: the watch, the unaged face, the empty half of the bench, then a two-word caption. Nobody tells you he is the same man; the picture does.
- **Cuts from current.** The bare "Forget it" x2 grows a setup (the veteran's unfinished ask) and a reversal (the nurse says it first). The vague button becomes a silent action plus a two-word caption. Nothing from the slate is lost except that his first "Forget it" no longer carries beat B alone.
- **Risk.** The nurse saying "Forget it" can read as her being the one who forgets, which would blame the carer. Mitigate with the tired mouthed delivery and a visible flinch (she is quoting, not agreeing). "Quick learner" may be one line too many: if Jack wants it leaner, cut to "Forget it. [he smiles]" and keep the pre-emption alone. Veteran's line in A is the longest and may need trimming to "They said I'd be seen right, sir..."
- **Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, three times (fat cat 1919, nurse today as the echo, fat cat today). The only alteration is the added tag "Quick learner." on the last one, which can be dropped without loss.

Alternative buttons for Jack, in the same shape:
1. Silent: the nurse and the veteran, a century apart, both stand and leave the bench in the same pose; the fat cat does not look up. Hold on the one remaining person: him.
2. Sound: a distant brass band, then a distant round of clapping, both fading to nothing. He turns the page.
3. CAPTION only: "He never has to learn it either."
4. Fat cat, to camera, pleased: "They always get there in the end." (most explicit, most at risk of sermon).
