# Shape 07: The chorus, two pigeons on the bench back

Two pigeons perch on the back of the bench in both eras (they never change either, so the whole park is stuck). The soldier and the nurse say almost nothing; the pigeons are the Greek chorus who clock the echo out loud, so the lead never has to preach. The fat cat only ever says "Forget it." The pigeons name the repeat, one beat late, flatly, as if they have seen this rota before.

- **Why it works.** The echo is the joke, and the pigeons make it impossible to miss without anyone human lecturing: "Quick, that" teaches the audience to hear "Forget it" as a ritual, so in beat B the same two words land with a second laugh, and "Heard that one" is the audience's own thought said by a bird. The fat cat stays the sole decision-maker and target; the soldier and nurse are only ever sympathetic and brief. The thanks-then-forgotten idea is carried by the nurse's frozen pay (she was thanked, the clapping is implied by her scrubs and the cut) and by the soldier's leg. The button is two pigeon lines and a silent third victim, with no stated moral.
- **Cuts from current.** Nothing in the fat cat's lines. Cut: any narrator, any "thanks" speech from him, any explanation of the clapping or the war (the picture does it). The slate's open button gets the pigeons plus a silent action.
- **Risk.** Pigeons could read as cute or twee and soften the dark; the chorus can steal the fat cat's authority if it chatters; "Same bloke" and "Same bench" both say the point, so Jack may keep only one. Two extra voices cost TTS casting time and AI pigeon voices may sound silly in a way that is wanted or not. Silent third victim risks a fourth beat going long. Dropping to one pigeon works if the budget slips.
- **Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, twice, spoken by the fat cat only, never by a pigeon.
