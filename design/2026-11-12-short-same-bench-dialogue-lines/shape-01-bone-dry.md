# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

Cut every word that the picture can say. The only spoken line is the protected "Forget it.", said twice in the same flat voice so the echo does the work, and the button is a third use of it, said before the stranger has opened their mouth. Captions are two words. No explanation, no moral: the repetition is the argument.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAPTION (top, ink-on-newsprint) | 1919. | 0.8 |
| FAT CAT (1919, to the veteran) | [not looking up from his paper] Forget it. | 1.2 |
| (beat A hold: veteran's crutch slides, he stands, goes) | silent | 3.0 |
| CAPTION | Today. | 0.8 |
| FAT CAT (today, to the nurse) | [not looking up from his phone-sized paper, identical pose] Forget it. | 1.2 |
| (beat B hold: she looks at her pay slip, goes) | silent | 3.0 |
| FAT CAT (button, to a third silhouette just arriving at the bench, before it speaks) | [eyes still on paper] Forget it. | 1.0 |
| CAPTION (last frame, held) | Same bench. | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **14.0** |

**Why it works.** Two words, three times, with the pose, the paper and the delivery identical: the audience writes the echo themselves before the nurse appears. He is the only one who speaks, so he is the only one with agency and the only target; the soldier and nurse never get a line to be blamed for. "Thanked then forgotten" is carried by the two year captions and the pay slip, not by speech. The button is the third "Forget it." delivered pre-emptively: he does not need to hear the request, which is the whole man, and it is the same man because it is the same sentence. The caption "Same bench." lands the unaged-cat gag without anyone saying it.

**Cuts from current.** No spoken thanks, no "after all you've done", no narrator, no mention of war, applause or pay. Beat C gains a third "Forget it." where the current slate had an open button. The "hasn't aged a day" idea is carried by the identical drawing, not by words.

**Risk.** Three identical lines can read as lazy if the performance is not varied by a hair: flat, then flatter, then bored. Without any thanks line, "thanked then forgotten" depends on the art (a medal, a clapping-hands poster scrap on the bench in B). The pre-emptive third use may confuse if the third silhouette is not clearly "next person". Fallback button: silent, he turns the page of his paper, and the page is dated 1919 on one side and today on the other. Alt caption: "Next."

**Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, all three times.
