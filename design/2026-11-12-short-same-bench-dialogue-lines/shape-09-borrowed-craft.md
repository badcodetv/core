# Shape 09: Borrowed craft (match cut, Groundhog Day repetition, the look to camera)

I stole structure, not jokes. Four borrowed tools: (1) the match cut across time, as in the bone-to-satellite cut in 2001: A Space Odyssey, where identical framing does the explaining so no words are needed; (2) loop repetition, as in Groundhog Day, where the same day returning IS the argument; (3) the silent-era intertitle (Chaplin-style economy): a two-word caption carries the whole time jump, so no spoken line has to; (4) the look to camera from The Office (UK), where a held glance says what a line would ruin. Up's opening montage is the proof that a few seconds of wordless repetition can carry a whole life. So: the dialogue is almost nothing, the second scene is the first scene re-played with one word changed, and the button is silent. Pushed to the extreme: the only spoken words in the whole short are the protected two.

## Dialogue 1: Same Bench: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAPTION (intertitle, A) | 1919. | 0.5 |
| VETERAN | [holding out his cap, quiet] They said I'd be looked after, sir. | 2.0 |
| FAT CAT | [not looking up from his paper] Forget it. | 1.0 |
| (hard match cut: same bench, same framing, same pose, ink colours shift) CAPTION (intertitle, B) | Now. | 0.5 |
| NURSE | [holding out her lanyard, quiet] They said they'd look after us, sir. | 2.0 |
| FAT CAT | [not looking up from his phone, which is a newspaper] Forget it. | 1.0 |
| BUTTON (silent action) | [Cut to wide on the bench. Both beats' sitters have gone. Fat cat alone, unaged, turns to camera and raises his top hat to the viewer. Two beats of silence. Then a single page-turn sound.] | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **10.0** |

**Why it works.** The match cut makes the echo land before a word is said: same bench, same angle, same hat, so the viewer does the "oh, it's him again" arithmetic themselves, which is far stickier than being told. The speakers' lines are a deliberate copy with one word swapped (looked after / look after us), the Groundhog Day trick: repetition with a tiny change reads as a pattern, and a pattern reads as a system. The fat cat gets the only protected line, twice, with no variation, so he is the constant and the soldier and nurse are the variables: he is the decision-maker and the target. "They said" supplies the thanked-then-forgotten beat with no stated moral, because somebody promised and somebody else shrugged. The button borrows the look to camera: a silent tip of the hat to the audience says "same man, every time" and is also the arrogance, in under four seconds. Spare time (4 s under target) can go to a held last frame or letting beat A breathe.

Alternate buttons, same shape, pick one:
- Silent: the hat-tip above (default).
- CAPTION over the held wide: "Next." (he is already looking at the empty space beside him, patting it.)
- CAPTION: "Same bench. Same man." (more explicit, riskier as it states the point.)
- Sound only: a faint 1919 gramophone crackle that cuts to a phone notification chime on the fat cat's hat-tip, the sound doing the era jump in reverse.
- Callback caption in 1910s ink lettering: "Forget it. (Now with reminders.)" only if Jack wants a joke over a gesture.

**Cuts from current.** Nothing in the protected lines is cut. Added: two one-word captions and one short plea per victim; the button is now a silent action. No pandemic, government, politician or date beyond the generic "1919" and "Now".

**Risk.** The veteran/nurse plea ("They said...") may be too sincere for the sarcastic register and could read as pleading rather than being brushed off; if so, cut it and let the held-out cap and lanyard carry it (this takes the short to 7 s and makes it purer, but loses the thanked-then-forgotten setup, which would then need a caption). A silent hat-tip may be read as ambiguous or as an actual joke about class rather than a button. "Now." may feel too bare against 1919. The match cut depends on the drawn frames being truly identical, so the art pipeline has to hold the fat cat and bench pixel-stable.

**Protected lines.** "Forget it." survives verbatim, spoken twice by the fat cat, identical delivery both times; that exact repetition is the joke.
