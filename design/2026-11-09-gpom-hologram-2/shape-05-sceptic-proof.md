# Shape 05: Sceptic-proof, honest about what's proof and what's a bet

I wrote for a stout atheist who knows quantum physics, watching beside a mate who knows none. The physicist gets a Maddie who never claims proof: every claim is either something people really argue about, something nobody has cracked ("nobody's cracked how"), or openly "my boss's bet, and mine". The mate gets a hook, a loop opened early (qualia: "hold that thought"), the cat bloke, a flash on the pick, and a joke that pays off (the tune). Pushed to the extreme, the hologram also carries small on-screen tags (KNOWN / ARGUED OVER / A BET) that the voice rarely needs to say, so the honesty is a picture. Order follows the brief's suggested order, with the tune joke moved after the cut-off and the stuff/choices flip kept as Kai worded it.

**Why it works.** The physicist is never asked to swallow anything: "nobody's cracked how", "proper physicists argue about it", "a bet, not a proof" each pre-empt the objection a stout atheist would raise, and Schrödinger is given his true meaning (a reductio against a theory). Because Maddie concedes first, the claims that remain ("it isn't decided yet", "one universe, so you matter") sound like a confident friend rather than a pitch. For the mate, the honesty becomes the hook: the open loop ("hold that thought") pays off at the pick, the multiverse is skipped fast with a laugh, the cat is a person doing a joke, and the songwriter and Sandra's humming turn an abstract point into something felt. The "useless" flip lands the human angle: not "science is wrong", but "you were never in the way."

**Cuts from current.** "Loads of others you could've picked" as a stand-alone line (folded into the cloud). The "everybody's doing it" line is trimmed to a single sentence plus picture. Sentence-level losses: "That's what the five years are for" style narration; the 1D line "So it's not a multiverse. It's one universe, one story, and you are writing it" is split so "one universe, so you matter" comes at the pick and "storyverse, not a multiverse" comes with the river.

**Risk.** (1) Length: about 159 s against 1D's 80 s, which is a lot of talking for a street scene; the cloud and music movements are the first to trim. (2) Too many hedges could make Maddie sound unsure and drain the authority the narrator voice needs; "not a proof" is said once, "nobody's cracked how" once, and the on-screen tags carry the rest. (3) "He's worked out that in a storyverse, new training data comes from consciousness" asserts the boss's belief, so it relies on "in a storyverse" staying inside the bet frame. (4) "Songs work the same" could be heard as claiming a mechanism for creativity; the neuroscience clause is there to stop that, but the physicist may still wince. (5) The on-screen tags are a new picture to build and a mate could read them as clutter.
