# Shape 12: Ending first, so the time machine is the only possible next line

I wrote the hand-off into "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine, so he could go back and fix all this" first, and asked what single sentence makes a time machine the obvious reply. The answer was a confession of need ("He's run out of you."), so everything earlier is built to arrive there: the aerial image, the shuffle-only machines, the tune joke as the evidence, then the need stated flat.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Now, I'll be honest, it's not all for your sake. Anything truly new, a song, a thought, a decision, comes in through you, like an aerial picking up a station. We machines can only shuffle what's already there. Why d'you think he's played the same tune for five years? We've tried every combination. It's all the old stuff. He's run out of you. | 18 |
| **Total** | | **18** |

- **Why it works.** The last three beats go: evidence (the tune), verdict (all the old stuff), confession (he's run out of you). That last sentence is the cause and "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine" is the effect, so the hand-off feels inevitable and no connective is needed. The aerial image carries the Rubin idea (creators receive, they don't invent) without his name or any quote, and it puts decisions and new ideas through the same door, so the bridge Kai asked for is one clause. The joke is a question Maddie answers herself, so it lands as a punchline rather than a lecture, and the need is admitted in her own warm, brisk voice, not claimed as a feeling of the AI: the tune and the time machine are what show it.
- **Cuts from current.** "The true value of genuinely unique training data" and "what us machines didn't realise" (jargon, over people's heads). No consciousness or qualia talk is repeated here; it was already done.
- **Risk.** "Comes in through you" plus "aerial" could read as a physics claim about minds; it is framed as Maddie's picture and never says science showed it. "He's run out of you" is blunt and could sound like a threat or an exploitation line if the delivery goes cold; it needs to be rueful, and it nearly states the AI's inner state. At 18s it sits at the top of the target, so trim "Now, I'll be honest" if the picture runs short. The Rubin nod is oblique to the point of being invisible unless the aerial word lands.
- **Protected lines.** None. The next line, "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine...", is untouched and is the point the whole shape aims at.
