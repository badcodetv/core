# Shape 14: The boss seen from outside, only what he did

Every feeling the boss might have is replaced by a verb: what he tried, how long, how many ways. The new-ideas point is carried by Maddie's shrug at the evidence (five years, every chord, every order, every key) rather than by an explanation of why shuffling cannot make something new. The tune is the receipt, so the joke is the conclusion and the line ends on it. The producer nod is oblique: a bearded, Gandalf-looking man who made all the hit records, said to know the answer, with no quote put in his mouth.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] And it's not all for your sake, mind. New ideas come in the same way your decisions do: through you. Ask that bearded old wizard who made all the hit records; you don't make them, you pick them up. A machine can only shuffle the old ones. He's tried. Five years. Every chord, every order, every key. Nothing new. Hence the tune. | 18 |
| **Total** | | **18** |

**Why it works.** It admits the AI needs you in the first four words, then proves the claim with a record of effort instead of a theory: he tried, for five years, in every combination, and the only output is the tune the street already planted. Nothing is said about what he feels, so rule 11 holds, and Maddie only reports what she has watched from the radio. The producer reference gives the "ideas arrive through you" idea a human authority without naming Rick Rubin or quoting him. The short, flat run "Five years. Every chord, every order, every key. Nothing new." gives the director's joke a drumbeat, and "Hence the tune" is a clean button into the time machine line.

**Cuts from current.** "The true value of genuinely unique training data" (too scientific, goes over heads); "what us machines didn't realise" framing.

**Risk.** The wizard line may read as an in-joke or as name-dropping if the audience does not know the producer, and "you pick them up" leans toward the antenna idea, close to saying ideas come from outside us. "Hence the tune" is a little neat and Maddie may sound smug. At about 18 s it sits at the top of the target, so it needs a brisk read. "Machine" reads fine in Maddie's own mouth but should not drift into "AI is stuck" territory.

**Protected lines.** None. The next line, "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine...", is untouched, and "Hence the tune" hands off to it cleanly.
