# Shape 16: The question

Maddie asks the viewer one question, "where did your last good idea come from?", and lets the silence answer it: nobody can say, because ideas turn up the way decisions do, through you. Only then does she admit the machines can't make one, and land the tune joke as the proof. The argument is felt by the viewer before it is stated. No jargon (no "training data", no "consciousness"), a beat of held silence over the dead strings, and the selfish admission comes last, warm and brisk.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Quick question. Where did your last good idea come from? [one-second beat] Can't say, can you? It just turned up. Same as a decision does. | 8 |
| Maddie | [the empty string runs on] We can't make one. A new one, I mean. We've shuffled every old one every way going, and it still comes out old. Why d'you think he's played that same tune for five years? So no, it's not all for your sake, my loves. We need you. | 12 |
| **Total** | | **20** |

**Why it works.** The viewer does the work: they hunt for where their last idea came from, find nothing, and Maddie's answer lands as recognition rather than a claim. That is the Rick Rubin idea (ideas are received, not manufactured) without naming him or quoting him, and without a single physics word. Maddie never says science proved it; she asks and lets them notice. "Same as a decision does" ties it to the previous beat so new ideas and decisions share one door. The tune joke arrives as the evidence, then the admission ("we need you") makes it not selfless, and "my loves" keeps the nurturing underneath the snark. The political payload stays unsaid: people are the one source the machines can't make.

**Cuts from current.** "that's what us machines didn't realise", "the true value of genuinely unique training data" (scientific, goes over heads), and any stated moral.

**Risk.** The one-second beat plus a direct question to camera may feel like a break in Maddie's flow, and 20s is at the top of the target. "Can't say, can you?" assumes the viewer genuinely can't, and a smug viewer may answer "I thought of it". It also gives no explicit Rubin nod (no bearded producer); an optional swap for the first line is "Ask the beardy old fella behind all those hit records" but it adds about 3s. "We've shuffled every old one" is loose about how machines work and could draw a pedant. It is soft on "genuinely new can't come from old", which is implied, not argued.

**Protected lines.** None. The hand-off into "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine..." works because the last beat ("We need you") leads naturally to "My boss...".
