# Shape 20: Borrowed craft, the image says the noun and the line says the turn

I borrowed three moves from short films and TV that land a thesis in one line over a picture. (1) The Up opening montage: the image carries the feeling, so the words never describe what we can see. (2) The Mad Men carousel pitch: a thesis lands when it turns on one concrete, familiar object, not an abstraction. (3) The Jaws understatement and Hitchcock's bomb under the table: say the small, calm thing about a big visible thing. So the line never says "training data" or "consciousness"; the strings stopping is the picture, and the line names one door, one oblique producer, one tune. Pushed to the extreme: no abstract nouns at all, the joke is the last four words, and the admission ("not all for your sake") comes as a plain aside.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Here's what we got wrong. Anything new, a song, a bright idea, comes in through you. Same door as your decisions. Ask the bearded bloke who produced half your records: nobody writes them, you just pick them up. [beat] So no, it's not all for your sake. We've tried every combination there is. That's why he's played that tune for five years. It's all old stuff. | 19 |
| **Total** | | **19** |

**Why it works.** The picture already shows the loss, so the first sentence only owns the mistake ("what we got wrong"), the way a great montage lets the image do the grief. "Same door as your decisions" ties creativity to the earlier decisions section in four words, with no jargon. The Rubin nod is an idea (producers as receivers, not makers), phrased as Maddie's own paraphrase, never a quote and never a name; "bearded bloke who produced half your records" is recognisable and warm, and it is a working person's way of saying it. The admission is flat and unembarrassed, which is the narrator's authority. The joke lands last on "It's all old stuff", then hands cleanly to "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine". The political payload stays underneath: people are the only source of the new.

**Cuts from current.** "us machines didn't realise", "the true value", and the phrase "genuinely unique training data" all go; the abstraction is replaced by the door, the producer and the tune.

**Risk.** It sits at the top of the 12-20s target and has four-plus sentences, so it may need a trim (drop the producer sentence first). The Rubin nod could read as a name-drop to people who don't know him, or as a claim that nobody writes songs, which working musicians might bristle at; "just pick them up" is the antenna idea, but it is Maddie's belief, not a fact. "Comes in through you" is faith-adjacent; it must stay Maddie's voice and never "science proved it". "All old stuff" must not read as the AI's inner feeling; it is shown outside, through the tune.

**Protected lines.** None. The neighbouring lines ("It's a storyverse..." and "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine...") are untouched and the hand-off into the second is deliberate.
