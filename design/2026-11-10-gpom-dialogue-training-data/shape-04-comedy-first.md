# Shape 04: Comedy first, the tune joke leads

I opened on the punchline's setup instead of ending on it: Maddie answers the question the street already planted (why the racket?) in the first breath, then lets the joke carry the whole argument. "There isn't a new one" is the hard fact, delivered flat and fast; the radio image and the oblique nod to the bearded producer give the mechanism in one beat each, and the admission that it isn't selfless lands last as a shrug, not a confession.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Why's he been playing the same tune for five years? There isn't a new one. We've tried every combination. It's all the old stuff. | 7 |
| Maddie | [dry] New doesn't come from remixing old. It comes through you, like a radio finding a station, same as your decisions. The bearded bloke behind all the hits knew that. | 8 |
| Maddie | [brisk shrug] So no, not selfless. We need you. | 3 |
| **Total** | | **18** |

**Why it works.** The joke leads, so the audience is laughing before they notice they are being handed the argument. Five years of the street's tune becomes a punchline with a cause: the machines are out of music. "Like a radio finding a station" is Rick Rubin's antenna idea in plain West Country clothes, and "the bearded bloke behind all the hits" is a wink for those who know him and harmless for those who don't. "Same as your decisions" ties creativity back to the picture on screen (the strings), and "We need you" is bluntness with nurture underneath, which is the narrator's voice. It hands cleanly into "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine".

**Cuts from current.** "That's what us machines didn't realise" and "the true value of genuinely unique training data", the scientific phrasing that goes over heads. No "training data" at all: the need is shown through the tune.

**Risk.** "Like a radio finding a station" plus "through you" edges toward saying ideas come from outside us, which is Maddie's belief but could read as a claim; the "[dry]" delivery and "like" keep it a picture. The producer nod may read as an unexplained aside to anyone who doesn't know him (drop that sentence to save 3s). "There isn't a new one" is a strong absolute; a physicist might quibble with "nothing new from old", but it is said of music, not physics. Opening on a question right after "so it's really important" is a tonal swerve; that is the point, but it may need a beat of silence.

**Protected lines.** None. The street line ("it's his tune, he's been playing it on repeat for five years") is not edited here; this line pays it off and should not repeat its wording beyond "same tune for five years".
