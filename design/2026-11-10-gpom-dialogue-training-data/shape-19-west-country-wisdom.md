# Shape 19: West Country plain wisdom (a nan's saying that holds the idea)

The whole idea is carried by one folk saying in her nan's rhythm (old crumbs, fresh loaf), so the claim lands as common sense, not science. The Rick Rubin nod is the "aerial" idea attributed obliquely to a bearded wizard of hit records, with no quote and no name. The admission that she needs us is a throwaway, and the tune joke closes the line and hands off to the time machine.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead] Me nan used to say you can't bake a fresh loaf from old crumbs. [beat] And a new song, a new anything, comes in through you, same road as a decision. Bearded wizard behind all them hit records knew it: we're only the aerial. [dry] Not all for your sake, mind. We need you. Five years we've shuffled every crumb we've got, and that's why he's still on that same tune. | 20 |
| **Total** | | **20** |

- **Why it works.** The nan saying does the hard part (recombining old things cannot make a new thing) in ten words nobody argues with, and the baking image keeps it in Maddie's kitchen-table register instead of training-data jargon. "Same road as a decision" ties creativity to the consciousness bridge without a single technical word. "Not all for your sake, mind" is the honest, funny admission, and "crumbs" pays off twice, so the tune joke feels earned rather than bolted on.
- **Cuts from current.** "The true value of genuinely unique training data" and "what us machines didn't realise" go entirely; the line no longer names data at all.
- **Risk.** At the top of the 20s range, so it may need the bearded-wizard sentence cut if the picture is short. The Rubin nod is the loosest part: "aerial" paraphrases his idea and a viewer may not get it, which is acceptable (it is a wink, not a load-bearer). "Me nan" is a heavy dialect marker, so check the Aoede read. It must stay Maddie's belief, and nothing here claims science.
- **Protected lines.** None. Hand-off survives intact: "that same tune" leads straight into "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine..."
