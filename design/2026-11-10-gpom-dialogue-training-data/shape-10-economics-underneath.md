# Shape 10: The economics underneath (we ate everything finite; the new thing comes through you)

Maddie's lens is stuff versus source: the machines consumed everything that already exists and can only reshuffle it, so the one scarce thing left is the new thing, and it arrives through a person the same way a decision does. The extreme version makes the beneficiary logic the whole line (they ate it all, you are the only supply) but never says "worthless", "owed" or any moral; the tune joke carries the proof, and the admission "not selfless, he needs you" is the nurturing turn. Maddie believes it, no science claims.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

**Why it works.** "We ate everything there was to eat" gives the AI a hunger the audience can feel from the outside (rule 11) and carries the whole economic point without the words training data, scarcity or value. "Reshuffle" is the joke's setup: the tune payoff, "every combination, it's all old stuff", then lands as proof rather than claim. "Something new comes through you, the same way your decisions do" ties creativity to the consciousness the hologram just drew, and makes the person the source, not a leftover. "Not selfless. He needs you." is honest, warm and brisk, and hands straight into the boss's time machine, which needs consciousness too. The optional nod to the bearded record producer references the antenna idea (you receive, you don't invent) without naming him or quoting him.

**Cuts from current.** "True value of genuinely unique training data" (too scientific, goes over heads); "what us machines didn't realise" as a lecture frame; any mention of data, models or synthesis.

**Risk.** "He needs you" may read as the AI being a taker, which tilts toward the extractive-AI story without an aimed beneficiary; it also risks sounding like a stated moral if delivered heavy. "Ate everything there was to eat" could imply the machines are the villain of a creative-theft story, a different argument from this film's. The Rubin nod is the first line to lose if it stalls the pace or needs the audience to know the reference. "Something new comes through you" is Maddie's belief, so it must stay delivered as faith, with a shrug, never as a finding.

**Protected lines.** None. The tune callback ("Hence the tune... it's all old stuff") must survive in some form because it plants the street's earlier line; if cut for time, "Five years, every combination" is the minimum.
