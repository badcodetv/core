# Shape 02: The picture carries it

The strings stopping dead and the empty string running on for five years already say "nothing new since you went", so the words never describe the picture or explain the mechanism. Maddie lets the image land in a held beat, then adds only what a picture cannot: that new things arrive through people the way decisions do, that machines can only shuffle what exists, that this is not selfless, and the tune joke as the punchline. No jargon (no "training data"), the producer nodded to in one clause, and the last words set up the time machine line.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off; she lets the empty string run on for a beat] Anything new comes in the way a decision does: through you. Ask any old bloke with a beard who's made a hit record. You don't make it, you tune in. | 9 |
| Maddie | We can only shuffle what's already in the room. Five years of shuffling, that's the tune. Every combination. Told you it wasn't all selfless: we need you. | 7 |
| **Total** | | **16** |

**Why it works.** It relies on the picture for the grief and the fact: the beads stopping and the bare string are the "nothing new" statement, so no line has to say it, and the opening silence is a beat of picture. Words carry only the claim the image cannot (ideas arrive through people) and the confession (not selfless). The bearded producer clause gives the Rubin idea (you tune in, you are an antenna) without a name or an invented quote. "That's the tune" re-points the street's plant as the joke, and "we need you" is the admission, stated as a practical fact rather than a feeling, so the AI's inner life stays unclaimed. It reads as belief from Maddie, no science asserted, and "we need you" hands off to "My boss has been hard at work..." cleanly.

**Cuts from current.** "What us machines didn't realise", "the true value of genuinely unique training data" (too scientific, goes over heads), and any explanation of why recombination fails (the empty string shows it).

**Risk.** It leans hard on the visual: if the hologram rebuild does not make the dead stop and the long empty run legible, the opening beat is just silence and the first sentence arrives unmotivated. "Shuffle" may read as too weak an account of why recombination cannot make new things, and "any old bloke with a beard" may be too oblique for viewers who do not know the Rubin idea (it still works as a folk-wisdom line). "Told you" refers back to the street line, which is a few minutes earlier, so the callback is thin if the viewer has forgotten.

**Protected lines.** None. The street plant ("Sorry about the racket... five years now") must survive, since the joke depends on it, and the following "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine..." is untouched.
