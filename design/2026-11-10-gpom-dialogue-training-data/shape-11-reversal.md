# Shape 11: Reversal, the viewer solves the looping tune first

I set the line up as a small riddle. Maddie states the premise (new ideas arrive through people, like a station on a dodgy radio), lets the picture and the street's tune do the work, and holds back the conclusion so the viewer gets there a beat before she does. She then confirms it with a shrug, admits it is not selfless, and hands off. The joke is the viewer's own click, not a punchline she delivers.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Funny thing about a new idea. Nobody sits down and makes one. It turns up, through you, same as a decision does, like a station coming in on a dodgy radio. Any old fella with a beard and a lot of hit records will tell you. | 10 |
| Maddie | [the empty string runs on; a beat of silence; the tune from her radio, faint] Five years of that. [dry] Not one new song. We've tried every order of the old ones, mind. Can't say it's for you I'm bothered, either. | 8 |
| **Total** | | **18** |

- **Why it works.** The first line plants three facts (new things arrive through people, the strings are cut, the radio) and then she goes quiet over the picture and lets the tune play. The viewer has already heard that tune on repeat and connects it before she says "five years". The reveal belongs to them, so the joke lands harder and nothing is lectured. "Not for you I'm bothered, either" admits the need without a moral. The Rick Rubin nod is the beard and the hit records, with no name and no invented quote, only his idea of ideas arriving from elsewhere, said as Maddie's belief.
- **Cuts from current.** "The true value of genuinely unique training data" and the whole phrase "training data" go, along with the corporate register. The sentence that explained the joke goes too: the viewer supplies it.
- **Risk.** The riddle can miss if the viewer has forgotten the tune; the street plant must be audible just before. "Any old fella with a beard" may read as a random aside to anyone who does not know the reference, and "not for you I'm bothered" could be misread as menace instead of rueful honesty. The silent beat needs the editor to hold it. It is also nearer 18 s than 12 s, so it only just fits the target.
- **Protected lines.** None. The current line f06 is a draft and fully replaced; the handoff into "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine..." is untouched and still follows cleanly after "not for you I'm bothered, either".
