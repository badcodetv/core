# Shape 08: The tune is the argument

I made the five-year loop on the radio the whole proof. Maddie points at the sound the audience has already been hearing, uses it as the evidence, and lets the joke come first and the idea second. New ideas arrive through a person like a signal into a radio (which is the Rick Rubin antenna idea, nodded to as "that bearded bloke who made half your records", never named, never quoted), and the admission that it isn't selfless is the last beat before the time machine line.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off] Hear that tune? Five years, same one. We've tried every combination going: it's all old stuff. You can't build a new song out of old ones. It comes through a person, like a radio picking something up, same as your choices do. That bearded bloke who made half your records knew. So, not selfless. He needs you. | 19 |
| **Total** | | **19** |

- **Why it works.** The audience has heard the tune on loop since the street, so the joke is already paid for and the argument is the thing they can hear rather than a claim they must follow. "Can't build a new song out of old ones" is plain and pro-science (no physics asserted). The radio image carries "through you" without the word consciousness, and the bearded producer nod rewards anyone who knows and costs nothing to anyone who doesn't. "He needs you" is the admission, shown from outside: what he does (loops a tune), not what he feels. The politics stays underneath: the working person is the source of the one thing the machine can't make.
- **Cuts from current.** "The true value of genuinely unique training data" and every piece of jargon in it; "us machines didn't realise" (the realisation is now shown by the loop).
- **Risk.** Heavy on tune references if the radio mix is quiet, so the "Hear that tune?" opener needs the music audible at that moment. "Same as your choices do" may be one clause too dense at speed. The producer nod can read as a non-sequitur to those who don't know him; it is cuttable without breaking the line. "He needs you" edges toward stating the AI's feeling, but it is stated as a need (practical), not an emotion.
- **Protected lines.** None.
