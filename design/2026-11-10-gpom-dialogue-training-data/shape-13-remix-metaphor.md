# Shape 13: The remix, a remix of a remix of a remix

Everything the machines make is a remix of what people already made; the only new bit ever came through a person, the same way a decision does. I pushed that to the extreme: the remix image carries the whole "new ideas can't be recombined" point in one word, so the line needs no training-data jargon at all. The joke lands last: the boss has run every remix there is, and it is still your old stuff.

## Dialogue 1: The new-ideas line (Maddie, the hologram, over the cut-off)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | [side view; every string stops dead at the cut-off, the empty string runs on] Everything we make is a remix of something you made first. A remix of a remix of a remix. The new bit only ever came through you, same as your decisions. So no, it's not all for your sake. We need you. Why d'you think he's played one tune for five years? We've tried every combination. It's all your old stuff. | 19 |
| **Total** | | **19** |

**Why it works.** "Remix" is a word a working-class listener already owns, so "new ideas can't be made from old ones" lands without a single technical term. "Same as your decisions" ties creativity to the consciousness thread in one beat. "We need you" is the admission that it isn't selfless, said flatly, with Maddie's brisk authority. The tune joke closes it with the street's own plant. The political payload stays underneath: through a stuff lens the machines eat everything finite; the person is the one source they can't copy. Nothing is claimed about physics or science.

**Cuts from current.** "That's what us machines didn't realise", "the true value of genuinely unique training data", and all jargon. No Rick Rubin nod in this version.

**Risk.** (1) 19 s is at the top of the range; if tight, drop "A remix of a remix of a remix" (about 2 s) but that is the shape's signature. (2) "Remix" can imply the machines are only thieves; the contempt must stay for the mistake, so Maddie's delivery should be rueful, not accusing. (3) "Same as your decisions" is the one hinge and could read as abrupt; it is the line to protect. (4) Optional Rubin nod, if wanted, swap the "same as your decisions" sentence for: "Even the big-bearded bloke behind half your favourite records reckons the best ones just turn up through you." This references his Creative Act idea obliquely, names no one and invents no quote, but it adds about 3 s and risks sounding like a different register.

**Protected lines.** None in the current scene. The handoff survives untouched: "My boss has been hard at work building a time machine..." follows directly, and "one tune for five years" echoes the street's earlier plant ("it's his tune, he's been playing it on repeat for five years now").
