# Shape 05: Chill guy from the future

Totally unbothered, polite and warm, a holiday rep or a customer-service voice reporting the end of the world as a minor scheduling matter. The calm is the joke and the dread: the more cheerful the delivery, the worse the reel underneath looks. Every line is a service announcement; none of them raise their voice, and the care shows only in how gently it is all said. Pushed to the extreme: BadCode apologises for the inconvenience of civilisation ending.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-2 s) | Hi there! [sunny, a little too pleased] Greetings from the future. | 2.0 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | [one slow breath, perfectly pleasant] The future's been cancelled. Apologies for any inconvenience. | 3.8 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel) | [softer, like holding a door open] Here's what to do next. | 1.6 |
| **Total** | | **7.4** |

- **Why it works.** The hook sounds like the warm opening it plays over, so the nostalgia is never interrupted, only welcomed; "Greetings from the future" tells us who BadCode is in one breath and lands inside two seconds. The cut to the masked figure turns the same cheerful register into menace: the voice has not changed, the face has, and "cancelled" is the first word that tells us the cheer is a lie. "Apologies for any inconvenience" is pure customer service for an extinction, which is dry, sarcastic and total authority with no shouting. The reel then speaks for itself, with nothing to explain it. The protected line returns on black in the warmest register, so the polite voice is finally the nurturing one: a rep who has stopped joking and is about to help.
- **Cuts from current.** "Hello. I'm from the future." becomes "Hi there! Greetings from the future." (more chipper, more service-desk). "It didn't go well." goes entirely: it was understatement from a sad narrator; here the understatement is replaced by a bureaucratic euphemism ("cancelled", "inconvenience") that says more and sounds funnier. "Here's what to do next." stays.
- **Risk.** Corporate cheer can read as a brand voice mocking itself rather than BadCode, or as a joke about customer service with the point lost. "Apologies for any inconvenience" could feel flip about people who really are suffering (cost of living, war), which breaks the voice rule that contempt aims at the mistake and never at people; the on-black line must carry the care to rebalance. It also leans on delivery: read flat, it is just corporate. It depends on the voice casting (the current temp voice, calm and very polite, is close to right). "Cancelled" may be too cute and dilute the horror; a fallback is "The future didn't make it. Apologies for any inconvenience."
- **Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim, nothing after it. The only change is a stage direction ([softer, like holding a door open]) so it reads as a rep ending the call with real care.
