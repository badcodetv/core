# Shape 02: The picture carries it

I treated every word as a cost and spent it only where no shot can do the job. The warm opening, the masked figure and the reel already say "this is nostalgia", "this is someone watching you" and "it went wrong", so the lines do the one thing pictures can't: put a dry, certain voice over them, so the viewer knows who is talking and that the warmth is doomed. I pushed this to the extreme of about 4 seconds of speech, so the director can see how little is needed and how much of the 8-second target is optional.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, C1-a blue hour into the 00s meme-edit shots; hook lands by 2 s) | Hello. [flat, kind] Enjoy this. | 1.6 |
| BadCode (masked figure, to camera, 4-7 s; the mask shot is held still, no cutaway) | I'm from the future. [one beat of silence after, then hard cut to the reel] | 1.3 |
| BadCode (on black after the reel, music at nothing) | Here's what to do next. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **4.4** |

- **Why it works.** "Enjoy this" is the whole turn: said kindly over the warmest shot in the film, it tells the viewer the warmth is about to be taken away, which is exactly what the cut to the figure then confirms. The figure no longer has to say "it didn't go well", because the reel (the 0.5 s real-footage shots of data centres, climate disaster, cost of living, war) says it better than any line, and the silence after "I'm from the future" lets the viewer fill in the rest. Who BadCode is arrives in the figure shot, the mask carries the menace, and the voice carries the care. The two quiet gaps (after the figure, and the dead black before the button) are doing the writing. The last line stays protected, so the promise of a way out lands on a pure silence, which is the strongest place for it.
- **Cuts from current.** "It didn't go well." goes entirely (the reel is that sentence). "Hello. I'm from the future." is split: "Hello." stays on the hook, "I'm from the future." moves to the figure, where the mask makes it land as a reveal rather than a greeting. "Enjoy this." is the only new text.
- **Risk.** (1) "Enjoy this" could read as a plain instruction, or as bossy, rather than gallows-kind; it depends on the voice delivery (the temp Sadaltager voice is polite and deadpan, which suits it, but casting is still owed). (2) "I'm from the future" on its own never says the future went wrong; if the reel is weak or the viewer scrolls before it, the "went wrong" half of the premise is missing and the short reads as a generic sci-fi teaser. (3) Under-speaking may fail Kai's note that the lines are too basic only if the lines are still basic; here they are short but the character is in the delivery, so a flat read would sink it. (4) At 4.4 s of speech the piece sits well under the 8 s target; the director may want more voice.
- **Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim, with nothing after it, as Jack ruled.
