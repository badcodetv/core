# Shape 09: borrowed craft from trailers, cold opens and channel intros

I took four habits from how trailers and intros compress a premise and pushed each to its limit. (1) The tagline as a finished fact: Alien's "In space no one can hear you scream" and Fallout's "War never changes" state the world in one sentence and never explain it. (2) The cold open that is the thumbnail: the first two seconds must work with the sound off in a feed, as Mr. Robot's and Breaking Bad's cold opens do, so the hook is the shortest line that carries the whole premise. (3) The trailer stinger: the joke or promise that lands after the music dies, the way many film trailers keep their last line for after the silence. (4) Withholding: a trailer never narrates the spectacle it is showing, so the reel gets no words. The whole script is built so each moment does exactly one job and none repeats another.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-2 s) | Enjoy this. [one beat] I checked. | 1.8 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | Hello. I'm from the future. [a beat, no blink] Yours. | 3.2 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel, silence first) | Here's what to do next. | 1.8 |
| **Total** | | **6.8** |

**Why it works.** The hook is a tagline in the Fallout manner: a calm statement of fact that turns the warm picture into a countdown. "I checked" is the dry, total-authority, slightly nurturing move: it has the receipts, and it is letting you have this one good minute first. It lands inside two seconds and works as text on a muted feed. The figure then does the cold-open reveal, giving the identity line the old script had ("Hello. I'm from the future.") and spending the saved words on one word, "Yours.", which is the stinger of the middle: it takes the sci-fi premise off the screen and puts it on the viewer. That says "a future that went wrong" without the words "went wrong", because the hook's "I checked" already told you how it looked. The reel is silent of words, as a trailer's money shots are. The button is Jack's ruled line, untouched, and it is the only promise in the piece, so it carries the follow.

**Cuts from current.** "It didn't go well." goes: it is the weakest line (Kai's 'very basic interpretation') and it narrates what the reel is about to show. "Hello. I'm from the future." is kept but moves from the hook to the figure, so the warm opening gets a line that bites instead of a greeting.

**Risk.** "Yours." is one word that a flat voice or a bad cut will make into nothing; it needs the beat before it and a held close-up. "Enjoy this. I checked." could read as menace without the nurturing warmth, which is the voice-casting job. Past-tense authority is lost: nothing now says "it went badly" in words, so the reel alone must carry the horror (that is the intent). Two-word hook ends after the picture's first cut point, so timing against the synthwave needs checking. These are guesses until heard.

**Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim, alone on black, nothing after it.
