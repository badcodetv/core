# Shape 10: Ending first, plant the debt

I wrote the last moment first: on black, after the reel, "Here's what to do next." must feel like a promise being kept, so following the channel is the only way to collect. Working backwards, the figure's line makes the promise (it came back with a task, not just a warning) and the hook sets the tone of a patient, slightly smug guide who will be brief. Nothing is said in the reel. Nothing is said after the button; the logo carries the follow.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-3 s, hook lands by 2 s) | Hello. I'm from the future. [warm, unhurried, a half-smile in the voice] You're doing lovely. | 3.4 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | It didn't go well. [one beat, no blink] I came back with homework. | 3.0 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel and the slowed music, about 17 s) | [a full second of nothing] Here's what to do next. | 2.0 |
| **Total** | | **8.4** |

- **Why it works.** The ending is "homework": the figure says it came back with a task, so "Here's what to do next." on black is the assignment arriving, and the logo that follows is the only place to collect it. "You're doing lovely" is the nurturing-underneath, sarcastic-on-top voice over the 00s warmth, and it is a lie the cut then exposes, so the turn from nostalgia to the figure carries a double meaning without a word of explanation. "It didn't go well" keeps its deadpan; "homework" is only BadCode's word, dry and bossy and kind. The reel stays silent, and the full second of nothing before the last line gives the slowed music somewhere to land.
- **Cuts from current.** Nothing is dropped. Two additions: "You're doing lovely." on the hook and "I came back with homework." on the figure.
- **Risk.** The hook is now 3.4 s, past the 2-second target for the whole thing; "Hello. I'm from the future." alone lands in about 2 s, but "You're doing lovely" may crowd the first cut. If so, cut it, or lay it under the first cut of the figure. "Homework" could read as childish or as a school joke and soften the horror, and it makes "what to do next" sound light, which may undersell the reel; if the director wants the promise graver, swap for "I came back with instructions". The ending only works if the button's second of silence survives the music slow-down. This is a guess about delivery, not tested.
- **Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim and stays last; only a stage direction (the second of silence) is added before it, and nothing is spoken after it.
