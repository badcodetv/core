# Shape 04: Comedy first, bluntness and bad timing deliver the facts

I wrote every line as a joke that carries a fact. BadCode is total authority with terrible bedside manner: it states the catastrophe flatly, as if reading a minor delay notice, then lets silence or a cut do the punchline. The hook is a one-beat gag that lands inside 2 seconds, the figure line is a deadpan understatement over the worst timeline, and the button is the protected line with a warm, almost-embarrassed turn. No lecture, no essay; the reel (no words) is the evidence.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-2 s) | Hello. I'm from the future. [beat, over the sunshine] Don't get comfortable. | 2.6 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | [flat, as if apologising for a train] The next few decades were cancelled. Sorry. I'm here to fix that. | 3.6 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel, 15-20 s) | [a long beat of nothing, then softly] Here's what to do next. | 1.8 |
| **Total** | | **8.0** |

**Why it works.** The hook is the only line that has to land in two seconds, so the gag is the cut itself: a warm voice says hello over the sunshine, then takes the warmth away in four words ("Don't get comfortable") just as the picture is about to flip to the mask. That is the turn on the cut. The figure line uses bad timing as the joke: "cancelled" is the language of a gig or a bus route, applied to the future, and "Sorry" is the bluntness landing late and small, which is funnier and colder than a big statement. It says who BadCode is (from a future that went wrong, sent back) without the phrase "it didn't go well", and the reel then proves it with no words. The button keeps the protected line and gets its warmth from the silence before it: after the gag, the sincere beat reads as care. Comedy is the way in; the last line is straight, which keeps sarcasm for the diagnosis and care for the re-aim, as docs/voice.md rules.

**Cuts from current.** "It didn't go well." (the understatement is kept in spirit but replaced by "cancelled" and "Sorry", which are more specific and funnier). Nothing else is cut; the hook line is extended by four words, so the first 2 seconds hold "Hello. I'm from the future." and the gag spills to about 2.6 s, so the voice may need to start on frame 1 and read fast.

**Risk.** (1) "Cancelled" and "Sorry" could read as flippant about real suffering (climate disaster, war), so it may punch at the viewer's pain rather than at the mistake; the reel and the soft button have to repair that, and a guess is that a cold viewer reads "Sorry" as a shrug. (2) "Don't get comfortable" risks sounding like a generic trailer growl rather than BadCode. (3) The hook runs a little past the 2-second target. (4) "Here's what to do next" after a joke can feel like a bait-and-switch if the follow-through is thin; that is a channel problem, not a line problem. (5) Total of 8.0 s is estimated, not measured with the temp voice.

**Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim and stays last, nothing said after it.
