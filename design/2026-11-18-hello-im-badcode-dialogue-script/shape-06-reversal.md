# Shape 06: Reversal, the viewer works out the point first

BadCode never announces the future, never says it went wrong, never says "hello, I'm BadCode". The hook is a warm instruction that sounds like kindness, the figure refuses to explain itself and points at the reel, and the viewer does the arithmetic (warm past + a voice that says "while you can" + a masked face that won't tell the year = it ended badly). Only the button states anything plainly, and it states the promise, not the diagnosis. The same approach is used for every dialogue: say less than the viewer already suspects, and let the reveal be theirs.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-2 s) | Enjoy this one. [a beat] Properly. | 1.8 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | Don't ask what year I'm from. [beat, deadpan] Look. | 2.6 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel) | Here's what to do next. | 1.6 |
| **Total** | | **6.0** |

- **Why it works.** The hook is a kindness with a threat folded inside: "properly" implies this will not be on offer for long, so the viewer feels the wrongness half a second before the cut confirms it. The figure then withholds the one fact the channel is built on (when it is from) and turns the question into an order, "Look", which hands the next 7 seconds to the reel and makes the reel the answer rather than an illustration. By the time the music dies, the viewer has already concluded the future went wrong, so the protected button lands as relief, not as another complaint. Dry, authoritative, and the care is in "Enjoy this one": it wants them to have the good thing before it shows the bad.
- **Cuts from current.** "Hello. I'm from the future." (the future is now implied, never said); "It didn't go well." (the reel says it, and saying it first spoils the inference). No greeting at all, so the title "Hello, I'm BadCode" has to carry the hello, or the figure's presence does.
- **Risk.** Cold viewers who do not catch the implication may read the hook as a creepy wellness line and not understand what BadCode is until the end, and nothing in the words says "from the future" for a first-time channel visitor (the trailer job); if so, restore one plain clause such as "Don't ask what year I'm from" becoming "I'm from further on. Look." "Properly" and "Look" depend entirely on delivery (a half-beat before each) and on the cut landing on "Look"; a flat read makes both lines bland. Total speech is about 6 s against the 8 s target, so there is room for one extra line if the director wants more character, but it would weaken the shape. Unverified guess: that the viewer infers the point without being told; test it on someone who has not seen the channel.
- **Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim, last and alone on black, nothing after it.
