# Shape 07: Meme captions

Every line is written as the caption of a 00s/2016 meme edit: a setup-and-spoiler rhythm, one idea, quotable, and readable on screen as the burned-in caption (the edit already carries the colourful meme text, so the spoken line and the caption are the same words). I borrowed the grammar of the format ("Spoiler:", "Enjoy this.", the patch-notes joke) rather than any specific meme, and pushed it until the figure speaks in a tagline and the hook is a deadpan instruction to savour the footage. Only BadCode speaks; the reel has no words; the protected last line is untouched.

## Dialogue 1: BadCode's lines (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BadCode (voice over the warm opening, 0-4 s) | Hello from the future. [beat] Enjoy this. | 2.3 |
| BadCode (masked, to camera, 4-7 s) | Spoiler: it went badly. [beat] I'm the patch. | 3.0 |
| BadCode (on black, after the reel) | Here's what to do next. | 1.6 |
| **Total** | | **6.9** |

- **Why it works.** The hook lands inside two seconds with "Hello from the future." and the second sentence, "Enjoy this.", is the turn: a friendly instruction that tells the viewer the warmth is borrowed, so the cut to the figure feels like the bill arriving. "Spoiler: it went badly." is the exact grammar of a meme caption and says "future that went wrong" in four words, with the total authority of someone who has read the ending. "I'm the patch." answers who BadCode is (a fix sent back, in software language the channel's audience already speaks) and carries the care underneath: the contempt is for the mistake, the figure is here to repair it. It also sets up "Here's what to do next." as the patch notes. Every line fits on one on-screen caption card, so the words survive muted autoplay.
- **Cuts from current.** "I'm from the future" becomes "from the future" inside a greeting (the "I'm" is gone, so it reads as a sign-off or a postcard). "It didn't go well." becomes "Spoiler: it went badly.", which is blunter and more meme-shaped, and gains a second sentence that says what BadCode is for.
- **Risk.** The meme grammar may date quickly or read as a brand trying too hard, and "Spoiler:" plus "patch" is borderline cute against Kai's "BadCode wouldn't say that" note (it could swing from basic to glib). "Patch" also leans on software jargon some viewers will skip. "Enjoy this." may read as sneering at the warm footage rather than caring about the viewer; the delivery must be dry and warm, never mocking. If the figure line runs over 3 s it crowds the 4-7 s window.
- **Protected lines.** "Here's what to do next." survives verbatim, as the only words on black, with nothing after it.
