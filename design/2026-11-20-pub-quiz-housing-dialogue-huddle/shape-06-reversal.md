# Shape 06: Reversal, the student gets there first

The student, who guesses lowest, does the sum out loud from the plasterer's job before the banker can finish his sentence, and the plasterer (who has seen the rooms) is the one who doubts it. The banker confirms rather than explains, and Bob settles it quietly with his own nine months and writes the number. Every guess is flagged as a guess; no one states a statistic. Joke slots are left open, no jokes written.

**Why it works.** The viewer is the student: low guess, then the same doubt. Letting him reach "billions" by arithmetic before the banker finishes his sentence makes the argument something the viewer does with him, not something an expert tells them, and it keeps the banker calm and right without a lecture. The plasterer's doubt is the slight argument, and it is the more convincing for coming from the man who has seen the rooms. Bob arrives after the sum is done and adds the one thing no sum has: he was there. He never explains, he writes.

**Cuts from current.** The banker's full explanation ("renting it, not buying it. It's billions, not millions") is cut to a half sentence that the student finishes, then a short confirmation. The student's "That's not possible" push-back moves to the plasterer. The plasterer's "paying for every room" line is trimmed.

**Risk.** The student's "fifty quid a night, a few thousand rooms" are guesses, but spoken as a sum they may sound like claims; keep the "say" and consider a beer-mat joke slot to mark them as rough. A student out-reasoning a banker could read as the banker being slow (punching down at him) unless his reply stays warm. Bob must not look like a pupil to the student; the order of lines (Bob last, writes the number) is meant to guard that, and the director should check it. "Nine months" is invented personal experience, for Jack to confirm.

**Protected lines.** None. "Pens down" survives verbatim as the final line. The student's "A hundred million? That's loads." and the plasterer's "I've done three of them B&B jobs for the council this year" survive nearly unchanged; the banker's "Nightly's the dearest way to buy anything" is split between banker and student.
