# Shape 02: The picture carries it

Every line the image can say is cut. The answer sheet, the pen, the countdown clock and the next table's leaning heads do the climbing, the doubting and the pressure; the speech is left with one short reason each from the plasterer, banker and Bob, whispered. Shots relied on: the answer sheet in close-up (the number crossed out and rewritten), the clock face or screen countdown, the eavesdroppers' heads tilting in, Bob's pen. No jokes written; slots only.

## Dialogue 1: The huddle

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | [writes "£100m" on the sheet, close-up of the pen] A hundred million? | 2.5 |
| Plasterer | [crosses it out, hard] More. Three B&B jobs for the council this year. Every room, every night. | 5 |
| Student | [whisper, hopeful, writes over it] Hundreds of millions, then. | 2.5 |
| Banker | [calm, doesn't look up] Nightly's the dearest way to buy anything. It's billions. [JOKE SLOT: what the banker lets slip about himself] | 4.5 |
| Student | [stops writing, the sheet in shot] Billions? On rooms? | 2 |
| (clock) | [CLOCK: 0:09 on screen; next table's heads lean in; the table goes dead silent; a long beat until they lean back] [JOKE SLOT: the eavesdroppers] | 5 |
| Bob | [quiet, to the sheet, not to anyone] I was in one. They paid by the night. [JOKE SLOT: Bob's line, if any] | 3.5 |
| Bob | [takes the pen; close-up: crosses out, writes "£2.9bn" in a steady hand; the others watch the pen, not him] | 3.5 |
| Quizmaster | [off-screen] Pens down. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **30** |

- **Why it works.** The climb is shown, not spoken: the sheet goes from £100m to crossed-out to hundreds of millions to billions, so the viewer reads the numbers rising before anyone says "billions". Each of the three gets one reason from their own life (the plasterer's jobs, the banker's trade, Bob's room) and nobody explains it to the camera. The slight argument is the student's stalled pen on "Billions? On rooms?". The eavesdroppers are a silent shot that stops the whole table, so the pressure is felt and not described. Bob never argues; his pen is the proof, and the others watching his hand instead of him makes him the one who knows without anyone pitying him.
- **Cuts from current.** The student's "That's loads" and "That's not possible" (the sheet and the stalled pen say it). The plasterer's "They're paying for..." explanation shortened to the three-jobs fact. The banker's "You're renting it, not buying it" (the line "dearest way to buy" already carries it). Any spoken line for the eavesdroppers. Bob's silence is partly broken: he gets one personal line, because the brief needs a reason from his own life.
- **Risk.** If the sheet is not legible in shot, the climb vanishes and the student's lines go thin. The five-second silent clock beat could read as dead air in a 45-55 s short; 30 s of whispering with this many stage directions needs strong editing. Bob's "I was in one" sits close to a confession and could tip toward pity if delivered heavily: it must be flat and unremarkable, and the pen beat must follow at once. The banker's "billions" is a guess, not a claim, so it stays inside the rules.
- **Protected lines.** None protected. "Pens down" survives unaltered as the last line.
