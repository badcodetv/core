# Shape 08: The pub itself is the argument

The quiz is held in a pub with rooms upstairs: a chalkboard "ROOMS, PER NIGHT" behind the quizmaster and a VACANCIES sign in the window. The table never needs to explain the maths because the room does it: they count doors, nights and neighbouring signs, and the guess climbs as they look around. Each person's reason is tied to something in sight. No statistic is stated; every number is a guess or a sum done out loud. Jokes are slots only.

## Dialogue 1: The huddle

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | [whisper] A hundred million? That's loads. | 2 |
| Plasterer | [nods at the ceiling] Them rooms upstairs. I did them up for the council. They pay for every one, every night. | 6 |
| Student | That's one pub, though. | 1.5 |
| Banker | [low] Count the doors. Doors times nights. Then do it for every town with a sign like that. [JOKE SLOT: what the banker lets slip about himself] | 6 |
| Plasterer | [points at the window] VACANCIES. And next door. And the one past that. | 3.5 |
| Student | Half a billion, then? | 1.5 |
| Banker | Nightly's the dearest way to buy anything. You're renting it, not buying it. More. | 4 |
| Student | Billions? On rooms? That's not possible. | 2 |
| Quizmaster | [over the speaker] Ten seconds. | 1.5 |
| Next table | [lean in; the table goes silent until they lean back] [JOKE SLOT: the eavesdroppers] | 2.5 |
| Bob | [looks at the ceiling, takes the pen] Count the doors. [JOKE SLOT: Bob's line, if any] [writes a number] | 3 |
| Quizmaster | Pens down. | 1 |
| **Total** | | **34.5** |

**Why it works.** The set does the persuading, so nobody has to lecture: the board, the sign and the ceiling are the evidence, and the viewer can check them with their own eyes. Each reason comes from the person's own life or work (the plasterer's job, the banker's sums, Bob's "count the doors" from having slept in rooms like these) and is pointed at something in the room. The guess climbs from a hundred million to half a billion to "billions" as the table notices more. The student's doubt is the slight argument, and the clock and the eavesdroppers cut in. Bob never explains: he looks up, says three words and writes.

**Cuts from current.** "I've done three of them B&B jobs this year" (a count we can't back up, swapped for "I did them up for the council"); "It's billions, not millions" (the climb now happens through the student's guesses); the student's "That's not possible" survives but follows the window sign instead of coming from nowhere. Bob's silent pen-taking gains one line.

**Risk.** It needs a pub with rooms and a visible sign, which the look must carry in one frame; if the sign reads as a hotel it blurs "B&Bs and nightly lets" (the quizmaster's answer must still say it in full). "Count the doors" could read as a Bob-aphorism, close to a lesson; cut it to a look and the pen if so. Over target at about 34 s: the student's "Half a billion" and the second "VACANCIES" beat are the first to go. The pub having rooms is a set choice and not a claim about the real pub trade.

**Protected lines.** None protected. Survive nearly intact: "A hundred million? That's loads." (student), "Nightly's the dearest way to buy anything. You're renting it, not buying it." (banker), "Billions? On rooms? That's not possible." (student), "Pens down." Altered: the plasterer's line loses the count of jobs and gains the ceiling gesture.
