# Shape 05: The other side wins: the doubter sets the terms

The student, the one who doubts, takes charge of the huddle and sets terms nobody can wriggle out of: one reason each, in plain words, or he writes a hundred million and they lose. Each expert has to give something up to win him round (the plasterer admits he has only seen three jobs, the banker drops the jargon and the certainty, Bob gives up his silence). The student is never a fool: he is the viewer, and he is the judge. No jokes written; every laugh is a [JOKE SLOT].

**Why it works.** The doubt stops being a speed bump and becomes the engine: the student is the viewer's proxy and each person only wins him by giving something up, so the argument is visible as a negotiation, not an information dump. The guesses climb on his terms (a hundred million, half a billion, billions), each rise earned by one reason, and the clock and eavesdroppers squeeze the bargaining. Bob wins by being the one the student actually believes; he is persuasive, not pitied.

**Cuts from current.** The student's "That's loads" and "That's not possible" merge into his terms and his concessions. The banker's "It's billions, not millions" goes (he now only gives direction). The plasterer's "More." is replaced by his admission.

**Risk.** Runs about 38 s, over the 30 s target; the Quizmaster's countdown line and the plasterer's first sentence are the first cuts. The student bossing the table could read as the lesson-giver if played arrogant; the terms must sound like a quiz player under pressure, not a teacher. Bob's "Someone paid, every night" edges toward explaining the point; if Kai finds it preachy, drop to "Someone paid every night." and let the student's concession carry it. Banker conceding the number risks making him vague rather than calm and right.

**Protected lines.** None protected. The current lines survive only in altered form: "They're paying for every room, every night" (plasterer, kept), "dearest way to buy anything" (banker, kept inside his concession), "Billions? On rooms?" (student, kept, now a demand), "Pens down" (kept).
