# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

Every line is cut to the fewest whispered words that still carry its beat: one fact, one reason from the speaker's own life or work, no connective talk. The silence, the leaning-in and the clock do the rest of the work, and the joke slots are left in the gaps where the cut words used to be. No jokes are written anywhere.

## Dialogue 1: The huddle

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | A hundred million? | 1.5 |
| Plasterer | More. Three B&B jobs this year. Council pays per room, per night. | 4.5 |
| Student | Billions? On rooms? No. | 2 |
| Banker | Nightly's the dearest way to hold anything. It's billions. [JOKE SLOT: what the banker lets slip about himself] | 4 |
| Next table | [lean in; the table goes silent until they lean back] [JOKE SLOT: the eavesdroppers] | 4 |
| Plasterer | [barely audible, glancing at the clock] Two? Three? | 2 |
| Bob | [takes the pen, writes] Four rooms in a year. [JOKE SLOT: Bob's line, if any] | 4 |
| Student | [reading the number upside down] That's... | 1.5 |
| Quizmaster | Pens down. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **25** |

**Why it works.** About 35 spoken words, so the argument is carried by pace and silence rather than talk. The guesses climb in three steps (a hundred million, "billions", "two? three?"), the student's "No" is the slight argument, and the plasterer, banker and Bob each give one reason from their own life: the jobs, how nightly renting prices, and the rooms Bob moved through. Bob says almost nothing and writes the closest number, so he is the one who knows, never pitied and never explaining. Nobody states the moral. The estimate runs about 5 s under target, and the joke slots and the clock's pauses are expected to take it to about 30.

**Cuts from current.** "That's loads", "I've done three of them... for the council", "You're renting it, not buying it" (the renting point is folded into "dearest way to hold anything"), "That's not possible", and the banker's and student's second sentences. The student's second doubt is cut to one word. Bob's two-bar silence becomes one short line of personal experience.

**Risk.** Too lean: the plasterer's "per room, per night" and the banker's "billions" are the only support for the jump to billions, so a viewer could miss why. Bob's "Four rooms in a year" is a personal-experience claim and needs Jack's sign-off that it fits Bob's background, and the line may read as a pitch for sympathy if it is delivered heavily. "Hold" is slightly stiff for a pub. The slots now carry most of the colour, so if none gets filled the beat is thin. No number is stated as a fact beyond guesses.

**Protected lines.** None. The only line kept close to draft 1 is "Pens down."
