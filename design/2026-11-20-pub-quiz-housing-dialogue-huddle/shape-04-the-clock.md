# Shape 04: The clock drives every line

Every line is built around the countdown: the quizmaster calls the time at fixed points and each call cuts someone off mid-sentence, so no one finishes a thought and the argument is half-sentences, overlaps and a number being forced out. The reasons (the plasterer's jobs, the banker's "renting not buying", Bob's room) are each squeezed into a clause. Nobody explains; they are trying to win. No jokes are written; every laugh is a [JOKE SLOT].

**Why it works.** The viewer feels the thirty seconds as pressure, not as a lecture. The guesses climb because the clock won't let anyone defend a low number: fifty million, a hundred, "not millions", then billions, then three. Each of the plasterer, banker and Bob gets one reason from their own life, and each is cut off at the point the reason lands, so the unfinished sentence does the work. The student's doubt is the slight argument, and the eavesdroppers are a hard stop the clock makes worse. Bob never argues; he gets the last reason, and the countdown hands him the pen.

**Cuts from current.** "Billions, not millions" as a spoken moral is gone (replaced by "not millions" mid-argument). The student's separate "That's not possible" becomes a cut-off "it can't be, that's--". Bob's silent writing gets one short line of his own life, no pity. Plasterer's "They're paying for every room, every night" is now truncated.

**Risk.** It runs long (about 37 s against 30) with all the interruptions, and overlapping whisper dialogue can be unintelligible once voiced, especially with cut-offs in AI Studio voices (cut-offs need pause tags and may sound like errors). Constant interruption could read as chaotic, not tense, and the quizmaster's countdown could feel like a gimmick if he calls too often (cut to two calls). Bob's line must stay plain and never tip into a sad story: "Three rooms" is a guess at his detail, mark for Jack to rewrite. Guesses of "three" or "four" are guesses, not claims, but "Add it up" / "Four's too much" edges close to the banker asserting; check against rule 3.
