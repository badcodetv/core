# Shape 03: The logic said plainly, one step at a time

Each line is one step of a sum the table does out loud in whispers: a guess, a doubt, a number multiplied, a correction. Every speaker's own work or life supplies one step. Every figure that is not the answer is flagged as a guess by the speaker, so nobody states a statistic as fact. The clock cuts in between steps. Jokes are slots only.

## Dialogue 1: The huddle

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | [whispering] A hundred million? Has to be. | 2 |
| Plasterer | Higher. I've done three B&B jobs for the council this year. They pay for every room, every night. | 5 |
| Banker | So don't guess the total, build it. A room, a night, a year. | 4 |
| Plasterer | Say eighty quid a night. Guess. | 2 |
| Banker | Eighty a night is nearly thirty thousand a year. For one room. [JOKE SLOT: what the banker lets slip about himself] | 5 |
| Quizmaster | [from across the room] Twenty seconds. | 1 |
| Student | Right, but that's one room. That's thousands, not billions. | 3 |
| Banker | How many rooms, though? Say a hundred thousand households. Guess. | 3 |
| Plasterer | [fast] Then it's billions. Thirty thousand times a hundred thousand. | 3 |
| Student | Billions? On rooms? That's not possible. | 2 |
| Next table | [lean in; the table goes silent until they lean back] [JOKE SLOT: the eavesdroppers] | 2 |
| Banker | Nightly's the dearest way to buy anything. You're renting it, not buying it. | 3 |
| Bob | [quietly, not looking up] I was in one eleven months. Same room. Somebody paid for every night of it. | 4 |
| Plasterer | So it's the nights that add up. | 2 |
| Bob | [takes the pen, writes a number] [JOKE SLOT: Bob's line, if any] | 2 |
| Quizmaster | Pens down. | 1 |
| **Total** | | **44** |

**Why it works.** The viewer can follow the sum themselves: a room, a night, a year, a hundred thousand households. The guesses climb because the arithmetic forces them to, and the student's doubt is the natural objection to each step. The plasterer, banker and Bob each add one step from their own life (the jobs, the price of renting versus owning, the eleven months), so nobody lectures. Bob does not do the sum. His step is what a night costs when you are the one in the room, and then he writes, which makes him the one who knew. The clock and the next table interrupt at the steps, not at the end, so the pressure is felt.

**Cuts from current.** "A hundred million? That's loads" becomes a plain guess. "It's billions, not millions" is replaced by the sum that earns it. The B&B jobs line is kept but trimmed. Nothing else is lost.

**Risk.** It runs about 44 s against a 30 s target, so it needs about 14 s trimmed: drop the "Right, but that's one room" exchange and one clock line to get to about 36. The sum is slightly maths-heavy and could sound like a lesson if delivered flat. The eighty-quid and hundred-thousand figures are guesses, but a listener could take them as facts, so they must be voiced as guesses ("Say...", "Guess."). The sum lands near the answer by design, which risks sounding too neat. The eleven months is personal experience for Bob and must not be played for pity.

**Protected lines.** None.
