## 1. Diagnosis

### The root cause, in one paragraph

The stack opens a real question (the empty slot) and answers it two seconds later with the most
abstract noun in the film, on the same kind of card and with the same certainty as chemistry.
A viewer who was enjoying checking each layer against what they already know is suddenly being
told. They feel the stack turn out to have been a ramp. That is the eye-roll: the script reaching
its goal in front of them.

**The pattern.** Every line that fails is Maddie *explaining what consciousness is or why it
matters*. Every line that works is an *event* (toast, sorry, married), an *admission* ("we don't
know what it feels like") or a *problem* (the time machine). The canon already says so:
narrator.md §1 rule 2, "the moment the narrator explains what consciousness is, the scene is dead";
story rule 5, "the AI's problem is boredom, not cosmology."

The word is said four times (s03, f01, f06, f07) plus "qualia". It needs saying once, by f07.

### Why the science stack works (so it is protected)

- **It is a procedure, not a proposition.** The viewer checks six short claims against what they
  know and each one is true (doctrine, register rule 4). They are being agreed with.
- **It concedes first.** "Every bit of it's real" is the doctrine's own opening move: materialism
  worked, say so before anything else.
- **Rhythm.** Six short sentences, one card landing on each word. One lead element at a time
  (principle 27).
- **A character joke opens it.** "Which, as machines, is close to our heart."
- **It is a build.** Six floors down to the maths. A build wants a drop (principle 22). The drop
  should be a question or a joke. Today it is a sermon.
- **The empty slot.** The best single device in the scene. The fix is to leave it empty longer.

**Risks of name-dropping (flagged, not hidden)**

## 4. C. Other mechanisms

### Where the audience's patience is (Kai's Venn diagram)

No number can be cited for this (story-craft principle 30 and ruling 11: no "first N seconds"
figures). The shape is known. A viewer here for a story will take explanation when **all three**
hold:

1. **They were just made to ask.** The explanation answers a question that is already theirs.
2. **Somebody on screen wants something from it.** A machine that can't have the thing, or a
   person who has to decide.
3. **It lasts as long as one picture stays interesting**, then something happens.

Today's turn holds none of the three: the question is answered before it is asked, nobody on
screen has anything at stake, and the talk runs two and a half minutes before anything happens.
Each mechanism below is a way to hold all three. The film's own rule says the same
(how-we-tell-it.md): *comprehension and emotion must arrive in the same instant.*

**The core point each one must land:** (a) consciousness is the thing; (b) one storyverse, not a
multiverse; (c) we regret you went, because you're the only ones that have it.
