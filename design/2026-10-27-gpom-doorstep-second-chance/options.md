# GPOM doorstep: the second chance

Eight routes for the doorstep (scene B, beat 17) to get from *"Your boss killed everyone we ever loved"* to the hundred agreeing, or clearly about to, with the heart moved from "the machine needs us as fuel" to "we get a second chance to not fuck this up", in 30 to 45 seconds. Nothing in the film has changed: the script in `scenes/second-half-b-the-door/script.json`, the voices and the cut are as they were; this is ideas for Kai to pick from, drawn from the seven research files beside it.

## The turn, in one sentence

The old lady hears "it runs on you" as *we die so he lives*, and the scene is won the moment she hears it instead as *the us back there, with everyone we lost, still alive, gets to know what we know*, which is why the line that turns it should be hers, not Maddie's.

## What the research says works

- **Let the listener say it.** Ask one question, give one fact, and let the sceptic state the conclusion; self-persuasion beats a pitch, and it keeps Maddie from lecturing [03 #2, 04 #2, 06 #5].
- **One regret question.** The best-evidenced attitude-change technique in the set is an exchanged story, not an argument: "anyone down there got something they'd do different?" puts five years of *if only* on screen in one line [04 #3, 06 #5].
- **"Them" is "you": the one-word recognition.** The people being saved are the speaker's own earlier selves; the turn is the lady seeing it, in a word ("Alive") or a face ("Thirty-eight, at the Co-op") [01 #5, 02 #3, 06 #8].
- **Swap the beneficiary.** The lady assumes the boss gains. Nobody won here; the gain is theirs. Said as a small fact, never a speech [01 #4, 02 #7, 02 #9].
- **Swap the noun, never say the loss word.** Maddie never says power, fuel or die; she says go, back, again. The flip is a tiny edit of the lady's own phrase ("Not like a battery. Batteries don't get to go again.") [01 #3, 03 #8, 05 #1, 05 #5].
- **Concede first, hand over control.** "I'd shut the door on me" plus "nobody's making you" lowers reactance in a hostile listener; the yes then arrives as logistics ("How long have we got?"), never as "yes" [03 #5, 03 #10, 04 #5].
- **A picture instead of a proof.** One image of *now* (a bus, a lit high street) lands "back then, still alive" faster than any line, and seeds scene E's bus [07 #1, 07 #9, 03 #4].
- **One of their own says it.** A voice from the corridor behind her turns a one-woman refusal into the hundred hearing the offer; the group decides, which also solves "a hundred people on a doorstep" [03 #7, 06 #7].
- **The embarrassed messenger.** Hope sells best understated and slightly rude; the whispered *"this time we won't fuck it up"* is already this [01 #7, 05 #9].

Timing rule used below: about 2.5 words a second, plus 0.8 s a [pause], 1.5 s a [big pause], 2 s a [long beat]. Every option opens on Kai's kept first line and keeps *"It runs on... [big pause] well... [big pause] you."* unless it says otherwise.

- **Why it lands**: the viewer the-reader.md describes has lost people and places and knows nobody is giving them back. ## Ranking

Harsh, against the brief: the turn must be a second chance, not a sacrifice; it must be the lady's, not Maddie's; under 45 s; no moral; a stern woman and an embarrassed robot.

1. **Option 2, "Every one of us".** The only one where the second chance is said by the hundred, about themselves, before Maddie offers it; "Nobody won this one" carries Kai's first point in six words; the best-evidenced technique in the set. Over length untrimmed. **Recommended spine.**
2. **Option 4, "Thirty-eight, at the Co-op".** The most moving and the most concrete; the turn is a woman seeing her younger self. Its "she" needs one clear beat to land, and it has the most lady lip-sync.
