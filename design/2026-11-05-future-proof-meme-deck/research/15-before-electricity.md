# 15 - Fact-check: "the political system was invented before electricity"

Source grades: P = primary/official, S = secondary (reputable summary), H = hearsay/weak. [GK] = general knowledge, NOT verified by a search this session; verify before it goes on a slide.

## Verdict
**As worded, the hook is FALSE and will get Kai corrected in the comments.** Electricity was known long before 1832 (and Faraday's induction is 1831, one year BEFORE the Great Reform Act). The "system" also was not invented in one go: it was patched in layers up to 1928 (franchise) and 1969 (age 18) [GK].

**Safe, TRUE rewordings (pick one):**
- "Parliament was designed before the light bulb." (core: 1265 Parliament; 1832 Act; 1872 ballot all pre-date Swan/Edison 1878-79)
- "We still vote the way people did before the telephone, before a single house had mains electricity." (1872 ballot < Bell 1876 [GK] < Godalming 1881)
- "The one-seat-one-winner map we use today was drawn in 1885. The first public electricity supply was 1881. The first iPhone was 2007." (Careful: 1885 is AFTER Godalming. Do not say "before electricity" about the 1885 map.)

## Key nuance: when did first-past-the-post "start"?
- No clean invention date. Plurality (most votes wins) is the medieval default; it was never designed against alternatives. Unlock Democracy argues the template was set by 1832-1884 (opinion, S): [post](https://unlockdemocracy.org.uk/blog1/2021/12/22/guest-post-we-need-another-great-reform-act).
- The modern one-MP-per-seat version dates from **1885**. That is the date to use if a slide says "FPTP as we know it". It is after the light bulb. So the joke is "older than the telephone/phone-in-your-pocket", not "before electricity".
- Best primary to chase next: Commons Library "History of the parliamentary franchise" (not opened; cited by one result).

## Other TRUE "older than X" lines (ranked for punch)
1. **Parliament (1265) is ~760 years old; the ballot box has been secret for 153 years; your phone has existed for ~19.** Math from the table; dates P/S above.
2. **The last big redesign of seats (1885) is older than the car, cinema and radio** [GK: Benz 1886, Lumieres 1895, Marconi 1895-1901; verify].
3. **Women got equal votes in 1928, one year before the first TV broadcast trials in Britain (1929 Baird BBC)** [GK, verify; do not use unchecked].
4. **We voted in public (shout it out) until 1872.** Secret ballot is a UX patch, version 2.0 of a 600-year-old system. Source S above.
5. **Before 1832, under 1 in 10 adult men could vote; Old Sarum-type seats had almost no voters.** S above. (Old Sarum detail [GK].)
6. **A 1928 voting system, 2026 problem set: you pick ONE party bundle for 5 years; your phone updates apps weekly.** Framing, no sourcing needed.
7. US sidebar (if a global audience): Electoral College 1787 [GK]. Not UK reader; skip unless needed.

## Traps / do-not-say
- Don't say "invented before electricity" (false, see verdict). Don't say Edison "invented electricity" or the light bulb outright; Swan (UK) and Edison were near-simultaneous. Use Swan: a British win, fits the reader.
- Pearl Street is not the first power station; Godalming (1881) beat it.
- "Westminster was lit by electricity in the 1880s": unconfirmed (search fragment only). Don't claim.
- Reader fit (the-reader.md): frame it as "the tool is old, nobody asked you to update it", not "you were duped". Name the decision-makers who kept it (the people who benefit from bundled votes), not the system as villain.
- Don't imply old = bad on its own; the hook works because it is a *version number* joke (v1832, patched to 1.9 in 1928, no update since).
