# 12 - Pillar 1 memes: "why do I have to buy the whole party bundle?"

Searches used: 3 of 8. Web search found NO existing viral meme for political bundling; the idea is real and named, the meme is not. That is an opening: we would be first, not copying.

Caveat for the deck: the sources are US-centred and mostly right-libertarian opinion. UK reader needs UK examples (FPTP, a manifesto with 100+ pledges, a coalition/manifesto trade-off). Not researched here; flag for another file.

## Analogy bank (ranked by how fast the UK reader gets it)
1. **Sky/cable bundle**: pay for 400 channels to watch 3. Everyone gets this. Best opener.
2. **Streaming vs the album**: Spotify lets you take the one song; politics still sells the album.
3. **Meal deal**: you want the sandwich, you get the crisps and the drink you don't. Peak British.
4. **Set menu vs a la carte**: "table of 40 million, one set menu, every 5 years."
5. **Pre-filled shopping trolley** (the Mises cart): two trolleys at the door, you may not touch the shelves.
6. **Software**: a monolith vs modular (the pillar's own jargon; keep as the punchline, not the setup).
7. **Phone contract**: 24 months, can't change one thing mid-term.

## Template fit (vote for one bundle every 5 years)
- **Two Buttons (sweating guy)**: best fit. Both buttons are whole bundles, each with a policy you hate. Honest to the problem.
- **Drake**: best for "bundle (no) / pick per issue (yes)". Cleanest for the pillar's thesis.
- **Distracted Boyfriend**: voter / party you voted for / a single policy from the other lot. Shows the cross-pressure.
- **Expanding brain / galaxy brain**: ladder of ideas, ends on composable voting. Good for the closing slide.
- **Change My Mind table**: "You can't vote for one thing at a time." Good for talk-to-camera.
- **Is This a Pigeon?**: "Is this a manifesto?" at a bundle of 80 pledges. Mild.
- **"This is fine" dog**: five-year wait between votes. Pairs with the pre-electricity hook.
- **Original (non-template) slides**: receipt / till screen / Sky "choose your package" UI. Avoids stale-meme risk and brand use, fits an own-look story. Recommended for the hero slides; templates as seasoning.

Rights: base images of templates belong to others (https://www.kapwing.com/templates/memes note, hearsay). Never name a real party or person as the butt of a joke without a decision to; target the system.

## 10 concrete concepts (pillar 1)
1. **The Till** (original): receipt for "Party Package, 5-yr": Housing (want) / Tax rate (want) / Thing you hate x6 / Thing you can't stand x3. Total: your whole say. Footer: "No refunds."
2. **Sky Package** (original, fake generic UI): "Choose your Government: Basic (Red) / Premium (Blue). 400 policies, you watch 3. Cancel: 5 years." Voice: "You'd never accept this from a telly provider."
3. **Two Buttons**: left "Fix the NHS + the one policy I hate", right "Fix the border + the other policy I hate"; sweating voter. Caption: the buttons are not the problem, the button count is.
4. **Drake (redrawn)**: no: "One vote, 400 policies, 5 years." yes: "One vote per issue, any day."
5. **Meal Deal**: sandwich = housing, crisps = something you hate, drink = something else. "You voted for the meal deal. Congratulations, you endorsed the crisps."
6. **Album vs Single**: "1970s: buy the album. 2008: pick your song. Politics: still vinyl, pressed once every 5 years." Hook: system older than electricity.
7. **Distracted Boyfriend**: boyfriend = voter, girlfriend = "my party", other woman = "one sensible policy from the other lot". Label the jealous girlfriend "whips". (Check it does not read as gendered; maybe swap to a redrawn neutral cast.)
8. **Set menu for 40 million**: waiter: "The set menu is Option A or Option B, decided once every 5 years. No substitutions. Allergies are noted but not respected."
9. **Ostrogorski in one slide** (explainer): three voters, three issues, each issue wins 2-1 for X, yet party Y wins the seat. Tiny 3D bars with three.js, bundle vs unbundled result flips. Source: arXiv above (needs a clean worked example before use).
10. **Expanding brain**: 1 "pick a team", 2 "pick the least bad team", 3 "build your own manifesto", 4 "your vote per issue, composed like software, and small-ideas.org is a first glimpse of it". Last line ties Martin Lewis's small-ideas.org (verify what it actually does before the claim; not researched here).

## Voice notes
Name the decision-maker: "whoever designed the ballot", not "the system is rigged". Never "you've been duped": the joke is the bundle, the voter is the sane one in the room. Narrator tone: "It was designed before electricity. It has not been updated. I checked."
