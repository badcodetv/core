## Hook (best opening)
Political OS "invented before electricity": one person one vote on everything at once; allegiance to one of two or three monolithic parties; a government shipped "on CD" every five years, then we wait. Since then: internet, AI. This matches Kai's brief verbatim.

## Three pillars (all framed as "hypotheses, probably wrong, posted on purpose")
1. **Composition over inheritance -> composable politics.** Software: small parts, loose interfaces, composed; no single piece carries the system. Politics: you pick red or blue and *inherit* the whole bundle. Fix: compose your position from small interoperable parts; housing needn't be chained to defence.
2. **Continuous integration -> continuous, testable governance.** Software once shipped on physical media every ~6 months; CI plus automated tests means Amazon deploys "100,000+ times a day" (UNSOURCED in the repo, verify before putting on a slide). Politics: government on CD every five years. Point: **tests make fast change safe**; proposals tested, measured, rolled back or iterated.
3. **Data-driven decisions -> no proposal without its experiment.** Today: "trust me, I know what to do", failure never measured. Caveat: diplomacy and judgement stay human. Implies **competence-qualified participation** (to merge code you know the codebase) as the "antidote to media manipulation" (headline-swingable one-vote-on-everything model).
   - **Blind spot (added 2026-09-07, R9):** a better scoreboard can still exclude people; nobody speaks for those whose needs don't fit the instrument. Open thread.

Closing frame: "borrowed from how we build resilient systems, because a society *is* one."

## What would confuse a lay audience (flags)
1. **Jargon:** "composition over inheritance", "continuous integration", "interfaces", "merge", "rollback", "codebase". Lead with the political pain, name the tenet second or as a small tag. "Inheritance" also means money from your parents (collision with the billionaire/inequality theme).
2. **Composition vs how voting actually works:** the canon never specifies mechanism (multi-issue ballots? delegation? per-issue referenda?). "Vote for more than one thing" invites "so... referendums? Brexit?" Reader will assume Swiss-style or proportional representation. Needs one concrete, tiny example and an honest "this is a hypothesis."
3. **CI claim:** "100,000+ deploys a day" is unsourced and feels like tech-bro boasting; needs a source or a softer line. "Continuous governance" can sound like permanent chaos/election fatigue. Stress: tests make change SAFE; rollbacks.
4. **"Competence-qualified participation"** reads as "only clever people vote", a rejoin-the-elite trap for the working-class reader (the-reader.md). Dangerous: reads as disenfranchisement. Probably keep OFF the main deck or reframe heavily ("bring the evidence", not "pass a test").
5. **"Invented before electricity"** is the hook but is fuzzy: one person one vote (UK full adult franchise 1928) and Westminster far predate bulbs, but the exact chronology needs checking (other researchers). Fact-check before the slide.
6. **Blind-spot warning** (a better scoreboard excludes people) is intellectually honest but obscure; translate to plain English ("what if the thing we measure leaves someone out?") or hold back.
7. **AI-narrator framing** (timelines, branches, postmortem) assumes viewers have seen GPOM; a standalone deck needs a 10-second "who is talking" cold open.
8. **Automation-gains doorway** (GPOM) vs the three pillars: two different first questions. The deck must pick one front door. Also the-reader.md advises thinking twice about automation-fear without a named beneficiary.
9. "Good branch / bad branch / fork" is git language; use plain "the road we're on / the road we could build".
10. Billionaire Coin: fun but "cap at ~$1bn real money" will trigger "is this communism / who decides / how enforced?" Use as a joke-first provocation, state it is a seed.
