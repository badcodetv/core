## Strongest honest hook
Do NOT claim "we can vote on everything from our phones tomorrow". The strongest true version is a split:
1. **Ask more often, on more things, is a different job from counting a binding election.** Our system bundles ~600 questions into one X every 5 years. That is a design choice from before electricity, not a physical limit.
2. **Low-stakes, advisory, non-secret channels already work and are used by the state:** petitions (UK, thresholds above), Polis in Taiwan (Uber), Estonia for real elections (with caveats).
3. Meme line: "Your bank can ask you 14 questions to move 50 quid. Parliament asks you one question every five years." (Framing only, no stat.)
4. Name the decision-maker, not the tech: "Westminster chose a ballot box every five years" not "tech could fix it".

## Strongest objection to pre-empt on a slide
**"Internet voting can't be made secure; and at home someone can stand over you."** This is the mainstream expert position (Schneier, academic letters), and the Estonia criticism (stored-not-tallied, 2014 breach claim) is the nearest rebuttal to "but Estonia does it". Being flippant here loses the sceptical reader and the technical audience.

Honest answer slide (suggested): 
- "Agreed: binding secret ballots from a phone are a hard, unsolved problem. We are not asking for that first."
- "Start where the stakes are advisory and the cost of a hack is low: petitions, ranked priorities, Polis-style maps of what people actually agree on. Keep the paper ballot for the big, binding vote."
- "Estonia shows people will use it (51%); it does not prove it's safe. Both are true."
- Pillar 3 tie-in: pilot, measure, publish the failures.

## Secondary objections (one-liners)
- Petitions are ignored: true-ish; 10k gets a reply that can be a "no"; 100k is debate, not law. Use as the "inheritance of broken pipe" gag: the input exists, the wire to policy doesn't. That is exactly the gap small-ideas.org claims to fill (not researched here).
- AI deliberation: Habermas Machine / CCAI are small, early, and one critic says mediation is not just consensus-making. Pitch AI as a *summariser of disagreement* (shows dissent), never a decider. Name who owns and audits the model, or it is "the machine decides" and feeds the wrong fear (the-reader.md).
