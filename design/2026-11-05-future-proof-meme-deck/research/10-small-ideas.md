## Mapping to "composition over inheritance"
Honest version: it is a good ANALOGY, not an instance. Be careful.
- Inheritance = you take the whole party bundle (one parent class) and get every method, wanted or not. Composition = pick small parts and assemble.
- Small Ideas gathers small, single-issue, separable fixes, each judged on its own, not bundled into a manifesto. That is the "small composable parts" half.
- It is NOT composition in the voting sense: the public submits, a cross-party panel (a handful of people) selects ~50. Nobody votes per issue; the assembly step is still done by the old institutions. In code terms: nice library of small functions, still one gatekeeper doing the import.
- Its explicit "non-controversial" filter removes exactly the parts that need composing across disagreement. Composition's hard problem (what happens when parts conflict) is untouched.

## Where Future Proof goes further
1. Voting, not just suggesting: per-issue say (the "why can't I vote for more than one thing at a time?" hook), not a panel's shortlist.
2. Controversial ideas allowed, with a way to run them as experiments (pillar 3: no proposal without its experiment).
3. Continuous (pillar 2): Small Ideas is a one-off call with a deadline; governance as CI means a standing pipeline with tests and rollback.
4. Names the decision-maker: who is obliged to answer an idea? Small Ideas "takes" ideas to government; nothing in what we found obliges a reply. A Future Proof slide can ask "what happens after submit?" as a question, not an accusation.

## Respectful way to reference it
- Say it is good and that it is the point: "Martin Lewis has just asked the country for small ideas. That is composition over inheritance showing up in the wild." Frame as ally, demonstrating that the instinct is mainstream (a trusted UK consumer voice, fits the-reader.md: a name working-class UK readers trust, not a left-coded one).
- Cross-party panel is a gift for the reader rules: Reform and Conservative figures sit on it, so it cannot be painted as left-wing. Say so only after verifying the panel list.
- Do not imply endorsement by Lewis, Nesta or Involve; no "partnered with". Do not use their logos, colours, name styling or the phrase "Small Ideas" as our title. Link out, name them in text, screenshot only if a licence/fair-use call is made (default: no screenshots; describe).
- Criticise the design gently, never the people: "great start; the vote is the next step." Contempt for the mistake, not Lewis.
- Time-sensitive: deadline 12 Nov. A "go and submit one" call-to-action slide is useful and generous, but only publish before the deadline and after verifying it. Worth putting in a first, quick video.
