# 04 — Reader rules for a political meme slideshow

Sources: repo only (the-reader.md, voice.md, using-ai.md, story-craft/principles.md). No web claims. Rule numbers: R = the-reader.md, P = principles.md, G = P's gates.

## The core tension
Memes are smug by default (the "wake up sheeple" / "look how dumb they are" genre). This reader is the one that genre loses. A meme deck wins only if the **joke's target is the chooser or the mechanism, never the voter**, and the reader is the **hero who has noticed something real** (R1, R2, G1).

## What loses the reader (DON'T)
1. **"You've been duped / wake up / sheeple."** "You're stupid with a citation" (R2). Also supplies the guilt that drives scapegoating.
2. **Mocking voters** (Reform voters, Leave voters, "they voted against their interests"). The diagnosis is right; only the culprit list is curated (R theory). Lee/Coe test (P18): if a viewer who believes the opposite saw only this slide, whose contempt does it confirm? If the scapegoat's or the smug side's, rebuild.
3. **Pupil framing**: "Let me explain how democracy works", "most people don't realise". Reader becomes the audience being taught (R1).
4. **Fact-checking their beliefs / repeating the lie to refute it** (R3, R10). Never restate an immigration claim to knock it down. Proportionality, not denial (R4); simply keep it off the deck.
5. **"The machines are coming" / "AI is inevitable"** fatalism (R5, using-ai never-say). Also "we can't afford X" is a lie we're mocking; never use it as our own line.
6. **Automation fear with no aim** (R6, now advice): a slide that raises anxiety needs somewhere to send the anger (the owner, the chooser).
7. **Irony with no consequence on screen** (P18). Sarcastic meme with no number, no receipt, no image of the cost may be read sincerely.
8. **Pandering / cosplay** ("alright mate", flat-cap imagery, football metaphors every slide). Reads as contempt (R honest limits).
9. **Sincere-tragic or moralising ending**, a call-to-action lecture (P21, ruling 4). Reactance.
10. **Meme formats whose subtext is a group to look down on** (soyjak/wojak-style "NPC" templates, "average voter" strawmen). Punching down (voice.md).
11. **Naming private individuals as villains**; real-person impersonation. Named public figures only in public, checkable acts (ruling 13).
12. **Jargon for its own sake** ("composition over inheritance" on a slide with no translation). Programming terms are the narrator's in-joke, not the reader's vocabulary.
13. **Hiding the AI-ness** or leading with contrition: deck images made with AI get the one-line disclosure in voice, no justifying clause; no apology slides (using-ai s0, R/using-ai intro).
14. **Calling the anti-AI and anti-immigrant positions "the same manoeuvre"**; untested, stays out (R limits).

## What wins (DO)
1. **Punch up at the chooser, named by act**: the system, the institution, the decision-maker in a public, checkable act (R5, ruling 13). The target is the *design* ("a system built before electricity"), not the people using it.
2. **Shared frustration first, joke second.** The meme says what they already believe: rigged, gains go up, nobody pays (R portrait). Be in the pub with them, not at the lectern.
3. **Hero, not pupil**: the avatar is someone wronged (Bob with a ballot that has one box; "you get one vote per five years, for a bundle of 40 things").
4. **Deadpan literalism** (P20): true-and-absurd facts straight, one notch. "In the UK you vote once every ~5 years. Your bank app updates weekly." The joke only works if true; check each fact against the source before it goes on a slide.
5. **Bathos** (P22): enormous thing in committee-minute register. Build first, drop second.
6. **One costed, concrete, banal object per idea** (P2): a pizza-topping ballot, a supermarket shelf, an app update. Abstraction arrives as recognition.
7. **Sarcasm diagnoses, care re-aims** (G4, R9): jokes through the diagnosis, then the "here is who / here is the fix" slide is played straight and short. Carry hope as a (dry) line (ruling 5).
8. **Show the trick on a third party** (P17): the meme demonstrates the bundle-voting trick on a pizza / a phone contract, not on the viewer's own vote (inoculation).
9. **Pair anger with awe/scale** (P13): after a grumble slide, a "and yet we could" slide (the network of small ideas, many ideas feeding one policy).
10. **Quotable into the pub** (G5, R8): every slide has one line a person can say tomorrow without the deck.
11. **Every slide carries weight alone** (P14): a number, a picture, a legible mechanism. Captions carry only what the picture can't (P25).
12. **Care register for the future-voice**: the narrator wants us to make it. Contempt for the mistake, never the people (voice.md).
13. **Scarcity budgets** (P26): one direct address, one signal-glitch, one fake-bulletin gag per deck. Meme formats are cheap; repeating one reads as a template.
14. **Small-ideas.org as proof of mechanism, not endorsement**: "ordinary people sending in small ideas and someone stitching them together" is the reader-friendly story. Do not imply Martin Lewis endorses BadCode or Future Proof (no impersonation / affiliation claims). Verify what the site does from primary source before any claim (not covered in repo; other research files).

## Slide-level checklist (run per slide)
- [ ] Who is the butt of the joke? (must be a chooser or a mechanism)
- [ ] Is the reader the hero, or the pupil?
- [ ] Lee/Coe: whose contempt does this confirm if seen alone?
- [ ] Is the fact checkable, with a source on the speaker notes?
- [ ] Is irony paired with a consequence on screen?
- [ ] Is there one quotable line?
- [ ] No "duped", "wake up", "sheeple", "actually", "obviously"; no fatalism; no repeating a claim to refute it
- [ ] Anger aimed somewhere (advice, not gate)

## Deck-level shape (from the rules)
Joke through the diagnosis, straight through the re-aim. Open on a person in the situation, not on a lecture (ruling 14). One direct address, spent last, aimed at the chooser (P21). Disclose AI in one in-voice line with a link, no justifying clause. Evidence for effect sizes is small; the bet is cumulative presence (R limits); do not promise conversion.
