## Draft by draft (unkind)

### installer: 42. The best skeleton, and not funny enough.
- **What works.** It is the only draft where the OS gag *escalates* and then *pays off*. The administrator's button gets longer every pillar: "Remind me later" (4) → "Remind me after the next election" (8) → "Remind me when it's someone else's problem" (12). On 17 "later" is greyed out ("You have used all your laters. (154 years of them.)"), the narrator says "I'm from later... I am the reminder", the bar goes from 3% to 4% and the clock finally changes. A 14-year-old could repeat the three points from slide 16 alone.
- **Reader rules.** Clean. "Not you, Mel. You're a standard user. The administrator is whoever's in charge that year. And they've got a lot on." is the best blame line across all six: the blame goes on whoever holds the button, the voter is never the joke, and it is warm about it.
- **Weak spots.** The words in between are explanations, not jokes. Slide 1: "Go on, ask it out loud. Nobody has ever answered it." is stagey, and the second half isn't true. Slide 9 is plain explanation ("Software calls that continuous integration. Laws get dropped in one lump, from a height."). Slide 7, "Somebody built the box that way. Wasn't Mel.", is a reader rule spoken aloud as if it were a punchline. Each pillar needs a step page, a diagram, a meme and a nag: four slides when two or three would do.
- **Verdict.** Use it as the spine. Remove the step pages and put the escalating "Remind me" button on each pillar's meme. Swap the explanations for the standup draft's click punchlines.

### standup: 37. Funniest by a distance, and the fattest.
- **What works.** Almost every click lands a joke: "Same five people. Same opinions." beat "Slightly more pints." / [Yes] [YES] "And there's no undo." / "They lost the receipt for the receipt." / "Your bus timetable gets tested on you." / "Side effects may include: all of us." / [Pay] [Move house] "Peer pressure, now evidence-based." / "Download size: smaller than one manifesto." / "I'm the error message." / "I've taken the liberty of removing a button." / "Four percent. Don't laugh, that's the fastest it's moved since 1872."
- **Weak spots.** Pillar 1 takes four slides (meal deal, pizza, pub, congratulations), and the meal deal and the pizza make the same joke. Slide 4, "You'll laugh, then you'll want them.", is the worst line in the six drafts: telling a room it will laugh is how you stop it laughing. Slide 2, "Democracy v1832", muddles the 1872 clock, and "nobody has ever tried turning it off and on again" is wrong, because an election *is* turning it off and on (the customer-support draft gets that joke right). The receipt line "Thing you hate x6. Thing you can't stand x3" is anti-politics sneer, not design blame.
- **Reader rules.** The Participedia toast "2,400+ experiments, 160 countries" puts a perishable count on screen (everyone else says "thousands"). "Pol.is: where the other side secretly agrees with you" goes beyond ledger row 37, which only covers four camps becoming two.
- **Verdict.** This is where the jokes come from. Its structure doesn't make the cut.

### ruthless: 38. The right length, and slightly too polite.
- **What works.** 13 slides and 2:48, which proves the size target is reachable. The cookie banner (5) is the single best meme in the six drafts, because everyone in the room has clicked one this week: "We value your democracy." / "By voting, you accept all policies in this bundle, including the ones you can't stand." / [Accept all] [Accept all] / "Manage preferences (coming soon since 1872)". Slide 7: "Your phone has Ctrl-Z. Your country has 'we'll look into it'." Slide 3: "Three updates. Three minutes. Let's get it off three percent." It also has the only pillar-3 button joke: [Run on everyone] [Run on everyone].
- **Weak spots.** "Remind me later" sits on the buttons, but nobody is ever seen clicking it, so the gag never escalates and the turn on 11 has nothing to pay off. The last slide ends on an earnest line ("You're not gambling by changing the system. You're gambling by keeping it.") followed by "Four percent. It's a start." That is a shrug, not a button. "Mel calls it: let me pick." is a dud. "Light bulb (someone else's)" makes no sense.
- **Verdict.** Use its slide budget (one diagram and one meme per pillar) and its cookie banner.

### customer-support: 34. Great jokes resting on a fact we can't use.
- **What works.** "Your call is important to us. It has been since 1872." / "Position in queue: 1 of everyone" / "It's working exactly as designed. That's the bug." This line is the whole reader rule turned into a joke: the design is the fault, so Mel never is. "Requirements: 1 pencil. Known issues: all of them." / `scared_straight_final_FINAL.jpg` / and the best closing line of the six, "Somebody's had the mouse since 1872. Ask for a go."
- **Reader rules: heavy penalty.** The opening gag ("We only get to do that every five years." / "Brilliant. So it's working."), the ticket ("Duration: 5 years") and the survey ("next survey: 5 years") all rest on ledger row 2/6, which is **CHECK**. The draft admits it, which doesn't fix it. The fallback, "once a parliament", kills the joke. "Status: WAITING ON CUSTOMER. That's you." leans toward putting the fault on the viewer.
- **Weak spots.** Four slides per ticket (opened, diagram, meme, resolved), so it is as long as round 1 felt. "You'd call it common sense, if common sense came with a button." goes nowhere.
- **Verdict.** Take three lines from it. If parliament.uk confirms the five-year maximum, the turn-it-off-and-on-again exchange goes back in, because it is the best joke in the pile.

### patch-notes: 32. Every point is said twice.
- **What works.** "The ballot got a pencil. On a string." (slide 4, as a split next to the smart bulb) is a perfect, cheap, true picture. "Please do not turn off your country." / "Time remaining: a general election" / "Reason: WE CHECKED." / "The pencil is still on a string. We're not animals." / "Status: WON'T FIX".
- **Weak spots.** "Known issues" followed by "Fixed in this version" means every pillar is told as a bug and then again as a fix: six passes for three points. "Breaking changes" (17) brings in new ideas at minute three ("Policies without a stop number will not install"). "Resolution: WORKS ON MY MACHINE" is a developer in-joke that a working-class room won't get. It has no tower diagram, so pillar 2 is told only in memes. The ending, "I'm the reminder" with the clock flipping, comes after "go and look" has already been said, and there is no 4% payoff.
- **Reader rules.** "A computer can summarise. A named person signs." is a fourth idea, off-brief, and it says "the computer". "Remind me in 5 years" relies on the CHECK fact.
- **Verdict.** Take the pencil and "do not turn off your country". Leave the rest.
