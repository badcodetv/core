# Explore directory: where Future Proof sends people

Checked live on 2026-11-05. Every URL below loaded in the site check unless noted. "Last sign of activity" is what the page itself showed, not a guess. Hooks are the narrator's lines: dry, from the future, aimed at the mistake and not the people. Small-ideas.org is deliberately not on the list (it is one example among many and gets no special weight).

Pillars: 1 = composition over inheritance (vote issue by issue). 2 = continuous integration (update, test, reverse). 3 = data-driven (no policy without its experiment).

Mix: 14 projects, 9 UK-based (marked UK), pillars 1 x5, 2 x3 (plus 2 shared), 3 x6.

## The 14, in the order a viewer should explore them

Order logic: the cold open, then the pillar-1 "a jury for politics" ideas, then "report the bug" tools, then the evidence tools, then the try-it-tonight closers.

### 1. RadicalxChange (quadratic voting)
- URL: https://www.radicalxchange.org
- Do there today: read how quadratic voting lets you spend a budget of credits across many issues, and see the pilots.
- Pillar: 1 (the answer to "why can't I vote on more than one thing?")
- Last activity: blog post 3 Nov 2026, "Help Shape Digital Democracy Edinburgh 2027".
- Hook: "You get one vote every five years, for a bundle of 400 promises. Cute. Here is a website that lets you spend your votes like a budget."
- Caution: US-centred, founder-led, crypto-adjacent. Keep to the voting idea, not the movement. Not UK, but it has an Edinburgh 2027 event.

### 2. Sortition Foundation (UK)
- URL: https://www.sortitionfoundation.org
- Do there today: learn how councils pick citizens' assemblies by lottery and read the case for permanent ones.
- Pillar: 1 (ordinary people pulled in for one issue at a time)
- Last activity: header image about May 2026; no dated news posts visible.
- Hook: "Jury service, but for the people deciding your bin collection. Apparently this is radical."
- Caution: you cannot self-apply; you only get in if invited. Sources: https://cop-demos.jrc.ec.europa.eu/citizen-engagement-organisations/sortition-foundation-uk

### 3. PB Scotland (UK)
- URL: https://pbscotland.scot
- Do there today: find a community vote on how local money gets spent (participatory budgeting).
- Pillar: 1
- Last activity: event report for 16 Feb 2026 (58 participants). Dates on the site conflict slightly, so do not quote exact ones on stage.
- Hook: "A village hall, a ballot box, and the council's money. The most dangerous weapon in the country is a folding table."

### 4. Independents for Frome / Flatpack Democracy (UK)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independents_for_Frome
- Do there today: read the DIY guide idea (book: Flatpack Democracy, 2014, 2.0 in 2019) for running a no-party town council.
- Pillar: 1 (no party whip, decide each issue on its merits)
- Last activity: 10 Feb 2026 Park ward by-election, which they lost 255 to 249; they now hold 16 of 17 seats. (Corrected: 10 of 17 in 2011, then all 17 in 2015, 2019, 2022.)
- Hook: "A whole town sacked the party bundle and just voted on things. Somerset, 2015. We were warned."
- Caution: town-council power only. Official group site not checked; link is Wikipedia.

### 5. CONSUL Democracy
- URL: https://consuldemocracy.org
- Do there today: browse the free software that cities use for proposals, debates and budget votes; any council could copy it.
- Pillar: 1 and 2 (proposals and votes run continuously)
- Last activity: 21 Oct 2026 CONSULCON26 Munich report; v2.5.0 released 22 April 2026.
- Hook: "Madrid built a suggestion box that the government is legally scared of. It is open source. Your council has no excuse."
- Use "250+ cities and organisations" (site's own figure), not the old "200 in 39 countries".

### 6. FixMyStreet (UK)
- URL: https://www.fixmystreet.com/
- Do there today: report a pothole or broken light to your council from your phone and watch it get fixed.
- Pillar: 2 (report the bug, patch it, check it)
- Last activity: reports timestamped 5 Nov 2026; the site's own counts show 22,068 reports in the past week and 54,285 fixed in the past month.
- Hook: "Somebody finally gave the country a bug tracker. Turns out the bug is the pothole."

### 7. WriteToThem (UK)
- URL: https://www.writetothem.com/
- Do there today: type a postcode and write to your councillor, MP, MSP, MS or MLA in two minutes.
- Pillar: 2 (a feedback loop between public and lawmakers)
- Last activity: no dates on the page (loads and works).
- Hook: "Pull request, but the maintainer is your MP. Please read the contributing guide. It is one box."

### 8. Loomio
- URL: https://www.loomio.com
- Do there today: start a free group and decide one thing properly with a proposal and a vote.
- Pillar: 1 and 2 (consent-based decisions, one at a time)
- Last activity: 27 Feb 2026 case studies. Built by a worker-owned co-op in New Zealand.
- Hook: "Every group chat that ever argued about where to eat could have used this. Every government too."
- Caution: its case studies include NYC-DSA, so it has a left-political showing. Present it as a tool, not a movement.

### 9. TheyWorkForYou (UK)
- URL: https://www.theyworkforyou.com/
- Do there today: type a postcode and see how your MP actually voted, issue by issue.
- Pillar: 3 (the record, not the rosette), also 1 (votes shown per issue)
- Last activity: Commons debates and divisions dated 4 Nov 2026. Also covers Scottish Parliament, Senedd and NI Assembly.
- Hook: "Your MP's voting record, unbundled. It is like reading the terms and conditions, except somebody read them for you."

### 10. Democracy Club (UK)
- URL: https://democracyclub.org.uk
- Do there today: use WhoCanIVoteFor and Where Do I Vote?, or volunteer on the data.
- Pillar: 3 (open election data)
- Last activity: 15 Oct 2026 blog on English council boundary reviews; mapping Northern Ireland polling places for 2027.
- Hook: "Volunteers built the country's list of who is standing. The official version was, historically, a PDF and a prayer." (Check the PDF line is fair before using; it is a joke, not a sourced claim.)

### 11. What Works Network (UK government)
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-works-network
- Do there today: read the evidence on what works in health, education, crime and more, from independent centres.
- Pillar: 3 (and 2: test and update)
- Last activity: page last updated 4 Nov 2026. Nine What Works Centres, plus 3 affiliates and 1 programme.
- Hook: "The government already has a department for checking whether things work. It is called a good idea we are not using enough."
- Use "nine centres", not a bigger number.

### 12. Full Fact (UK)
- URL: https://fullfact.org/
- Do there today: check a claim before you share the meme.
- Pillar: 3 (no claim without evidence)
- Last activity: live, but the homepage is undated; recency not confirmed from dates.
- Hook: "Before you repost the chart with the red arrow: this is the people who check the chart with the red arrow."
- Caution: it checks claims; it does not run policy experiments. Keep that distinction honest.

### 13. Participedia
- URL: https://participedia.net
- Do there today: browse real cases of democratic experiments from around the world and see what happened.
- Pillar: 3 (a library of what was tried and what came of it)
- Last activity: no dates visible. Counts on the site now: 2,442 cases, 400 methods, 886 organisations, 160 countries.
- Hook: "2,442 experiments in running a country differently. You were told nobody had tried."

### 14. Pol.is (Computational Democracy Project)
- URL: https://pol.is (explainer: https://compdemocracy.org)
- Do there today: join a Pol.is conversation, or read how it maps where opposing groups quietly agree.
- Pillar: 3 (consensus mapped from data), also 1
- Last activity: thin. pol.is rendered only its title; compdemocracy.org footer shows 2025, no dated news. Link the explainer as well as the tool.
- Hook: "A website that shows left and right where they secretly agree. The algorithm has been in a better mood than the comment section since 2012." (Joke only; check the year before using it.)
- Caution: compdemocracy.org was not fetched directly in the check; description comes from the check notes.
