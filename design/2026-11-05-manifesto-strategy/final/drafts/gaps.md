## Prime Minister

### The Union scope sentence

Goes in the foreword, after "Every chapter can be voted on alone" and before the signature line.

> This manifesto speaks for the whole UK on money, defence, foreign policy, work and how laws get
> tested. On health, schools, housing, councils, transport and the courts, which Scotland, Wales and
> Northern Ireland run for themselves, it speaks for England, and asks their governments to run the
> same tests. Where they do, we compare notes in public.

Notes:
- "Work" is UK-wide in the pledge but not in the machinery: social security is devolved in Northern
  Ireland (kept in parity in practice) and partly in Scotland [K, wonk §7.9]. Work and Pensions' trials
  (the bridge, a human reads your claim) start in England and Wales unless Edinburgh or Belfast ask in.
  Put that on the W&P page as one clause, not here.
- "The courts": England and Wales share one system; Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own [K].
  Policing is devolved in Scotland and Northern Ireland, not in Wales [K]. Home's courts rule below is
  therefore "England and Wales".
- Transport is mostly devolved [K]; Energy's bus rule below is England-only, matching the fare cap it tests.
- 47 words. Dry, three clauses, stops. No fiction.

## Chancellor of the Exchequer

### Growth and productivity: how the UK gets any of the gains

**The honest position.** The UK will not own the biggest model, the chips or most of the cloud
(ministers/02 says the same). Its gain, if there is one, comes from firms here *using* the tools: the
café's books, the builder's quotes, the council's casework. So the rule is about spreading use, and the
test is whether the pay moves with the output. That is arc row 5 made into a trial, measured by the
Transition Count row the PM page already lists ("wages against growth": median real take-home pay
against output per head).

**Facts.** Output per hour about 0.7% higher than a year earlier in April to June 2026, on the ONS's
preferred measure, and down 0.8% on the quarter; the two ONS measures disagree widely and a new method
lands in February 2027 [M: [ONS release](https://www.ons.gov.uk/releases/productivityflashestimateandoverviewukapriltojune2026andjanuarytomarch2026),
[Productivity Institute Q2 2026](https://lab.productivity.ac.uk/insights/q2-2026-uk-productivity-growth/)].
Do not print a "lost decade" figure: this session did not source one.

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* the Treasury, which funds AI as data centres and zones, not as
use in ordinary firms. *Rule:* a first line of the Plant Rule: help for small firms to adopt the tools
(an adviser and part of the cost), stepped out by lot among applicants, so the not-yet-drawn are the
comparison and nobody gets less than today. *Ledger:* Small to Medium band (under £1bn), from the Plant
Rule's investment line. *Fails if* (provisional): after two years, output per worker in helped firms is
not at least 3% above the not-yet-drawn, **or** it is and their median pay hasn't moved with it.
*Passes:* open to every small firm. *Stops:* year three unless MPs vote to keep it. *Leaves out:* the
self-employed with no staff, counted on their own line. *Needs:* the Transition Count.

**Page-ready (Chancellor's voice):**

> Output per hour, last year: up 0.7%. Pay for the people producing it: see the next line. We don't
> own the biggest machine and won't pretend to. The gain is in the café and the builder's yard using
> it, so we help small firms take it up, in an order drawn by lot. Fails if, two years in, their
> output isn't at least 3% above the firms still waiting, or it is and the pay didn't follow.

(The second sentence is a deliberate deadpan hand-off to the Transition Count row; drop it if the page
can't carry the pay figure that day.)

### Competition in cloud, chips and models

**Facts.** The CMA's cloud market investigation (July 2025) found AWS and Microsoft each hold 30 to 40%
of UK cloud spend and recommended "strategic market status" for both [M:
[The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/31/cma_aws_microsoft_sms),
[Data Centre Review](https://datacentrereview.com/2025/08/aws-microsoft-to-face-competition-probe-as-cma-concludes-uk-cloud-market-investigation/)].
In March 2026 the CMA chose voluntary commitments on exit fees and interoperability instead, and opened
a designation case on Microsoft's business software [M:
[Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102mqfd/cma-decision-to-shelve-cloud-services-sms-investigations-raises-questions-around),
[Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366640828/CMA-to-launch-strategic-market-status-investigation-into-Microsoft-Amazon-Web-Services-off-the-hook)].
Primary CMA case page not opened: fact gate. Chips and frontier models: no UK lever beyond procurement
and the switch list; say so.

**The honest position.** We don't break up anyone. We treat the regulator's own choice as a test
(neutral-test amendment: the government's idea, measured), and we make the state the one customer that
can always leave.

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* the CMA board, in March, by taking promises over designation;
departments, by signing cloud contracts with no priced exit. *Rule:* (a) a strategic steer asking the
CMA to publish, a year on, whether the promised cuts to exit fees happened, and to designate if they
didn't (the steer power exists under the 2024 digital markets law [K, check]; the CMA stays
independent); (b) every public cloud or AI-model contract carries a priced exit plan, and one
department switches supplier each year as a drill inside Defence's run-by-hand programme. *Ledger:*
Small. *Fails if* (a): exit fees and switching costs are not measurably lower by spring 2027; then
designation, not another round of promises. *Rights:* none. *Needs:* Foreign's switch list, Defence's
drill budget.

**Page-ready (Chancellor's voice):**

> Two firms take up to four pounds in every five spent on cloud in this country. The regulator was told
> to curb them and accepted a promise instead. Fine: a promise is a test. If the fees for leaving
> haven't fallen in a year, it designates them. Meanwhile the state never signs for a server it can't
> walk away from, and one department walks away each year, to prove it can.

(Fact gate on "four pounds in every five": 30–40% each sums to 60–80%; if the CMA figure isn't
sourced, use "Two firms hold most of the cloud this country runs on.")

## Home Secretary

### Courts and the Crown Court backlog (England and Wales)

**Facts.** The Crown Court backlog passed 80,000 cases, a record [M:
[Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/crown-court-backlog-hits-new-record-but-moj-silent-on-jury-trial-reform/5127983.article),
[Express & Star](https://www.expressandstar.com/uk-news/crown-court-backlog-nears-80000-in-new-record-5647729)];
the Criminal Bar reported it stabilising in June 2026 [M:
[CBA](https://www.criminalbar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Crown-Court-Backlogs-Stabilise-24.06.26-FINAL-copy.pdf)].
On 3 October 2026 the Burnham government scrapped the plan to end jury trial for cases likely to carry
under three years; the Courts and Tribunals Bill returns to Parliament with the amendments [H:
[ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-10-03/controversial-plan-to-curb-jury-trials-scrapped),
[Irish News/PA](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/controversial-plan-to-curb-jury-trials-scrapped-JETFQVPD3BPGBGHPESH4VWZRCA/)].
New Justice Secretary Alex Norris [M, single source: Novara]. Sitting days have been capped by budget
[K, check the 2026-27 figure].

**The honest position.** The jury fight is over for now, so don't relitigate it. The backlog is a
capacity decision (rooms, judges, sitting days, legal aid), and it can be tested. The page already says
"the system at the back is full because the front stopped working"; this is the front.

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* the Ministry of Justice and the Treasury, by capping sitting
days below what courtrooms and judges can run. *Rule:* in Crown Court centres drawn by lot from those
that volunteer, every sitting day the judges and rooms can run is paid for; victims and defendants get
a trial date within a set time of charge, published by court. Plus one rights line under the PM's
statute: software may list cases and transcribe hearings; none recommends bail or a sentence.
*Ledger:* Medium band. *Fails if* (provisional): after two years, the median wait from charge to trial
in the trial courts has not fallen at least two months more than in matched courts. *Passes:* every
court is funded to run its rooms. *Stops:* year three unless MPs vote to keep it. *Leaves out:*
magistrates' and family courts, counted on their own line. *Needs:* the Office for Tests (lot,
matching).

**Page-ready (Home's voice):**

> Eighty thousand cases are waiting for a Crown Court. Some victims wait years for a date. Juries
> aren't the reason, and the plan to cut them has gone. The reason is courtrooms standing empty because
> the days to sit in them weren't paid for. In courts drawn by lot, we pay for every day they can sit.
> Fails if waits there don't fall two months more than in courts like them within two years. And no
> software recommends a sentence or bail. A judge decides, and says why.

(That is six sentences; for 1–3, keep the first, the fifth and the sixth.)

## Education

### SEND (special educational needs and disabilities)

**Facts.** The Schools White Paper *Every Child Achieving and Thriving* (23 February 2026) proposes
individual support plans for every child with SEND, new support levels below an EHCP, national
standards, and £4bn over three years; it is a proposal, not law, and EHCPs are not abolished now [M:
[ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-02-22/send-reform-delayed-government-schools-white-paper-to-be-published),
[LabourList](https://labourlist.org/2026/02/send-reform-ehcps-reserved-2025/),
[Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/schools-white-paper-send-reform-behaviour-changes-and-other-expectations)].
The government expects the number on the highest level of support to fall from 2030 [M: Irish News/PA].
The legal deadline for an EHCP decision is 20 weeks [K, SEND Code of Practice 2015]; roughly half are
on time nationally [K, check DfE EHCP statistics]. Council high-needs deficits sit off the books under a
statutory override [K, check end date].

**The honest position.** The parent's fear is that "reform" means the legal right goes and the new
help doesn't arrive. The rule: the right stays until the replacement has passed a test. The money is
the "Not ready".

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* DfE, by proposing the new tiers; councils, by missing the
20-week deadline with no consequence. *Rule:* (a) no child loses an EHCP or its legal backing until the
new support levels have been trialled in council areas drawn by lot and passed; (b) every council's
on-time rate published monthly, and a council below the floor answers to the minister within 30 days.
*Ledger:* Small for (b); the trial runs on money the White Paper already announced. *Fails if*
(provisional): in the trial areas after two years, tribunal appeals per thousand plans are not lower
than in matched areas, or fewer than 8 in 10 children have their support in place within 20 weeks.
*Passes:* the new levels go national with the legal guarantee written in. *Leaves out:* children
educated at home and those in independent special schools placed privately, counted separately.

**Page-ready (Education's voice):**

> What's a legal right worth if it arrives late, or not at all? About half of the plans for children
> with special needs are decided inside the twenty weeks the law allows. Every council's on-time rate
> goes up monthly, and no child loses a plan until whatever replaces it has been tried in areas drawn
> by lot and has worked. How councils pay for it all: Not ready. It will be when the new system's
> trial shows what it costs, and we print that figure beside its test.

(Strike "About half" unless the DfE figure is sourced; fall back to "Too many of the plans".)

## Health

### Social care

**Position already built** (ministers/06 Flagship 4); what was missing is page text that gives a rule
before the "Not ready", per the opposition's #16. Health's one "Not ready" is already on its page (full
care funding), so this adds **no second one**.

**Facts.** £500m first Fair Pay Agreement for care workers, negotiations from April 2027, in effect
April 2028; the Casey Commission's final report brought forward to summer 2027 [M, gov.uk, cited in
ministers/06]. NICE says home-care visits under 30 minutes should be exceptional [K]. 5 million unpaid
carers in England and Wales at the 2021 Census [K].

**Contract, compressed** (from ministers/06, unchanged except the number): *Rule:* (a) no council
replaces a care visit with a sensor or a screen without the person's say-so and a review at three
months (a right, Kept); (b) volunteer councils buy home care by the hour with the person, not by the
task, for 18 months, against councils like them. *Fails if* (provisional): unplanned hospital
admissions of people in care fall by less than a tenth against comparison councils, or care-worker
turnover doesn't fall. *Passes:* any council may switch, and the national cost goes into the Casey
response. *Leaves out:* self-funders and young carers, counted separately.

**Page-ready (Health's voice):**

> A sensor can tell you she fell. It can't pick her up. No council swaps a care visit for a screen
> without her say-so, and a review three months on. Where councils volunteer, care is bought by the
> hour, not the task, and if hospital admissions don't fall, it stops. Full care funding: still Not
> ready, until Casey reports next summer and our trial does.

(Avoid "by a tenth" on this page: Home's revised draft uses "at least a tenth shorter"; the number
lives in the contract, not the sentence.)

## Housing

### Council finance

**Facts.** The government gave councils in-principle "exceptional financial support" for 2026-27 in
February 2026, ahead of budget-setting [H:
[gov.uk EFS 2026-27](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptional-financial-support-for-local-authorities-for-2026-27)];
2025-26 was a record round with about 30 requests approved [M, CIPFA via search summary]; councils
from North Somerset to York were warning of section 114 notices or seeking support this year [M:
[Local Government Lawyer](https://www.localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/governance/396-governance-news/98866-unitary-seeks-exceptional-financial-support-to-avoid-section-114-notice)].
The 28 October Budget devolved business rates (mayors keep 20% from 2027) [M, LAND §2 row 5]. Council
tax in England still rests on 1991 property values [K].

**The honest position.** Nearly every trial in this manifesto runs through a council, and a council on
emergency support cannot volunteer for anything. So the small rule protects the method; the big
question (how councils are funded at all) is the one "Not ready" on this page.

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* the Treasury and MHCLG, by funding councils year to year and
lending them their own deficits. *Rule:* every trial pays its full running costs to the council up
front, ring-fenced so they can't be pulled into the deficit; no council is left out of the lot for
being on emergency support; trial money is never counted against its support. *Ledger:* inside each
trial's own budget (Small). *Fails if* (provisional): councils on emergency support make up less than
half their share of the volunteer pool after the first year; then the offer is wrong, and we say so.
*Needs:* the Office for Tests; the Chancellor's national/local split.

**Page-ready (Housing's voice):**

> A council that's skint can't volunteer for anything. So every trial here pays its own way, up front,
> in a pot the deficit can't touch, and a council on emergency support goes into the draw like any
> other. How councils are paid for, on 1991 house prices and borrowed time: Not ready. It will be when
> the ledger shows, council by council, who pays and what their home is worth, and we can price the
> change without guessing.

## Energy and Climate

### Transport

**Facts.** The national bus fare cap in England outside London drops from £3 to £2 for twelve months
from 1 January 2027, with £400m; one of Burnham's first moves [M:
[ITA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iota.org.uk/blog/national-bus-fare-cap-in-england-to-return-to-2-for-duration-of-2027),
[TransportXtra](https://www.transportxtra.com/publications/transit/news/81553/bus-fare-cap-extension-and-cut-shows-new-pm-s-thinking)].
Councils can franchise their buses, as Greater Manchester did under Burnham [K, check the 2025 bus law
for the "any council" power]. The pump is already on Energy's page.

**The honest position.** We'd likely have done the same. That's why it gets a test (neutral-test
amendment: the policy we expect to like, measured like the rest). Twelve months is a stop date
already; we add the pass/fail and the vote.

**Contract, compressed.** *Who decided:* the Department for Transport, by a national cap with a twelve-
month end and no published test. *Rule:* the £2 cap's ridership, fares lost and routes kept published
monthly by area; renewal in 2028 put to MPs on that review; councils that want to run their own buses
funded in an order drawn by lot. *Ledger:* Medium (the £400m is already committed). *Fails if*
(provisional): bus journeys outside London in 2027 are not at least 5% above their trend, or routes
keep closing at the 2026 rate. *Passes:* the cap stays, voted. *Leaves out:* places with no bus to cap,
counted on their own line.

**Page-ready (Energy's voice):**

> A £2 bus fare from January. Brilliant: we'd have done it too. So it gets the same treatment as
> everything else: if bus journeys outside London aren't at least 5% above their trend by the end of
> next year, we say so, and MPs vote on keeping it with the numbers in front of them. And where a
> village has no bus left to cap, it's counted, first.
