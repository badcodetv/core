## Position

We promise to enact Future Proof. In plain words: no law you live under without a test, a stop date and a person who answers for it.

The rules that will set your wage, your bill and your benefit for a generation are being written now, by ministers and boards with a machine's help. None has a test or an undo. I've seen how this goes when nobody tests it.

A human you can ask. Since February 2026 the law lets a machine's decision stand if you're told and can ask for a human. If a person rubber-stamped it, you get nothing. We close that: you're told, a named official signs, a person hears your appeal by a deadline. Rights don't expire; we measure them. Fewer than nine appeals in ten heard on time, and the minister answers to Parliament within 30 days.

Test every law before you live in it. Nobody's benefit is lowered before the review into it comes back. Every new bill publishes its test, the number that would prove it wrong and a stop date before MPs debate it. Trials go where towns volunteer; nobody gets less than today. It stops on its date unless Parliament votes to keep it. Passes, and it goes national.

Publish the write-up of what went wrong: independent, no blame. A dropped policy stays on this page, as large as the promise was. If after two years fewer than one review in ten has changed anything, this rule has failed.

Name who it leaves out, and count them, so nobody calls the gap a success.

A say on more than one thing. People picked like a jury and paid for their time choose a few questions a year. Parliament answers every point they agree on within three months.

Every number here is provisional until its trial starts. We speak for the UK on money, defence, foreign policy, work and testing laws; for England on what Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run themselves, and we ask them to run the same tests.

Agree with one chapter and not the rest? Fine. Each can be voted on alone. Other parties are welcome to print the number that would prove them wrong. We'll wait.

Drafted by AI, checked and signed by people. Not a real party. Yet.
