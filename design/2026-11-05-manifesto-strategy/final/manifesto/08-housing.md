## Position

A planning permission is a drawing. A roof is a roof. England was promised 1.5 million homes by 2029. By September the government counted about 437,900: 29% of the way, over two-fifths of the time gone. Councils built 9,241 for social rent in 2024-25.

Shortage is a decision. When a council buys a field, the law pays the owner for a permission the public hasn't granted yet. Since 2024 councils building affordable homes may ask to leave that out. We make it the default for social rent, as the new towns did: the rise pays for the road, the school and the surgery. Small farms their owners work are exempt. New council homes stay out of Right to Buy, for good.

Who gets them: people who live, work or grew up in the town, alongside those in greatest need. People escaping abuse and care leavers keep their right to a home wherever they need one.

The landowner loses a windfall; the waiting list gains homes; rents repay the borrowing.

The test: volunteer councils with the longest lists, drawn by lot, against councils like them. Fails if no land is bought by year two, or if by year five the homes don't cost 15% less or local build costs outrun the region's. Numbers provisional. It stops on its date, year six, unless Parliament votes to keep it. Passes, and it goes national.

Second, homes before servers. A data centre pays for its own wires, under Energy's grid rule, and waits behind the houses. The town writes the deal, and residents have the last word. Fails if a housing site is delayed anyway, or consents slow three months for nothing. Stops in year three unless Parliament votes to keep it.

Third, if software turns you down for a tenancy, a named person hears your appeal within ten working days. A right, so no stop date: under nine in ten on time, and the Housing Secretary explains why to MPs.

Fourth, one you'd expect us to oppose: street votes, law since 2023, never switched on. On where councils volunteer; dropped if under twenty streets vote yes in three years.

Left out: private renters, families in temporary accommodation and new arrivals in housing need, who can't wait five years. Counted every quarter. How councils are funded at all: Not ready.

The Housing Secretary signs. You get the count every quarter, and MPs vote on each stop date in front of you.
