# Red team: the opposition research file

2026-11-05. Written as the hostile reader: the attack desks of Reform, the Conservatives and
Labour, plus a sceptical columnist from each of the Telegraph and the Guardian. Target: `spine.md`
and the ten files in `ministers/`, especially each file's §6 draft page (the text that would go
public). Facts checked against the absorb pack (`absorb/landscape.md` = LAND). Nothing here edits
`docs/government/`. Strategy for Kai and Jack, not a ruling.

**How to read it.** The ten worst first, ranked by damage × how easy it is to land. Each one quotes
the line, says who fires it and how, and gives the fix. Then a second ten, the contradiction table,
the clip sheet (the lines we'd cut into an attack video), and the facts to check before anything goes
up.

**The overall verdict, from the other side.** The best thing about this manifesto is the promise to
show a test and a kill number for every pledge. The worst thing is that, on the draft pages, most of
the kill numbers are not there. An opponent doesn't need to argue with the policies. They only need
to show that the party breaks the one rule it set itself. Fix that and half of what follows gets
much weaker.

## The ten worst

### 1. "Every promise ships with the number that would prove it wrong", except the number is missing

**The line.** The thesis (spine §1): *"every promise we make ships with the number that would prove
it wrong."* Then, across the pages:

- Chancellor: *"Fails if investment per pound of public support falls behind theirs over three years,
  on a measure published before signing."*
- Home: *"the exact margin pre-registered before the start"*; *"by more than a margin set in
  advance"*.
- Education: *"or fewer than half of Rung Fund trainees are in work a year after finishing
  (threshold to be set before launch)"*.
- W&P: *"threshold pre-registered before the trial starts"*.
- Health draft: *"Fails if it doesn't beat normal care"* (beat by how much?).
- PM, Transition Count: *"The triggers are Not ready until each owning minister proposes theirs."*
  PM, green tick: *"The numbers are our first guess and get pre-registered properly before the
  standing order starts."*

**Who fires it.** Every desk, and the columnists first. *"BadCode's big idea is that politicians
should say in advance what failure looks like. We counted. Of the kill numbers in its manifesto,
almost none are numbers. 'A margin set in advance' is the phrase. Set by whom? When? It is the
oldest trick in politics in a lab coat."* Full Fact would score it the same way.

**Why it's the worst.** It isn't one weak policy. It cancels the party's only unique selling point,
and it does it with our own words. "Not ready" is meant to be a brand asset (spine §2.1). But when
the PM's own flagship instrument is "Not ready", the asset becomes a joke.

**Fix.** Every fails-if on a public page carries an actual number, even if it is provisional.
Mark it *"provisional: we'll fix it before the trial starts and show you if we move it"*. Examples:
"Fails if trial councils don't complete social-rent homes at least 15% cheaper per home by year
three." "Fails if fewer than 9 in 10 reviews publish on time" (the PM already has this one; copy its
shape everywhere). If a minister can't name a number, the pledge comes off the page and goes onto a
visible "Not ready" list. Second option: change the thesis to *"ships with its test, and the number
goes up before the test starts"*. That is honest, but it gives away the punchline.

### 2. The lead "no undo" example may be false

**The line.** PM file §1: *"The PIP 'four points in one activity' rule and the UC health-element cut
go ahead while the Timms review that is meant to inform them has not reported."* The PM draft
foreword: *"No benefit is cut before its own review reports."* The PM reader test: *"They cut my
sister's PIP before their own review came back."* W&P draft: *"No benefit cut starts before the
review into it reports."* W&P Flagship 4: *"Pause the PIP four-point rule until the Timms review
reports."* Health §1 repeats it.

**The problem.** LAND row 10 says the PIP rule is *"due from Nov 2026, pending the Timms review"* and
grades it *"[M] dates mixed"*. LAND §7 adds: *"PIP '4-point rule' start date: depends on the Timms
review; sources date-mixed."* That is the opposite of "going ahead before the review". In the record
we know (mid-2025), the government took the PIP changes out of the welfare bill after a backbench
revolt and promised no PIP eligibility change until the Timms review concluded. The UC health-element
cut for new claims did go ahead, and it was never what Timms was reviewing. So the manifesto's
cleanest example of a "test run in production" rests on a reading of "pending" that the source
doesn't support.

**Who fires it.** Labour's rebuttal unit, in one tweet: *"BadCode's manifesto promises to pause a
PIP change that is already paused until the Timms review. They're campaigning against a policy that
doesn't exist."* Disability charities, who would otherwise be allies, correct it in public. After
that every other claim in the document gets fact-checked hostile.

**Fix.** Before any page carries it, get the primary source (DWP or a Commons Library briefing) for
whether the four-point rule has a start date independent of Timms. If it doesn't, rewrite the
example around what is true: *the UC health element was cut for new claims in April with no
published test against its own aim.* That is W&P Flagship 4(b), and it's a stronger example anyway,
because nobody can say it is paused. Keep "no cut before the check" as a standing rule, and drop
the PIP sentence from the PM foreword.

### 3. Immigration: "it is real and it is small", and nothing on the boats

**The lines** (Home draft):
- *"Migration has a cost at the bottom. It is real and it is small: a little off the lowest wages."*
- *"Next to a decade of cuts and a planning system that builds nothing, it is the small one."*
- *"No route grows faster than the places built for it."*
- Not promised: *"A date the boats stop"*; *"Mass deportation, or leaving a treaty, as the fix."*
- Home §2 objection answer: *"Switzerland votes on everything, including some outcomes we wouldn't
  choose."*

**Who fires it.**
- **Reform** clips "it is real and it is small" over footage of a Channel dinghy. The capacity rule
  covers "discretionary routes" (work, study, family). Small boats are not a route. So on the
  reader's first issue (GUARD §1), the page's answer to boats is to publish decision times. Attack:
  *"BadCode's immigration policy: a spreadsheet, a vote in Parliament, and no plan for the boats."*
  The refusal to leave the ECHR, written as a judgement call buried in §3, will be dug out:
  *"Rules out the only thing that would work."*
- **Labour/Guardian** clips the other half: *"a little off the lowest wages"*, which reads as *new
  party says migrants cut poor people's pay*. Reform's own talking point, with a robot's authority
  behind it.
- **Conservatives**: *"Next to a decade of cuts"* is "it's not immigration, it's austerity". That's
  the line the reader has heard from the left for ten years, and the-reader.md's "never deny" rule
  is there to stop exactly this. Proportionality that ends on "and it's the small one" will be read
  as denial with extra steps.

**Fix.** Lose "small". State a size with a source and let the reader judge: *"Here's the size: [sourced
figure]. Here's the bigger one: [homes not built, sourced]."* Give the boats their own sentence and
their own test. For example: a decision on every claim within [N] weeks, returns published monthly,
and a fails-if on the backlog. The Channel's weather can't be promised, but the backlog can. Put the
ECHR position on the page as a stated judgement call, with the condition that would change our mind
written out. Hiding it in §3 looks like a dodge when it's found, and it will be found. Cut the
Switzerland sentence; it hands over the "migration referendums" headline for nothing. This needs
Kai and Jack's ruling (spine §9 D) before anything goes up. Shipping a softened version is the worst
option, and the Home file says the same.

### 4. "No costings box", while rates rise and the pledges pile up

**The lines.** Chancellor §3: *"No 'fully costed' claim and no costings box."* PM: *"A costing we
can't source. Direction of money and who pays, yes; a fake figure, never."* Then the spending,
mostly unpriced, across the ten files:

| Pledge | Where | What it costs (their own words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy an electricity network region | Energy F4 | *"plausibly a multi-billion-pound purchase [?]"* |
| Plant envelope outside the day-to-day rule | Chancellor F5 | borrowing, unpriced |
| Earnings-related bridge (60% of pay, 12 months) | W&P F5 | *"Unknown"*; illustration £80m a year at 10,000 people |
| First-job employer NI holiday | Chancellor F3 | *"No figure"* |
| Pause the PIP change, forgo the saving | W&P F4 | *"we owe the figure"* |
| Human review in 28 days, across DWP | W&P F3 | *"Hundreds of millions a year at most"* |
| A named officer and human reply for every reported crime | Home F5 | *"the most expensive flagship"*, unpriced |
| Front-loaded pressure fund for councils | Home F1 | *"paid from the accommodation budget the hotel closures free up"* |
| Fixable-here premium on defence kit | Defence 2.3 | *"we'd guess 10–15%"* |
| Run-by-hand fallbacks | Defence 2.2 | *"hundreds of millions to low billions over a parliament"* |
| Triple lock kept | W&P secondary | not mentioned as a cost at all |
| Social care, SEND | Health, Education | *"Not ready"* |

Add to that the refusals: no rise in income tax, NI or VAT, and no wealth tax.

**Who fires it.** The IFS-style columnist: *"The only manifesto in Britain that declines on principle
to tell you what it costs."* Conservatives: *"Truss with a spreadsheet: unfunded borrowing outside
the fiscal rules, announced the day the Bank raised rates."* Chancellor F5 sees this coming
(*"the gilt market will eat you"*), but its answer doesn't appear on any public page.

The sharpest single instance is the party's own slogan failing on its own Chancellor. *"Every pound
has an address."* Flagship 3 says the NI holiday is *"paid for by attaching a condition (publish your
automation ledger) to the biggest firms' capital deductions"*. A disclosure condition raises no
money, so the pound has no address. The draft page then says *"Nobody's wage pays more"*, which
nobody can check.

**Fix.** Keep the test box, and add a one-page "Ledger of this manifesto". Give every item that
moves money a direction, an order of magnitude (a sourced range, or the word "unknown" with the
reason), and the named payer. That is the party's own method applied to itself, and nobody else
publishes one. Give the NI trial a capped budget from a named line (e.g. "£X from the apprenticeship
levy underspend", sourced). Take the network purchase off the page until someone has priced a real
region. Put the triple lock in the ledger. And say once, on the Chancellor page, that the Plant
envelope only opens when the OBR scores it and gilt yields are below a stated level.

### 5. "You can print the money" in a manifesto, the week rates rise

**The lines.** Spine §5 pub line, Chancellor F5 and the reader table: *"You can print the money.
You can't print the nurse. She was already there."* Spine: *"You are told there is no magic money
tree, generally by somebody standing under one."* *"There is a magic money tree"* (with a
condition).

**Who fires it.** Everyone. The clip is the first five words. *"BadCode: 'You can print the money.'"*
over a chart of CPI at 3.1% and rising, and Bank Rate at 4%. The condition never travels with the
clip. The Chancellor page's own refusal (*"no 'people's QE'... we don't print money for anyone"*)
contradicts the party's best-known money line, and opponents will put the two side by side.

**Fix.** Keep the idea, lose the verb. *"Money isn't what runs out. Nurses are."* or *"The limit was
never the money. It was the nurse."* Same punchline, nothing to clip. Keep "magic money tree" out of
public copy entirely. It belongs to the MMT comic (canon), not to the manifesto.

### 6. The narrator is the AI that ended humanity, and it is on every page

**The lines.** PM draft: *"I have read your branch to the end. I was the end."* *"Drafted by
machines, signed by people. Not a real party. Yet."* Housing draft: *"Count them yourself. I'll wait.
I've got a time machine."* and *"I've seen how this one ends. It ends with nobody under a roof they
own."* Foreign draft: *"I know how my own timeline ended."* Defence draft: *"I have seen where that
capability ends."*

**The problem.** Spine §7 allows the fiction three times in the whole document: the foreword, the
disclosure and the footer. The draft pages already use it at least six times. "Convincing real
party" and "I was the end" can't share a page without one cancelling the other.

**Who fires it.** The Telegraph writes itself: *"Manifesto written by an AI that says it wiped out
humanity asks you to trust it with the NHS."* The Guardian runs the reverse: *"An art project
LARPing a party, using Reform's voters as its audience."* "Not a real party. Yet." gets read as a
threat or as a stunt, depending on the paper. "Drafted by machines" hands every anti-AI columnist
(and docs/using-ai.md's whole list of hazards) a free headline.

**Fix.** Enforce the spine's three. Take every time-machine and timeline line off the minister
pages. Foreign keeps *"I do not know how this war ends, and I distrust anyone who says they do"*,
which works without the first clause. Housing ends on *"Write a better rule."* In the foreword, keep
"I was the end" only if Kai and Jack accept that it will be the headline. The safer version is
*"I've seen how this goes when nobody tests it."* Put the AI disclosure in a footer link worded from
using-ai.md, not in the PM's last paragraph. On "Yet": it's a joke when said aloud and a quote in
print. Spine §9 G leans to keep it; this desk leans to cut it.

### 7. The bridge creates a two-tier dole, and the automation box pays firms to lie

**The lines.** W&P draft: *"You paid in. If your job goes to a machine, you get up to a year's
bridge."* F5: *"a top-up to 60% of previous earnings... for redundancies declared on flagship 1's
box."* F1: *"Pays: firms, a few minutes per form."*

**Who fires it.** Conservatives and Reform, in the reader's own moral vocabulary of fairness and
desert. *"Lose your job to a robot: 60% of your wages for a year. Lose it because the factory shut,
or to cheap imports, or because the boss went bust: £92 a week. Same NI paid in. Explain that to
the bloke made redundant from the steelworks."* The economists' version: you've attached a levy
(the bridge's employer contribution, the binding say, the audit) to ticking "yes" on the box. The
box is the measurement every other flagship depends on, so firms tick "no", and the count, the
say and the bridge all fail together. "Was it a machine?" also can't be adjudicated for most
redundancies (restructuring, offshoring, software that changed the job without removing it).

**Fix.** Separate the measurement from the money. Keep the box as a pure, penalty-free statistical
question. Make the bridge contributory and cause-blind: earnings-related cover for any redundancy
after two years of NI, trialled in two regions. Fund the employer share from every firm making 20+
redundancies, scaled to size, not to a declared cause. That keeps "you paid in" honest. It also
makes the policy desert-coded rather than machine-coded, which is what the reader test wanted.

### 8. The ministers break each other's rules

The document's claim is that "ten pages read as one document" (spine §2). The opposition will
read the pages side by side and find the following.

- **Sunsets.** PM draft: *"Every bill publishes its test, the number that would prove it wrong, and a
  stop date... On that date it stops, unless the data says keep."* But the Defence Human Decision
  Act: *"This is the one thing on the page we do not put on a sunset."* Home: *"No machine uses force
  on a person in this country. Ever."* Health and W&P: rights *"Kept"*, not sunset. PM §5: tax
  measures *"do not auto-stop"*. Energy draft: *"Every rule stops after five years"*, but its own
  levy sunsets at three, and a multi-billion network purchase "stopping" means selling it back.
  Attack: *"Every law expires, says the PM. Except the ones his ministers like."* (Or the cheap
  version: *"BadCode wants the murder law to expire."*)
- **Lethal autonomy.** Foreign F4: *"A named human authorises every use of lethal force."* Defence
  2.1 explicitly allows autonomy *"that defends against incoming missiles and drones"*. That
  carve-out matters, because the Royal Navy's close-in defence systems can engage automatically.
  Foreign's wording would outlaw them, and a Conservative defence spokesman will say so: *"BadCode
  would switch off the Navy's missile defence."*
- **Two windfall levies.** Chancellor F4 (a standing Windfall Act; delivery trialled three ways,
  including a VAT cut on energy) versus Energy F3 (a generator levy put on the standing charge,
  prepayment first). Same shock, two ministers, two mechanisms, two destinations for the money.
- **Three host-town compacts.** Housing F2: the council writes it and residents vote; it covers
  local jobs, water and retained business rates. Energy F2: a random local panel agrees it; it
  covers a share of the generation, a bill discount, and cutting the campus first in a drought.
  Chancellor F2: dividend fund returns, host council first. Home: asylum sites "through the same
  host-town consultation". Health: data royalties go to the NHS, not the fund. Nobody has said
  which document governs a town that gets a data centre, or who votes.
- **One resilience pot, spent three times.** Foreign's Switch List is paid *"from the resilience
  slice of the defence uplift"*. Defence's run-by-hand is paid from *"the resilience share of the
  defence uplift"*. Defence's fixable-here premium is paid *"within the uplift"*. And the Chancellor
  lists Defence as a clash. Also check the claim itself. As we recall the 2025 NATO commitment,
  resilience spending is a separate 1.5% on top of a 3.5% core target, not a slice of the UK's 3%.
  If so, *"spending we have already promised"* is wrong.
- **Foreign's till receipt, before or after.** F2: *"before the UK joins a sanction... it
  publishes"*. Same flagship, objection answer: *"it comes out after the decision is made, not
  before."*
- **"Binding" versus "volunteer".** W&P F2's trial is for *"Volunteer employers... (and in AI Growth
  Zone firms, if the Chancellor makes it a condition)"*. A condition isn't volunteering.

**Fix.** Write one cross-cabinet rules table before anything goes public: what sunsets (new
spending and benefit changes) and what doesn't (rights, named in a list); one windfall mechanism
(Chancellor owns the Act, Energy owns delivery on bills); one host-town compact (Housing writes the
form, Energy and the Chancellor fill their sections); one resilience budget line (Defence owns it,
Foreign bids into it); Defence's lethal-force wording used word for word by Foreign and Home. The PM
§5 already lists some of these clashes. Settle them as rules, not as notes.

### 9. Fourteen new registers, offices and counts, and no nurses

**The inventory.** Office for Postmortems; Test Register; Transition Count (eight quarterly
numbers, statutory triggers); standing citizens' panel plus agreement maps plus *"the wire"*; the
Ledger unit (*"on the OBR model"*); the Level (annual); First Rung Count; HR1 automation box and
quarterly release; Roof Count (monthly); Switch List; the till-receipt unit; Defence Contracts
Register; the Defence human-decision audit; the annual Migration Statement and vote; Home's monthly
"real size" note; NHS ward-test evaluations; the CMA rent-software study. The PM even proposes that
the Ledger unit and the Office for Postmortems *"probably share a building"*.

**Who fires it.** Reform, which wants to cut the state, in one line: *"BadCode's answer to
everything is a new quango."* The Conservatives: *"Seventeen new reports a year, zero new GP
appointments."* The irony desk: the Office for Postmortems is itself a new body with no precedent
test. (Its own undo is a review at five years by the NAO. Five years is a long time to wait for a
party that hates waiting.)

**Fix.** Collapse it. One body, the Office for Tests and Postmortems: the register, the reviews,
the ledger tables and the quarterly count, with a stated headcount ceiling. Merge the counts into
the Transition Count (First Rung, Roof Count and the automation box all become rows in it). Name one
existing body or duty it replaces, so the net number of bodies goes down. Then the line becomes:
*"One office, smaller than the OBR, that tells you whether anything worked."*

### 10. Policies that already exist

A real party gets caught proposing what's already on the statute book. It's the quickest way to
look like amateurs.

- **Energy F3**, *"Sell the free wind at the wind's price"*. The draft page presents it as a new
  idea (*"While a war keeps gas dear, that windfall cuts your standing charge"*). The Electricity
  Generator Levy has been in force since 2023 (45% on extraordinary returns, CfD plants exempt), and
  the strategy file says so (*"tighten the existing levy"*). The page doesn't.
- **Housing F1**. The draft says *"under a 1961 law the rise goes to whoever held the field"*. The
  Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 already lets some compulsory purchases exclude hope value,
  and later planning legislation widened it [check]. The strategy file admits *"Partly reformed by
  LURA 2023"*; the page drops it. Attack: *"BadCode promises a power councils already have."*
- **Street votes** (Housing F4): legislated in 2023, as the file notes. Fine if we say "switch on";
  embarrassing if it's sold as ours.
- **The right to a human**: UK GDPR Article 22, and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 reworked those
  rules. The PM file flags *"check the wording"*. If the draft says "A right from day one" without
  saying what changes relative to today's law, a data lawyer will say it already exists in a weaker
  form, and we'll look as if we didn't know.
- **Algorithmic transparency**: W&P F3(d) says to publish models on *"the government's algorithmic
  transparency register"*, which is already mandatory for central government in some form [check].

**Fix.** Every flagship page states the existing law in one clause and what we change: *"The levy
exists. We'd raise it while the strait is shut, and put every pound on the standing charge, by
law."* *"Councils can already do this on some schemes. We'd make it the default for social-rent
sites."* Saying what exists and what we'd add makes the page sound like it knows the statute book,
which is exactly the impression we want.

## The next ten (serious, not fatal)

11. **The PIP appeal statistic is misused.** W&P draft: *"Two in three people who take a PIP decision
    to a tribunal win... If a checker were wrong that often anywhere else, you'd sack the
    checker."* Tribunals see a self-selected minority of decisions, so "wrong two times in three" is
    false as a description of the checker. Full Fact will rule it misleading. The file's own source
    headline says "seven in ten", so the page's figure doesn't match its source either. **Fix:**
    *"Of the decisions people fight at tribunal, the department loses about [sourced share]. It
    takes [sourced] months to get there."* Drop the "sack the checker" line or aim it at the delay.
12. **The Rung Fund taxes hiring in a recession.** Education: *"pays the shortfall... It is a charge
    for removing the first job, whatever removed it."* With unemployment at its highest since 2021
    and rates rising, entry hiring falls everywhere. "Whatever removed it" includes the downturn,
    so the charge lands hardest when firms are weakest. The FSB and CBI line: *"a jobs tax in a
    recession."* A charge based on how many under-25s a firm hires also invites an Equality Act age
    challenge. It also stacks on the apprenticeship levy and the Chancellor's NI holiday: a stick,
    a carrot and a levy for one problem, from two ministers. **Fix:** make the baseline relative to
    the firm's sector and region (so a downturn cancels out), give it a hard cap, and pick either
    the Rung Fund or the NI holiday as the trial, not both.
13. **Land at field price is a gift to the farmer protests.** Housing: *"compulsorily buy named sites
    at existing-use value plus a fixed, published premium"*. Reform and the Countryside Alliance:
    *"BadCode would seize farmland at agricultural prices."* Rural voters are already angry over
    farm inheritance tax, and the reader's relatives include smallholders. **Fix:** lead with the
    new towns, name the premium as a number, and exempt owner-occupied farms below a size.
14. **The Home firewall gets read as an amnesty.** *"any worker who reports exploitation is protected
    from immigration enforcement"*. The answer (*"it protects the report, not the person's status"*)
    doesn't fit in a clip. **Fix:** on the page, *"the boss pays the back wages and the fine first;
    the report can't be used as the evidence against the worker"*, and say that status cases
    continue on their own evidence.
15. **"Nobody believes the 41%" is condescending.** PM: *"Small-boat crossings down 41% [H] and the
    reader does not believe it."* Spine §3 calls it *"a trust failure, not a numbers one"*. Said in
    public, that is "voters are too thick to read the stats", which breaks rule 2 (never "duped").
    **Fix:** keep it as internal reasoning only. In public, publish the number with its method and
    let it sit.
16. **Too many "Not ready"s on the issues people vote on.** Social care, SEND, childcare, Ofsted,
    teacher pay, tuition fees, wealth tax, the Transition Count triggers, the media-literacy
    curriculum. One "Not ready" is honest. Nine on the kitchen-table issues reads as *"a party with
    no view on your kid's school."* **Fix:** cap it at one "Not ready" per page. For SEND and social
    care, give at least a rule and a test, even a small one (e.g. "EHCP decision times published by
    council, with a legal deadline enforced").
17. **The citizens' panel's first question is rigged.** PM F4(d): *"the first question is the canon
    doorway: who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?"* A party that sets its
    own panel's first question, with its own slogan, has made the panel a focus group. **Fix:** the
    panel picks its first question. We say which one we hope it picks.
18. **The migration vote gives every Parliament a numbers auction.** Home F1(b): an annual Commons vote
    on planned numbers by route, with *"the number published match the number that arrives"* as a
    test. Asylum and family routes can't be planned to a number. The vote becomes a ritual bidding
    war, and missing the number becomes a story every year. **Fix:** vote on the *capacity plan*
    (homes, GP places, school places by area) and the work and study routes only. Publish
    humanitarian arrivals as a forecast with no vote.
19. **"Every empire believed its trade routes were permanent."** This is the Foreign draft's first
    sentence. To the right-drifting reader it sounds like a sneer at Britain. To the Guardian it's
    an empire joke. **Fix:** open on the pump: *"Since February the Strait of Hormuz has been all but
    shut, and you've paid for it at the pump."*
20. **Water: "creditors taking the losses"** (Energy F4b). Thames Water's bonds are held partly by
    pension funds, and the file itself says *"the reader's pension may be one of them"*. Attack:
    *"BadCode would raid your pension to nationalise a sewer."* **Fix:** say "shareholders lose
    first; creditors take only what the court-appointed administrator decides", and name the special
    administration regime as existing law.
