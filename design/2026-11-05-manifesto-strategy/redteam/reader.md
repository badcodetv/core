# Red team: the target reader reads the ten draft pages

2026-11-05. Strategy review, not copy. Nothing here changes `docs/government/`.

**Who is reading.** Me: 45, a warehouse shift lead in a Midlands town that used to make things. Voted
Leave. Thinking about Reform because at least they sound like they're angry about the right things.
Distrust every politician. Hate being told what I "really" think. I read these pages the way I read
anything a party puts through my door: on my phone, in a break, looking for the catch.

**What I read.** The section 6 draft page of each file in `ministers/` (that is what the public will
see), checked against each file's own reader test and refusals, `spine.md`, `absorb/guardrails.md`
and `docs/marketing/the-reader.md`. Quotes are verbatim from the drafts.

## The cross-cutting problems (worse than any one page)

### X1. You only ever tell me what happens when it fails. Never what happens when it works.

Every page ends its test with some version of "Fails if ... Then we publish the postmortem and drop
it." (the word "postmortem" appears **12 times** across the ten pages). **Not one page says what
happens if the trial passes.** Zero hits for "if it works" or anything like it. Add "five volunteer
councils", "two areas that volunteer", "volunteer employers in one region", "a few councils that
volunteer" (six pages), and what I hear is: *nothing changes in my town for three to five years, and
then probably it gets dropped.* Reform promises me 100 days. You're promising me a pilot in somebody
else's town and a coroner's report.

The test box is the brand, so keep it. But every test needs its other half, the payoff:

> Fix (every page): after "Fails if ..." add one sentence: **"Passes, and it goes national the
> next year. You'll see the count either way."** And where the reader's town can opt in, say so:
> "Any council can ask to go first."

### X2. "Write a better rule." I'm not the one paid to write rules.

Home, Education, Work and Pensions and Housing all end on it (five times), and W&P adds "That's a
first draft, posted on purpose." The guardrails say the last line is aimed at the chooser, never at
the reader (GUARD §2, "moralise at the reader ... call-to-action aimed at them"). This is exactly
that. To me it reads as: *we haven't finished, you sort it.* It's the teacher handing back the
exam.

> Fix: end each page on the chooser or the receipt. W&P: "The board decides on a weekday. So can
> the forty people it was about." Housing: "Count the roofs. We will, every month, in public."
> Keep "write a better rule" for exactly one place, the PM's foreword, framed as a dare to
> other parties, not to me.

### X3. Boats: zero mentions. Pensions: one, in a page title.

Across all ten drafts, "boat" appears **zero** times. It's my first issue and the strategy files know
it (Home's own notes cite the 41% fall and the trust gap, and its refusals include "A date the boats
stop"). Leaving it off the Home page looks like the dodge the reader test fears: "you won't say".
Similarly, "Work and Pensions" never mentions the state pension, though the strategy has a
triple-lock judgement call ready (07 §2 secondary). I'm 45; the pension age is my retirement being
moved away from me.

> Fix (Home): one paragraph, early: "Small-boat crossings are down by about two fifths this year
> and nobody believes it. Fair enough. So: the number, every month, decided claims within a fixed
> time, and no date we can't keep." (Fact-gated, as the file already notes.)
> Fix (W&P): one line: "The triple lock stays this parliament. Before the pension age rises again,
> we publish how long people actually stay healthy, region by region, and Parliament votes on the
> rise alone."

### X4. The house style is showing. Ten pages, one form, the same phrases.

"So nobody calls the gap a success" (5 times), "Who it leaves out" (10), "unless the data says keep",
"Each one stands on its own. Take the ones you like." (twice). By page three I can see the template,
and a template is what a machine makes. The one thing this lot is meant to hide is that it sounds like
a committee, or worse, a chatbot.

> Fix: keep the fields, vary the words. Rule: no sentence longer than six words may appear on more
> than two pages. "So nobody calls the gap a success" stays on the PM page as the principle, and
> every other page just says who is counted.

### X5. Jargon I don't use, in places I can't skip it.

"Postmortem" (to me that's a body on a slab), "sunset", "branch", "data says keep", "matched ones",
"comparison", "computing power", "AI Growth Zone deal", "investment per pound of public support",
"one network region", "natural monopolies" (in the file, nearly on the page), "Test Register".

> Fix: postmortem → "the write-up of what went wrong" first time, then "the write-up". Sunset →
> "a stop date". Comparison → "against towns like it". "Data says keep" → "unless the numbers show
> it worked".

### X6. The time machine leaks onto every page.

The spine says the fiction appears three times and "no time-machine lore in the policy chapters" (§7).
The drafts carry it on PM ("I have read your branch to the end. I was the end."), Foreign ("I know how
my own timeline ended"), Defence ("I have seen where that capability ends"), Housing ("I've got a time
machine", "I've seen how this one ends"). Six hits. Once it's charming. Six times it's a gimmick, and
a gimmick in a manifesto says *not serious*.

> Fix: the PM foreword keeps it once. Defence keeps "I have seen where that capability ends" (it
> earns its place: it's the reason for the rule). Cut the rest.

### X7. Every page pivots to "the bigger story is something else".

Home: "Next to a decade of cuts and a planning system that builds nothing, it is the small one."
Housing: "The not-building is the bigger story." This is the proportionality rule written as a
ranking of my worries, and I hear it as *your concern is the small one, here's the important one.*
That's the lecture the rules forbid, in its politest form.

> Fix: say both are true and fix both, without ranking them in the same breath. Home: "Migration
> has a cost at the bottom. It's real. So the homes and GP places come with it, or the route doesn't
> grow." Let the size come from the policy (roofs tied to arrivals), not from a sentence comparing it
> to austerity.

### X8. Who is this for? The "who it leaves out" lines sometimes answer: not you.

Foreign: "the people abroad whose costs never show up in our fuel price. Both get counted." Education:
"anyone who wants to learn just to learn." To me these read as the party's real constituency peeking
through: the global conscience and the evening-class crowd. Leaves-out is good, but it should list
people like me and mine.

> Fix: Foreign → "small firms with one supplier and no spare, and the family on a prepayment meter
> who feels the war first." Education → "adults who aren't on a payroll: the self-employed, carers,
> and anyone retraining at forty-five."

### X9. Everything is ten-years-away process; where's this week?

The PM page is entirely machinery. Most pages promise counts, registers, panels, compacts. The
strategy's own reader-test notes flag "process over bread" (00 §4) and then the draft does it. I
need one thing per page I'd notice in my own week: the bill, the GP, the letter, the payslip.

> Fix: every page's first rule should be the one that touches a household within a year: the
> standing charge (Energy), the human on your benefit letter (W&P), the human on your NHS triage
> (Health), the scam refund (Home). The trials come second.

### X10. Sloppiness I will catch, and then I'll stop trusting the numbers.

PM page: "Every quarter, eight numbers: first jobs, wages, claims, bills, scams." That's five. A party
whose brand is "we count properly" that can't count to eight in its own foreword is finished with me.

> Fix: list all eight or say "five numbers". Then proofread every page for figures against the fact
> gate (spine §L): the Linton RAF base, 437,900 homes, 7.37 million, "two in three ... win" all need
> their URL before they go public.

## Page by page

### 00 Prime Minister: 4/10

The front door, and the hardest to get through.

- **"We promise to enact Future Proof."** First line, and it's a brand name I've never heard. It
  sounds like an insurance product. *Fix:* open on the wrong, then name the promise in plain words:
  "We promise one thing first: no law you live under without a test, a stop date and a person who
  answers for it. We call that Future Proof."
- **"I have read your branch to the end. I was the end."** "Branch" is programmer talk. I don't
  know what it means and I don't care. *Fix:* "I've seen how this goes. I was how it went."
- **"Test every law before you live in it. Every bill publishes its test, the number that would prove
  it wrong, and a stop date, before Parliament votes."** Sound idea, but it's about Parliament, not
  me. The good line is buried two sentences later: **"No benefit is cut before its own review
  reports."** That's the one I'd repeat. *Fix:* lead the paragraph with it: "Nobody's benefit gets
  cut before the review into it comes back. That's the kind of rule we mean: every law tested before
  you live in it."
- **"A panel picked like a jury chooses a few questions a year"** — to a Leave voter, "panel" means
  the clever people deciding for me, and "advisory first" means ignored. The file knows the Brexit
  trauma risk (00 §4) and the draft doesn't defuse it. *Fix:* "Ordinary people, picked like a jury,
  paid for their time. Not experts. You could get the letter." And drop "advisory" for "Parliament
  has to answer every point, on the record".
- **The best idea is in the wrong place.** "A human you can ask" is my actual machine fear (the
  reader research's sharpest number) and it's paragraph five. *Fix:* make it paragraph two.
- **"Every chapter stands alone. Take the ones you want."** Good, and the most different thing on
  the page; it's the honest answer to "I agree with Reform on boats and Labour on the NHS". *Fix:*
  promote it and say it in my words: "Agree with us on bills and not on anything else? Fine. Every
  chapter stands alone."
- **"Drafted by machines, signed by people. Not a real party. Yet."** Keep. That's honest and it's
  the only line here with any swagger.
- Eight numbers / five listed: see X10.

### 01 Chancellor: 6/10

- **The opening receipt** ("Bank Rate this morning: 4% ... Your electricity: up in October.") is
  the best opening in the cabinet. It's a till slip and the joke does itself. Keep it word for word.
- **"Markets work. Money is a good invention."** I never said otherwise. Conceding to an argument I
  didn't make reads as the speaker reassuring himself. *Fix:* concede through something I respect:
  "The man who runs the chippy knows what a pound is worth. So does the Treasury, until it's writing
  the cheque."
- **"every public deal with an AI firm"**, **"private computing power"**, **"The next AI Growth Zone
  deal"** — three AI phrases on a money page. AI isn't on my list; a Growth Zone means nothing to me.
  *Fix:* "Every public deal with a tech firm", "server farms", "the next deal where public money
  helps build one".
- **"The level. Employing a person should never cost a firm more in tax than replacing one."** This
  is a cracking rule and the line I'd repeat. But "We publish the gap, then close it from the bottom"
  is gibberish to me. *Fix:* "Right now it can be cheaper in tax to buy a machine than hire a lad.
  We'll switch that round, starting with first jobs."
- **"Fails if investment per pound of public support falls behind theirs over three years"** —
  Treasury-speak. *Fix:* "Fails if firms stop building here because of it. We'll have counted before
  we sign."
- **"Not promised: a cheque by a date, or that the machines will pay off the debt. They won't."**
  Great. Keep.

### 02 Foreign: 4/10

- **"Every empire believed its trade routes were permanent."** History lecture, first line. I've
  stopped. *Fix:* start at my till: "The war in the Gulf has been on your petrol receipt since
  February. Nobody here started it. Somebody here did decide we'd have no plan B."
- **"Far away is never far."** Fortune-cookie. Cut.
- **"Trade deals get reviewed clause by clause. We won't reopen the old argument. We'll read the
  receipts."** The file's own reader test calls Remain-coding "the biggest trap on the page", and
  this line walks into it: to me "reviewed clause by clause" + "won't reopen" = they'll reopen it a
  clause at a time. *Fix:* make it about British control and my shopping: "Every trade deal gets a
  receipt: what it did to the price of your food and the jobs here. Parliament votes on each part,
  not one take-it-or-leave-it." Never mention what the "old argument" was.
- **"the people abroad whose costs never show up in our fuel price"** — see X8. Cut from leaves-out.
- **The switch list** is good and concrete ("the foreign system is switched off on purpose and we see
  what still works"). That's the line. Lead with it after the till.
- **"I do not know how this war ends, and I distrust anyone who says they do."** Good honest line;
  but drop "I know how my own timeline ended" (X6).

### 03 Home: 5/10

This page is where the party wins or loses me, and it's three-quarters right.

- **"It is right to ask, and the question is not about the people arriving."** You've just told me
  what my question is about. That is the most patronising sentence in the set, because it's polite.
  *Fix:* "It's right to ask. Who decided, who pays, and where's the extra GP?" Then stop. Let me
  connect it.
- **"Next to a decade of cuts and a planning system that builds nothing, it is the small one."**
  See X7. To me that's denial in a cardigan. *Fix:* cut the comparison; keep "It is real and it is
  small at the bottom, and it gets worse wherever nothing's built. So we build it first."
- **"So migration gets its own vote."** The best idea in the whole manifesto for me. It's composition
  over inheritance in my words: I can vote on this without buying the rest. **"No route grows faster
  than the places built for it."** That's an actual answer to "would you cut it?", and it's buried.
  *Fix:* put both in the first three lines.
- **No boats.** See X3. The page starts with the RAF base and never says the word.
- **"Nobody gets hired to undercut you."** Excellent, keep; it sides with me and lands the fine on the
  firm. "so no boss can use a visa as a muzzle" is fine but "visa" invites me to picture the migrant
  as the beneficiary; *fix:* "so no boss can use a worker's papers to keep them quiet."
- **"No machine uses force on a person in this country. Ever."** Keep.
- **"Yes, it's a cost. Here's the size of it. Now write a better rule."** The ending tells me off.
  See X2. *Fix:* "Yes, it's a cost. It gets a vote of its own, and the homes come first."

### 04 Education: 7/10

- **"What's a ladder for if someone saws off the bottom rung? Your nephew did everything he was
  told."** Lands. The nephew is right: it's family, not me as the pupil.
- **"the saving is in the annual report"** — good bathos, one only. Keep.
- **"Is that the machines, the interest rate or trade? Nobody knows, because nobody counts."** Honest
  and I believe it. Keep.
- **The rule** (big employer's young hiring falls, it pays the gap into a town fund) is the best pub
  line in the manifesto. Keep "Small firms are out. Firms that keep hiring pay nothing." in the same
  breath, as it is.
- **"Where trainees work alongside AI, part of their week goes on doing the job themselves before
  they mark the machine's."** Reads like an office graduate problem. *Fix:* use a trade: "An
  apprentice learns to fit the boiler before he signs off the machine that diagnosed it."
- **"anyone who wants to learn just to learn"** — see X8.
- **"Write a better rule."** — see X2. The line before it ("Neither does anyone selling you a
  course.") is the perfect ending. Stop there.

### 05 Defence: 7/10

- **"No machine decides who dies. I have seen where that capability ends. It is not a debate
  here."** Strong. Authority without a lecture.
- **"Missiles and drones coming at us are fair game for a fast machine. People are not."** The best
  sentence on the page; it kills the "soft on defence" read. Keep.
- **"names, in public, the post that authorises it to fire and the post that can stop it"** —
  civil-service. *Fix:* "a named officer answers for every shot, the way a soldier always has."
- **"Where a firm paid dividends and ran no fallback, the dividend pays for it, not your bill."**
  Good aimed flame. Keep.
- **Missing:** the people. Not one line on soldiers, veterans, forces housing, kit that works. To me
  defence is the lad from our estate who came back and waited two years for help. *Fix:* one line in
  "who got the contract": "Every pound that goes on a contract that's late is a pound not spent on the
  people who serve. We'll print both."
- **"Threat: real. Off switch: ours. Keep it that way."** Good ending; it's aimed at the threat, not
  me.

### 06 Health: 7/10

- **"Your mum's hip is one of 7.37 million treatments on the waiting list."** Lands. **"Hitting a date
  is not the same as fixing a queue."** Lands.
- **"NHS England has told trusts to start using AI note-takers that listen to your appointment."**
  Fine, it's an actual decision with an owner. But "They may be good" is generous enough that I can't
  tell what you think. Keep the next line; it's the point.
- **"Nobody gets less care than today while we check."** Essential, keep where it is.
- **"Your records pay rent."** Cryptic; to me it sounds like someone's selling my records. *Fix:*
  "If a company makes money from NHS patients' records, the NHS gets a cut. No sale."
- **"Full care funding: Not ready. We won't print a figure we can't back."** Honest, but to someone
  with a parent in care it reads as "not bothered". *Fix:* pair it with something now: "Full care
  funding: Not ready. Until it is, the unpaid carer gets counted, and the visit stays a person."
  (Flagship 4 is right there in the file.)
- **Missing:** the GP. The 8am phone queue is the NHS I actually meet weekly. "A human within a
  working day" is close; *fix:* make it "if software triaged your GP request, you can still get a
  person on the phone the same day."
- **"Bed 4 first. Then the press release."** Good. Keep.

### 07 Work and Pensions: 8/10

The page I'd share.

- **"Nobody's job gets taken by weather. A board decides, on a weekday, and the saving goes
  somewhere."** Best opening line in the manifesto. Keep forever.
- **"Two in three people who take a PIP decision to a tribunal win ... If a checker were wrong that
  often anywhere else, you'd sack the checker."** Lands, and it lands on the system, not the claimant.
  (Fact gate, as noted.)
- **"So, five rules. Each one stands on its own. Take the ones you like."** "Take the ones you like"
  sounds like a pick 'n' mix. *Fix:* "Agree with one and not the rest? Fine." once in the PM page;
  here just "Five rules."
- **Rule two: "elect their own reps and get a binding say"** — to some of us that's union talk and
  we've been let down by unions too. The file's own concern (binding on whom?) is real. *Fix:* "the
  people whose jobs it is pick who speaks for them, and they choose how the saving's split: shorter
  week on the same pay, a share of the profit, or paid training for a job that exists."
- **Rule three, "The fraud check stays. Only the stop needs a signature."** First sentence is vital,
  second is unclear. *Fix:* "The fraud check stays. But nobody's money stops until a person has
  signed it."
- **Rule five, "You paid in."** Desert, perfect. Keep.
- **Missing:** the pension. See X3.
- **"That's a first draft, posted on purpose. Write a better rule."** See X2. The page earns a better
  last line.

### 08 Housing: 5/10

- **"A planning permission is a drawing. A roof is a roof."** Good.
- **"Count them yourself. I'll wait. I've got a time machine."** Sarcasm aimed at *me*: I'm the one
  being told to count. The snark should be for the promise-makers. *Fix:* "That's 29% of the homes at
  44% of the time. Somebody's keeping a different count."
- **"under a 1961 law the rise goes to whoever held the field"** — a statute lesson. *Fix:* "When the
  council says yes to houses on a field, the field's value jumps tenfold, and the farmer keeps all
  of it. The houses pay for nothing." (Number gated; "many times" if unsourced.)
- **"Where more people arrived and nothing was built, the queue got longer. The not-building is the
  bigger story."** First sentence good, second one ranks my worry. See X7. Cut the second.
- **Missing, and it's the first thing I'll ask:** who gets the new council homes? If the answer isn't
  on the page I'll assume it's not people from round here. The file's F1 doesn't settle it either.
  *Fix (needs a Kai/Jack ruling):* "The homes go first to people with a connection to the town: who
  live, work or grew up there." This is a real policy choice with real fairness costs; it should go
  through the contract, but the page cannot stay silent on it.
- **"It ends with nobody under a roof they own. Write a better rule."** The bleak line is fine; the
  homework isn't (X2).

### 09 Energy and Climate: 6/10

- **"Picture the grid as a bath."** An analogy is a lesson, and "picture" is the "imagine you are"
  move the rules ban. I know what the grid is; it's the thing on my bill. *Fix:* "A data centre wants
  as much power as a town, and ministers are consulting on whether it should pay less for it than you
  do. Brilliant machine. Wrong queue."
- **"Brilliant machine. Wrong queue."** Best line on the page. Keep.
- **"Sell the free wind at the wind's price. Old wind farms and nuclear stations sell at a price set
  by gas."** This is the marginal-pricing lecture the file's own reader test promised to avoid. Lead
  with the payoff: "the standing charge". *Fix:* "Cut the standing charge, prepayment meters first,
  paid for by the old wind farms being paid as if they burned gas."
- **"With no rival, the market is a toll booth."** Good. **"at a price someone else sets"** sounds
  shifty. *Fix:* "at a fair price set by an independent judge, not by us."
- **"Fails if bills where it runs rise faster than elsewhere."** Good kill number, my currency. Keep.
- **Missing:** fuel. I drive to work; petrol is the energy bill I feel twice a week. One line pointing
  to the war price at the pump would join this page to Foreign.

2. **"Migration gets its own vote ... No route grows faster than the places built for it."** (Home)
   The one idea no other party offers me, and it answers "would you cut it?".
3. **The Chancellor's till-slip opening.** Shows me the rigging without telling me I was fooled.
4. **"If you saw off the bottom rung, you pay for a new one."** (Education)
5. **"Two in three win at tribunal ... you'd sack the checker."** (W&P)
6. **"Missiles and drones are fair game for a fast machine. People are not."** (Defence)
7. **"Brilliant machine. Wrong queue."** (Energy)
8. **"Drafted by machines, signed by people. Not a real party. Yet."** Honest, and that's rarer than
   any policy.
9. **"Not promised: a cheque by a date, or that the machines will pay off the debt."** Refusals I
   believe more than promises.
10. **Every chapter stands alone.** If you said nothing else to me: *agree with us on one thing and
    not the rest, and that's fine.* No party has ever said that to me.
