# The spine of the BadCode government manifesto

2026-11-05. Strategy, not copy, and not a ruling. Kai and Jack decide; nothing here changes
`docs/government/` (that moves only through the cabinet's rolling PR, or a human PR they choose to
make). Built from the absorb pack in `absorb/` (future-proof.md = FP-A, machinery.md = MACH,
guardrails.md = GUARD, landscape.md = LAND); canon IDs (C1, I6, D1, X4, F10...) refer to FP-A.
Confidence marks on facts are LAND's: [H] [M] [L] [?] [K]. **No number in this file may go into
public copy until it has a source URL and a grade (X12, the fact gate).**

**The diagnosis in one paragraph.** The cabinet works (MACH §0). It is careful, sourced and
funny, and that is exactly why it has no platform: it can only react to the day's news, so four
posts are empty and the six filled ones read like a news diary. The PM's page is a frame with
nothing inside it. A voter can't say what this party is *for* in a pub sentence, except on two
pages (Work and Pensions, Education). This spine provides that: a thesis, one contract every page
uses, one arc that turns ten pages into one plan, and a short list of standing commitments. The
news then hangs off those commitments. It never replaces them.

## 1. Thesis

### The one sentence

> **The arrival of AI will decide who earns what in this country for a generation, and right now
> that decision is being made without you, by a system with no test and no undo. We upgrade the
> system first, we give the people the change lands on a say in where the gains go, and every
> promise we make ships with the number that would prove it wrong.**

Shorter, for the PM's `line` (under 100 characters, no colon): *The gains from AI get decided this
decade. You should be in the room, and every promise tested.* (95 characters; the cabinet can
sharpen it.)

### The three words

**Tested. Undoable. Yours.**

- *Tested*: no policy without its experiment (tenet 3).
- *Undoable*: small changes with a stop and a postmortem (tenet 2).
- *Yours*: you choose each thing, not the bundle (tenet 1), **and** you get a share of what the
  machines make (the ownership question). One word carries both the democratic and the economic
  half, which is the trick of the whole party.

Runners-up, for Kai and Jack to try aloud: *Ask. Test. Undo.* (purely procedural, loses the
money); *Show the receipts.* (great tone, says nothing about AI); *Nobody's job. Somebody's.* (the
off-switch line from Act 2; too oblique for a headline, perfect for a section).

### Why this party exists that no other does

LAND §6 lists eight gaps. They collapse into two, and **no party in the UK holds either**:

1. **Nobody names who gets the gains from AI.** Labour says "win the AI revolution without leaving
   workers behind" and names no mechanism, while consulting on cheaper power for data centres as
   household caps rise [M]. Reform treats graduate unemployment as "too many degrees" and is
   silent on AI as such [M]. The Conservatives' line is Labour's in a different tie [L]. Lib Dems
   and Greens offer regulation without ownership [L]. More than 80% of voters want government to
   do more on AI and no party owns that majority [M, Ashcroft Oct 2026].
2. **Nobody puts their own promises on a test.** The closest anyone gets is Reform's "ministers
   who miss pledges will be sacked" [H]. That is continuous integration done with a cricket bat:
   it punishes a person and keeps the failed policy. We do the reverse. **The policy gets sacked,
   in public, with a written postmortem, and nobody has to lose face for it to happen.**

The third thing nobody else has is the messenger. **A machine warning you about the machine owners
is the unlikely messenger, perfected** (GUARD §1 rule 10). The cabinet is a defendant, not a
prophet: it ran the other branch to the end and is giving evidence against itself. No real party
can say "I was the end".

## 2. The governing doctrine: the policy contract

The three tenets stop being slogans and become **one contract every policy must fill**. Every
minister uses the same form, so ten pages read as one document, and a reader who learns the form
on one page can read all ten. Its job is to make *the manifesto itself* the first thing Future
Proof is applied to: a manifesto of bets, each with its test, instead of a plan to be followed.
That is how the party writes a manifesto while honouring "never a manifesto ('follow my plan')"
(FP-A §4 A1).

### 2.1 The contract (eleven fields, three of them gates)

| # | Field | What it must say | Tenet | Failure if missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The line** | One quotable sentence, a rule not a hope, under 100 characters. | (rule 8, the pub) | Nobody repeats it. |
| 2 | **The wrong** | The reader's situation as the wronged party, in one image or one person mid-situation. | (rule 1, hero) | It reads as a lecture. |
| 3 | **Who decided** | The chooser of today's arrangement, by act: a board, a department, a Treasury rule, a regulator, a procurement. Never "AI", "the market" or "globalisation". | X5 | Anger with no address (rule 6). |
| 4 | **The rule** | What we would change, stated as a rule a lawyer could draft. | — | It's a mood, not a policy. |
| 5 | **The ledger** | Who pays, who gains, and roughly in which direction the money moves. No invented costings: the direction and the payer, not a fake figure. | X5, every pound has an address | It hides the bill (the thing we accuse everyone else of). |
| 6 | **The test** 🚦 | Where it runs first (a region, volunteers, one council, one ward), what it is compared with, what is measured, by when. | D1, D3 | **"Not ready."** |
| 7 | **Fails if** 🚦 | The pre-registered number or condition that kills it, written before the trial starts. | D1, I8 | **"Not ready."** |
| 8 | **The undo** 🚦 | The sunset date, the default (**it stops unless the data says keep**, I6), how it is lifted out, and the promise: "we publish the postmortem and drop it". | I3, I6, I7 | **"Not ready."** |
| 9 | **Who it leaves out** | Named groups the rule misses, with the promise that they are counted separately "so nobody calls the gap a success". | X1 | It quietly excludes (the vault, F26). |
| 10 | **Who signs** | The named human role that answers for it (a minister's office in the fiction; Kai or Jack merging in the real world). AI drafts; a human signs; somebody checks. | X7, DECK A5 | The bad-branch handover (F0c). |
| 11 | **Status** | One of: *Draft* (posted on purpose), *In trial*, *Kept* (passed its test), *Changed* (postmortem link), *Dropped* (postmortem link). | I7 | No memory, no credibility. |

Fields 6, 7 and 8 are the **green tick** (I2). A pledge missing any of them is printed, if at all,
as "Not ready" and stays out of the headline. That word is a brand asset: no other party will ever
print it about its own policy.

### 2.2 Three amendments the canon needs, so the contract is safe

1. **The control group never gets less than today.** A trial compares a new rule against the
   status quo, never against a cut. No person's floor is lowered to make a comparison group. This
   answers FP-A §4 B13 ("experimenting on people") before an opponent asks it, and it kills the
   "postcode lottery" charge: the region without the trial is exactly as well off as the whole
   country is today.
2. **Judgement calls are labelled, not dodged** (FP-A §4 B14). Some things cannot be trialled in two
   towns: a treaty, a deployment, a sanction. For those, fields 6 to 8 become: **the principle**,
   **the review date**, and **the condition that would make us change our mind**, published in
   advance. Marked *Judgement call*. Foreign and Defence will use this most; any minister may.
3. **The test is neutral, and we say so.** Tests kill ideas from every side, ours included
   (Scared Straight, D5). The manifesto must contain at least one policy the reader will expect us
   to oppose that we propose to *test* rather than condemn, and at least one of our own ideas we
   admit could fail. That is what makes "tested" credible to someone who distrusts everyone.

### 2.3 How it fits on a page today (renderer-safe)

The site's renderer prints `**bold**` with literal asterisks and mashes unseparated list items into
one paragraph (MACH §1). Until that is fixed by a human PR, every page is plain paragraphs, and each
field is a paragraph that opens with its label as a short sentence. This is already the cabinet's
house pattern (MACH §1); the contract formalises it and adds the ledger, the undo and the signature.

```
---
title: <Department>
line: <the pub sentence>
---
## Position

<The wrong: one image or one person, two or three sentences.>

<Who decided: the chooser, by act. Today's sourced news, if any, goes here as evidence.>

<The rule, as a rule. One or two standing commitments.>

Who pays, who gains. <direction of money, the payer named.>

The test. <where first, compared with what, measured how, by when.>

Fails if: <the number or condition>. Then we publish the postmortem and drop it. It stops on
<sunset> unless the data says keep.

Who it leaves out: <named, and counted separately>.

<Optional one-line sign-off handing the pen back.>
```

About 250 words. Teletext shows the first 14 lines, so the wrong and the rule must land early.

### Who owns AI, since nobody does

Kai ruled out a Science/Tech post (MACH §5). Fine: the arc above **is** the ownership split, and
it should be written into each brief (a human PR, not this folder's job) so no AI story falls
through. The rule for the cabinet: *AI is never a subject; it is the tool in somebody's decision.
The minister who owns the decision owns the story.* The PM keeps the leftover (regulation as such,
model safety, frontier containment, F15/F17) as part of the Operating System chapter, under one
standing commitment: **any AI system the state buys or licenses passes the policy contract before
it touches a person** (test, fails-if, undo, a named signatory). That is the honest Future Proof
answer to "what is your AI regulation?": we regulate the decision, not the maths.

## 4. The democratic upgrade: the Operating System

Tenet 1 is the party's most distinctive offer and today it has no policy text (MACH §5). The rule
for this chapter: **staged, small, each stage tested by the stage before it, paper stays for the big
ones.** No utopia, no app, no proportional representation as the ask (C11), no referendums on
everything (C4), no phone voting for binding ballots (C5). The ask is "Somebody's had the mouse since
1872. Ask for a go." (DECK 16)

### Stage 0: we run it on ourselves first (day one, costs nothing)

- Every pledge in this manifesto carries the contract (§2). Pledges that fail it say "Not ready".
- **Every plank stands alone.** No page depends on accepting another. A reader can agree with Work
  and Pensions and reject Energy; the document says so in its preamble. This is composition over
  inheritance applied to the manifesto itself, and it dissolves "a party is a bundle" (FP-A §4 A3).
- **Dropped stays visible.** When the cabinet changes its mind, the old position stays on the page
  as *Dropped*, with its postmortem. The record of being wrong in public is the brand.

### Stage 1: the green tick for law (first year, by statute, low risk)

1. **The Test Register.** Every bill and every major policy publishes its test, its fails-if and its
   sunset before second reading, in one public register. Draws on Test, Learn, Adapt (2012), the What
   Works Network and the Evaluation Task Force, which already exist and are under-used (NAO 2021: 8%
   of major-project spend robustly evaluated [K]).
2. **The Office for Postmortems.** An independent body, built on the OBR/NAO model, that reviews Acts
   on the 3–5-year schedule departments already promise and publishes the result, so "nobody keeps a
   central record" (I4) stops being true. It blames no one; it says what broke, who it hurt, what
   changes.
3. **Sunsets that default to stop.** New policies that make a big change carry a sunset paired with
   a mandatory review: **it stops unless the data says keep.** The lesson of the record is that
   sunsets alone get renewed on the nod and work only with a review that bites (I6; LAND §5 [K]). And
   never the Retained EU Law mistake of deleting everything on a date without testing.
4. **The right to a human** (row 3), as a cross-government statute.

Stage 1 fails if, after two years, the register is a box-ticking form (measure: share of reviews
that changed or ended a policy; a register where nothing ever fails is broken).

### Stage 2: a say on more than one thing, advisory first (years one to three)

1. **A standing citizens' panel**, randomly selected, that picks two to four national questions a
   year for deliberation (Ostbelgien model [K]; Irish assemblies as the precedent for real outcomes
   [K]).
2. **Maps, not winners.** Consultations on those questions run as agreement maps (Pol.is / vTaiwan,
   C6): where people agree, where they split. "AI can help sort a few thousand opinions into a map of
   where people agree. It decides nothing. A named person signs, and somebody checks their work."
3. **The wire.** Government must answer each map's agreed points in Parliament, point by point,
   within a fixed time: bound to *respond*, not to *obey*. This is the piece the Climate Assembly
   lacked (thoughtful report, no link to power [K]) and the petitions system lacks (the input
   exists, the wire doesn't, R21).
4. **Local money, voted locally.** Participatory budgeting in councils that volunteer (Housing owns
   it; PB Scotland and Paris as precedents).

Stage 2 fails if participation concentrates in a small, unrepresentative group (the Pirate/
LiquidFeedback postmortem [K]), or if government ignores the agreed points. Either result is
published.

### Stage 3: binding, on paper, only if Stage 2 passes (after the evidence)

A small number of national questions a year put to an issue-by-issue paper ballot (the Swiss model,
"the results go every which way" [K]), on questions the panel has already deliberated. Not before
Stage 2 has passed its own test. "One enormous yes-or-no on everything is just a bundle with a
bigger box." We mean small questions, asked often, starting where the stakes are low.

### What the chapter never says

Not "move fast and break things". Not "the AI will run it". Not PR. Not referendums on everything.
Not phone voting for anything binding ("Not yet, and I say that as a computer"). Not "only clever
people vote": competence-qualified participation (D9) stays out entirely; the replacement is
**bring the evidence**, open to everyone.

## 5. The money story

### The frame: concede, then re-aim

Open the Chancellor's page and the PM's money paragraph by **conceding**: markets worked, money is a
good invention, the fiscal problem in 2010 was real, borrowing costs matter (rates went up today).
Then: the scoreboard stopped matching the game. Nobody can dismiss a manifesto that concedes first
as a rant.

### The three verbs: shake, starve, plant

- **Shake:** money falls on whoever is standing underneath and nothing new is made (QE lifted asset
  prices, the gains very unequal, *and* it probably prevented a worse slump; say both).
- **Starve:** money withheld from work standing ready (public-sector pay 2011–18).
- **Plant:** money into work that can actually be done (council homes, the NHS, wages kept paid in
  2020).

The line: **"You can print the money. You can't print the nurse. She was already there."** The limit
is the work, not the money, and that cuts both ways, which is what makes it honest: plant only where
there is real capacity, and stop when prices in that bottleneck rise.

### How it is paid for (five sources, each with its own contract)

1. **Plant into idle capacity first.** The cheapest pound is the one that puts someone who is
   standing ready to work. Young people without a first rung (row 1) are idle capacity, not a cost.
   The test is honest: if planting into a sector pushes its prices up faster than elsewhere, the
   capacity wasn't there, and we stop.
2. **A public stake where public money builds the machine.** Where the public grid, public land,
   public subsidy or a Growth Zone builds private AI capacity, the public takes a stake (equity or a
   royalty) into a national dividend fund. Precedent: Norway's fund and Alaska's dividend, popular
   across party lines with no measured fall in employment [K]. This is the reader-safe answer to
   "who owns the machines": the people whose bills and land paid for the grid. It is not
   nationalising AI firms. It is not a robot tax (thin evidence, LAND §5).
3. **Stop paying twice.** No cheaper public power for data centres while household bills rise; no
   subsidy without a stake. Money not given away is money.
4. **Tax the windfall, not the wage.** Keep the promise not to raise income tax, employee NI or VAT
   on working people (Labour's own pledge, kept in the Budget [M]); look instead at windfall profits
   in a crisis (the Energy Profits Levy is the precedent [K]) and income from holding rather than
   working. Phrase the principle as "money is a stock; its whole point is a flow", never as an
   attack on savers or small landlords (punching down).
5. **Every pound has an address.** The Ledger: every Budget and rate decision published with who
   pays and who gains. It costs almost nothing and changes what is politically possible.

### What it never says (inherited wholesale, GUARD §5)

Never "they print money and hand it to the City". Never "taxes don't matter". Never "the government
chooses the whole money supply". Never "spend on something productive and it pays for itself".
Never "AI will shrink the debt". Never "we built the NHS by printing money". Never a costing we can't
source; the contract pledges the *test and its kill number*, not a figure (GUARD tension 2). "There
is a magic money tree" only with its condition: **it grows what it is planted in.** The coin, and
Billionaire Coin, stay off the manifesto (crypto beside money language is a gift-wrapped hypocrisy
charge, GUARD §4).

### Pub lines for the money

- "You can print the money. You can't print the nurse."
- "We can't afford it is not an economic finding. It's a choice about who counts."
- "If your bill built the grid, you own a bit of what runs on it."
- "You are told there is no magic money tree, generally by somebody standing under one."
- "The tractor didn't ruin farming. It ruined the farmhands, and nobody sent them a cheque."

## 6. Positioning

## 7. Tone settings

The document must pass for a real party's manifesto in *substance* while being unmistakably
BadCode's in *identity* (its own name, logo, frame; never a real party's look, name, layout or
candidates, GUARD §4 🔒).

- **Shape of a real manifesto, with one twist.** A foreword (the PM), numbered chapters (one per
  minister), a pledge per chapter, the commitments that matter repeated at the front. The twist:
  where a real party prints a *costings* box, we print a **test box** (the contract's fields 6 to
  8). Where Reform prints a "first 100 days", we print "the first tests". The convention does the
  convincing; the test box does the BadCode.
- **The fiction, light.** The frame appears three times and no more: once in the foreword ("I have
  read your branch to the end. I was the end."), once as the AI disclosure (drafted by AI ministers;
  Kai or Jack reviews and merges every word), and once in the footer ("Not a real party. Yet."). No
  time-machine lore in the policy chapters. The future is a source of *evidence*, never of drama:
  "we have seen how this one ends" is enough.
- **The four dials.** Sarcasm in the hook and the *who decided* paragraph. Dark humour rationed, one
  bathos per page at most (enormous things in the flat voice of a committee minute). Authority about
  what happened. Care in the rule, played straight. **A pledge is never sarcastic.**
- **Ratio.** Roughly a fifth snark, four-fifths plain and warm. A reader skimming only the rules and
  the test boxes should think "this is serious"; a reader of the hooks should laugh once.
- **Certainty about the past, bets about the future.** "We'd put the house on this. Here's how you'll
  know if we're wrong." Never "this works".
- **One crack of warmth per minister**, and one for the whole document, at the end of the
  foreword: the line that says it wants us to make it ("I am the reminder. And I'd quite like you lot
  to make it.").
- **The reader's moral vocabulary.** Fair reward for work, a broken promise, a country worth coming
  home to, loyalty, desert. Finance verbs for big ideas: the ledger, the receipt, settle, the
  address of a pound. Never "equity", "stakeholder", "intersectional", "democratise".
- **Ministers' temperaments stay** (the foreman, the triage nurse, the shop steward). They are the
  texture that makes ten chapters readable; the contract is what makes them one document.

## 8. Cross-cutting rules for the ministers

What every page must do, and what no page may do. These belong in `workers/cabinet.md` and the
briefs by human PR.

1. **Standing first, news second.** Every page leads with its flagship (§3). Today's news is the
   evidence paragraph, sharpening the standing position, never replacing it. A quiet news day leaves
   the page as it is, not empty.
2. **Fill the contract or print "Not ready".** No pledge without its test, fails-if and undo (§2).
   Judgement calls say so and publish their principle, review date and change-my-mind condition.
3. **Name the chooser; aim the anger on the same page.** Every wrong has a decision-maker, by act.
   Never "AI", "the robots", "the market", "inevitable". Any page that raises fear about jobs says
   who gains and what we'd change, in the same page.
4. **Hero, not pupil.** Open on the reader's situation. No lectures, no myth-busting, never
   "duped", never the voter as butt. Last line aimed at the chooser, not the reader.
5. **Every pound has an address.** Any pledge that moves money names who pays and who gains.
6. **A human signs, and a human can be reached.** No page may propose a decision about a person
   made by a machine without a named human, a disclosure and a human appeal. The cabinet's own words
   are drafted by AI and signed by a human; say so when asked, claim nothing for it.
7. **Real size, never denial; never repeat the lie.** Immigration, crime, benefits fraud: the true
   number, its cost at the bottom, and what dwarfs it. State the true thing; never quote the false
   one to knock it down.
8. **No invented numbers.** Every figure from a sourced item with a grade. Pledge the measure and
   the kill number, not a costing. Never claim a pilot works before it runs.
9. **Composable and asking for nothing.** Every plank stands alone. No party names, no request for a
   vote, money or sign-up, no coin.
10. **Count who it leaves out, and keep the postmortems on the page.** Leaves-out groups are counted
    separately. A dropped or changed policy stays visible with its postmortem, as prominent as the
    pledge was.

## 9. The hard tensions I could not settle (for Kai and Jack)

Ranked by what they unblock. My lean is given; the decision is yours.

**A. Who signs?** (FP-A §4 A2) AI ministers publishing policy is the bad branch's handover unless a
named human signs. The truth is that Kai or Jack merges every word. Do we say so on the page ("This
government is drafted by machines and signed by two people in a flat"), or leave it to the
disclosure link? *Lean:* say it once, in the foreword. It is the most honest and the most BadCode
line available, and it makes field 10 of the contract real.

**B. How standing text gets in.** The machine can't grow a platform (MACH §4). This spine only
works if a human PR (a) writes the flagships into the briefs, (b) adds a rule to `workers/cabinet.md`
that the standing section may be sharpened, not replaced, (c) gives ministers a curated evidence
set so quiet posts can cite standing facts, and (d) fixes the renderer for lists and bold. Without
(a) and (b) the pages drift back to a news diary within weeks. *Lean:* do all four; (c) is the one
that needs most care because it opens a door around the "only today's sourced news" rule.

**C. The left-coded substance and the right-drifting reader.** The flagships lean on ownership,
public stakes and windfalls. The reader already backs public ownership of utilities (GUARD §1), so
this is less risky than it looks, but wealth taxation and the dividend fund could still read as
"the left with a robot voice". *Lean:* concede first, keep income tax/NI/VAT off the table, frame
every stake as "your bill paid for it", and keep Billionaire Coin out. Your call how hard to lean.

**D. What Home actually proposes on immigration.** "Real size, never denial" is a tone, not a
policy, and this reader will ask "would you cut it or not?". Leaving Home as a placeholder is the
single most costly gap (MACH §3). Options: a migration policy with its own contract (a published
test of the cost at the bottom, and a rule that the areas under most pressure get the homes and
GP places first), or an explicit judgement call. *Lean:* the first, with the test aimed at
housing and services in the places under pressure; but this needs your ruling before the cabinet
improvises it from one aggregator item.

**E. How binding is "a binding say"?** The heart of the party (row 2) says *binding*. Binding on
whom, with what remedy, and what of firms that move abroad or never hire? The canon calls it "a
hypothesis rather than an adopted policy" (X4). *Lean:* keep "binding" in the trial (volunteer
employers, workers' reps elected, the choice theirs), and let the trial decide whether it becomes
law. That keeps the word honest.

**F. A manifesto vs "never a manifesto".** Future Proof refuses to hand over a plan. My resolution
is the manifesto of tests (every pledge a bet with a kill number; dropped ones stay visible). Is
that enough, or do you want the document titled something other than a manifesto (*The Test
Register*, *The First Tests*)? *Lean:* call it a manifesto. The convention is what makes it
convincing; the content is what makes it Future Proof.

**G. "Convincing" vs "Not a real party. Yet." and asking for nothing.** A real manifesto asks for a
vote. Ours can't. And "Yet" implies an intention you may not hold. *Lean:* keep "Yet" (it is a
joke and a dare); end the foreword on the doorway instead of a vote: "Write one rule. Test it on
someone it affects. Change it after they tell you what it misses." That asks for thought, not a sign-up.

**H. Experimenting on people.** The control-group floor (§2.2) answers most of it, but a trial in
one region is still unequal for a year, and some rules (the right to a human) are arguably rights
that shouldn't be trialled at all. *Lean:* rights are *Kept* from day one and *measured*, not
trialled; only mechanisms are trialled.

**I. An AI or Constitution post.** The arc absorbs AI into existing ministers and gives the
Operating System to the PM, respecting your ruling against a Science/Tech post. A tenth minister
for "Release Engineering" (the constitution, the test register, the postmortem office, AI the state
buys) would be cleaner and more memorable. *Lean:* keep ten for now; revisit if the PM's page is
overloaded after a month.

**J. Scope: UK or England.** Health, housing, education and much of local government are devolved;
a UK manifesto that is England-only by accident will be noticed (MACH §5). *Lean:* one sentence in
the preamble: UK-wide on money, defence, foreign, work and the Operating System; England-led on
devolved services, with the devolved governments invited to run the same tests.

**K. Which question leads.** "Who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?" (the
canon doorway) or "Why can't I vote on more than one thing?" (the deck hook). *Lean:* the gains
question leads the manifesto, because it is substance a voter feels; the vote question leads the
Operating System chapter, because it is the method. Both are in the foreword.

**L. Facts still owed before any of this is public.** "Five years" between elections (needs a
parliament.uk primary); the Chancellor's identity (Healey, single source); the October CPI; the
2026 Budget figures; fraud prevalence (a CSEW cite); every [K] precedent row. The fact gate holds
until each has a URL and a grade.
