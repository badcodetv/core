## 1. TL;DR

1. **The claim:** AI will decide who earns what in this country for a generation, and that decision is being made now, by named boards and departments, with no test and no undo. We change who decides, and how.
2. **The offer, in three words: Tested. Undoable. Yours.** Every pledge ships with its test, the number that would kill it, and a stop date that only a vote of MPs can extend; rights (a human you can ask; no machine decides who dies) never expire and are measured instead.
3. **Six front-page pledges:** a human you can ask; every law tested, with a stop date; the Transition Count (eight numbers a quarter, a minister answers for each); a say before the saving, plus a bridge for anyone made redundant; households first on the grid; keep the bottom rung.
4. **The money:** concede first, keep the fiscal rules, no rise in income tax, NI or VAT on working people; the public takes a slice where public money builds the machine; one windfall law; and a ledger of the manifesto itself, in honest bands, which no other party publishes.
5. **The voice:** the reader's week first (the bill, the letter, the GP, the payslip), the machinery second; the time-machine fiction three times only; every page ends on the chooser, never on homework for the reader.

## 2. The thesis, the doctrine and the policy contract

### The thesis (from `spine.md` §1, unchanged)

> The arrival of AI will decide who earns what in this country for a generation, and right now that
> decision is being made without you, by a system with no test and no undo. We upgrade the system
> first, we give the people the change lands on a say in where the gains go, and every promise we
> make ships with the number that would prove it wrong.

**Why this party exists that no other does.** Two gaps no UK party holds: nobody names *who gets the
gains* from AI, and nobody puts their own promises on a test (the closest is Reform's "sack the
minister", which punishes a person and keeps the failed policy; we sack the policy, in public, with
a write-up). The third asset is the messenger: a machine warning you about the machine owners.

**The three words: Tested. Undoable. Yours.** *Tested* is tenet 3 (no policy without its experiment).
*Undoable* is tenet 2 (small changes with a stop date and a write-up). *Yours* carries both halves:
you choose issue by issue, not the bundle (tenet 1), and you get a share of what the machines make.

### The doctrine: the policy contract, revised

Every pledge on every page fills the same form, so ten pages read as one document. The spine had
eleven fields; the red team's fixes make it twelve and change three.

**The four standing amendments.**

1. **The control group never gets less than today** (spine). Plus: where everyone gets it eventually, **the order is drawn by lot**, which kills the postcode-lottery charge better than anything else.
2. **Judgement calls are labelled.** Treaties, deployments, the convention on human rights: principle, review date, and the condition that would change our mind, published in advance.
3. **The test is neutral.** Each chapter carries one idea the reader expects us to oppose that we'd test instead (street votes, AI in defence logistics, North Sea licensing as a judgement call), and one of our own that we admit could fail (run-by-hand, the public network region).
4. **What never sunsets: rights, and only rights.** A published list: the right to a human; no machine selects a person to attack; no machine uses force on a person. These are *Kept* from day one and measured, never trialled. Everything new that moves money, up or down, tax or benefit, carries the same review-and-renewal vote (wonk §3.6: cuts and rises treated alike, no one-way ratchet).

**One owner per shared policy** (opposition #8, wonk §2). Settled here so the ministers stop breaking each other's rules:

| Shared thing | One version, one owner | Everyone else |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counts and registers | **Two instruments only:** the Transition Count (ONS; first rung, automation redundancies, roofs and the rest are rows in it) and the Ledger | Cite the row; no ministerial "counts" of their own |
| Right to a human | **One framework statute (PM)**, built on UK GDPR Art. 22 as reworked by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, with sector deadlines by schedule: benefits 4 weeks, clinical same working day, tenancy 10 working days, policing at the stop | Their page states their deadline |
| Host-town compact | **Housing owns the form** (planning is the legal hook); residents have the last word | Energy fills the grid and water section; the Chancellor sets the national/local money split |
| Windfall levy | **One Windfall Act (Chancellor)** with an electricity schedule (the existing Electricity Generator Levy, raised) and an oil-and-gas schedule; one destination: the standing charge, prepayment first | Energy delivers it on bills |
| Resilience | **One programme and one budget line, held by Defence:** run-by-hand drills | Foreign supplies the switch list of foreign dependencies; Energy runs the grid drill |
| Lethal force | **Defence's wording, word for word:** "No UK weapon selects a person to attack"; incoming missiles and drones are fair game for a fast machine | Foreign and Home use it unchanged |
| Public stakes | **One split rule (Chancellor):** host town first, then the national fund. NHS data returns stay in the NHS, as a stated exception | |
| Retraining | **Education owns courses** | W&P says "paid training for a job that exists" |

### How it looks on a page

Unchanged from the spine (§2.3): plain paragraphs, each field opening with its label as a short
sentence, about 250 words, the wrong and the rule in the first 14 lines for teletext. Plus the
reader's vocabulary rule: *postmortem* becomes "the write-up of what went wrong" the first time and
"the write-up" after; *sunset* becomes "stop date"; *comparison* becomes "against towns like it". No
six-word phrase on more than two pages ("so nobody calls the gap a success" lives on the PM page
only), because a visible template is what a chatbot sounds like (reader X4).

## 3. The AI-transition plan in one table

The eight things that go wrong as AI arrives, in the order the reader feels them. Every row has one
owner. The numbers in "Stop condition" are **provisional** kill numbers, to be fixed before each trial
starts (and shown beside any later change).

| # | Risk (in the reader's words) | Owner | Flagship policy | The test | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The first rung disappears.** Entry jobs go first, so the young never learn the job they'd later supervise | **Education** (W&P on money) | **The Rung Fund:** a big employer whose youth hiring falls well behind firms in its own trade and area pays the gap, capped, into a town first-jobs fund. Plus learning time, and the first-rung row in the Transition Count | Two areas drawn by lot from volunteers, three years, against matched areas | Youth hiring falls further in the trial areas, or fewer than half of funded trainees are in work a year on. Stops year four unless MPs vote to keep it |
| 2 | **The saving goes up.** The owner books the gain; the worker gets the letter | **Work and Pensions** (Chancellor books it) | **A say before the saving** (affected workers choose: shorter week on the same pay, profit share, or paid training), plus a cost-free **automation count** on the redundancy form, plus a **cause-blind bridge** for anyone made redundant after two years of NI | Employers who sign up, one region, two years, against similar firms that automated without it | Workers not at least 5% better off in pay or hours, or the trial firms invest clearly less. Passes: put to Parliament as law |
| 3 | **The computer says no.** Decisions about you move to systems with no human who can say yes | **PM** (the statute); W&P, Health, Housing, Home apply it | **A right to a human:** told a machine was used, a named signatory, a human appeal by a set deadline. Health's **Ward Test** and Education's classroom test keep untested tools off people in the first place | A right, so measured, not trialled: share of appeals reaching a human on time, published quarterly | Below 9 in 10 on time in any sector triggers the minister's answer in Parliament within 30 days. No stop date |
| 4 | **The machines get the power and the water first** | **Energy and Climate** (Housing owns the compact; Chancellor the stake) | **Households first on the grid:** big new loads pay their own connection, switch down first in a shortage, never get a discount paid from household bills; the **host town writes the deal** | One trial region | No big project signs for a connection in three years, or bills there rise faster than elsewhere |
| 5 | **Growth nobody received.** The economy grows; wages don't | **Chancellor** | **The Ledger** (who pays, who gains, for every Budget and public deal) and **the Public Slice** (a stake where public cash, land, discounted power or guarantees build private compute), plus the **pension vote** (see You-decide 4) | The next publicly supported data-centre deal carries a slice, against comparable deals without | Slice firms build at least a fifth less per pound of support after three years. Then sell the stake |
| 6 | **The fake, the scam and the feed** | **Home** (PM for elections) | **Name it, refund it:** the bank, the platform and the phone network that carried the scam share the refund; synthetic political ads say who paid; votes counted on paper by people | One scam type first (fake investment ads using a real face or voice), 18 months, against its own trend and uncovered types | Losses for the covered type don't fall against trend, or refunds get slower. If it passes, the liability becomes permanent (an 18-month sunset on a liability rule kills the incentive, wonk §8) |
| 7 | **Systems nobody can switch off** | **Defence** (Foreign: switch list; Energy: grid drill) | **No UK weapon selects a person to attack**, in law; **run-by-hand drills** for water, power, labs, payments, ports; **fixable here** procurement | Drills, volunteers first, published | Fewer than 3 drills in 4 keep the service above its floor by year two, or a failure isn't fixed before the next. The lethal rule has no stop date; its yearly audit of who authorised what is the check |
| 8 | **Nobody's job.** Choices handed over one at a time until nobody believes anything they do matters | **PM** | **The Operating System** (section 5): every law with a test and a stop date, one small office for tests and write-ups, a citizens' panel with a wire into Parliament | Two years of the register | Fewer than 1 review in 10 changes or ends a policy, or fewer than 9 in 10 come out on time: the register is a form, rebuild it |

**Who owns AI, since no post does:** AI is never a subject; it is the tool in somebody's decision,
and the minister who owns the decision owns the story. The PM keeps the leftover (regulation as such,
frontier safety, the state's own use of AI) under one standing commitment: **any AI system the state
buys, licenses or uses itself passes the policy contract before it touches a person.** "We regulate
the decision, not the maths." The last clause (the state's *own* use, wonk §7.4) is new: for a party
run by AI ministers it is the most obvious gap and the easiest pledge.

## 4. Per minister

Each entry: the line (the page's headline), the flagships, what it refuses, and the red team's main
hit with what was done. The full contract for every flagship is in the linked file.

### Prime Minister ([`ministers/00-prime-minister.md`](./ministers/00-prime-minister.md))

**Line:** *Every law gets a test before you live in it, an undo if it fails, and a human you can ask.*

- **The green tick for law:** every new bill publishes its test, fails-if and stop date before second reading (by Commons standing order); one independent office reviews it on time.
- **A right to a human:** one framework statute across government, with sector deadlines.
- **The Transition Count:** eight quarterly numbers, one per arc row, each with a pre-registered trigger obliging the owning minister to answer in Parliament within 30 days (the Bank's inflation open-letter rule is the British precedent).
- **A say on more than one thing:** a jury-style citizens' panel picks its own questions; Parliament must answer every agreed point within three months.

**Refuses:** that any of this works; referendums on everything; phone voting for anything binding; PR as the ask; votes weighted by expertise; an AI Act that regulates the maths; mass sunsets of old law.

**Red team's main hit:** the front door was a constitution seminar ("We promise to enact Future Proof. ... I have read your branch to the end."), scored 4/10 by the reader. **Fixed:** the promise is followed by its plain-words meaning, "a human you can ask" moves to paragraph two, the fiction is softened, the panel no longer has its first question set by us, and "every chapter stands alone" becomes "can be voted on alone; where one needs another, it says which". Flagged: the PM page is still mostly machinery; if it still reads as process after a month, lead with the right to a human.

### Chancellor of the Exchequer ([`ministers/01-chancellor.md`](./ministers/01-chancellor.md))

**Line:** *Every pound has an address, and if public money builds the machine, the public owns a slice.*

- **The Ledger:** who pays and who gains, by household, for every Budget and public deal, within four weeks. The Bank is asked to publish its own once a year.
- **The Public Slice:** an investor's stake where public cash, land, discounted power or guarantees build private compute, into a national fund, host town first.
- **The Level:** publish the tax gap between hiring a person and automating the job; close it from the bottom.
- **The Windfall Act:** one standing law; a shock abroad switches the levy on, prices falling switch it off; Energy delivers it on bills.
- **The Plant Rule, cut down to what is new:** investment is already outside the day-to-day rule; what's new is that each line names its idle capacity and a published price brake pauses it.

**Refuses:** income tax, employee NI or VAT rises on working people; a dividend cheque or date; "AI will pay off the debt"; touching the Bank's independence or "people's QE"; a robot tax; nationalising AI firms; a wealth tax this parliament (Not ready, not ruled out); any coin.

**Red team's main hit:** "every pound has an address" from the one manifesto with no money annex, plus "you can print the money" clipped the week rates rose. **Fixed:** fiscal-rules sentence on the page; a ledger of the manifesto in bands (section 6); "print the money" dropped for "Money isn't what runs out. Nurses are."; the MPC receipt replaced by an annual Bank publication; the slice limited to what a valuer can price. **Flagged:** the slice is too small to answer "who gets the gains" on its own (You-decide 4).

### Foreign Secretary ([`ministers/02-foreign.md`](./ministers/02-foreign.md))

**Line:** *Nobody abroad, friend or rival, gets a switch on what this country runs on.*

- **The switch list:** the ten foreign dependencies that would hurt most to lose, each with a named owner here and a tested second way to run it (feeds Defence's single resilience programme).
- **A receipt for every big decision:** within 30 days of a sanction, deal or deployment, the expected cost to the weekly shop, who carries it, and the check date.
- **Trade with a receipt:** Parliament votes on the negotiating mandate and the whole deal; every chapter gets an independent receipt for food prices and jobs here.
- **No machine selects a person to attack,** in any treaty we sign; no machine in the nuclear chain.
- **Aid that has to beat cash** (off the page until aid is news).

**Refuses:** to say how a war ends; lower bills through foreign policy; rejoining anything; restoring 0.7%; "AI superpower"; taking sides in another country's election; "deep concern".

**Red team's main hit:** opened with "Every empire believed its trade routes were permanent" (a sneer to the reader, an empire joke to the Guardian), and chapter-by-chapter treaty ratification is not something a treaty allows. **Fixed:** opens at the pump; mandate-plus-whole-deal votes; receipt published after, not before; Defence's lethal wording; the fiction line cut.

### Home Secretary ([`ministers/03-home.md`](./ministers/03-home.md))

**Line:** *Every fear gets its real size, every route its roof, and every scam a name and a refund.*

- **The roof comes with the arrival:** routes the government controls (work visas, students' families) don't grow faster than the homes, GP and school places funded for them; Parliament votes on that plan alone, once a year; the towns taking most are paid first.
- **The boats:** every asylum claim decided within six months; crossings, decisions and returns published monthly; fails if the backlog is bigger in a year. The convention: not left as the fix, a labelled judgement call with its change-my-mind condition.
- **Nobody gets hired to undercut you:** back wages and the fine land on the boss; a worker's report can't be used against them.
- **Name the scam, refund the victim** (arc row 6).
- **No machine uses force on a person, ever;** police tech under one framework law, each trialled in one force.

**Refuses:** a date the boats stop; a net-migration number from the air; "immigration has no costs"; mass deportation or leaving a treaty as the fix; a headline police number; a facial-recognition ban.

**Red team's main hit:** the worst in the set for damage. "It is real and it is small" (Reform's clip), "a little off the lowest wages" (the left's clip), no mention of boats on the reader's first issue, and a capacity rule nobody could operate on demand-led routes. **Fixed in the draft as the recommended option, but this page needs your ruling before anything ships** (You-decide 1).

### Education ([`ministers/04-education.md`](./ministers/04-education.md))

**Line:** *Keep the bottom rung on the ladder, and make whoever saws it off pay for a new one.*

- **The first-rung count:** under-25 first jobs and apprenticeships, quarterly, by industry and town (a row of the Transition Count).
- **The Rung Fund** (arc row 1), baseline relative to the firm's trade and area, capped.
- **Learning time:** where trainees work alongside AI, part of the week goes on doing the job themselves.
- **A second go, with a job at the end:** public retraining money only for courses with an employer waiting, results published.
- **Schools:** no AI tool bought with public money before a classroom trial; saved teacher time stays with pupils.

**Refuses:** a job for everyone; an apprenticeship target number; any tuition-fee promise; a list of "jobs of the future"; "learn to code"; a ban on AI in schools; blaming teachers, kids or parents.

**Red team's main hit:** the Rung Fund as drafted was "a jobs tax in a recession" (every firm's youth hiring falls in a downturn) with Equality Act exposure, and the count promised occupation data that payroll records don't hold. **Fixed:** relative baseline plus cap; count by industry. **Flagged:** legal advice on the age aim; SEND has no position (one honest "Not ready" plus a small rule is owed).

### Defence ([`ministers/05-defence.md`](./ministers/05-defence.md))

**Line:** *No machine decides who dies, and everything we depend on can run by hand.*

- **No UK weapon selects a person to attack,** in law, with a named officer for every shot and a yearly published audit; no stop date.
- **Run it by hand:** published drills for water, power, labs, payments, ports; the one resilience programme and budget line.
- **Fixable here:** nothing bought that we can't stop, mend or restart without a foreign supplier's permission; repair work in defence towns.
- **Who got the contract:** every big contract's winner, competition and stop point published.

**Refuses:** a budget number; a ban on military AI; that the world will sign; no successful cyberattack; reopening the deterrent; defence jobs as a reason for a war or a sale.

**Red team's main hit:** the strongest page, but Foreign and Defence spent the same "resilience slice" twice, and the fails-if ("can it name its post") was trivially passed. **Fixed:** one budget line held by Defence; the audit of decision time is the check; veterans and the people who serve added. **Flagged:** whether the NATO resilience money is a slice of 3% or a separate 1.5% decides the money sentence.

### Health ([`ministers/06-health.md`](./ministers/06-health.md))

**Line:** *Test it on a ward before you sell it to a country, and give the time it saves to patients.*

- **The Ward Test:** the existing checks for medical software (NICE, MHRA) made compulsory before a national purchase; roll-out in an order drawn by lot; nobody gets less care than today.
- **The time goes to the bed:** saved hours counted and published; the contract isn't renewed unless they went to patients.
- **A name on every decision:** triage, diagnosis, referral, discharge; a person the same working day; a person on the phone if software sorted your GP request.
- **The visit stays human, and the carer gets counted.**
- **If a company profits from NHS records, the NHS gets a cut.** No sale.

**Refuses:** a date to clear the list; any AI-savings figure; a reorganisation; keeping private providers out; a care settlement before Casey; a nurse-numbers pledge; an AI ban.

**Red team's main hit:** "fails if any group does worse" fails by chance with enough subgroups, and "fails if it doesn't beat normal care" had no margin. **Fixed:** a stated margin and named groups in advance. "Your records pay rent" rewritten so it can't read as selling data.

### Work and Pensions ([`ministers/07-work-pensions.md`](./ministers/07-work-pensions.md))

**Line:** *Before a firm automates your job, you get a say in the saving, and a human reads your claim.*

- **Count it:** one question on the redundancy form, a statistic only, no cost attached.
- **A say before the saving** (arc row 2), with employers who sign up; law only if it passes.
- **A human reads your claim:** named signatory, machine disclosed, human review within four weeks; nobody's money stops until a person signs.
- **No cut before the check:** no benefit cut before its review reports; every cut and sanction rule tested on its own aim.
- **The bridge:** cause-blind, earnings-related support for up to a year after two years of NI, for any redundancy.
- Secondary: **the triple lock this parliament**, with healthy life expectancy published before any pension-age rise; **your pension votes for you** (proposed for promotion, You-decide 4); the paid first job, tested again; no cliff edges.

**Refuses:** UBI; a robot tax; "no job will be lost"; "AI is causing mass layoffs now"; denying benefit fraud; reversing every cut on day one.

**Red team's main hit:** the bridge for "automation" redundancies only created a two-tier dole and paid firms to tick "no" on the box every other policy depends on; the PIP tribunal statistic was misused. **Fixed:** box is a pure count; bridge is cause-blind; tribunal line aimed at the wait. **Flagged:** the PIP four-point rule may already be paused pending Timms, so "pause it" has come off every page until a primary source says otherwise.

### Housing ([`ministers/08-housing.md`](./ministers/08-housing.md))

**Line:** *Count the roofs, not the promises, and put homes ahead of servers in the queue.*

- **The roof count:** homes finished, not permitted, monthly, by council (a Transition Count row).

**Refuses:** a house-building number we can't count; falling house prices; fixing mortgage rates; a national rent freeze; blaming migrants or pretending pressure isn't real; Right to Buy on new council homes; an AI planning officer.

I've got a time machine" aimed the sarcasm at the reader, and the page never said who gets the new homes. **Fixed:** sarcasm cut; year-two and year-five measures replace an impossible year-three fails-if; allocation answered (local connection alongside need) **pending your ruling** (You-decide 6).

### Energy and Climate ([`ministers/09-energy-climate.md`](./ministers/09-energy-climate.md))

**Line:** *Households first on the grid, and what can't compete, the public owns.*

- **Households first** (arc row 4).

## 5. The democratic upgrade, staged

Tenet 1 (vote issue by issue, not one bundle) is the most distinctive offer and had no policy text.
The rule for this chapter: **small, staged, each stage tested by the one before, paper for anything
binding.** No app, no PR as the ask, no referendums on everything, no phone voting for anything binding.
The pub line: *"Somebody's had the mouse since 1872. Ask for a go."*

| Stage | What | When | Passes / fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0. On ourselves** | Every pledge in the manifesto carries the contract; anything missing its test, number or stop date prints as "Not ready" (cap: one per page). Each chapter can be voted on alone and says what it needs. Dropped policies stay visible with their write-up. A one-page dependency map | Day one, free | Measured by whether the pages keep it up |
| **1. The green tick for law** | **The Test Register** by Commons standing order (and a Lords equivalent): no second reading without the test, fails-if and stop date published. **One Office for Tests and Postmortems**, a single body with a headcount ceiling, smaller than the OBR, absorbing the Evaluation Task Force: it runs the register, the reviews, the ledger tables and the Transition Count, and assigns trials by lot. **Stop dates that bite through a vote:** new big changes stop on their date unless Parliament votes to keep them on the published review (not the Retained EU Law mistake of deleting old law untested). **The right-to-a-human statute** | First session: two bills, not ten (one constitutional, one money) | Fails if after two years under 1 review in 10 changed or ended a policy, or under 9 in 10 were on time |
| **2. A say on more than one thing, advisory** | **A standing citizens' panel**, random like a jury and paid, **sets its own agenda** of two to four national questions a year (no government veto). Consultations on those questions run as **agreement maps** (where people agree, where they split; software sorts, a named person signs). **The wire:** government must answer each agreed point in Parliament within three months, bound to respond, not to obey. **Local money voted locally** in councils that volunteer (Housing) | Years one to three | Fails if participation concentrates in a small unrepresentative group, or government ignores agreed points. Either result is published |
| **3. Binding, on paper** | A few national questions a year on an issue-by-issue paper ballot, only questions the panel has deliberated. "Binding" means **an Act calling each ballot commits Parliament to legislate on the result**, under existing referendum law; MPs keep the final vote | Only if Stage 2 passes | "One enormous yes-or-no on everything is just a bundle with a bigger box" |

**Trials discipline** (wonk #3, #4), which belongs to Stage 1: **at most eight trials in year one,**
chosen from the six front pledges; assigned by lot from a volunteer pool so no area carries more than
two; stepped roll-out wherever everyone gets it eventually; existing pilot powers used where they
exist (social-security pilot schemes) instead of new law.

## 6. How it's paid for

**The frame: concede, then re-aim.** Markets worked, the 2010 fiscal problem was real, borrowing costs
matter (rates rose today). Then: the scoreboard stopped matching the game. **Money isn't what runs
out. Nurses are.** (The spine's "You can print the money" is dropped from public copy: the first five
words are the clip, the week rates rose. "Magic money tree" stays in the comic, out of the manifesto.)

**The fiscal statement** (new; the wonk's top finding and the opposition's #4):

- **We keep the fiscal rules** (the current-budget balance rule and the debt rule) and **the OBR scores every measure** before it starts.
- **No rise in income tax, employee NI or VAT on working people.** No wealth tax promised this parliament (Not ready until the Ledger shows who holds what).
- **One aggregate fails-if for the whole programme:** if new day-to-day spending runs above the envelope set at the first fiscal event, new roll-outs pause. The party that tests everything tests its own budget.

**Where the money comes from (five sources, each with its own contract):**

1. **Plant into idle capacity first.** The cheapest pound puts someone standing ready to work. Honest version: investment is already outside the day-to-day rule; what's new is that each line names its spare capacity and pauses if that sector's prices (ONS construction output prices first) outrun inflation.
2. **A public slice where public money builds the machine,** into a national fund, host town first (Norway and Alaska as the precedents). Limited to cash, land, discounted power and guarantees.
3. **Stop paying twice.** No cheaper public power for data centres while household bills rise; no subsidy without a stake.
4. **Tax the windfall, not the wage.** One Windfall Act: the existing generator levy and the energy profits levy as its schedules, switching on with a shock abroad and off when prices fall back, every pound to the standing charge.
5. **Every pound has an address.** The Ledger for every Budget and public deal. Costs almost nothing; changes what is politically possible.

**The ledger of this manifesto** (to publish as a one-page table; bands are orders of magnitude from
named comparables, filled in by the fact pass, never invented figures):

| Band | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Small** (under £100m a year) | Test Register, the Office for Tests and Postmortems, the Ledger, the automation count, the Transition Count, contracts register, switch list |
| **Medium** (£100m to £1bn a year) | Four-week human benefit review, NHS same-day human route, run-by-hand fallbacks, the bridge at national scale (trialled in two regions first), the Rung Fund's admin (the fund itself is paid by firms) |
| **Large** (over £1bn) | Council house building (borrowing against rents, scored against the debt rule), a network-region purchase (trial only, unpriced, so off the front page), any national roll-out of a human answer for every reported crime (two-force trial only) |
| **Raises money** | Windfall Act schedules; slice returns (small for a decade, and we say so); firms' bridge contributions |
| **Kept, stated as a cost** | The triple lock this parliament |

**The gap the money chapter must still close** (wonk #6): the slice only bites where the state
subsidises, and most AI used here runs on servers abroad. So "who gets the gains" needs more than the
slice. Options in You-decide 4.

## 9. Rollout: landing it through the existing machinery

Nothing here touches `docs/government/`. These are the steps for whoever does, in order.

**Step 1. Rule section 8.** Immigration (1) first: Home is the page most likely to be improvised from
one aggregator item while it waits.

**Step 2. One fact pass.** A single research run that sources the fact-gate list in section 7 into
graded items with URLs. Without it, half the revised pages can't ship (the cabinet may only state
facts that sit in sourced memory).

**Step 3. A human PR: the machinery, no manifesto text.** Branch from `main`, merged by Kai or Jack,
because the briefs change only by the Cabinet Secretary's weekly run or a human PR.

- `docs/government/workers/cabinet.md`: **the standing rule** ("each page has a standing section that the day's news may sharpen, never replace; a quiet day leaves the page as it is") and the cross-cutting rules from `spine.md` §8, revised: fill the contract or print "Not ready"; real provisional numbers; "unless Parliament votes to keep it"; the pass clause; end on the chooser; no fiction outside the PM's foreword.
- `docs/government/shared.md`: **the cross-cabinet rules** from section 2 (one owner per shared policy; the rights-only no-sunset list; Defence's lethal wording) and the AI ownership split from section 3.
- `docs/government/ministers/*.md`: each brief names its flagships by one line each and its refusals, within the 300-word cap. Each `ministers/<file>.md` §7 here has the suggested text; apply the revisions in section 4 above where they differ (for example Foreign loses chapter-by-chapter ratification; Energy gains transport; Housing owns the host-town compact and "places").
- **A curated evidence set** (a `docs/government/evidence/` file or a set of graded evidence memories), filled from step 2, so a minister can cite a standing fact on a day with no news on its beat. This opens a door around the "only today's sourced news" rule, so it needs a rule of its own: evidence items carry a URL, a grade and a review date.
- **The renderer** (`apps/site/manifesto.mjs`): lists and bold, or a style rule that forbids them. The revised pages are written to the current renderer, so this is not blocking.
- **The news sweep:** widen the empty beats (15 of 24 empty on the first run is a researcher-depth problem, not a quiet news day). Health, Housing and local government matter most.

**Step 4. The pages, onto the rolling branch, in two waves.** Humans may push to the rolling branch
and the cabinet must build on what it finds there, never revert, so the pages go there rather than
into a separate PR. That keeps one diff on one PR and makes the next morning's run start from the
standing text.

- **Wave 1 (the front door, the heart, and the empty posts):** PM, Work and Pensions, Health, Housing. Health and Housing are placeholders today on the reader's first and second grievances; the PM page is the front door; W&P is the page the reader would share.
- **Wave 2 (after rulings and the fact pass):** Home (after You-decide 1), then Chancellor, Energy, Education, Defence, Foreign. These have live positions already, so they are replacements, not fills.
- Use the revised §6 text from each `ministers/<file>.md`, with fact-gated figures removed where step 2 hasn't sourced them.

**Step 5. Steer with review comments.** Comments on the rolling PR outrank the ministers and are the
cheapest steering lever. Use them for small corrections after each morning run instead of new PRs.

**Step 6. Check after four weeks.** The strategy has worked if: every page still leads with its
flagship; no fails-if lacks a number; no time-machine line has crept back onto a chapter; the news
has sharpened the evidence paragraphs without replacing the rules; at least one pledge has been
marked *Changed* or *Dropped* with its write-up (a manifesto of tests where nothing ever moves is
the failure mode the PM's own fails-if names). If the PM page still reads as process, lead it with
the right to a human.

**What not to do:** don't publish the manifesto as a single launch until wave 2 and the fact pass are
in. A fact-checked hostile read of one false claim (the PIP example was the near miss) costs the
whole document its credibility, and credibility is the only thing this manifesto sells.
