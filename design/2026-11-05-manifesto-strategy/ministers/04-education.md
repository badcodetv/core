## 0. What this post is for, in one paragraph

Education is the minister for **the start**. The live page already holds the best AI-arrival policy
in the manifesto (learning time: MACH §3), but it is one pilot, it rests on one BBC story, and it
says nothing about schools, colleges, apprenticeships or the adult who needs a second go. The job of
this strategy is to turn one good pilot into a standing platform of four bets that all serve one
idea: **the first rung of working life is being removed by a decision, and the people who made that
decision should rebuild it.** Everything else this department could say (tuition fees, Ofsted,
childcare ratios) is either somebody else's fight, a sinkhole of unsourced cost, or a news-day
reaction. We keep it out of the standing text on purpose.

## 1. Mission

**The line** (frontmatter, 83 characters, no colon):

> **Keep the bottom rung on the ladder, and make whoever saws it off pay for a new one.**

Runner-up if Kai and Jack want it gentler: *Keep the bottom rung on the ladder. Learn the job before
you mark the machine's.* (79). The current line (96 characters) is the right idea at a length nobody
will repeat (MACH §3). The proposed line adds the one thing the current one lacks: **the chooser and
the bill in the same breath** (GUARD rule 6, the anger has an address).

**The problem this post exists to prevent.** Bad-branch arc **row 1, "the first rung disappears"**
(spine §3), which is the education end of **F3, rung two: work automated with the gains kept**:
*"Intelligence itself is being automated; white-collar work is next ... the people who did
everything right are the least prepared."* The mechanism the canon describes is quiet, not
dramatic. Nobody experienced gets fired first. **The entry job is simply never advertised**, because
the tasks a 22-year-old used to learn on (the first draft, the data entry, the junior check, the
first-line call) are the tasks a model does cheapest. Ten years later there is nobody in the middle
who learned the work, and the people "supervising the AI" cannot tell when it is wrong. That second
step is **F12, humans "reduced to reviewers" who can no longer follow the designs**, and its end
point is the deepest row of the arc (row 8, "nobody's job"): a country that cannot check the machine
because nobody was ever allowed to do the work by hand.

**The real news it is tied to (LAND, graded):**
- 16–24 unemployment about 16%; overall unemployment highest since 2021 [M].
- Graduate job postings at their lowest since 2020, while demand for AI skills is at a record [M]
  (Indeed, via Reuters/KFGO; Cyprus Mail).
- BBC: new graduates whose working day is mostly supervising AI agents [M, single source in the
  digest].
- **The cause is disputed** (NIESR: AI, trade and rates all candidates) [M]. **The government does
  not track AI displacement** (LAND §6.2, itself an inference; check before stating as fact).
- The two answers on offer: Reform says too many degrees, cut them, and promises 600k apprenticeships
  a year [H/M]; Labour reportedly offers youth AI bootcamps [L].
- Background, US only, for the strategists not the page: the Stanford "canaries" paper on 22–25s in
  AI-exposed occupations (GUARD §4 quotes ~19% below trend; my memory of the August 2025 paper is
  ~13% relative employment decline. **Check before any use, and never as a UK figure.**) [K].

The honest framing that follows from a disputed cause is the strongest one we have: **nobody knows
how much of this is AI, because nobody is counting, and that is itself the decision we would
change first.** It also passes the test gate in a way no rival's answer does.

## 2. The flagships

Four, in order of how much the rest depends on them. Flagship 1 is the measurement the other three
are judged by; flagship 2 is the headline; flagship 3 is the existing pilot, kept; flagship 4 is the
second go for adults. A fifth idea (schools and AI tools) is in §2.5 as a secondary commitment.

### Flagship 1. The First Rung Count

**The policy in one sentence.** Every quarter, publish how many people under 25 got their first job
or apprenticeship, by occupation and town, flagged by how exposed that occupation is to automation,
so the country can see the rung going before it is gone.

**Why now.** Youth unemployment at ~16% and graduate postings at a 2020 low [M], with the cause
disputed (NIESR) [M] and no official AI-displacement series (LAND §6.2). Every party is arguing the
cause by slogan: degrees, bootcamps, rates. Our answer to "is it AI?" is "nobody's counting; we
will".

**Future Proof mechanism.** Tenet 3 (data-driven decisions, D1: "how will we know it worked?"). It is
the test instrument for flagships 2 to 4 and for Work and Pensions' "say before the saving", which
names "the young person whose first job was never created" as the group it leaves out. This is how
that group gets counted (X1).

**The contract.**
- *Who decided:* the Department for Education and DWP publish starts and claimant counts; nobody was
  asked to join first-job hiring to occupation exposure. A data gap is a choice about what gets seen
  (the Founding Deletion: "a footnote fell off").
- *The rule:* ONS, using HMRC payroll records (which already carry age, employer and start date) and
  a published occupation-exposure index, releases a First Rung Count quarterly, with a town-level
  table. Method published in advance; independent methodology review.
- *The test:* run it as an Official Statistics in Development for four quarters first. Compare its
  early figures against the Labour Force Survey and job-posting data.
- *Fails if:* after four quarters it cannot match LFS youth employment trends within a pre-stated
  margin, or the town tables are too small to publish without disclosure risk. Then we publish why
  and fund a survey instead.
- *The undo:* a measurement, not a law; it stops if the method review says it misleads. Sunset
  review at three years.
- *Ledger:* public cost only (ONS analyst time). Who gains: anyone arguing about youth jobs with a
  number instead of a guess, including people who will use it against us.
- *Who it leaves out:* young people outside payroll entirely (gig, cash-in-hand, self-employed, the
  NEET who never applied). Counted separately via LFS so nobody calls the payroll gap a success.
- *Who signs:* the Education Secretary's office jointly with DWP; ONS owns the method.
- *Status:* Draft.

**Scale and cost.** Small: an analytical team inside ONS plus HMRC data access, plausibly low
single-digit £m a year [K, unsourced estimate; do not print]. HMRC RTI-based flash estimates already
exist, which is why this is cheap [K, check exact series].

**Strongest precedent.** The US Census Bureau's LEHD/QWI (quarterly workforce indicators from
payroll records by age and area) [K]; the UK's own HMRC RTI "pay as you earn" experimental statistics
since 2019 [K].

**Strongest objection.** "Exposure indices are guesswork; you'll blame AI for what rates did."
**Answer:** that is exactly why we publish the method and the comparison with rate-sensitive sectors.
If entry hiring falls as much in low-exposure jobs, the count says *it isn't AI*, and we say so. The
test is neutral (spine §2.2.3).

### Flagship 2. The Rung Fund: the firm that removes the rung pays for a new one

**The policy in one sentence.** A large employer whose under-25 hiring falls well below its own
recent baseline pays the shortfall into a local first-job fund, which pays for paid apprenticeships
and first-job places with employers who are still hiring young people, in that town.

**Why now.** The entry door is closing [M] while the apprenticeship levy, the one existing tool
aimed at this, has been reshaped into a "Growth and Skills Levy" [K, check status] and the October
Budget devolved money to mayors (mayors keep 20% of business rates from 2027) [M], so there is now a
local body with a skills budget to run a fund. Reform's 600k apprenticeships [H/M] names a target
and no payer; we name the payer.

**Future Proof mechanism.** X5 (name the decision-maker, never the technology) turned into a rule:
the employer who designed the job out books the cost of the rung. Tenet 2 (small and undoable: two
areas first, sunset that defaults to stop). Tenet 3 (fails-if pre-registered against the First Rung
Count). Ownership invariant from GUARD §5: *someone is going to own this either way.*

**The contract.**
- *The wrong:* your nephew did everything he was told, and the firm that took on twenty school
  leavers a year in his town now takes on two, and puts the saving in the annual report.
- *Who decided:* the board that chose to stop hiring at entry level, and a levy system that charges
  every large employer the same whether it trains young people or replaces them.
- *The rule:* employers above the levy threshold (small firms are exempt) whose under-25 starts fall
  more than a set share below their own three-year average, measured from payroll records, pay a
  top-up per missing start into the area's Rung Fund. Employers who keep or raise youth hiring pay
  nothing extra; employers who take on Rung Fund trainees get the place paid. **It is not a tax on
  using AI. It is a charge for removing the first job, whatever removed it.**
- *Who pays, who gains:* money moves from large employers who cut entry hiring to young people in the
  same area and to employers (often smaller ones) who still hire them. The Treasury pays nothing
  extra; administration comes out of the fund.
- *The test:* two combined authorities that volunteer, for two years. Compared with matched areas on
  the First Rung Count.
- *Fails if (pre-registered):* under-25 payroll starts in the trial areas fall relative to the
  comparison areas (the charge deters hiring rather than restoring it); or fewer than half of Rung
  Fund trainees are in work a year after finishing (threshold to be set before launch); or large
  employers measurably move entry jobs to just outside the boundary. Then we publish the postmortem
  and drop it.
- *The undo:* sunset at three years; **it stops unless the data says keep.** Collected money left
  unspent is returned pro rata.
- *Who it leaves out:* young people who want neither an apprenticeship nor a payroll job (the
  would-be self-employed); young people in towns with almost no large employers, where the fund will
  be small; anyone whose shortfall employer is headquartered elsewhere. Counted separately.
- *Who signs:* the Education Secretary and the mayor of each trial area.
- *Status:* Draft.

**Scale and cost.** Self-funding by design; its size depends entirely on how far entry hiring falls,
which is the point: if the rung isn't going, the fund is tiny and costs nobody anything. Honest
uncertainty: we cannot say how much it would raise until the First Rung Count exists. **Print no
figure.**

**Strongest precedent.** France's apprenticeship tax plus the "bonus-malus" contribution on firms
with too few apprentices (firms over 250 staff below a quota pay more) [K, check current form];
Germany's dual system as the model of what a protected rung looks like [K]; the UK Future Jobs Fund
(2009–10), whose DWP evaluation found a net benefit per participant [K, check]. Weakest link:
there is no direct precedent for a charge triggered by a fall in *youth* hiring; this is a new bet,
and we say so.

**Strongest objection.** "You're taxing the firms that create jobs, and they'll just hire fewer
people of any age." **Answer:** small firms are out; firms that keep hiring young people pay
nothing; the money goes to the employers who are still hiring. And the second half of the objection
is exactly our fails-if: if starts fall in the trial areas against the comparison, it's dropped, in
public, with the postmortem. Second objection, from the other side: "baseline gaming, outsourcing
entry roles to agencies." **Answer:** agency placements count as starts for the end user; that rule
is itself in the test.

### Flagship 3. Learning time: do the work before you mark the machine's

**The policy in one sentence.** Where an employer puts AI agents into entry-level roles, part of
each trainee's week is spent doing the underlying work themselves, checked by someone senior, before
they are asked to sign off a machine's version.

**Why now.** The BBC graduates [M] who supervise AI agents before they have ever done the work. This
is F12 caught early: a generation trained as reviewers of work they cannot do.

**Future Proof mechanism.** X7 and spine rule 6 (a human signs, and somebody checks their work). A
person cannot check a machine's homework if nobody let them do the homework. Tenet 3 for the test.

**The contract.** As on the live page, which already passes the gate (MACH §3), tightened:
- *The test:* one year, volunteer employers with their local colleges, in a few towns; a comparison
  group of trainees in similar firms without it.
- *Fails if:* at twelve months, learning-time trainees are not measurably better than the comparison
  group at doing the job unaided and at catching seeded errors in machine output (both measured
  blind), or more than a stated share of volunteer employers walk away on cost.
- *The undo:* voluntary; ends at the trial unless the data says keep. If it keeps, the next stage is
  a condition on Rung Fund places, not a national mandate.
- *Ledger:* employers pay the trainee hours (a real cost, in output forgone); trainees gain the
  skill; the public gains people who can tell when the machine is wrong.
- *Who it leaves out:* everyone who never got through the door. They are flagship 2's job and are
  counted alongside, every quarter.
- *Status:* Draft (posted on purpose since 2026-11-05).

**Scale and cost.** Public cost: evaluation only (low £m [K, unsourced]). Private cost: perhaps a day
a week of trainee time; we don't know, which is why it's a trial.

**Strongest precedent.** Medicine and aviation already do this: junior doctors examine before they
read the scan report; pilots hand-fly before they monitor the autopilot, and aviation regulators
have warned repeatedly about automation dependency eroding manual skill [K]. EEF's trial method for
the measurement [K].

**Strongest objection.** "Employers won't pay for slower trainees." **Answer:** some won't; that's in
the fails-if. The ones who do are betting their own people can catch the error that costs them a
client. We'd put the house on that bet; here's how you'll know if we're wrong.

### Flagship 4. A second go, with a job at the end

**The policy in one sentence.** Public money for adult retraining pays only for courses with a named
local employer committed to interviewing the people who finish, and every course publishes how many
of its students are in work a year later.

**Why now.** The arrival is not only about the young: the W&P page offers affected workers
"retraining for a job that actually exists" as one of their choices [the live W&P page]. Today
"retraining" is the word that lets everyone off (the brief: "Retraining is not a plan if there's
nothing to train for"). Labour's reported bootcamps [L] are the shape we'd test, not condemn.

**Future Proof mechanism.** Tenet 3 at its plainest: publish the outcome. Spine §2.2.3: this is our
"one policy the reader expects us to oppose that we test instead" (the bootcamps), and our own idea
that we admit could fail.

**The contract.**
- *The wrong:* the redundancy letter comes with a leaflet for a course, and the course ends in a
  certificate and no interview.
- *Who decided:* a funding system that pays colleges and providers for starts and completions, not
  for whether anyone got a job.
- *The rule:* (a) adult skills money (including whatever the new Lifelong Learning Entitlement and
  devolved adult skills budgets pay for [K, check names and status]) is tied to an employer interview
  commitment for occupational courses; (b) every provider publishes job outcomes at 12 months,
  course by course, in plain words; (c) adults made redundant by an automation decision covered by
  the W&P rule get a paid place, with the cost booked against the saving (W&P and Chancellor own that
  money).
- *Who pays, who gains:* money shifts from providers whose courses lead nowhere to providers and
  employers whose courses lead somewhere. The employer gets trained recruits; the redundant worker
  gets an interview, not a leaflet.
- *The test:* one or two devolved areas, two years, compared with matched areas on job outcomes and
  earnings at 12 and 24 months.
- *Fails if:* employment at 12 months is not higher than the comparison, or courses for older and
  disabled learners collapse because providers cherry-pick the easy cases. Then we publish and drop it.
- *The undo:* sunset at three years, default stop.
- *Who it leaves out:* people who want to learn for its own sake (literacy, a language, an evening
  class with no job attached). **This rule deliberately does not touch that money;** say so on the
  page. Also people in places with no employers to sign up, who are counted separately.
- *Status:* Draft.

**Scale and cost.** A re-aiming of existing adult skills spending, not new money [K: the adult
skills budget in England is in the low £bn; check]. New money only for (c), booked against the
automation saving.

**Strongest precedent.** The strongest in this whole document: US **sectoral training** RCTs (Year
Up, Project QUEST, Per Scholas; Katz, Roth, Hendra and Schaberg 2022 review) show large, lasting
earnings gains when training is built with employers for real vacancies [K, strong evidence]. UK
**sector-based work academies**, whose DWP evaluation found more time in work [K, check]. The
cautionary tale: Individual Learning Accounts (2000–01), closed for fraud by providers paid for
sign-ups [K]; that is why outcomes, not enrolments, are what we publish.

**Strongest objection.** "Employer commitments will be cosmetic; the interview guarantees nothing."
**Answer:** correct, which is why the fails-if is jobs at 12 months, not interviews held.

### 2.5 Secondary commitment: schools (test it in a classroom before you sell it to every school)

Not a flagship, but the page must say something about schools or it reads as a jobs page. Two short
standing rules, both Future Proof, both cheap:

1. **No AI tool bought for schools with public money until it has passed a classroom trial**, run the
   way the Education Endowment Foundation already runs them, and any teacher time it saves stays with
   pupils (smaller groups, tutoring, marking feedback), not with a budget cut. The twin of Health's
   "test it on a ward". Precedent: EEF's trial of ChatGPT for lesson planning (2024), which reported a
   large cut in planning time with no fall in quality [K, check figure (~31%?) before use]; EEF has
   run 100+ school RCTs (LAND §5 [K]).
2. **No grade about a child decided by a machine without a named human who signs it and an appeal to
   a person.** The precedent is ours: in August 2020 an algorithm set a year's A-level grades and was
   reversed within days [K, check dates]. The undo worked because the outcry was big enough; a
   Future Proof state would have tested it on last year's results first. This is the education
   branch of the PM's right to a human (arc row 3).

And one sentence of the brief's best instinct, kept as a value rather than a programme: **school
should make people harder to fool and better at arguing back, including with us.** Pupils learn how a
scam, a fake video and a feed are made by making one, in the open. Owned jointly with Home (arc row
6). Kept as a sentence, not a flagship, because a curriculum pledge invites the "lecture" trap
(GUARD rule 2) and we have no trial design for it yet. **Not ready.**

## 3. What this minister will NOT promise, and why

1. **Not a job for every young person.** GUARD §5 bans "a guaranteed job for everyone instantly":
   skills, geography, health and matching are real. We promise places funded by the firms that
   removed them, and a count that shows the gap.
2. **No target number of apprenticeships** (no "600k"). A start is not a finish and a finish is not a
   job. We publish starts, finishes and jobs a year later (FP-A D1's own worked example), and let the
   count embarrass us if it must.
3. **No tuition-fee or student-loan promise either way.** Not "scrap the loans" (cost we cannot
   source), not "cut degrees" (the cause of graduate unemployment is disputed and blaming the degree
   blames the graduate). Our position on degrees is a test, not a verdict: publish earnings and job
   outcomes per course, and let people choose with the receipt.
4. **No list of "jobs of the future".** Nobody knows which jobs survive, including a machine from
   2054; the future is evidence of what went wrong, not a map of what to train for. Anyone selling
   you that list is selling a course.
5. **Not "learn to code".** "Skills" is the magic word that lets the employer off (the brief). We
   don't tell a 50-year-old welder to become a prompt engineer.
6. **No ban on AI in schools or colleges.** Pupils will use it; a ban is a pledge nobody can test or
   keep. We test the tools and keep the homework human where the learning is.
7. **No promise to fix childcare, Ofsted, SEND or teacher pay in this chapter.** All real, all owed a
   position, none yet with a test design or a sourced fact. Rather than improvise them from one news
   item, the page says "Not ready" if asked, and the cabinet builds each with its contract when the
   news carries it. (SEND is the most costly gap; flagged in §7.)
8. **Never blame teachers, never blame kids, never blame parents.** The decision-makers are the
   board, the funding rule and the department.

Credibility comes from item 4 above all: a machine from the future that says "I don't know which jobs
survive" is the most trustworthy thing on the page.

immigration first, the economy second (GUARD §1).

**Immigration.** The reader will connect youth jobs to migration ("they're giving the jobs to people
from abroad"). This page must not deny it or restate it. The proportionate move is Home's and Housing's
to make; Education's contribution is structural and true: the Rung Fund pays for **local** young
people's first places in **their** town, which answers "who looks after our kids" without naming an
enemy. Never quote the migration claim to rebut it (rule 10).

**The Lee/Coe check on the line.** Someone who believes the opposite reads only "make whoever saws it
off pay for a new one". Whose contempt does it confirm? The employer who cut the rung, a public act
by an institution, not the young person or the worker. Passes.

## 5. Interfaces

**Depends on**
- **Work and Pensions.** The split is already settled by the PM (MACH §0): Education owns the
  **rung** (young people, first jobs, training), W&P owns the **say on the gains** (existing workers).
  W&P's page names "the young person whose first job was never created" as its leaves-out: flagship
  1 counts them, flagship 2 funds them. Flagship 4(c) (paid places for automation-redundant adults) is
  one of the options in W&P's "say before the saving"; W&P owns the trigger, Education the course.
  Jobcentre youth support is W&P's.
- **Chancellor.** The Rung Fund is a charge; it belongs in the Ledger (every pound has an address),
  and the Chancellor must agree it does not sit alongside the levy as double-charging. The "plant into
  idle capacity" argument (spine §5 source 1: young people without a first rung are idle capacity,
  not a cost) is the Chancellor's macro case for Education's spending.
- **Housing (owns "places", councils, mayors).** Every trial in this chapter runs through a combined
  authority. Housing's local-pilot machinery is ours.
- **PM.** The right to a human (exam grades) and the Test Register (the First Rung Count's method and
  the AI-tool trials sit in it).

**Clashes, to settle in advance**
- **Chancellor vs Rung Fund.** The Chancellor's instinct (no costing we can't source) collides with a
  self-funding charge whose size is unknown. Resolution: print no figure; the fund's size *is* the
  measurement.
- **Health's "spend the saving on the nurse" and Education's "time saved stays with pupils".** Same
  rule, two departments. Make it one cross-government sentence in the PM's AI clause so it doesn't
  read as two different promises.
- **Home on "harder to fool".** Scams and deepfakes are Home's (arc row 6). Education teaches; Home
  refunds. Agree that Education never claims media literacy reduces fraud (untested).
- **W&P on retraining.** Risk of both pages promising retraining in different words. Rule: only
  Education describes courses; W&P only says "retraining for a job that exists, see Education".

### Revised draft (closing pass, 2026-11-05: the red team's fixes applied)

What changed from the draft the red team read (sources: `../redteam/opposition.md`, `../redteam/reader.md`, `../redteam/wonk.md`; the overall strategy is `../README.md`):

1. The count is by industry and town, not occupation: payroll records carry no occupation code (wonk #10).
2. The Rung Fund baseline is now relative to firms in the same trade and area, with a cap, so a downturn costs nobody (opposition #12, wonk §5: pro-cyclical and Equality Act exposure). Legal advice on the age aim is still owed.
3. Fails-if gets real numbers and a window long enough to see the effect (three years), with the stop date after it (four years) and a vote, not 'the data says keep' (opposition #1, wonk #2, #5).
4. Areas drawn by lot from volunteers (wonk #4). Pass clause added (reader X1).
5. Learning time told through a trade, not an office (reader). Leaves-out now names adults off payroll, not 'anyone who wants to learn just to learn' (reader X8).
6. 'Write a better rule' cut; the page ends on the line before it (reader X2).
7. Assumes the Rung Fund, not the Chancellor's NI holiday, is the one first-jobs trial (You-decide 5 in README.md).

```markdown
---
title: Education
line: Keep the bottom rung on the ladder, and make whoever saws it off pay for a new one.
---
## Position

What's a ladder for if someone saws off the bottom rung? Your nephew did everything he was told. The firm in his town that used to take on a dozen school leavers a year now takes on a couple, and the saving is in the annual report.

Youth unemployment is around one in six, and graduate job adverts are at their lowest since 2020. Is that the machines, the interest rate or trade? Nobody knows, because nobody counts. So first we count: every quarter, town by town and industry by industry, how many under-25s got a first job or apprenticeship.

Then the rule. A big employer whose hiring of young people falls well behind other firms in its own trade and area pays the gap, up to a cap, into a fund for that town, which pays for first jobs and apprenticeships with the firms still taking young people on. Because it's measured against firms like it, a downturn that hits everyone costs nobody. Small firms are out. Firms that keep hiring pay nothing.

The test. Two areas first, drawn by lot from those that volunteer, against areas like them. Fails if, after three years, young people's hiring in the trial areas has fallen further than in the comparison, or fewer than half of the trainees it paid for are in work a year after finishing. Then we publish the write-up and drop it. Passes, and any area can have it. It stops after four years unless MPs vote to keep it, with the results in front of them.

And two smaller rules. An apprentice learns to fit the boiler before he signs off the machine that diagnosed it: where trainees work alongside AI, part of the week goes on doing the job themselves. And public money for adult retraining pays only for courses with an employer waiting at the end, with every course's job results published.

Who it leaves out: young people outside payroll jobs, towns with no big employers, and adults who aren't on a payroll at all, the self-employed, carers, anyone retraining at forty-five. They're counted on their own line.

Which jobs will survive? I don't know. Neither does anyone selling you a course.
```

## 8. Open questions for Kai and Jack

1. **Is the Rung Fund too clever?** It is the most original policy in this chapter and the least
   precedented (France's bonus-malus is the nearest). If it reads as a tax on jobs to the reader's
   small-business cousin, the fallback is a simpler rule: levy money an employer doesn't spend on
   under-25 starts goes to the town's first-job fund. Less bite, less risk.
2. **SEND.** The most expensive and most painful schools issue for working-class families, and the
   page is silent. Do you want a standing position built (it needs research we haven't done), or is
   "Not ready" the honest answer for now?
3. **Universities.** Leaving fees alone is a deliberate refusal. Fine for a manifesto of tests, but a
   real party would be asked. Lean: "we publish what each course leads to; we don't tell your kid
   not to go."
