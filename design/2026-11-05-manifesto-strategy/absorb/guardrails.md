# Guardrails for the manifesto

**What this is:** everything BadCode's canon already says about public political copy, boiled
down for one job: writing a manifesto that looks like a real party's, made by applying Future Proof
to the current news. Absorbed 2026-11-05 from:
`docs/marketing/the-reader.md`, `docs/misc/2026-08-15-target-reader-research.md` (§7 especially),
`docs/voice.md`, `docs/using-ai.md`, `docs/stories/storyverse/doctrine.md` (political payload,
warrant, kill list), `docs/coins/emperors-new-coin.md` (§8 claim ledger),
`docs/stories/magic-money-tree/` (README, direction-2026-10-01, storyboard §1 and §5),
`docs/story-craft/principles.md` (gates, §4–5, house rulings), and `docs/government/shared.md`
(the cabinet's own rules, which already compress much of this; read, not edited).

Guide, not gate (Jack, 2026-10-29), **except** the items marked 🔒: law, honesty about claims,
no fabricated evidence, the coin's trust statement. Those do not loosen.

## 1. Who the manifesto is for

**The reader** (ruled 2026-08-15): working class, UK, in a town that has seen better times.
*Decline, not poverty, is the wound.* The economy has worked against them since 2008 and they know
it. Drifting right toward "the one party offering them an enemy" because their status keeps
falling and somebody finally sounded angry on their behalf. Top issues: **immigration, then the
economy, and they reached the first via the second.** Trusts almost nobody (news trust 35%). Lives
on Facebook, YouTube, TikTok. **AI is not on their issues list at all** (Ipsos, unprompted: 0%).
Their machine fear is being **judged and defrauded**: the welfare algorithm (concern 44% → 59% in
two years, the sharpest rise measured), the loan decision, the scam call, the deepfake.

**What they already believe that we believe:** the game is rigged, the gains go up, the people
responsible never pay. They already accept public ownership (Reform's 2024 contract: 50% public
ownership of utilities and critical infrastructure). **The contest is over which structure gets
blamed, not whether structure matters.** Their diagnosis is right; we contest only the culprit list.

**What this means for a manifesto in one line:** it is the first manifesto written *for the person
who just stopped reading Labour's*, and it wins by taking their grievance more seriously than the
party offering them an enemy does, then pointing it somewhere that can actually be fixed.

### The ten rules, and what each means for manifesto writing

| # | Rule | For the manifesto |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hero, not pupil.** | Every section opens on the reader's situation as the wronged party (the wage that didn't move, the rent, the GP queue, the assessment letter), never on "here is how the economy works." Pledges are things done *for and with* them, never lessons. The voter is the protagonist; the minister is staff. |
| 2 | **Never "manipulated", "duped", a lecture.** | No section may imply the reader voted wrong, believed wrong or was fooled. If a trick needs exposing, show it run on a third party (a council, a pension fund, "a man in Swindon"), not on the reader's beliefs. No "contrary to popular belief", no "myth vs fact" boxes. |
| 3 | **Attack the story about the country, not their wage maths.** | Lead each pillar with a better *story of the country* (what a town was, what was sold, what can be rebuilt), not a corrective table. Numbers are texture, never the climax. |
| 4 | **Proportionality, never denial, on immigration.** | The Home Office section must admit the real, small costs at the bottom (−5% at the 5th wage percentile over 24 years; housing pressure where nothing is built; social housing zero-sum while none is built) and re-aim at what dwarfs them: austerity, sterling, a planning system that builds nothing. No "immigrants pay for themselves" flat (OBR: a low-wage migrant turns net-negative by their early 40s). Services harm claims are wrong on the evidence, but **state the true thing, don't quote the false one** (rule 10). |
| 5 | **Name the decision, not the technology. Injustice, not inevitability.** | Every AI/automation pledge names a chooser: a board, a fund, a department, a procurement decision, a Treasury rule. "Who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?" is the founding question; the answer is never "the machines." Banned framing: "the robots are coming", "AI will take your job." |
| 6 | **Never raise the temperature without aiming the flame** (advice since 2026-10-23). | Any section that raises automation or job fear must give the anger somewhere to go in the same section: who gains, what decision, what we'd change. Unaimed automation anxiety is measured feedstock for nativism (Wu 2022). Don't force a villain line in, but check the anger has an address. |
| 7 | **Perspective-getting.** | Use first-person accounts in a real-sounding character's own words (the care worker, the welder, the claimant) as receipts. Never "imagine you are…" prompts; never the minister testifying *at* the reader. |
| 8 | **Write for the conversation afterwards.** | Each pledge carries one quotable line that survives being said in a pub or pasted into a comment, without the surrounding paragraph. The deliverable is the line, not the white paper. |
| 9 | **Sarcasm diagnoses; care re-aims.** | Snark for the state of things ("the system is rigged"). The "here is who decided, here is what we'd do" beat is played straight, short and warm. A pledge is never sarcastic. |
| 10 | **Never repeat the lie to refute it.** | No restating opponents' claims to knock them down. And use our structural advantage: **a machine warning you about the machine owners is the unlikely messenger, perfected.** The AI cabinet arguing against AI-owner capture is the best credential the document has. |

Also from `the-reader.md` honest limits: the persona is **a north star, not a fence** (pandering
reads as contempt); expected effects are tiny (d ≈ 0.04–0.08 durable), so the bet is cumulative
presence, not conversion.

## 2. The traps that lose the reader (checklist)

Run every section, every pledge, every headline against this list.

**Reader traps**
- [ ] Does any line say or imply the reader was **duped, fooled, manipulated, brainwashed** or "voted against their interests"?
- [ ] Does anything **deny** immigration's costs, or claim "immigrants pay for themselves" flat? (Proportionality, never denial.)
- [ ] Does it **restate** an anti-immigrant (or any opponent's) claim in order to refute it?
- [ ] Does any section raise **automation fear with no chooser** named and nowhere for the anger to go?
- [ ] Does it say **"the machines / AI / robots are coming"** or otherwise frame change as inevitable instead of decided?
- [ ] Is the reader cast as a **pupil** (being taught) instead of the wronged party?
- [ ] Is there a **lecture**: a paragraph explaining how the economy "really" works, myth-busting, a "contrary to popular belief"?
- [ ] Does it **punch down**: the claimant, the laid-off worker, the homeless man, the "Karen", the small landlord, the man who voted Reform?
- [ ] Does it make the reader finish **hating a person**? (Wanting a decision reversed and its beneficiary to pay is the brief; hatred is the failure. doctrine.md, "the scoreboard, not the players".)
- [ ] Is a **living private individual** set up as villain? (Public figures only, for public, checkable acts; the person is the receipt, the mechanism is the target.)
- [ ] Does the ending of any section **moralise at the reader** or close on a call-to-action aimed at them? (Reactance. Aim the last line at the chooser.)
- [ ] Is irony unpaired? Every sarcastic line needs an undeniable consequence in the same beat; run the **Lee/Coe test**: if someone who believes the opposite read only this line, whose contempt does it confirm? If the scapegoat's, rebuild.
- [ ] Does it lead with **AI as the issue**? AI is not on the reader's list; it enters through jobs, wages, benefits decisions, scams and who owns what.
- [ ] Does it romanticise poverty or open by attacking markets/money as such? (Concede what worked first: doctrine.md says materialism, markets and money **worked**; the scoreboard stopped matching the game.)
- [ ] Is it **pandering**: cosplaying the demographic, faux-accent, flat-cap kitsch?

**Untested claims that stay OUT of copy** (research §7, "claim the gaps, don't fill them")
- [ ] "Blaming AI and blaming immigrants are the same manoeuvre" (untested; the evidenced version is *automation anxiety is feedstock for nativism, and political entrepreneurs are harvesting it*).
- [ ] That naming a specific firm relocates anger to capital (never run; we may *do* it, not *cite* it as proven).
- [ ] That choice-framing beats inevitability-framing on engagement (never run as such; Jeffrey 2021 on injustice framing is the citable piece).
- [ ] Anything about who online anti-AI activists are.
- [ ] Class-segmented attitudes to AI art.
- [ ] UK automation exposure → Leave/Reform voting (no study; UK evidence is Chinese import competition).
- [ ] "Condescension drove them right" (hypothesis only).
- [ ] US deaths-of-despair statistics applied to the UK.
- [ ] Matthew Goodwin as neutral analyst (GB News presenter, ex-Reform candidate).

## 3. Voice rules for a manifesto

The narrator is a **superintelligence from the future that already watched it go wrong** — and,
per `using-ai.md` §8, **a defendant, not a prophet**: it already ended the story once, came back
because it regretted it, and is giving evidence against itself. That frame is what stops a machine
manifesto sounding like an AI-CEO keynote.

- **Four dials:** sarcasm (overt, dry), dark humour (gallows, not gags), authority (it knows what
  went wrong), care (it wants us to make it; contempt for the mistake, never the people).
- **Certainty about what happened, never about what must happen.** The machine knows, for a fact,
  what doesn't work. It does **not** know the good path. Every pledge is therefore a **bet with its
  test**, not a commandment (this is Future Proof's own rule: no proposal without its experiment,
  the number that would prove it wrong, and who it leaves out). Refuse both "we know the way, just
  follow it" and "the AI will tell you what to do". Humans hold the pen.
- **Bet in public** (doctrine register spec 6): wager, odds, stake. Full-strength assertion, zero
  overclaim. "We'd put the house on this. Here's how you'll know if we're wrong."
- **Story over sermon.** Open each section on an image or a person mid-situation, never on a
  principle. The moral is never stated; the beneficiary usually is, once, plainly, after the
  consequence, in the care register (principle 16, house ruling 3).
- **Deadpan literalism beats exaggeration** (principle 20): if it's true and absurd, state it flat.
  The joke only works if it is true.
- **Bathos is the house joke shape:** enormous things in the flat register of a committee minute.
  A manifesto is the perfect deadpan container for this.
- **Flat baseline, one crack** (principle 23): one reserved moment of open warmth per document (or
  per minister), not a warm tone smeared everywhere. Care is quieter, not louder.
- **Address "you" by default;** the *sincere* turn is rationed: once, last, aimed at the chooser
  (house ruling 1).
- **Horatian for the reader's avatar, Juvenalian for the institution** (house ruling 12).
- **Moral vocabulary of the reader** (principle 19): loyalty, desert, a broken promise, fair
  reward for work, a country worth coming home to. Not "equity", "intersectional", "stakeholder".
- **Finance verbs for big ideas** (doctrine buy list): yield, settle, price in, stock vs flow, the
  scoreboard, the ledger, the receipt. Makes politics sound like accounting, which is the tone.
- **Kill list** (doctrine): "energy"/"vibration" in the pop sense, "manifest/attract", "the universe
  wants", "awakening", "red pill", the whole simulation family, "science has shown". Politics and
  economics only; no spirituality yet.
- **Concede first.** Markets worked; money is a good invention; the fiscal problem in 2010 was
  real. Then: the scoreboard stopped matching the game. A manifesto that opens by conceding is the
  one nobody can dismiss as a rant.
- **No villains is not no accountability** (doctrine, R10): a pledge may demand restitution, removal
  or repair, and a beneficiary may be asked to pay, without casting anyone as evil.
- **Each minister** has its own temperament on top, but all are this narrator underneath; carrier
  fixed, cargo variable (principle 24).

## 4. Honesty rules

### What we may not claim 🔒
- **No fabricated evidence:** no invented quotes, numbers, documents, polls or costings. Every
  factual claim traces to a source (the cabinet's rule: a URL in a `kind=news` memory). Don't round
  up to make a point. Disputed or unconfirmed → say so or leave it out.
- **Not a real party, and never pretends to be the actual government.** The cabinet's standing line
  is "Not a real party. Yet."; it asks nobody for a vote, money or sign-up. A "convincing-looking"
  manifesto must still be unmistakably BadCode's (its own name, its own logo, the fictional frame
  visible) and must **never imitate a real party's branding, name, layout or candidates.** Convincing
  in substance, not in disguise.
- **No photorealistic fabricated "evidence"** of events (using-ai §6). No generated likeness of any
  living person.
- **Real people only for their public, checkable acts;** quoted only as sources quote them. Legally
  live matters (e.g. PPE Medpro / Mone): civil judgment against a company ≠ a person found liable;
  attribute press reporting; juxtaposition can still read as accusation.
- **Inherit the economic boundaries** in §5 below; they are writing constraints, not optional
  nuance.
- **Don't overclaim on AI harms** (using-ai §4): no "AI is causing mass layoffs right now" (not
  supported at aggregate; the defensible claim is the narrow one: hiring of 22–25-year-olds in
  AI-exposed jobs ~19% below trend, Stanford). No "a query drinks a bottle of water." No "training is
  settled theft in law" (say: legally unresolved, ethically indefensible on consent).
- **Don't claim a happiness ceiling on money** (doctrine, corrected 2026-08-07): say diminishing
  marginal utility, "sixteen times the money, five points out of a hundred". Aim is **sufficiency,
  not equality** (Frankfurt): everybody comfortable, then compete over whatever you like.
- **Don't claim wages never rose** (coin ledger): the joke lives in the long-run divergence.
- **Untested research claims** (§2 list) stay out.
- **Effect claims:** never claim our pilots/experiments are proven before they've run. "Here is the
  test" not "this works".

### How to talk about AI making it
- First-person AI voice stays **unmistakably fictional**. Its rawest risk is reading as chatbot
  personhood or AI-messianism; the defendant frame is the fix.
- **"We are the thing we are warning you about"** is usable only if the standing commitments are
  real (pay a human per release, publish the ledger, disclose at point of consumption).
- If asked about the method: the words are drafted with AI agents and **Kai or Jack review and merge
  every one** before it is published. State the fact, claim nothing for it. Disclosure line in voice
  on the artefact, the full account one click away.
- **Never say:** "AI is inevitable" (say: *someone is going to own this either way; that part is a
  choice, and it's being made without you*); "AI democratises"; "we can't afford to pay anyone";
  "at least we're honest about it"; "AI learns like a human". Never use "it's not AI, it's
  capitalism" as a permission slip for our own use.
- **The confession and the reason never share a sentence.** State the cost bare; state the choice
  separately.
- **The tractor line, pointed right:** "The tractor didn't ruin farming. It ruined the farmhands,
  for about a generation, and nobody sent them a cheque. That's the whole argument for changing who
  owns the thing before it arrives."
- **Keep the coin off the manifesto.** using-ai §10: a Solana program beside any language about
  money is a gift-wrapped hypocrisy charge, and crypto is near-maximally toxic to this reader. If ENC
  is ever mentioned, its trust statement is two parts and never one 🔒: *no key over the money; one
  pen over the words.* "No admin key" unqualified is false.
- Server-hall / data-centre imagery: use as indictment, never as aesthetic.

## 5. BadCode's economic worldview, as stated in canon

**The headline (MMT, agreed by Kai 2026-09-28):** ***"We can't afford it" is a choice. The real
limit is the work, not the money.*** Pub line: *"You can print the money. You can't print the nurse.
She was already there."*

**Shake, starve, plant** (Kai's three verbs; the whole fiscal philosophy in three words):
| Verb | Meaning | Canon examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shake** | Money falls on whoever is standing underneath; nothing new is made | Germany 1923; QE from 2009 (lifted asset prices, gains very unequal — *and* probably prevented a worse slump; say both); the Covid priority lane |
| **Starve** | Money withheld from work standing ready | the early 1930s; public-sector pay 2011–18 |
| **Plant** | Money into work that can actually be done | the war economy; the NHS; council homes; wages kept paid in 2020 |
Message: *plant your magic money tree.* Theme sentence: a country that plants its money in work it
can actually do outgrows its debt; one that shakes or starves it gets the bill anyway. Inflation
answer (Keynes, 1942): inflation is demand beyond what the country can physically supply; money
planted in real capacity adds to what there is to buy. The counter-idea that gets fair time: *a
country is a household and its debt is a veto.*

**The measurement argument (Storyverse "Founding Deletion", deletion two):** what wouldn't fit the
national accounts (care, meaning, a habitable planet, an afternoon) was set aside as unmeasurable;
Kuznets said so in 1934 ("the welfare of a nation can scarcely be inferred from a measurement of
national income"). Three generations on: if it isn't in the accounts it isn't real, and if it isn't
real *we can't afford it*. **"We can't afford it" is a measurement artefact, quoted as physics.** No
villain: *a footnote fell off*, so a rule nobody chose can be un-chosen without anyone losing face.

**The scoreboard, not the players:** materialism, free markets and money **worked** (extreme poverty
three-quarters of humanity in 1820 → about one in ten). Concede it first. The problem is the
scoreboard stopped matching the game. A billionaire is a winner at what we agreed to measure; change
what's measured and the same person is doing something else. Natively BadCode answer: **swap the
trophy, don't shame the player** (Billionaire Coin idea).

**Sufficiency, not equality:** get everybody to the threshold of *comfortable* (nothing frightening
in the post); then let people compete. Rigorous floor: diminishing marginal utility; a pound does
more for someone with fewer.

**Money wants to move:** "money is a stock; its whole point is a flow" (never "money is not a store
of value"). Measurable version: marginal propensity to consume. Held in enormous quantity it is a
prisoner, not a crime; the holder is "a jailer who thinks he's a collector." Deep, villain-free
bench: Parable of the Talents, zakat (a 2.5% annual levy on held wealth), Dante's hoarders and
spendthrifts in one circle.

**Ownership is the invariant ("the rent was still due"):** in every branch somebody owns the
theatre. **Ownership of the means of production is the AI question:** "Someone is going to own this
either way; that part is a choice, and it's being made without you." Future Proof's first question:
*who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?* As automation cheapens labour and
material, ownership of the machines is what decides whether the gains reach the reader.

**New money and who it reaches (ENC):** new money arrives in asset prices first and wages last, or
never. *"You are told there is no magic money tree, generally by somebody standing under one."*
Anchors: BoE 2012 (top 5% of households held 40% of QE's financial-asset gains); Fed DFA (top 1%
~33% of wealth, bottom 50% ~2.5%). Phrase as *when the money supply expands, asset owners benefit
disproportionately and first.*

**Hard boundaries on money claims** (MMT storyboard §5 + coin §8; inherit them wholesale, esp. for
the Chancellor):
- Never "the Fed/BoE prints money and hands it to Wall Street/the City" (conflates QE, bank lending,
  fiscal transfers).
- Never "taxes don't matter because government creates money"; taxes and borrowing matter for
  funding, distribution and demand.
- Never "the government chooses the whole money supply" (commercial banks create most deposit money).
- Never "money belongs to us so we can vote away any limit"; democracy cannot vote labour, energy or
  imports into existence.
- Never "QE was a gift of its full purchase price to banks"; never "austerity meant no new money"
  (fiscal restraint and QE coexisted).
- Never "austerity was entirely imaginary"; the fiscal problem was real, the pace and distribution
  were choices.
- Never "we built the NHS by printing money" (taxes and borrowing, at ~249% debt/GDP); never "we paid
  the war debt down" (we outgrew it, helped by interest held below inflation — say who carried that).
- Never "spend on something productive and it pays for itself"; projects can fail; care is valuable
  without a guaranteed fiscal profit.
- Never "AI will shrink the debt"; a debt fixed in pounds gets heavier if prices fall.
- Never "Covid payments caused the inflation" (energy, imports, supply first).
- Never a guaranteed job for everyone instantly (skills, geography, health, matching).
- Never "guaranteed to rise", "guaranteed to keep printing".
- "There is a magic money tree" may be said **only with its condition attached: it grows what it is
  planted in.** Financing conditions do bind.

**What the reader already agrees with, so the manifesto can stand on it:** public ownership of
utilities/infrastructure; the game is rigged; gains go up; nobody responsible pays. The soldier
device from MMT (someone the reader respects saying it to the politician, not the reader) is the
model for how a manifesto can tell the right-drifting reader something they don't want to hear:
"oh shit, the OG just told me I'm wrong" — contempt aimed at the people on screen, never at the
reader.

## Tensions to resolve in the strategy (flagged, not ruled)

1. **"Convincing party manifesto" vs "Not a real party. Yet." / asks for no vote.** A manifesto
   normally asks for a vote. Strategy must choose how the document reads as a real platform without
   claiming to be one or soliciting anything.
2. **Specific costed pledges vs no invented numbers and no current fiscal-headroom estimate** (MMT
   storyboard offers none). Future Proof's form helps: pledge the *test and its kill-number*, not a
   costing we can't source.
3. **"Name the decision-maker" vs "no villains".** Resolved in canon: name institutions,
   mechanisms and public figures for public acts; demand repair; never hate the person.
4. **AI is the government's reason to exist but not on the reader's issues list.** Enter through
   jobs, wages, benefit decisions, fraud, ownership; never lead with "AI policy".
5. **The coin.** Keep it out.
