## 6. The gaps no party is filling

1. **Who owns the machines.** Every party treats AI as growth or as risk. **None names who gets the gains.** No sovereign compute fund, no automation dividend, no public stake in the Growth Zones that the public's grid is subsidising. This is the Norway/Alaska gap, and the reader-safe framing: name the decision-maker, not the technology.
2. **The first rung.** Youth and graduate unemployment is at a decade high. Reform blames degrees, Labour offers bootcamps. **Nobody guarantees a job or an apprenticeship that AI can't eat**, or measures what AI is actually doing to entry-level hiring. The government does not even track AI displacement.
3. **Power bills versus data centres.** Government is consulting on *cheaper* electricity for AI zones while household caps rise. **Nobody says "households first on the grid"** or makes compute campuses pay for their own generation. OpenAI is writing its own "community compact" because the state hasn't.
4. **Undo buttons.** Welfare cuts (PIP and UC) go ahead before the Timms review reports. The Budget makes permanent changes in a temporary energy shock. **No party proposes sunset-and-review by default** or a published postmortem when a policy misses.
5. **Pledges as tests.** Reform's "ministers sacked if pledges missed" is the closest anyone gets to accountability. It is a crude version of CI that Future Proof can out-design: a public dashboard, pre-registered targets, automatic review.
6. **Voting on more than one thing.** More than 80% want AI regulated and no party owns it; 41% fewer boats and nobody believes it. **Bundled parties can't represent split voters.** No one offers citizens' assemblies with teeth, issue-by-issue votes or vTaiwan-style consultation on AI.
7. **Experiment before rollout.** Basic income, four-day weeks and AI in public services are argued by slogan. **Nobody promises a pilot with a pre-registered success test before national rollout.** The What Works machinery exists and is under-used (NAO: 8% robustly evaluated).
8. **AI security of the state itself.** NCSC warns of AI malware on infrastructure by 2028. The defence uplift is about kit, not resilience of the digital state. Estonia-style resilience is unclaimed.
