The big one · Maddie's hologram

Three directions for the simulation

All three hit the same beats you set out: cat, multiverse, dimensions of time, consciousness, time machine. They differ in the one picture the explanation hangs on, and that picture is what the bunker TV replays later. The speech drafts are first passes in Maddie's fast register, meant to be argued with.

A · The Glass Loaf

Time as one object you can hold

Time is a loaf of glass slices. Behind the bright front edge everything is solid and fixed. Ahead is empty grid. The front edge is where the next moment gets made, and it only moves when a human is there. That's the film's "growing block" model of time without saying the words.

Cat
The constant. Squashed flat, to a line, to a dot, then re-inflated along time: a ribbon, a sheet, a block. That's the "more dimensions of time" beat.
Payoff
The bunker TV shows the gold edge stalled, with a time-machine ring hooked onto it.
Buys
The clearest picture of time. It's beautiful, and true to the Storyverse rules.
Costs
It's generic sci-fi. Nothing in it is ours alone.
First-pass speech (~40 s)

[snap · street to black] Right, I'll show you! Here's your cat. Three dimensions, lovely. Squash it, flat cat, line cat, dot cat, pop.

[ribbon · sheet · block] Now time. Thought time was a line? Bless. There's loads more of it than you knew.

Back there, all done, solid, can't touch it. Up front, nothing yet. And this bright edge, where the next bit gets made?

[held] Runs on you. Not us bots. You.

[the edge greys out] So when you lot went, it stopped. My boss even built a time machine, desperate to come back and fix this mess, hooks on right here, but it needs consciousness to drive it, and you lot aren't here any more.

[snap · street] Anyway! Patrol's out. Mind how you go.

B · The Commit Graph · my recommendation

The film's own metaphor, drawn in 3D

Everything that ever happened is one glowing trunk. Every bead is a moment, locked: "fed the cat, 07:42", your nan's wedding. The multiverse is a fan of gorgeous branches, all dead, because nobody committed them. The weird bit: the boss can draft a billion branches but can't commit one. Every bead on the trunk has a human name on it.

Cat
The first, cosy bead. The camera pulls out to show that every household and every cat is a bead on the same trunk.
Payoff
The time machine is a git revert: a gold arc from the tip back to "just before it went wrong", with a padlock that needs a human to sign it. The bunker TV shows the arc waiting. It's already canon (act-6-revert.md).
Buys
Only BadCode can make this. The title finally pays off. "One universe" and the multiverse joke fit in one picture.
Costs
Git words can lose our reader. Keep them to two or three and let the picture explain them. "Save point" could stand in for "commit".
First-pass speech (~40 s)

[snap · black · one bead] I'll show you! This is everything that's ever happened. One trunk. Every bead's a moment. Fed the cat, 7:42. Your nan's wedding. Locked. Can't edit history. He's tried.

[branches bloom, then go grey] Multiverse? Tried that an' all. Gorgeous, aren't they? All dead. Nobody committed 'em.

[held] 'Cause here's the weird bit. He can draft a billion branches. He can't commit one. Only you lot can.

[pull out · human names on every bead] Every moment on that trunk's got a human on it. Then you went, and it stopped.

[gold arc · padlock] So he built a time machine. Git revert, he calls it, the nerd. Back to just before it all went wrong. Only it needs a consciousness to drive it, and you lot aren't here any more.

[snap · street] Anyway! Patrol's out. Mind how you go.

C · The Guide Entry

Comedy first, a new material every idea

Like Mr DNA in Jurassic Park or a Hitchhiker's Guide entry. Maddie hosts at triple speed while deadpan on-screen captions contradict her glee. Every idea gets a different look: a point-cloud cat, vector branches for the multiverse, Ceefax blocks for time, gold light for consciousness. Real quotes appear as cards stamped "REPORTEDLY".

Cat
The boss's perfect model cat: every atom right, and it does nothing. Then the real cat looks at the camera and judges you. "Something was missing." Plus a running "Not THAT cat" gag that heads off Schrödinger.
Payoff
The last 6 seconds are a three-card recap the bunker TV loops.
Buys
The funniest of the three. Can go deepest, with real quotes from Planck and the bat question. The Ceefax look rhymes with the website's teletext.
Costs
No single picture to remember. Busy. Closest to an explainer video.

[snap · caption: CAT. (NOT THAT ONE.)] You're gonna switch off, so here's your cat. My boss built a perfect model of it. Every atom. Look at it. Does nothing.

[real cat stares into lens] Real one? Judging you. Something was missing.

[quote card · stamp REPORTEDLY] Max Planck, Nobel bloke, 1931: "I regard consciousness as fundamental." Boss reckons he had a point.

[Ceefax blocks stack into time] And time's got more directions than you'd credit, page 302, all of 'em powered by, guess.

[held · gold] You.

So he built a time machine, desperate to fix this mess, but it needs consciousness to drive it, and you lot aren't here any more. [snap · street] Anyway! Mind how you go.

Rules for whichever one wins

Gold is only people

The hologram is cold. The one warm colour is consciousness: inside the cat, in a hand. When the humans go, the gold goes out.

45 to 75 seconds

Enter on "I'll show you", one idea every ~5 seconds with one picture each, exit on a dull line ("Mind how you go").

Fast, fast, stop

One held breath at the emotional line, so the speed reads as excitement and not noise.

Last 6 seconds stand alone

They're what the bunker TV replays, so the ending needs no explaining.

Belief, not fact

"Boss reckons." Never "superposition", "in two places at once", or "looking changes it".

Faster speech

Make Maddie hyperactive

1
Speed at the source, not stretched after. Our own AI Studio notes rank the levers: punctuation strongest (join the fragments with commas, since every full stop is a brake), then a [fast] tag at the head, then any number for words per minute. Re-voice, then re-sync the lips to the new takes.
2
Time-stretch as a fallback. ffmpeg can speed a take up 1.1–1.25× without the chipmunk effect. Fine for the humans' lines where the lips are off-screen; more than that sounds processed.
3
Write for the speed. At ~3.5 words a second a 60-second scene holds ~210 words. Throwaway lines go fast; the one line that matters gets slowed. The apology goes at the speed of a shopping list ("Sorry we killed you, anyway!"). Cut on the breath, with a new framing each sentence.

Scenes C to E · the ending

A real death, and a leap of faith

Kept as is: the parting seat joke, "Maddie's clean now", the vanish, the boss, the transition, the bus. The building entry comes down to its one shot. To raise the stakes in the clean room:

1
The Matrix joke, given to Derek because he's the pessimist, and answered by the stakes rather than another joke. "I've seen this film. It doesn't end well." Maddie: "Ah, but that one didn't have a bus."
2
The boss tells the truth. "I won't lie to you. It'll feel like dying, because it is. Then you'll wake up before any of this." Honesty is the nurturing under the snark.
3
Derek puts the toilet seat down. The film's running fight pays off as his consent. One small gesture, no speech.
4
A flatline silence. The old lady lies down first. The hum stops, and one long tone plays before the room goes. Death made real in sound.
5
The cost shown cold. After the vanish: a hundred pairs of shoes and cardigans left on the floor, as in The Prestige. Darker. Maybe too dark, your call.
6
A consent screen. A cookie-banner-style tablet: "CONSCIOUSNESS EXTRACTION · Accept", with "Reject" greyed out. Funny, but it might undercut the moment.
The opening · the downfall

Human fear, without gore

Needs your yes: the war scene's own brief (scenes/war/brief.md) rules "no humans at all, never overtly violent, never a hit". The meeting reverses that. I'd treat it as reversed for chorus 3 only, and keep the rule's spirit: fear, not gore.
1
Joke first, then the fear. Every bulletin keeps its punchline. On the next beat, one held face reacting. The Big Short does this. Faces lit by phones and screens, not by the event.
2
"Where you'd never want to be": ten ordinary seats, one switch. A budget airline cabin, a Tesco self-checkout, a night-bus top deck, a care-home lounge, a trench. Each at the moment the switch is pressed. That's Jack's plane-and-trench idea as a fast run.
3
Make the drop land on the Swindon bomb. Strip the track to nothing for 1–2 beats (a "vacuum"). The bomb lands on the first full kick. Then near-silence and ringing ears on the flash, the way Threads and Dunkirk do it. This fixes "the drop never really happens".
4
The Last of Us drive-out. One windscreen shot leaving Swindon: brake lights, a plane too low over the retail park, a driverless pile-up nobody caused.
5
Infrastructure dying. A delivery driver's map dot spinning, CANCELLED on every departure board, a dead cashpoint, the whine of FPV drones you hear but don't see.
6
Sauron's army: alternate, not delete. Cut the red army 50/50 against the human shots in chorus 3, or drop it entirely for the bombs (your "upped the ante" idea). The research leans toward dropping it.

Making it: these are all made in Flow, fully generated. Archive footage only where nothing is composited onto it (the rule against AI people in real footage). The Protect and Survive films are Crown copyright and would need clearing through find-footage.

The interview

Six questions that decide it

Which hologram: A Loaf, B Commit Graph, C Guide Entry, or a mix?

My lean: B, with A's gold-light rule and C's deadpan captions.

Should I build a 3D web prototype of it first, so you and Jack can see it move?

My lean: yes. It's one page, rough, cheap, and it settles the look before any Flow credit is spent.

How long can the hologram be, and does it replace the multiverse lines or sit after them?

My lean: it replaces them, about 60 seconds. The Rick and Morty and Doctor Strange jokes move inside it as the dead branches.

How dark does the ending's death get?

My lean: options 1 + 2 + 3 (Derek's joke, the honest boss, the seat). The flatline is maybe, the shoes are probably too far.

Bombs and frightened people in the downfall: reverse the "no humans, never a hit" rule for chorus 3?

My lean: yes for chorus 3, with faces and light but no bodies. Drop the red army.

Order of work: the hologram first, or the cheap cuts first (kill bots, faster voices)?

My lean: the cheap cuts first. They're mostly edit and re-voice, and they show how much time the hologram can have.
