## 2. What makes regret land without sentiment

- **Earn it by the character's own arc.** *The Iron Giant*'s ending is read as working because the Giant rejects his design, and because "I am not a gun" is set up earlier. Sources: [Digital Trends retrospective](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/the-iron-giant-25th-anniversary-retrospective-superhero-movies/), [Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/568342/iron-giant-at-20/). [web-B] Reviewers, not formal analysis, and they disagree on the exact final words. Lesson: the payoff words must be planted. Here the planted words are "He misses you something rotten" and the opening tune.
- **Cut it short and plain.** Rutger Hauer says he trimmed the opening of the "tears in rain" speech and wrote the closing lines himself ("From 'All those moments' on, that's mine"). Sources: [Looper](https://looper.com/743419/the-improvised-rutger-hauer-line-that-changed-blade-runner-forever), [Slashfilm](https://slashfilm.com/stories/blade-runners-rutger-hauer-improvised-one-of-its-most-profound-lines). [web-A] on the trimming, but sources dispute how "improvised" it was, and I found no list of the cut lines. Lesson: the remorse speech was made shorter and plainer, not bigger.
- **Understatement carries weight.** Reviews of Wallace & Gromit credit Gromit's silence and eyebrow for the feeling ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/wallace-gromit-humour-comedy-style/)). [web-B] I found no sourced writer explaining why this works for grief, so treat the next point as inference.
- **[craft] A cheerful sidekick about an absent boss.** The regret should leak out as a slipped-in fact, not a confession. The cheer stays on, the sentence is too small for the news, and the pause does the crying. She reports the boss's feelings ("he misses you something rotten") and never names her own. The joke lands first, then the line she can't joke about. I have no source that tests this.
- **No pleading.** The audience came in braced for a villain. A flat admission gets more sympathy than an apology, because the audience supplies the forgiveness. [craft]

## 3. The swear at a UK turn

- No research source exists for "swear at a grief turn". British Comedy Guide forum posts say swearing's comic force depends on rationing and on the taboo staying potent ([example](https://www.comedy.co.uk/forums/post/745695/)). They also say good swearing can be comedy in itself, and that swearing for shock marks weak writing. [web-B] Forum opinion, 2006 to 2011. Frank Skinner is reported to have dropped most swearing so more conservative audience members could enjoy his act. That is his own account of his own act.
- [craft] Inference: one swear, flat and late, from a character who has been "mind how you go" polite, is the rationed version. It also admits blame in plain language, which suits `docs/marketing/the-reader.md` (no lectures, name the decision-maker). Risk: if Maddie swears earlier or more than once, it becomes a bit and the regret turns into a joke. Offer the clean version beside it: "we properly messed it up" or "we made a right mess of it". Both are UK idiom.
- Kai wants "we kind of fucked it up" in his own words. Keep "kind of" in the swear draft. The hedge is the comedy: a cosmic failure described as a slight mishap.

## Takeaways for the writers

- Plant the regret early: "he misses you something rotten" is the plant. The big line then pays it off instead of arriving cold.
- Keep Maddie cheerful throughout, and let the one flat sentence crack the cheer. Do not let her go solemn.
- Report the boss's feelings, not her own. "We" appears once, in the verdict.
- The speech's order: facts (what he tried, what was missing), then the verdict. The verdict sentence is short and has no adjective.
- Make no appeal for forgiveness and ask for nothing. Then "Patrol's out. Mind how you go." works as a deflection back into the everyday, and it pays off on the bus.
- One swear, late, with "kind of" in it. Offer a clean twin ("right mess of it") so Kai can compare.
- Call it a wager ("he reckons"), never a result. Do not use superposition, holography or two-places-at-once.
- Treat the multiverse wink as a gag that clears the ground, then spend the real feeling on "you're gone". Do not let the jokes outlast the news.
