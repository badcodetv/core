## Ordinary people
- Psychology work finds the "you are a simulation" idea meets strong resistance in both children and adults (philarchive summary, https://philarchive.org/rec/SUBILC, B, abstract only, I did not read the paper). So "you're a fake" starts uphill.
- Popular culture owns the idea through The Matrix and Musk, not through physics. That is my inference (C); no source retrieved. Practical upshot: the audience already has a picture ("alien computer") and a mood (shrug, or cool).
- Multiverse fatigue is real as talk, disputed as fact. Marvel's Multiverse Saga averages 72.9% on Rotten Tomatoes against 84.7% for the Infinity Saga, and The Marvels had the worst Marvel opening (TheWrap, https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/marvel-mcu-stakes-2026-avengers-doomsday-spider-man-brand-new-day/, B). Counter-view: "it wasn't the multiverse that let these movies down", and Across the Spider-Verse made over $600m (BGR, https://bgr.com/entertainment/youre-not-sick-of-the-multiverse-youre-sick-of-bad-movies/, C opinion). Blame is also put on homework: you must have watched many earlier films (Ringer, https://www.theringer.com/2023/02/23/marvel/marvel-studios-phase-5-quantumania-multiverse-saga-superhero-movie-fatigue, C opinion).
- Safe read: mocking the *trope* (endless variants, homework, "another you") is a shared joke. Mocking the *idea* is not needed. The audience does not have to have seen Rick and Morty; the Central Finite Curve (a walled-off set of universes where every Rick is the smartest man) is fan lore, and a wink should work without it (https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/rick-morty-season-5-finale-explained-central-finite-curve-evil-morty, B).
- Repo, UK reader: never "you've been duped", name decision-makers, keep it concrete (`docs/marketing/the-reader.md`, A). Cosmology said in a lecturing voice breaks that rule.

## Physicists
- Hossenfelder calls the simulation hypothesis pseudoscience: a programmer explains nothing ("back to mythology"), searches for code-like signs came up empty, and a discrete lattice struggles with Lorentz invariance (summary at https://bigthink.com/thinking/why-the-simulation-hypothesis-is-pseudoscience/ and https://rosetta.to/u/sabinehossenfelder/the-simulation-hypothesis-is-pseudoscience, B; I did not watch the video). Scott Aaronson disputes the Lorentz point but agrees the hypothesis is lazy (https://scottaaronson.blog/?m=201703, B). Even she concedes "some kind of computation" is not unscientific in itself (C, a commentator's reading).
- Planck length is *not a pixel*. Physics sources say it is where quantum-gravity effects are expected to matter, not a proven smallest length or a lattice (Physics Forums Insight, https://www.physicsforums.com/insights/hand-wavy-discussion-planck-length/, C-to-B). This is the likeliest dunk on any "the universe is made of tiny squares" line.
- Many-worlds is not the physics consensus. Surveys are informal and self-selected: Copenhagen about 36% in one, many-worlds about 11% in a Physics Magazine survey (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11058, https://astrobites.org/2026/06/03/in-to-the-multiverse-of-opinions-do-physicists-actually-agree-about-the-universe/, B; I saw only the search summaries). Physicists split on interpretations, so "none of that stuff is true" about the multiverse is a claim they will push on.
- Hard problem: about 62% of surveyed philosophers say there is one (2020 PhilPapers, via https://selfawarepatterns.com/2021/11/07/what-philosophers-believe-2020-edition/, B). **GAP:** I found no physicist survey on consciousness.
- Repo, physicist test: "Could a physicist read this without wincing?" Bostrom hedged carefully and was still quoted as "we live in a simulation" (`docs/stories/storyverse/doctrine.md` around line 596, A). Best defensible line: "the data underdetermine the metaphysics" (doctrine, A).

## Words that trigger "woo" (repo list, A: `doctrine.md` ~line 636 onward)
energy (pop sense), vibration, frequency, manifest, quantum + any mind word, "the universe wants", awakening, higher self, divine/mystical, "science has shown", "reality is an illusion", the word woo itself, spirit/life force, the whole simulation family (simulation, admins, red pills, glitches, NPCs, base reality). Buy list: record, copy, witness, ledger, wager, concede, dial vs wind, photograph, mystery.

## Takeaways for the writers
1. Aim the joke at the multiverse *trope* (variants, homework, the same man fighting himself), never at physics. Anyone who has seen a superhero film gets it.
2. Keep "simulation" to one correction (Kai's rule). Give the correction fast: "you're not wrong, you're not fake". Anyone with a Matrix picture in their head needs the picture replaced, not argued with.
3. Do not call the world "pixels", "code" or "a grid", and do not say the Planck scale is the smallest unit. That is the first dunk. If numbers appear at all, say it runs on rules and leave the grain out.
4. "Projection" and "you only see four dimensions of something bigger" is the safe physicist-compatible line. Say it as Maddie's boss's wager ("he reckons"), never "science has shown".
5. Do not claim the multiverse is *disproved*. Say the boss tried it and "it didn't work for him / he found nobody home". Experiment-failed in the AI's own story, not a verdict on physics.
6. Consciousness: say what it *does* in the story (it was the missing piece) and avoid mechanism words. No "quantum" near any mind word.
7. Cheerful and plain beats cosmic. Working-class reader: a delivery robot being practical and a bit embarrassed will land where a lecture will not. Regret does the persuading, not the physics.
