### The Mancunian direction — the reference, described, never named

**→ VOICE DIRECTION**

```
# AUDIO PROFILE: Gaz M.
## "The man who already knows how it ends"

A working-class northern Englishman in his forties. Not a presenter and not a performer — a bloke telling you something across a table, who happens to have been right about all of it.

### DIRECTOR'S NOTES

Style:
* The permanent smirk: you should be able to hear the curled lip. Bored, cocky and completely unbothered, and he finds the man he is describing beneath contempt.
* The underplay: he throws every punchline away rather than landing it, and delivers sarcasm as if it were a weather report. He never signals that something is funny.
* The grain: a gravelly voice with real grit and a little smoke in it, worn and lived-in — but never boomy, never an announcer, never a movie trailer.
* Placement: he speaks through his nose, chin lifted, jaw pushed forward, like a man answering a question he resents.

Accent: Gaz is from Salford, Greater Manchester. Hard flat northern vowels, a short hard "a" in bath and last, drawn out and leaned on and drooping at the ends of words. Swallowed consonants, dropped T's and dropped H's. Never Received Pronunciation, never London, never American.

Pace: Slow and unhurried, around 130 words per minute. Ordinary conversational speech with uneven sentence lengths. He leaves gaps and does not rush to fill them, and every full stop is a real stop with a breath in it. His pitch swoops up inside a sentence and drops away at the end of it — never droning, never on one note.
```

**→ SAMPLE CONTEXT**

Gaz is the voice you hire for dry British satire — dark comedy, kitchen-sink documentary, deadpan political commentary. He reads out facts he finds contemptible without ever once raising his voice about them.

## 2. camping → `docs/stories/camping/sound.md` § Rules for this story

**From** `docs/ai-studio/README.md` § "RULED 2026-09-04 — the missing ingredient was ANGER" (kept
there, retitled "Camping's narrator, RULED…", with a scope note). Copy as a Camping rule, e.g.
`**The narrator is angry, banked down (Jack, 2026-09-04).**` followed by:

````markdown
**Jack, on the induction-video swing: *"it is not very BadCode, it needs to be gritty, deadpan, with
a hint of sarcasm and anger."*** 🔴 **The register inversion is rejected — do not retry it.** A
cheerful corporate narrator is a legitimate comedic engine and it was not Camping's narrator. (Another
story may want it.)

🔑 **And the note names the thing every previous direction was missing.** Every block so far asked
for **bored, unbothered, cannot be bothered, beneath contempt** — all of which are *absence* of
feeling, and absence of feeling is exactly what came back as **"boring"**.

> **Deadpan is a lid on something, not an absence of it.** The flat delivery only reads as deadpan
> if there is visible pressure underneath it. Ask for the pressure, not the flatness.

**`voice.md` (the narrator's words) agrees, and always did.** [`../voice.md`](../voice.md): *"overtly sarcastic, dark
humour, **total authority**… nurturing underneath the snark. **The contempt is for the mistake, not
the people.**"* That is not boredom. **That is anger with manners.**

| Ask for | Not |
|---|---|
| Anger banked down and held still | Boredom |
| Sarcasm used as a weapon | Sarcasm as a joke |
| Tightening under pressure | Getting louder |
| Control | Flatness |

⚠️ **Say it as pressure, never as flatness** — LiveKit's rule bans *"quiet", "flat", "no rush"* as
flatness-inducing. **"Banked", "contained", "held", "compressed", "clipped" are all safe** because
they describe force being restrained rather than force being absent.

````

## Engine facts for docs/suno-gpt

*Added by the story-files/memory agent, 2026-10-29.* These came out of memory files that are being
deleted or cut to pointers, and are not (or only partly) in `docs/suno-gpt/` yet. Written as engine
facts; all were measured on Camping, so the numbers belong to that song. The shared-docs agent places
them (suggested homes in brackets) and ticks each.

- [x] **Variety comes from the named instrument family in the opening tokens, not the production
  sentence** (`files/suno-tag-mechanics.md`, beside the "weights the beginning" fact ~L114). Measured on
  Camping takes, 2026-09-18 (MFCC + spectral contrast + chroma fingerprints): six lanes each led by a
  different instrument family sat **1.7× further apart on average and 2.3× further at the extremes** than
  three lanes that differed only in production language (`docs/stories/camping/songs/camping.md` §v6.42,
  L9380, L10147). One axis is not enough: lanes that changed only the guest instrument while the opening
  sentence and the drum sentence stayed byte-identical still read as one record (r50–r53); moving the
  beat/subgenre, the instrument family and the opening together did not (r54). Lanes sharing 68–74% of
  the Style box were heard as "way too similar" (r69). Check: longest shared run between any two Style
  boxes, before generating.
- [x] **Duration is the only measured pacing lever, and a set target is obeyed tightly**
  (`files/suno-v6.md` §8b, after L634, and the Duration row ~L234). v6, Camping: a 195 s target gave
  12/12 takes at 3:14–3:16 (r48) and 24/24 within ~1 s (r54); 190 s gave 16/16 within a second (r70–r72);
  Auto on the same lyrics ran 4:38 and 4:55 against a 2:46 reference. Same words in less time is a forced
  speed-up. ⚠️ Obedience loosens when the target squeezes the words: at 155 s for the same ~60 lines, 9/14
  landed on target but two came back at 2:43 and 2:58 (r55) — when using Duration for pacing, check the
  returned length before blaming the prompt. ⬜ Whether Custom duration costs extra credits is unpriced.
- [x] **A ban loses to a positive instruction** (`files/suno-tag-mechanics.md` or `suno-voices.md` §4a).
  An Exclude-box ban cannot beat a Style-box or lyric-cue instruction asking for the same thing:
  `autotune` sat in the Exclude box the whole time and a tuned gang vocal still arrived, because Style
  said "a room of men singing the hook in bare block harmony" and three cues said "half sung" (Camping
  r66→r67, 2026-09-23); `shouting` was banned and still came, summoned by "hyping each other" (a hype
  man). Fix order: (1) a positive target the unwanted thing cannot satisfy, (2) matching section cues,
  (3) the bans last. Grep all three boxes for the summoning words before touching the Exclude list.
- [x] **The Lyrics box out-votes the Style box on casting** (`suno-voices.md`). An inherited cue reading
  "an older English storyteller" beat a Style box asking for grime MCs (Camping r58–r59, 2026-09-21).
  Rewrite every section cue when the cast changes; assert the old words are gone in code (a no-op
  replace looks like success).

  Six times on Camping a carried-over ban blocked the thing asked for (`piano` against the liked piano
  opening, `wobble bass` against the reference's bass, `major key` against Canon in D, `pipe organ`
  against an organ lane). Cross-lane bans are the worst: they travel forward as if they were rules. Read
  the ban list against each round's brief.
