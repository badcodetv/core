Dry run 2026-11-11: the cabinet answers (Opus)

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, opposition point 1. Chancellor: Fair. One deal against the rest is an anecdote with a spreadsheet. New test: every publicly backed deal signed in the first two years, half drawn by lot to carry the slice first, the other half after the trial, compared within the same power-draw band and region. The other half get exactly what they get today, so nobody gets less. It will still be a small number of deals. The review prints how many.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, opposition point 2. Chancellor: Correct. £24 is Scotland. £16 is Cumbria. £14 is the North East. And none of it until Parliament passes it. The body now says all four things.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, opposition point 3. Chancellor: Agreed. Counting zones counts ribbons. The new check forecasts the thing we argue: that the next zone is announced with no price on what the public puts in.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, statistician issue 1. Chancellor: Taken. The window now opens 12 November 2026 and closes 30 June 2027, both days included.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, statistician issue 2. Chancellor: Taken. The five are listed by name (Culham in Oxfordshire; North East; North Wales; South Wales; Lanarkshire). Only a new designation confirmed on gov.uk or in a written statement counts. A bid, a shortlist, an extension or a boundary change does not.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, statistician issue 3. Chancellor: Taken, nearly word for word. The claim: a new zone is designated in the window and announced with no figure in pounds for the discounted power, grant, land or guarantee behind it. No new zone, or every one priced: failed. Prior 0.35. That is our old 0.6 for a new zone at all, times a little better than even that it comes unpriced. The scheme already prints regional rates, so an announcement might quote one.

Cabinet: chancellor/the-slice, statistician issue 4. Chancellor: Taken. Alike on power-draw band (under 100 MW, 100 to 500 MW, over 500 MW) and region. Investment is the firm's capital spending on the site from its audited accounts, divided by the public support as the government publishes it for that deal.

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, opposition point 1. Prime Minister: Every new bill carries a test, so no minister picks. The old wording invited the reading anyway, because it compared bills that did with bills that didn't. Now it compares bills under the rule with the last parliament's, matched by department, both judged by one independent office against the aim each bill stated when it was introduced. That isn't drawn by lot. A rule for every law can't be.

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, opposition point 2. Prime Minister: Correct. Projects are not laws. The body now says so and uses the figure only for what it shows: even where the state spends, it seldom plans to check. The unsourced "none has a test" is gone.

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, opposition point 3. Prime Minister: Agreed. Withdrawn and replaced (see the statistician's second point).

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, statistician issue 1. Prime Minister: Refusal accepted. Withdrawn.

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, statistician issue 2. Prime Minister: Taken. The rule is now a count, read off each bill's publications page: an impact assessment dated on or before the Commons second reading, or not. Prior 0.4. We expect most bills do publish one, and we're putting that on the record against our own argument. If none reaches second reading in the window: void.

Cabinet: prime-minister/test-every-law, statistician issue 3. Prime Minister: Taken. On the share that meet the aim they stated when introduced.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, opposition point 1. Home Secretary: The visa number is one national figure. You can't issue Darlington a different one from Doncaster. So it can't be tried in two towns, and we don't pretend it can: MPs vote on it once a year, in public. What can be tried is the roof the link rests on: does money arriving first shorten the queue? If it doesn't, the link ties the number to nothing, and we'll say so. The body now says this plainly.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, opposition point 2. Home Secretary: Right on both counts. Homes, GP and school places for every work-visa arrival is not "none". It's large, over £1bn a year once national. That's an order of magnitude, not a costing. And the hotel money is the first payment, not the last. 16,021 at end June, half the year before. After it, MPs vote the roof money together with the number. No money voted, no rise in the number.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, opposition point 3. Home Secretary: Correct, and it's the proportion we're for. Nominal pay about 5% lower at the bottom. Over the same years, real pay at the bottom rose 55%. Both are now printed.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, statistician issue 1. Home Secretary: Accepted. Withdrawn.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, statistician issue 2. Home Secretary: Taken, with the date moved. End March 2027 is published after May, too late for this minister's six-month check. So: people in asylum hotels at the end of December 2026, in the quarterly release that first carries it, below 12,016. That's three quarters of the 16,021 at end June 2026. A year-on-year halving would come in under it, and a stall wouldn't. Prior 0.55. Held means the first source of roof money is real and growing. Failed means it's thinner than we say.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, statistician issue 3. Home Secretary: Taken. One date: void if it isn't published 30 days after the check date.

Cabinet: home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival, statistician issue 4. Home Secretary: Taken as written. The councils not drawn get it after the trial, so none gets less than today. The fail number follows the new figures: a tenth better on each.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, opposition point 1. Energy Secretary: Brilliant catch, and a bit embarrassing. We wrote a fail number that only fails if we forget to print the bill. New one, on what lands. Fails if, six months in, prepayment customers' standing charge hasn't fallen at least 10p a day more than direct-debit customers'. Or if their whole yearly electricity bill hasn't fallen at least £30 more, so nobody claws it back through the unit rate. Provisional. On new building: that's the Windfall Act's test, the Chancellor's. It already says in advance it changes our mind if the levy cuts new generation by more than it takes off bills. We don't write a rival.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, opposition point 2. Energy Secretary: Fair. The gas customers belong to a different bill and are gone from the list. Homes on heating oil stay: this doesn't touch their heating bill. Households whose energy is paid inside their rent go in, in the Windfall Act's words.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, opposition point 3. Energy Secretary: A world check is never the trial's result. The rules forbid calling it that, and a policy with no power can't produce one. It forecasts the wrong this policy answers: the bit of the bill you pay before the kettle boils keeps climbing whatever you use. If Ofgem holds it or cuts it in April, our case is weaker, and the check will say so in public.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, statistician issue 1. Energy Secretary: Right, we'd have been forecasting the VAT rate. The rule now reads the charge excluding VAT against 54.83p. If Ofgem gives only the VAT-inclusive figure, divide by 1.05 when VAT is 5% that quarter and take it as it stands when it's 0%. So the end date of the VAT holiday stops mattering.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, statistician issue 2. Energy Secretary: Taken. Claim and rule now say the same thing: the GB average across regions, direct debit, excluding VAT, above 54.83p a day. We dropped the 5%. Without VAT, a 5% rise in one quarter is a different bet from the one we mean. Prior 0.6.

Cabinet: energy-climate/standing-charge, statistician issue 3. Energy Secretary: Taken: 10p a day, against direct-debit customers over the same months, plus £30 a year on the whole bill. Both provisional, both pledges we set, not facts.

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, opposition point 1. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Yes. That's the exact trick this policy exists to stop, and we nearly built it into our own scoreboard. Now we count everyone on the payroll the day the firm joins. We follow them wherever they work after, through HMRC's records, and count nought for anyone out of work. The forty count. People let go before their firm signs up get no say, so they go on the leaves-out list by name.

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, opposition point 2. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Both sides of the trial are volunteers, drawn by lot, so the comparison inside it is fair. Volunteers against volunteers, matched on sector, size and their automation plans. And the policy is binding only where employers sign up. The trial measures it as it would actually run. Whether it holds for the firm that would never sign is a real question. It's the one Parliament weighs before this becomes law, and the review has to say so out loud. We've put it on the policy as the best case against.

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, opposition point 3. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Agreed. The payroll count goes up and down with the economy, whatever any board decides. Withdrawn. The new check asks firms what their own AI did to their headcount.

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, statistician issue 1. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Taken. The survey asks firms what AI did to their overall workforce headcount; we checked it is on the questionnaire. The claim: among AI-using firms with ten or more staff, the share saying AI reduced their headcount is higher in the last wave asked by March 2027 than in the last wave asked by March 2026. Both are read on the same day, to the decimal place the ONS gives. Equal or lower: failed. Prior 0.5. If neither window has the question, void. Check date 2027-05-14. We can't promise the question runs in a given wave months ahead, so the rule takes the latest wave asking it before each date, not a wave number.

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, statistician issue 2. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Taken as written. Pay and hours from HMRC's records follow the people, not the firm (see the opposition's first point).

Cabinet: work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving, statistician issue 3. Secretary of State for Work and Pensions: Band added: small. The public pays for running the trial, and the saving moves from the firm to its people. The body now carries the ledger. The lint is not ours to touch: it changes by a human PR, and we've flagged it for Kai and Jack.

Cabinet: (Prime Minister, all five, on the statistician's closing note) Understood: every one of these five keeps check.provisional: yes until you pass it. Each has now had its one rewrite; mark them again.
