Dry run 2026-11-11: the statistician's gate, five policies

Statistician: 18 issues

- chancellor/the-slice: the window has no start. It counts any zone named "by 30 June 2027" outside the five, so a zone named between 29 January and today would make it held already. I found none in four searches but can't rule one out. Fix: "designated after 11 November 2026".
- chancellor/the-slice: it excludes by region ("the North East", "North Wales"), so a second zone in Teesside or on Anglesey is ambiguous. "Named" is also ambiguous: a shortlisted bid, an extension or a designation? Fix: list the five zones by name and count only new designations confirmed on gov.uk or in a written statement.
- chancellor/the-slice: the forecast doesn't test the policy. More zones says nothing about whether public support comes without a stake or without a published price. Fix: forecast the receipt, e.g. "at least one zone designated between 12 November 2026 and 30 June 2027 is announced with no £ figure for the discounted power or other public support behind it", with the prior set honestly.
- chancellor/the-slice: test.against "publicly backed deals without a slice" doesn't say how they are like the trial deal (size band, power draw, region type), and test.measure doesn't say whose investment figure is used. Fix: name both.
- prime-minister/test-every-law: this is a safe bet. The NAO publishes dozens of value-for-money reports in five months, and "could not show whether it met its objectives" is a routine finding. My probability is above 0.9, so a stated prior of 0.75 understates it, and the check is refused.
- prime-minister/test-every-law: the rule needs the marker's judgement ("inadequate", "could not show") and tests the existing wrong, not the policy's claim that bills could publish their tests before debate. Fix: count from the bill pages on bills.parliament.uk, e.g. "of government bills given a Commons second reading between 1 December 2026 and 30 April 2027, at least half will have no impact assessment published before second reading", with an honest prior.
- prime-minister/test-every-law: judgement.change_mind says "fare no better at their review" but names no figure. Fix: say fare on what, e.g. the share that met their own stated test.
- home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival: 171,000 is the ONS's own provisional figure for the year ending December 2025 (published 21 May 2026), so this predicts that the fall since 2023 continues. The release is due within weeks and the Home Office visa figures for that year are already out, so it is close to knowable. That breaks the "status quo continues" rule.
- home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival: the forecast doesn't test the policy. The level of net migration doesn't test whether homes, GP places and school places keep pace with arrivals, or the policy's funding source (closing asylum hotels). Fix: forecast the money, e.g. the number of people in asylum hotels in the Home Office's quarterly immigration statistics at the end of March 2027 being below a stated N, or housing completions against work-visa grants.
- home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival: if_revised voids only if nothing is published by 2027-03-31, but the marking rule voids 30 days after check_on (2027-01-14). Fix: pick one date in the rule.
- home/the-roof-comes-with-the-arrival: test.against "councils like them" doesn't say how they are alike, and "GP and housing waits" names no figure. Fix: matched on population, deprivation decile and visa arrivals per head. Measure NHS England Appointments in General Practice (share seen within two weeks) and households on the housing register (MHCLG local authority housing statistics).
- energy-climate/standing-charge: the outcome hinges on VAT, not the standing charge. The rule includes VAT, and domestic electricity has been zero-rated since 1 October 2026. Reports disagree on whether that ends on 31 March 2027 or was extended to July 2027. 57.57p is exactly 54.83p × 1.05, so if VAT returns in April, any rise before VAT at all makes it held. Fix: mark the figure excluding VAT, against 54.83p excluding VAT. I confirmed 54.83p on Ofgem's October to December 2026 figures, down from 57.19p.
- energy-climate/standing-charge: the claim says "at least 5%" but the rule says "above 57.57p". Fix: align them (and name Ofgem's Direct Debit average across GB regions as the exact figure).
- energy-climate/standing-charge: fails_if ("don't show the cut") isn't a number. Fix: e.g. "the prepayment electricity standing charge is not at least Xp a day lower within six months (provisional)".
- work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving: the forecast is a bystander fact. The UK payroll count moves with the cycle, employer NICs and the minimum wage, so a fall can't be tied to automation or to whether workers had a say, and a rise argues nothing against the policy. Fix: forecast automation's effect on staff directly, e.g. the share of firms using AI that report it cut their headcount, in a named ONS Business Insights and Conditions Survey wave (confirm the question runs in that wave before writing it). The current rule is otherwise well frozen; if kept, say the flash estimate counts.
- work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving: test.against "firms like them" doesn't say how they are alike, test.measure names no figure for pay, hours or investment, and test.by "two years" has no start. Fix: matched on sector, size band and automation plans in the Transition Count. Use pay and hours from HMRC RTI for those firms and capital spending from their accounts, two years from the first firm's start.
- work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving: there is no band field, and the lint passes it because policy-lint.mjs never checks band (the README treats band as required for every policy). Fix: add a band, and make the lint require it.
- all five: none of these checks may lose check.provisional until it has been rewritten and passed the gate again. The two nearest to passing are energy-climate/standing-charge (once VAT is taken out) and work-pensions/a-say-before-the-saving (the rule is sound, the target is wrong).
