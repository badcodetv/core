The Government On Air

Round 2. One channel in A's broadcast look, holding all three things you asked for: watching it live, an overview of everything on the table, and going back through any argument. Four views, one screen, all built from the real 55 policies and the five real debates.

Click the tabs inside the screen. Tiles, events and markers all click through to the argument behind them. The debates are the 11 November practice run; the real ones start tomorrow at 07:30. Narrow the window for the phone version.

How a visitor moves through it

  1. Lands on Live. Something is always happening: an attack, an answer, a stamp. That's the hook.
  2. "What else is going on?" The floor shows all 55 at once, each lit by how far it's got. The one on air has a red light.
  3. "What's happened?" The record lists every event as it happened, and what's coming up next, like a TV guide.
  4. "Show me that one." Any tile or event opens Replay: the argument point by point, with what changed because of it.
Live

Someone is always speaking

The lower third (the caption bar news channels use) carries the whole drama: who's speaking, in their colour, and the stamp that lands when the minister concedes or argues back.

  • Four colours, one per role, used the same way everywhere: minister green, opposition red, statistician blue, human amber.
  • The strip at the bottom is the day: written, attacked, checked, answered, signed off, marked.
  • Between sittings it replays the latest exchange, so the screen is never empty.
The floor

Everything on the table, at a glance

A wall of monitors, like a TV gallery: one small screen per policy, grouped by minister. Each shows how far it's got, our odds and the day it's marked.

  • Six small bars on each tile show how far it is through the day's steps.
  • A dashed edge means no test yet, and it says so instead of hiding.
  • Filters: argued, due soonest, needs a test.
The record

What happened, and what's next

Results-night graphics for each event, newest first: conceded, argued back, signed off, flagged. Beside it, a TV guide of tomorrow's sittings and a countdown to the first marked prediction.

  • The running count of concessions sits next to a warning, because a cabinet that always agrees is a problem we print.
  • Held and missed get equal billing the day they arrive.
Replay

Any argument, point by point

A strip of markers, one per point, coloured by who raised it and filled by how it ended. Step through, or jump. Each point shows the attack, the answer and what changed in the policy because of it.

  • This is where "small, checked, undoable" becomes visible: every change has a reason sitting next to it.

Before I build it

  • Events that don't exist yet. You mentioned motions passing because their tests passed. The government has no power, so no trial runs yet. The events it can honestly show are concessions, signed-off changes, and predictions marked held or missed. The first is due 15 December. I've left trial results out rather than fake them.
  • The daylight pages. Each policy's full text page stays daylight and readable. The broadcast is the front of the channel; the reading pages sit behind it.
