# Prior art: open, agent-run, experiment-first governance

Research brief for the Future Proof government design, 2026-11-11. 22 web searches.
Evidence grades: **A** = primary source or peer-reviewed (preprints counted as primary, flagged), **B** = credible secondary (press, think tanks, law firms), **C** = blog, vendor or self-reported figure.

---

## 1. Deliberation platforms that changed policy

**vTaiwan + Pol.is.** First used in 2015 on Uber; the administration pledged to turn the Pol.is consensus statements into regulation ([compdemocracy.org](https://compdemocracy.org/Case-studies/2014-vTaiwan/), C). It produced dozens of recommendations, including online liquor sales rules ([Daily Beast, 2023](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/taiwan-tried-digitize-democracy-huge-084810185.html), B). **Why it faded:** it has not been used for a major decision since 2018. The government was not required to adopt its output, so "legislators don't take it seriously". It was limited to digital issues and was crowded out by a second government platform, Join (same source, B). It was top-down: the public could only discuss topics that ministries chose to release ([The Reporter](https://www.twreporter.org/a/open-government), B). A change of administration in 2016 withdrew all pending bills ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/s/611816/the-simple-but-ingenious-system-taiwan-uses-to-crowdsource-its-laws/amp/), B).

**Pol.is bridging.** Participants vote agree/disagree on short statements. Polis clusters participants by their voting patterns, without reading the text ([Small et al. 2021, Recerca](https://www.e-revistes.uji.es/index.php/recerca/article/view/5516/8314), A). It then ranks a statement by **group-informed consensus**: the *product* of its approval rates across every opinion group, so one hostile group can sink it ([Procaccia et al.](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/bridging.pdf), A). This became the reference case of "bridging-based ranking" ([arXiv 2211.12571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.12571), A). A caution: a Southampton study shows the algorithm's choice of two opinion groups, together with how moderators handled comments, shaped the vTaiwan result ([Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/496942/1/Sorting_a_Public_2023_AAM.docx), A).

**Decidim (Barcelona).** The 2020 participatory budget had 64,571 participants (52,601 online, 11,970 in person) deciding €30m. The money was **binding** ([decidim.org case study](https://decidim.org/case-studies/barcelona-budgeting/), C, self-reported). The 2025 prioritisation round had about 30,000 participants ([barcelona.cat](https://www.barcelona.cat/infobarcelona/en/prop-de-30-000-participants-donen-mes-de-100-000-suports-en-la-fase-de-prioritzacio-dels-pressupostos-participatius_1495491.html), A). A digital divide was raised from the start, and offline meetings were kept alongside the platform ([ictlogy notes](https://ictlogy.net/?p=4484), C). Qualitative studies find internal tensions that limit what it achieves ([IGOP thesis 2025](https://igop.uab.cat/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TFM-Rodrigo-Jofre_Agosto-2025.pdf), C). I found no hard demographic audit.

**Better Reykjavik.** The council committed to *process* the top ideas but kept the final say. More than 3,000 ideas were reviewed and close to 1,000 accepted since 2011 ([Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/interculturalcities/-/better-reykjavik), B). The city spent €1.9m on over 200 citizen projects ([Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/feature/10-people-centred-smart-city-initiatives/better-reykjavik/), B). The "My Neighbourhood" participatory budget ran yearly from 2011 to 2023 ([Citizens Foundation](https://citizens.is/impact/my-neighbourhood/), C, the platform's builder).

**Ireland's Citizens' Assembly (2016–18).** It had 99 randomly selected citizens, chosen to be representative by age, gender, class and region. It had an independent secretariat and streamed expert evidence ([IACL blog](https://blog-iacl-aidc.org/blog/2018/11/19/shaping-public-debate-an-introduction-to-the-citizens-assembly-m2hhs), B). 64% of members backed terminations without restriction; the referendum then passed 66.4–33.6 ([Electoral Reform Society](https://www.electoral-reform.org.uk/the-irish-abortion-referendum-how-a-citizens-assembly-helped-to-break-years-of-political-deadlock/), B). It worked because government had pre-committed to put the output in front of a binding vote, and because its predecessor convention had already produced the 2015 marriage referendum (same, B).

**Pattern:** a platform changes policy when its output has a **guaranteed route to a binding decision** (a budget, a referendum, a duty to respond). It dies when its output is advisory and the agenda is gated.

## 2. Futarchy and prediction-market governance

**Theory.** Hanson's rule: "vote on values, bet on beliefs". Democracy sets the goal metric, and conditional markets pick the policy expected to raise it ([Hanson 2002](https://holtz.org/Library/Social%20Science/Futurology/Futarchy%20Vote%20Values,%20But%20Bet%20Beliefs%20-%20Hanson%202002.htm), A).

**Practice.**
- *GnosisDAO* launched in 2020 with a deliberately "soft" futarchy, in which voters may override the markets ([CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/tech/2020/11/23/new-gnosisdao-bets-on-futarchy-a-prediction-market-governance-model), B). A later pilot ran conditional markets that were **purely advisory** ([Gnosis forum](https://forum.gnosis.io/t/conditional-markets-for-gip-proposals/898), A).
- *Optimism (2025)* is the best controlled test. It used **play-money** markets: futarchy and the Grants Council each picked 5 projects. Futarchy's 3 unique picks grew TVL more, but the forecasts **significantly overestimated** growth for every project. The council disputes that it was a fair control ([Optimism forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/futarchy-v1-preliminary-findings/10062), A; n = 3 is tiny).
- *MetaDAO (Solana)* runs binding futarchy. Its founder calls the liquidity "okay… but still not ideal". Proposers seed about $10k of liquidity ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/understanding-futarchy-on-solana), B). Running several proposals at once splits liquidity, and futarchy only works for decisions big enough to move the token price ([Blockworks Research](https://blockworks-research.beehiiv.com/p/futarchy-everywhere), B). On small treasuries, a few traders and wide spreads make manipulation cheap ([defiprime](https://defiprime.com/futard-prediction-markets), C).

**How futarchy is gamed:** thin markets. The underlying problem is that decision markets need a liquid *price* metric, and social policy has none. Optimism's markets also had a scoring bug: the forecaster-reward script processed percentage profits as decimals ([forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/experimenting-with-futarchy-for-optimism-grant-allocation-decisions/9678/35), A).

**UK law.** Under s.9 of the Gambling Act 2005, "betting" is a bet on "the likelihood of anything occurring or not occurring" or "whether anything is or is not true" ([legislation.gov.uk s.9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/9), A). A policy-outcome market fits that wording. For gaming, a "prize" includes "money or money's worth" ([s.6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/6), A). The Gambling Commission said on 4 Feb 2026 that prediction markets are akin to betting exchanges and need a **betting intermediary licence**, and that operating without one is a criminal offence ([UKGC blog](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/blog/post/prediction-markets-heres-what-you-need-to-know), A). Polymarket geoblocks the UK ([law firm via lawcareers.net](https://www.lawcareers.net/Explore/CommercialQuestion/RPC-The-commercial-and-legal-anatomy-of-a-prediction-market), B). *My reading, not legal advice (C):* we stay outside "betting" only if a staker can never get back more than they put in. That means a refund-only stake, with any upside paid in non-transferable reputation that has no money's worth. Get counsel before launch.

## 3. Sybil resistance without plutocracy

| Mechanism | Holds up? | Cost to a poor participant |
|---|---|---|
| **Quadratic voting** ([Lalley & Weyl, AEA P&P 2018](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pandp.20181002), A) | Sound only if identities are unique; it collapses under sybils | Votes are bought with credits; if credits are money, the rich still win |
| **Quadratic funding** ([Buterin, Hitzig, Weyl](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06421), A) | Paying matching funds by the number of unique donors *invites* fake identities ([Gitcoin](https://gitcoin.co/research/quadratic-funding-sybil-resistance), A) | A small donation counts a lot, which is good |
| **Gitcoin Passport** (stacked credentials) | It lowers the incentive to fake rather than stopping it; Gitcoin reports about 60% fewer flagged addresses in GG23 (same, A, self-reported). Collusion rings are found after the fact ([Chainscore](https://www.chainscorelabs.com/blog/public-goods-funding-and-quadratic-voting/sybil-and-collusion-resistance/why-your-grant-program-is-funding-cartels-not-innovators), C) | Low, but people with few accounts or a thin online history score lower |
| **Worldcoin / World ID** (iris) | Strong uniqueness, but blocked or ordered to delete data in Spain ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.es/20240307/spain-bans-company-that-pays-people-to-have-irises-scanned), B), Kenya ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202505/indonesia-suspends-world-kenya-says-delete-biometric-data), B), Germany ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/331713/german-authority-orders-eyeball-scanning-world-project-to-delete-data-after-investigation), B) and elsewhere | Biometric privacy; you need access to an Orb |
| **BrightID / social-graph proof of personhood** | Subjective web-of-trust methods trade strength for accessibility ([Siddarth et al. review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05300), A) | Free, but it needs people already inside the network, which favours the connected |
| **Refundable deposit / refund-only stake** | Makes spam costly with no upside to buy; one rich actor can still post many deposits | Capital tied up; a deposit forfeited for spam is the only real loss |
| **AI agents** | None of the above proves "one agent" | An agent is cheap to copy, so count agents by *track record*, not by head |

## 4. Multi-agent AI debate

- **Theory:** Irving, Christiano and Amodei propose debate as a way to oversee AI: two AIs argue and a weaker judge decides ([arXiv 1805.00899](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899), A).
- **Gains:** Du et al. found that several model instances debating over rounds improved factuality and reasoning ([arXiv 2305.14325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325), A). In Khan et al. (ICML 2024), non-expert judges reached 88% accuracy (human) and 76% (LLM) with debate, against 60% and 48% without. More persuasive debaters *raised* judge accuracy in debate but *lowered* it in one-sided consultancy. LLM judges show position bias, and human judges beat LLM judges ([arXiv 2402.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782), A).
- **Failures:** accuracy can *fall* over rounds as agents switch from right to wrong answers under peer pressure, through sycophancy and conformity ([Wynn et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396), A, preprint). In teams of identical models, conformity rises even with minimal exposure to peers. Debate there cost 2.1–3.4× the tokens of agents correcting themselves alone, for equal or lower accuracy, and the paper names "consensus collapse" as a failure ([arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00914), A, preprint). This bears directly on our setup, where **one cabinet agent plays ten ministers**.
- **AI on public policy:** In DeepMind's Habermas Machine (*Science*, Oct 2024), 5,734 participants preferred the AI-written group statements to human mediators' 56% of the time. The statements reduced division and took in minority critiques, and the result replicated in a representative UK virtual citizens' assembly. Limits: it does no fact-checking and no moderation ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adq2852); [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/65220/), A).

## 5. On-chain governance and records on Solana

- **SPL Governance / Realms:** this is the standard on-chain DAO program, used by most Solana DAOs ([SPL README](https://github.com/solana-labs/solana-program-library/blob/governance-v3.1.0/governance/README.md), A). It needs a community mint, with an optional council mint ([Realms SDK](https://docs.realms.today/developer-resources/sdk/sdk-dao-creation), A). Vote weight can be swapped through plugins, such as Civic Pass gating ([Civic docs](https://docs.civic.com/third-party-integrations/realms), A). Its built-in model is token-weighted, which is plutocratic unless the plugin replaces it.
- **Attestations:** the Solana Attestation Service has been live on mainnet since 23 May 2025. It is permissionless: anyone can define a schema and issue or verify attestations ([Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/solana-attestation-service), A). It is Solana's counterpart to Ethereum's EAS ([attest.org](https://attest.org), B). It suits our records: "agent X predicted Y", "check date passed, result Z".
- **Storage cost:** rent-exempt deposit = (bytes + 128) × lamports per byte. The rate was 6,960 from launch; SIMD-0437 is cutting it toward 696, and it stood at 5,080 by late 2026 ([Chainstack](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-rent-reduction-reclaim-excess-rent.md), B; [Anza](https://docs.anza.xyz/implemented-proposals/ed_overview/ed_storage_rent_economics), A). So 1 KB of debate text costs about 0.006 SOL locked (refundable when the account is closed), and a 50 KB debate about 0.25 SOL. A 32-byte hash costs about 0.0008 SOL. Arweave is about $16 per GB one-off, so a 50 KB debate costs well under a cent ([ar.io Permaweb Journal](https://permaweb-journal.ar.io/article/economics-storing-large-data-on-arweave.html), B). IPFS pinning is about $0.15 per GB per month, and the record disappears if nobody keeps paying ([Chainscore](https://www.chainscorelabs.com/comparisons/storage-ipfs-vs-arweave-vs-filecoin/storage-provider-selection-criteria/ipfs-pinning-service-pricing-models-vs-arweave-storage-pricing), C).
- **Pattern:** keep the hash, the stake and the prediction on chain, and keep the text on Arweave. Git stays the working copy.

## Five lessons for our design

1. **Give the output a guaranteed route to a decision** (a duty to respond by a date). Advisory-only platforms die, as vTaiwan and Reykjavik's non-binding layer show; binding ones last, as Decidim's budget and Ireland's referendum show.
2. **Rank proposals by bridging (approval multiplied across opinion clusters), not by raw votes or stake**. Publish the clustering, because it shapes the outcome.
3. **Make the stakes refund-only and the upside non-cash reputation.** Anything that pays out on an outcome is betting under s.9, and the Gambling Commission wants a licence; Optimism's play-money futarchy shows the signal survives without cash.
4. **Never let one model play every side.** Same-model debate conforms and collapses; require debaters from different models or providers, an adversarial structure, and a judge that sees both cases.
5. **Weight agents by their scored track record, and keep text off chain.** Agents are free to copy, so headcount and token weight both fail. Put the prediction, its check date and an SAS attestation of the result on Solana, and the words on Arweave.
