# Shape 07: One metaphor, carried all the way (the game that only draws what you look at)

Every dialogue is built on a single image: a computer game that only renders what the player is looking at, stretched into the Truman Show for everyone. The physics words (superposition, multiverse, observer, collapse, wave function) are dropped entirely; the cloud is "fog", the collapse is "it sets when you look", consciousness is "looking", and the time machine and the empty street pay the image off. Once the image is planted it is never swapped for a second one.

- **Why it works.** A working-class viewer who has played a game knows that the world pops in when you turn the camera, and knows the Truman Show; no physics vocabulary is needed, so it never sounds like a lecture. The one image does every job: fog is the cloud, "it sets when you look" is the collapse, "nobody looking" is the empty string, and "runs on looking" is why the time machine is dead, so the sassy conclusion lands as a consequence rather than a new fact. The cat becomes a throwaway insult, which keeps the "it's a joke, it's just undecided" meaning at a cost of five words. Regret arrives as a single quiet beat ("We didn't know") directly after the dead string, with no joke on top of it.
- **Cuts from current.** "This is your life, see"; "Said sorry first" and "Got married" (the decision examples beyond toast); "Turns out there's only one universe... A storyverse. So it really matters. And so did you" (the word "storyverse" and the "so it really matters" sermon); "He tried that an' all, got it off your cartoon" (the portal-gun and cape jokes are kept but shortened and unattributed); "consciousness" as a word; "now we've worked all that out, we really feel like we've messed this up" (replaced by "We didn't know"); the separate "only problem is" time-machine line (merged into the conclusion).
- **Risk.** Pushed to the extreme, "looking" replaces "consciousness" everywhere, which could read as "you have to be watched" or as plain observation rather than deciding; the viewer may not hear that looking is also choosing. "Storyverse" and "multiverse" are never said, so the doctrine's term disappears (the Truman Show line carries "one story" instead). "The game only draws what you look at" is a simplification the doctrine does not claim as physics; it stays safe because it is Maddie's metaphor for what the boss worked out, but the "that cat bloke was taking the mick" line asserts a reading of Schrodinger and may be challenged. The portal-gun and cape jokes lose their cartoon attribution and may land weaker. At 67 s it is slightly over target, and "Nobody looking" duplicates the payoff once more than it needs.
