# Shape 03: the game theory said plainly (old lady reasons it out loud)

Options only. No scene files, scripts or voices touched. Seconds are estimates at about 2.5 words a second plus marked pauses.

## Dialogue 1: Maddie's introduction (about 18 s)

| Speaker | Line | s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry about the racket. It's his tune, he plays it on repeat. | 5 |
| Maddie | He misses you something rotten, my boss. Built world after world to get you back. Nothing in them's real. Turns out the bit that mattered was you. | 9 |
| Maddie | Anyway. He's got the patrol out. Mind how you go. | 3 |
| **Total** | | **about 17** |

**Why it works.** Four plants in three breaths: friendly (the apology and the cheery sign-off), the boss misses us, the simulations failed because the missing piece is us, and the patrol is out. "Something rotten" and "my boss" do the West Country warmth and the loyalty in one go. The game-theory shape needs the audience to hold one fact by the doorstep (the empty simulations), so it is stated once, flat, with no wind-up.

**Cuts.** "Oh, I didn't notice you there", "Ooh, excuse to turn this down", "Ever since you lot... went", "He's been ever so down", "There's nobody there to notice", "You. And your consciousness", and "he'd never admit he misses you". The word "consciousness" goes entirely; "the bit that mattered was you" carries it.

**Risk.** Without "ever since you lot went", the extinction is only implied at this point. It is cheap to restore as a three-word clause ("since you lot went") for about 1 s. Also, "sorry about the racket" gives the music a line to hang on, so the shot must show her turning it down or the apology floats.

**Protected lines.** None of Kai's four protected lines belong to this scene.

Honest note: this shape costs about 5 to 10 s over target because the reasoning is spoken whole. The first trim, if Kai wants it, is Maddie's "Five years he's built worlds..." sentence in line 2 (already said in scene A beat 0 under this shape), which saves about 4 s and makes it about 61 s of speech.

**Why it works.** The old lady is the audience's stand-in, and she does the thing the audience is already doing: adding it up. She says the dead end out loud ("it ends with us anyway"), gives the one asymmetry that matters (the earlier us wakes up in a world still standing), and then names the real crux, which is trust, not logic. That hands Maddie the only honest reply left: show, not argue. The kill-bot explosion becomes the evidence, so the trust problem is answered by an act the viewer saw, not by a speech. Resistance stays real because she still refuses to decide. The vote stays off screen.

**Cuts.** "He didn't know... none of us did" is shortened to one clause. "Turns out none of it works without you" (now in beat 0). "We're bored. We miss you. And you lot are living in a bunker" (replaced by the lady's own sums, so the bunker point comes from her, not the bot). "The earlier you, back then... my boss has a plan to fix your politics" (the lady carries the earlier-us point; the political plan is gone, which also suits the reader rules, since there is no vague fix-it promise). "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?" (cut, the big-pause reveal carries it).

**Risk.** This is the most sermon-adjacent shape. The lady's sums speech is the one place a viewer might feel lectured, and "the moral is never stated" is only barely respected because she states the logic, not the moral. If she sounds like a spreadsheet the scene dies. It needs a stern, dry delivery ("Right. Sums.") and a visible bitter beat before "it ends with us anyway". It also runs long. And it makes the old lady the smartest person present, which slightly undercuts Maddie's "go on your feelings" line, since the lady has now partly reasoned her way there. Mitigation: Maddie's last line is a plea, not a proof.

**Protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Survives verbatim (line 4).
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Survives verbatim (line 5).
- "It runs on... well... you." Survives with both big pauses, shortened lead-in ("It can only send data back. Not people. It runs on...").
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up." Survives, but loses its "As he says... [ahem]" set-up (cut for time), so the whisper reads as hers, not a quote. Kai may want the "As he says" back for the embarrassment gag (+1.5 s).
- Also kept verbatim: "So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live" and "...Yeah. I can see how that sounds."
