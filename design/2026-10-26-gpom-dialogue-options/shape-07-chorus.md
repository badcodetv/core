# Shape 07: The Chorus

Sandra and Derek's bickering voices the doubts, so the old lady doesn't have to. The extreme version is that the humans' doubts arrive as a domestic row. Nobody ever argues the game theory aloud. A couple who can't agree on a toilet lid say it all by accident.

## DIALOGUE 1: Maddie's introduction (target 20 s or less)

The chorus here is the opposite of Sandra and Derek. It is two hollow simulated people, looping a squabble from a screen behind Maddie with nobody home. This plants the bunker row as a sound, and it shows "nothing in them's real" without Maddie explaining it.

| Speaker | Line | Est. sec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Pardon the racket. [turns it down] It's his tune. Plays it on repeat. Ever since you lot... went. | 7 |
| Sim voices (flat, looping, under the music, no lip movement) | "Did you leave the seat up?" / "Did you?" | 2 (overlaps Maddie) |
| Maddie | He's built so many worlds to get you back. Nobody's home in any of them. Turns out that was the bit that mattered. | 7 |
| Maddie | Anyway. Patrol's out again. Mind how you go. | 3 |
| **Total** | | **~17 to 19** |

**Why it works.** The viewer hears an argument and Maddie names it empty, so "consciousness is the missing piece" is felt before it is stated. When the real row cuts in at the bunker, it sounds like the same row with a pulse. That turns the loneliness into a punchline, and Maddie stays friendly without the AI's feelings being spelled out. "Ever since you lot... went" carries the missing-us line.

**Cuts.** "Oh, I didn't notice you there." The gloss on "excuse to turn this down". "He's been ever so down". "You. And your consciousness." The line "Of course, he'd never admit that he misses you" (the misses-us plant moves to "Plays it on repeat. Ever since you lot... went").

**Risk.**
- The sim row pre-empts the bunker toilet-seat joke, and the juxtaposition is the stated gag of beat C2. Keep the sim line different in wording and tiny in the mix, or drop the sim voices and keep Maddie's three lines.
- "Nobody's home" is close to saying consciousness outright. It is the one line that might read as lecture.
- The patrol warning is now two lines of mood, not a plant. Add a one-shot kill-bot glimpse if needed.

**Protected lines.** None of Kai's four are in scene A.

## DIALOGUE 2: The doorstep (target ~60 s)

Blocking: the bunker door is ajar. Sandra and Derek are visible or audible behind the old lady, still mid-row from earlier. They only ever speak in complaint or aside. Neither argues the case, and the old lady speaks only the resistance lines she already owns.

This runs about 8 s over target. The first things to cut are Derek's "missing ingredient" and Sandra's "shut the door", which brings it to roughly 64 s. The pause-heavy "runs on... well... you" line is the one place the estimate is soft.

**Why it works.**
- **The lady stays stern.** She is the wall. Sandra and Derek leak the doubt, and doubt from two people who won't stop bickering is funnier and truer than a speech.
- **The dead end is a joke.** "A hundred of us, five years, can't agree on a toilet seat" and "five more years of this, then" say the whole game-theory point and never state it. They are the only voices that argue the reasons to go.
- **Trust is shown, not argued.** The kill-bot self-destruct arrives as a half-grudging aside, which is how a crowd actually talks themselves round.
- **Maddie never asks.** She just answers.
- **The ending holds.** The old lady still refuses to decide, and "Come back tomorrow" now sounds like a woman buying time while her own people are tipping.

**Cuts.**
- "He didn't know you were down here. None of us did."
- "He's spent five years running experiments" (shortened).
- "We're bored. We miss you." (the miss-you plant already lives in scene A).
- "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?" (Maddie now explains in one beat).
- "But you'd still be alive" wording trimmed.
- "My boss has a plan to fix your politics" (the whisper stands in for it).
- "I know. And we're really sorry" is kept short.

**Risk.**
- **Chorus can steal the vote.** The audience hears Sandra and Derek already leaning, so the off-screen vote has no tension. Mitigation: keep Sandra's tone sour, keep "allegedly", and don't let anyone say "yes".
- **Sandra and Derek are not yet introduced.** Their first appearance is the bunker row before this, so the voices work, but the shot needs them visible in the doorway.
- **Sitcom risk.** The jokes could undercut the old lady's grief, since "killed everyone we ever loved" sits a few lines away from a toilet gag. Keep the toilet line to a mutter and cut Derek's "missing ingredient" first.
- **Length is over target.**
- Voice work: Derek and Sandra need new takes, because `voice3.py` is not touched here. They also overlap the existing mix.

**Protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." survives verbatim.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." survives verbatim.
- "It runs on... well... you." survives with the big pauses intact.
- The whispered "this time we won't fuck it up" survives. It loses "My boss has a plan to fix your politics. As he says..." and keeps "[ahem] [whispered, embarrassed]". Check that the line reads as embarrassed without the setup.
