# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

Fewest words that still carry every beat. Seconds are estimates at about 2.5 words/sec plus pauses. I worked from the doorstep `script.json` and the brief; I did not re-read the wider canon files.

## Dialogue 1: Maddie's introduction (scene A, beat 0)

| Speaker | Line | Est. sec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry about the racket. | 3 |
| Maddie | It's his tune. Plays it every day. Ever since you lot... went. | 5 |
| Maddie | He's built all these simulations, trying to get you back. Nothing in them's real. Turns out it was you that mattered. | 8 |
| Maddie | Anyway! Patrol's out again. Mind how you go. | 3 |
| **Total** | 41 words | **~19 s** |

**Why it works.** Four short beats: friendly, the boss is sad, the sims failed because consciousness was the missing piece, then danger. The music is the first proof of loneliness, so it carries the "misses you" beat without Maddie saying it. "Ever since you lot... went" is the only grief line and is left to hang. "Mind how you go" is the warm sign-off that makes the patrol warning sound like a favour.

**Cuts.** "I didn't notice you there"; the "excuse to turn this down" gag; "ever so down"; "There's nobody there to notice"; "You. And your consciousness."; "he'd never admit he misses you". The last was the only line that said "misses us" outright.

**Risk.** "Consciousness" is never said, only "it was you that mattered", so the audience has to infer that the sims lacked a mind. It may read as "he wants company" rather than "the sims were hollow". It also loses "ever so down", the warmest West Country colour. If Kai wants the word back, "Nothing in them's real. No one's home" costs about 1 s.

**Kai's protected lines.** None are named for this scene. The "Ever since you lot... went" phrasing survives.

## Dialogue 2: the doorstep (scene B)

| Speaker | Line | Est. sec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old lady | Your boss killed everyone we ever loved. We're not meeting him. | 4 |
| Maddie | I know. [pause] He didn't know you were down here. | 3.5 |
| Old lady | And why should we believe that? | 2 |
| Maddie | You shouldn't. [pause] I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings. | 6.5 |
| Maddie | He blew up his own kill bots to save you, mind. And you're in a bunker. That's not a future. | 5 |
| Old lady | And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody. | 5 |
| Maddie | I know. Sorry. [pause] Time machine. Sends data back, not people. Runs on... [big pause] well... [big pause] you. | 9 |
| Old lady | So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live. | 4 |
| Maddie | ...Yeah. [pause] But the earlier you live. World's not broken. This time we won't... [whispered, embarrassed] fuck it up. | 7 |
| Old lady | [rushed] I'm not deciding this for a hundred people on a doorstep. Come back tomorrow. | 5 |
| SFX | [door slam]. Then Maddie turns and rolls away. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **~52 s** |

**Why it works.** The structure is pure escalation. Refusal, then "how do we know", then the only honest answer (feelings). Maddie then plays her one card, the kill bots, so trust is shown by an act rather than argued. In the same breath she gives the dead end, "That's not a future", and the old lady's "Again?" lands on it. The reveal comes in three clipped fragments that build to the held "you", so the pauses do the work the dropped words used to. The lady then gets the ugly summary, Maddie concedes it, and the only comfort offered is the earlier selves living. The whisper is the punchline and the lady cuts it dead.

**Cuts.**
- "I wouldn't either".
- The whole "five years running experiments, Turns out none of it works without you" speech. Scene A now plants it and the audience already has it.
- "We're bored. We miss you."
- "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?", which was pure set-up for the reveal. The lady now reacts only to the result.
- "I can see how that sounds".
- "My boss has a plan to fix your politics".
- "He thought there was nobody left to drive it", and the "It can only" connective.

**Risk.**
1. The time machine is now three fragments with no "drive" framing, so the exposition is thin. A viewer who skipped scene C's setup may not know "runs on you" means dying. The lady's "kill ourselves" line does the translating, which is a deliberate dependency.
2. "World's not broken" is cryptic without "fix your politics". It is the one place the moral is nearly stated, and I kept it vague on purpose. Drop it if it reads as a slogan.
3. The kill-bots line is new, and it adds a plot claim that the humans may not know how Maddie knows about. Check it against scene A/C staging.
4. "Mind" and "Sorry." are flattened, so Maddie sounds slightly curt in the middle. The warmth now sits mostly in her accent and delivery.
5. "Again?" lands harder with the pre-line "Time machine." as a deadpan. That is good, but it makes Maddie sound a bit robotic.

**Kai's protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." survives intact.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." survives intact.
- "It runs on... well... you." survives, with "Runs on..." (dropping "It") in the telegraphic style, and with the big pauses.
- The whisper survives as "This time we won't... fuck it up", shortened from "this time we won't fuck it up". The "As he says... [ahem]" lead-in is cut.
