# Shape 08: the bunker is the argument

The humans are never told their life is a dead end. The audience is shown it, in one detail, and Maddie is too polite to mention it. The detail is **the doorframe tally**: five years of scratch-marks, bundled in fives, running up the inside of the bunker door. It runs out of frame. There is nothing after today's mark, because there is no plan for tomorrow. The same detail goes to the sound mix: the toilet-seat row from the earlier bunker scene is **still going, word for word, five years in**. It is the only thing left to argue about.

**Why it works.** The only thing it keeps is the chain: friendly, then he misses us, then the simulations were empty because nobody was there, then the patrol. Each of those is one sentence. "Nobody there to notice" carries the whole consciousness idea without the word, and "mind how you go" lets the threat arrive as a pleasantry. It sets up the bunker idea early, too: she is the first character to say that things are empty without people.

**Cuts.** The "oh I didn't notice you" turn, turning the music down, "ever since you lot went", "ever so down", "and your consciousness", and "he's got that patrol out again". That is about 36 s gone.

**Risk.** "Consciousness" is never spoken, so the audience takes "the bit that mattered" on trust. If the scene before it does not set up "nobody's home in the simulations", the line is vague. It also loses some of Maddie's chattiness. Nothing here is a protected line.

**Protected lines.** None sit in this dialogue.

## Dialogue 2: the doorstep

**Why it works.** The two weak links in the current version are Maddie's "this hasn't worked out for anybody" speech and the exposition. Both are replaced by pictures the audience has already been given. The humans' dead end is **seen** (the tally with no next mark) and **heard** (Sandra and Derek, five years on, still on the seat). Maddie never argues that their life is not a life. She looks at the doorframe, says one quiet sentence, and goes silent. Because the audience gets the point unprompted, the old lady's pride ("We're alive. That's more than he can say.") is the strongest resistance in the scene: she is defending something the viewer can see is not enough. The row cuts in right on her line, so the film makes the comment and nobody speaks it. The moral is never stated. Trust is covered by "go on your feelings", plus a later cut to the self-destruct footage if the cut wants to show it (not required in the dialogue).

**Cuts from the current version.**
- "He didn't know you were down here. None of us did."
- Maddie's recap of the experiments (D1 now covers it).
- "this hasn't worked out for anybody. We're bored. We miss you. And you lot are living in a bunker." That line is replaced by the tally.
- "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?"
- "And we're really sorry" is shortened to "Sorry. Truly."

**Risk.**
1. The whole shape depends on two picture beats (the tally, and a live-sync of the row). If the tally is not legible in the shot, "five years of marks" is an unexplained line.
2. "Not one spare for tomorrow" borders on telling. It is the single most on-the-nose line here, and the cut could reduce it to just "That's five years of marks, isn't it?".
3. The audience hears the row when the lady says "We're alive" and may read it as a punchline and not as grief. The line "Yeah." is there to stop that.
4. Sandra's line needs recording; her voice is not in the current script and costs a voice take.
5. It drops "Drive it?", so the bot has to explain the machine in one go. Spoken fast, that is a lot.
6. About 64 s, so a little over target.

**Protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Survives intact.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Survives intact.
- "It runs on... well... you." Survives intact with both big pauses.
- "this time we won't fuck it up." Survives intact, whispered, and is now the last thing Maddie says before the lady's "Come back tomorrow".
- Also kept: "So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live." with Maddie's "...Yeah. I can see how that sounds."
