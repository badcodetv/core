# Shape 02: the picture carries it

Principle pushed to the extreme: if an image we have already seen says it, the line goes. Words are only for what no shot can say (consciousness is the missing piece; "it runs on you"; the whispered promise). Nothing in script.json, voice3.py or the voices was touched.

---

## Dialogue 1: Maddie's introduction (beat 0)

Shots relied on: (a) Maddie in ruined Swindon with the tune blaring from her speaker, a hand/arm gesture to turn it down; (b) a kill-bot patrol visible far behind her, or crossing the frame, on "patrol's out" (the audience sees the threat, she only names it); (c) a held beat of her looking at the empty street on "went."

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry, that's his tune. Every day, on repeat. Ever since you lot... [beat] went. | 6.5 |
| Maddie | He's built so many worlds to get you back. [beat] Nobody in them to notice. That was the bit that mattered. | 6.0 |
| Maddie | Anyway! Patrol's out. Mind how you go. | 2.5 |
| **Total** | | **~15 s** (15 to 17 with the street beat) |

**Why it works.** The repeated tune plus "ever since you lot went" already says he misses us, so "he'd never admit it" is dead weight; the audience watches a machine's grief loop out of a speaker. The simulation failure is compressed to one image-sized idea ("nobody in them to notice"), which plants consciousness without the word. The patrol is shown, so the line only has to be a cheerful dismissal, which is where Maddie's warmth and the dread meet.

**Cuts.** "I didn't notice you there" (surprise is performed by the shot); "excuse to turn this racket down" (the action says it); "He's been ever so down" (the tune says it); "Only nothing in them's real" (merged); "You. And your consciousness." (the word "consciousness" is dropped; "nobody in them to notice" carries it); "he'd never admit that he misses you" (implied by the loop).

**Risk.** Without the word "consciousness" the later "you're the conscious ones" and "it runs on you" lose their plant by one step; it relies on "notice" being heard as "someone to be aware." Also, "the bit that mattered" is now carried by tone alone. If the tune shot is weak, "he misses you" never lands and the AI reads as merely busy. Fallback: put "consciousness" back as "Nobody conscious in them to notice."

**Protected lines.** None of Kai's four protected lines is in this beat. (Intro lines are untouched in spirit: "Ever since you lot... went." and "Mind how you go." are kept verbatim.)

## Dialogue 2: the doorstep (scene B)

Shots relied on: (a) the kill bots exploding and the smoke seen on the bunker CCTV (this replaces the whole trust argument; Maddie only points at it); (b) the bunker interior and the hundred packed in, seen from the Sandra/Derek row onwards (this replaces any speech about the dead end); (c) the old lady's face through the gap in the door on "Again?"; (d) a held wide on Maddie at the door during the two big pauses on "It runs on... well... you"; (e) the door slam and the roll-away shot Kai already asked for, left silent so "they have time" is felt.

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| Lady | Your boss killed everyone we ever loved. We're not meeting him. | 4.0 |
| Maddie | I know. I wouldn't either. Five years he's built worlds to get you back. Every one came out empty. Doesn't work without you. | 7.5 |
| Lady | And how the hell are we supposed to believe a word of that? | 3.5 |
| Maddie | You can't. [pause] I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings. | 6.5 |
| Maddie | He blew up his own bots for you. [pause] And you're all down here. | 4.0 |
| Lady | And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody. | 5.0 |
| Maddie | I know. Sorry. He's built a time machine. It runs on... [big pause] well... [big pause] you. | 7.0 |
| Lady | So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live. | 4.0 |
| Maddie | ...Yeah. I can see how that sounds. [pause] It only sends data back. The earlier you gets to live. [ahem] [whispered] This time we won't fuck it up. | 10.5 |
| Lady | [rushed] I'm not deciding this for a hundred people on a doorstep. Come back tomorrow. | 5.0 |
| SFX | [door slam] | 1.0 |
| (silent) | Maddie turns and rolls away down the hill | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **~61 s** (about 58 s of it voiced or sound) |

**Why it works.** The game theory is delivered as a sequence of things the audience has watched, not argued. Trust ("you can't prove it, go on your feelings") is immediately answered by the one proof available, the exploded kill bots, said in a single flat sentence rather than a case. The dead end is "you're all down here" laid over the image of the hundred, no speech needed. What remains in words is the three things only words can do: the machine runs on them, it sends data not people, and the lady's refusal to treat it as a gift. Resistance stays real because the lady keeps both her hardest lines ("Again? You killed everybody." and "kill ourselves") and Maddie concedes ("I can see how that sounds") instead of reassuring. The moral is never stated.

**Cuts.** "He didn't know you were down here. None of us did." (the AI's later explosion of its own bots covers the regret); "Turns out none of it works without you" shortened to "Doesn't work without you"; "this hasn't worked out for anybody. We're bored. We miss you" (the opening beat already did "misses you"); "I know. And we're really sorry" (kept as a single "Sorry"); "he thought there was nobody left to drive it" plus the lady's "Drive it? What do you mean?" (the exchange is gone, so "It runs on..." now follows directly from "time machine"); "not people" as its own sentence (folded into "only sends data back"); "the world wouldn't be broken / a plan to fix your politics" (cut entirely); "well, I'm not deciding this right now" (trimmed to "I'm not deciding this").

**Risk.** (1) "He blew up his own bots for you" is a trust argument stated as a fact; if the explosions shot or CCTV image is not prominent enough in the audience's memory, the proof is invisible and Maddie sounds like she is changing the subject. (2) Dropping "Drive it? What do you mean?" removes the lady's only question, so "It runs on... you" lands as a statement, not a reveal she pulls out of Maddie; the double big pause must be held to compensate. (3) Dropping "the world wouldn't be broken" and the politics fix leaves the earlier-you offer thin: "gets to live" alone does not say why that matters to a bunker of old people. (4) "The earlier you" is ambiguous in one hearing; the line may need a visual (the bus, the 2026 street) to read.

**Kai's protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Survives verbatim; only "There's nothing I can say that proves it" is cut, since "You can't." already says it.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Survives verbatim. "That" now refers to the bunker line before it, which reads fine.
- "It runs on... well... you." Survives verbatim with both big pauses, directly after "time machine."
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up." Survives, with "[ahem]" and the whisper; the lead-in ("My boss has a plan to fix your politics. As he says...") is cut, so the whisper now arrives with no setup and "he" is not named. If it reads as Maddie's own promise rather than quoting the AI, that is a feature and a risk; add "As he says..." (+1.5 s) if Kai wants the quotation.
