# Shape 04: Comedy first (GPOM dialogue options)

Shape: Maddie's bluntness and bad timing deliver the hard facts. Every fact the humans need arrives inside a joke or an apology that goes wrong. Seconds are estimates at roughly 2.7 words/s plus marked pauses.

## DIALOGUE 1: Maddie's introduction (scene A, beat 0)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry about the racket. | 2.0 |
| Maddie | That's his tune. On repeat, every day, since you lot... [pause] went. | 4.0 |
| Maddie | He'd never say he misses you, mind. Built loads of simulations to get you back. Nothing in them's real. Nobody there to notice. Turns out that was the bit that mattered. You. | 10.0 |
| Maddie | Anyway, his patrol's out. Mind how you go! | 2.5 |
| **Total** | | **~18.5** |

**Why it works.** The tune and the apology make her friendly in two seconds. The boss's loneliness is delivered as a "he'd never admit it" aside, the gossip register of a delivery driver, so it reads as character rather than exposition. The failed-simulation fact is a single flat run ("Nothing in them's real. Nobody there to notice.") and "You." lands as a small, embarrassed compliment. Then she changes the subject cheerfully to the thing that will kill them. The cheerful "Mind how you go!" after "patrol's out" is the joke, and it plants the threat.

**Cuts.** "Oh, I didn't notice you there", the "excuse to turn this racket down" bit, "He's been ever so down", "Ever since you lot... went" is kept but shortened, "And your consciousness" (the word goes; "nobody there to notice" carries it), and the second "Of course he'd never admit" beat is folded into one clause.

**Risk.** "Consciousness" is never said, so the later "You're the conscious ones" in scene B has to do the naming. That is fine for a first scene but it leans on the audience connecting "nobody there to notice" to it. The run in line 3 is still the longest block, about 10 s of uninterrupted talking. If it drags, cut "He'd never say he misses you, mind." and lose the loneliness plant (~3 s).

**Protected lines.** None of Kai's four protected lines are in this scene.

## DIALOGUE 2: The doorstep (scene B)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|
| Lady | Your boss killed everyone we ever loved. We're not meeting him. | 3.5 |
| Maddie | Fair enough. I wouldn't either. | 2.5 |
| Lady | And how are we supposed to believe a word of that? | 3.0 |
| Maddie | You can't. [pause] I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings. | 6.5 |
| Maddie | Look, it's not working out for anyone. He's bored, we miss you, and you're down here in a bunker. I've heard the toilet seat thing. Through the vents. Every day. | 6.5 |
| Lady | And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody. | 5.0 |
| Maddie | I know. Sorry. [pause] Anyway! Time machine. He thought there was nobody left to drive it. | 5.5 |
| Maddie | It can only send data back. Not people. And it runs on... [big pause] well... [big pause] you. | 6.5 |
| Lady | So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live. | 4.0 |
| Maddie | ...Yeah. I can see how that sounds. [pause] It sounded better on the way over. | 4.5 |
| Maddie | But the earlier you stays alive. World's not broken. And he blew up his own robots for you. In a panic, but still. | 6.5 |
| Maddie | [ahem] [whispered, embarrassed] He says this time we won't fuck it up. | 4.5 |
| Lady | [rushed] I'm not deciding this for a hundred people on a doorstep. Come back tomorrow. | 5.0 |
| SFX | [door slam] | 0.5 |
| Maddie | [through the door, bright] Tomorrow's good! I'll bring biscuits. | 2.0 |
| Picture | Maddie turns and rolls away down the hill (no dialogue) | ~2.0 |
| **Total** | | **~68 (about 64 of speech and sfx plus the roll)** |

If it must hit 60, drop "Every day." and the biscuits line (saves ~3 s) and trim the "It sounded better on the way over" pause. That gets to about 62.

**Why it works.** Every piece of game theory is attached to a joke that undercuts Maddie. The dead end is not argued; it is "you're down here in a bunker" plus the toilet seat row, which the audience watched two scenes ago, so they know exactly how bleak a hundred people in a hole with no future looks. The machine's cost is delivered with a big pause and then the word "you", and the old lady states it more brutally than Maddie ever would, so the resistance is real and unsoftened. Maddie's reply is not a rebuttal. It is "I can see how that sounds" and an admission she rehearsed it, which is the trust-by-honesty move: a salesperson who loses the pitch in front of you is more believable than one who wins it. The counter-fact (earlier you lives, world isn't broken) is stated plainly, quickly. The proof of good faith, he blew up his own robots, is the one thing she says that isn't an argument: it is something that already happened on screen. "In a panic, but still" stops it being a sales point. The whisper is the emotional landing and the old lady refuses to decide. The biscuits line is the bad-timing button that lets the audience breathe after the slam.

**Cuts.** "He didn't know you were down here. None of us did." (the AI's ignorance is cut; the destroyed kill bots do the work of showing regret). The "five years running experiments, building whole worlds" paragraph (scene A now plants it). "We're bored. We miss you." is kept inside one line but "This hasn't worked out for anybody" is compressed. "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?" is cut; "Anyway! Time machine." and "drive it" are both said by Maddie. "And we're really sorry." is shortened to "Sorry." "My boss has a plan to fix your politics." is cut entirely.

**Risk.** Three, in order:
1. The comedy can undercut the stakes. A hundred people dying and a biscuits joke may read as flippant if the voice performance is not exactly right; Maddie must be sincerely upbeat, never winking.
2. The toilet-seat callback only works if scene A of the bunker is fresh in the audience's mind. If there is a long gap, it is a miss, and the "dead end" fact goes thin.
3. "My boss has a plan to fix your politics" is gone, which loses the one explicit reason the new world will be better. The line now leans on "He says this time we won't fuck it up" alone. That is the most politically thin version of the pitch. It also invents a small fact ("blew up his own robots for you") that the lady never acknowledges; she should arguably react with a beat of silence. And there is a tonal risk that the audience hears "you've got nothing here anyway" as the film calling their lives worthless; this is why the toilet seat joke is affectionate (Sandra and Derek are alive, and arguing) rather than bleak.

**Protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Kept verbatim.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Kept verbatim.
- "It runs on... well... you." Kept verbatim, with both big pauses.
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up." Kept, reworded slightly to "He says this time we won't fuck it up" (the "As he says..." framing is tightened so the whisper still has its [ahem] lead-in).
