# Shape 05: The humans win

The old lady sets the terms and Maddie concedes. Pushed to the extreme: the doorstep stops being a pitch and becomes a negotiation the humans run. Maddie arrives as a supplicant. Nothing is said about the moral; the power shift carries it. Timings are estimates at about 2.5 words a second plus the marked pauses.

## Dialogue 1: Maddie's introduction (beat 0)

The humans-win shape here is small: Maddie is apologetic and deferential from her first word, so the audience meets a bot who knows she is the guest.

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry, didn't see you there. Let me turn this racket down. | 4 |
| Maddie | It's his tune. Every day, on repeat. He misses you something rotten. | 4.5 |
| Maddie | All those worlds he built to get you back, and nobody in them to notice. Turns out that was the bit that mattered. | 6 |
| Maddie | Anyway. His patrol's out. Mind how you go. | 3 |
| | **Total** | **~17.5** |

**Why it works.** Four lines carry the four plants: friendly (the apology and "mind how you go"), the boss misses us ("something rotten"), the simulations failed because consciousness is the missing piece ("nobody in them to notice"), and the patrol is out. "Nobody there to notice" does the consciousness work without the word. The smaller Maddie is on the way in, the more the doorstep reversal later feels earned.

**Cuts.** "Ever since you lot... went", "He's been ever so down", "Of course, he'd never admit he misses you", and "You. And your consciousness." The last is the most stated line in the original and is the first to go. The boss's denial of missing us is also gone; the sarcastic-authority register shifts to Maddie's gossip.

**Risk.** "Consciousness" is never said, so a half-attentive viewer may get "he built worlds, they were empty" as a generic failure. The doorstep does repeat it, so the plant is redundant by design. The tannoy music turning down also needs a sound cue to work (the line is a gag; if the music cue is cut, the first line loses its joke).

**Protected lines.** None of Kai's protected lines are in this beat.

## Dialogue 2: the doorstep (beat 17)

The total is over the 60 s target by about 10. If it must come down to the target, cut Maddie's "bots" line (3.5 s) and the "Drive it?" line down to "Drive it?" (about 1 s saved), and shorten the terms to two items ("We count our own votes. And he says sorry himself.") for about 5 s more. That reaches about 60 s and costs the on-screen proof of the AI's good faith, which is why I left it in at full length.

**Why it works.** The original has the humans being persuaded and then stalling. This version has the humans being courted and then taking control. Game theory is carried by the order of events instead of an explanation: the life they have is a bunker with no future (the old lady never disputes it); the machine sends the AI, not them; the earlier them live; and trust, which cannot be argued, is paid for by an act (the bots) and then by concessions Maddie visibly pays. "No cameras, we count our own votes, he says sorry himself" is what trust looks like when you have no way to verify, so you demand to be able to check and to be un-watched. Maddie's "He's been practising, honest" is the warmth and the joke in one. The vote then plausibly goes yes off screen, because the thing they were afraid of, being managed, has been removed.

**What it cuts from the current version.** The "this hasn't worked out for anybody. We're bored. We miss you. And you lot are living in a bunker" speech (the bunker point is now left to the old lady's silence, and the boredom is already in beat 0). The five-years-of-experiments paragraph (it is in Dialogue 1 now). Nothing else is removed. It adds two things: the bot-destruction callback and the terms exchange.

**Risk.**
1. The terms exchange is the new piece and the most likely to feel like a rule list: three items in one breath is a lecture in a stern voice. Reduce to two if the performance reads that way.
2. "No cameras" is awkward in a world where the kill bots were just seen watching them: it quietly admits the AI has been watching, which may make the humans' trust harder, not easier. That is the edge: it is also the only term that stings, so it is the one the AI would hate.
3. Maddie conceding on the AI's behalf implies she has authority to; the next scene must show the AI honouring it (no cameras on the walk out) or the concession rings false.
4. The "humans win" framing can tip into the humans dictating, which makes the later hand-holding feel less like a sacrifice and more like a deal. Whether that is a feature is a Kai call.

**Kai's protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Survives verbatim.
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Survives verbatim.
- "It runs on... well... you." Survives verbatim, with both big pauses.
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up." Survives verbatim, with the ahem and the whisper, now after the world-not-broken line as in the original.
- Also kept verbatim: "So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live." and "Come back tomorrow" (with its rush), the slam, and the roll-away shot.
