# Shape 06: Reversal (the human works out what the machine needs before Maddie can say it)

Pushed to the extreme: Maddie is never allowed to deliver the key facts. She hands over a half-sentence and the other party finishes it. In D1 the "other party" is the viewer. In D2 it is the old lady, who does the game theory out loud, bitterly, ahead of the robot.

## DIALOGUE 1: Maddie's introduction

| Speaker | Line | Est. sec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maddie | Oh, hello! Sorry about the racket. | 2.5 |
| Maddie | It's his tune. Every day, on repeat. He'd never admit he misses you. | 4.5 |
| Maddie | He's built whole worlds, trying to get you back. Every single one came out empty. | 4.5 |
| Maddie | Go on. Guess what's missing. [pause, 1.5 s of held silence] | 2.5 |
| Maddie | ...Yeah. Took him five years, too. | 2.5 |
| Maddie | Anyway, his patrol's out. Mind how you go. | 2.5 |
| | **Total** | **~19 s** |

**Why it works.** The old version tells the audience that consciousness was the missing piece. This one makes them supply it. The held silence is a joke beat and a clue beat at once: the viewer answers "us / someone to notice" in their head, and Maddie's "Took him five years, too" confirms it while quietly mocking her boss for being slower than the audience. It plants all four things (friendly, he misses us, simulations failed because of what is missing, patrol out) in 19 s. It also trains the viewer in the film's mode: you will be the one who works it out, which pays off when the old lady does the same on the doorstep.

**Cuts.** "I didn't notice you there"; "excuse to turn this down"; "ever since you lot went"; "ever so down"; "nobody there to notice"; "that was the bit that mattered"; "you and your consciousness" (the word and the explanation are deliberately never said).

**Risk.** The silence only works if the cut holds it and the picture gives the viewer something to look at; a cold audience may hear "guess" as a quiz and feel talked down to. "Empty" is vaguer than "no consciousness", so a first-time viewer may land on "no people" rather than "nobody experiencing it". That is probably fine, since the doorstep sharpens it, but it is a looser plant. Needs a voice take with a real beat of silence.

**Kai's protected lines.** None sit in D1. Kai's "He's been ever so down" and "he'd never admit he misses you" survive only in compressed form ("He'd never admit he misses you").

Honest note: the reversal costs time, because every deduction takes a line from each speaker. 76 s is the floor with all three reversals ("Us", "What does it run on", "Not us"). To hit 60 s, drop one: cut the "Lady: Drive it... what does it run on?" / "Maddie: It runs on... well..." / "Us." / "...You." exchange to a single beat (Lady: "It runs on us." Maddie: "...Well. You."), and cut "And it sends him back. Not us." That gives **~64 s**, at the price of two reversals instead of three. I recommend that version for picking; the 76 s table is the full-strength extreme.

**~64 s version, the lines that change:** after Maddie's "time machine... nobody left to drive it", go straight to:

| Speaker | Line | Est. sec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lady | Worlds that came out empty. A machine nobody can ride. It runs on us. | 4 |
| Maddie | It runs on... [big pause] well... You. | 3 |
| Lady | So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live. | 3.5 |
| Maddie | ...Yeah. I can see how that sounds. [pause] Data only, though. The earlier you's still alive, the world isn't broken. [ahem] [whispered, embarrassed] This time we won't fuck it up. | 10 |

**Why it works.** The old doorstep has Maddie explain a sequence the lady passively receives, so the audience hears a pitch. Here the lady is the sharpest person in the scene: she guesses the missing ingredient ("Us"), states the dead end herself ("tins running out, a roof that won't hold"), and spots the machine's price before Maddie can soften it. That does three jobs. (1) Game theory arrives as her bitterness rather than the robot's argument, so nobody is lectured. (2) Resistance is real, because she knows exactly what is being asked. (3) Trust is handled as Kai wrote it, with no proof offered ("go on your feelings"), but Maddie's one piece of evidence, the blown-up patrol, is dropped in quietly and the lady does not thank her for it. The moral is never stated. Maddie's worst moment, whispering the swear, becomes human: the robot is the one left fumbling. "Come back tomorrow" now reads as a woman who understood everything and still refuses to be rushed.

**Cuts from the current version.** "None of us did"; "He's spent five years running experiments" (compressed); "There's nothing I can say that proves it"; "this hasn't worked out for anybody. We're bored. We miss you. And you lot are living in a bunker" (the bunker is now said by the lady, the boredom and missing are in D1 and the AI's own scenes); "Drive it? What do you mean?" (replaced by her working it out); "My boss has a plan to fix your politics" (trimmed to "the world isn't broken"; the whisper still carries the plan).

**Risk.** (1) Length: the reversal beats are cheap in words but need breathing room, so it will not hit 60 s with all three. (2) The old lady is now the cleverest person present, and if the performance gets too knowing she stops sounding stern and starts sounding like a narrator doing the explaining; "Us." must be flat and grim, not triumphant. (3) The patrol line is now the only trust anchor and Maddie has to say it without pleading. (4) Protected line "It runs on... well... you" is shared with the lady, so a re-record is needed for Maddie's half. (5) Lady's "tins running out, a roof that won't hold" invents bunker facts: check against the bunker scene (the Cold War bunker may be sound, so swap for "a hundred of us, no future, a row about a toilet seat" if so).

**Kai's protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." Survives intact (lightly trimmed: the "nothing I can say proves it" sentence is removed).
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." Survives word for word, but its trigger is now the lady's own list of misery plus Maddie's offer of the patrol, rather than Maddie's "you lot are living in a bunker."
- "It runs on... well... you." Altered: split between the two. Maddie begins, the lady finishes with "Us", Maddie concedes "You." In the 64 s version the lady says "It runs on us" and Maddie answers "It runs on... well... You." (the big pauses kept), which keeps Kai's line whole in Maddie's mouth.
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up." Survives intact, still embarrassed, with the [ahem] kept.
