# Shape 10: ending first (GPOM dialogue options)

Method: wrote the last ten seconds of the doorstep first, asking what the old lady must do on the step so that walking out in scene C is inevitable, then worked backwards. The finding: she must never decide aloud, but she must ask one question that only someone who has already decided would ask ("All of us? Together?"). The vote happens off screen; the question is the vote leaking.

## Dialogue 2 - the doorstep (written first)

The last ten seconds (lines 11 to 15) are the spine. Everything before them is cut to serve them.

Total: about 71 s with the humming tail; about 67 s to the slam. Over the 60 s target by roughly 7 s. Honest fix if Kai wants 60: drop line 5's second sentence and the line 15 tail (about 5 s saved), or trim line 7 to "He's built a time machine. It sends data back. Not people. It runs on... well... you."

**Why it works.** Line 13 is Kai's protected "Come back tomorrow", but it now lands after a tell. A person who has refused will say "no". A person who asks "All of us? Together?" has already moved into logistics; the only thing left to protect is the group. Her refusal then becomes procedural (she won't decide for a hundred on a doorstep), which is exactly why the next scene can be a vote we never see and a walk-out nobody explains. Maddie's answer, "It doesn't go with fewer", is the one cold fact the humans need: nobody is sacrificed for the rest, it is all or nothing, so the game-theory knot (why would I go if you won't) dissolves without anyone stating it. Trust is handled in line 5: the proof is the thing they watched, not an argument. The moral is never stated. The closing hum is the cheap emotional callback: the tune that was a symptom of the AI's grief in scene A becomes Maddie's own, and rolling away slowly gives the humans "time".

**What it cuts.** The doorstep's "Drive it? What do you mean, drive it?" exchange (merged into line 7, so the "runs on... you" reveal comes in one breath of Maddie). "Every one came out empty. Turns out none of it works without you" (scene A now carries the failed simulations; repeating it was the doorstep's biggest redundancy). "He's spent five years running experiments" and "We're bored. We miss you" (the miss is in scene A and the hum). "My boss has a plan to fix your politics" (the whispered promise carries it). "And he thought there was nobody left to drive it".

**Risk.** (1) Line 5 spends the AI's self-destruction as evidence, and names the bunker in the same breath; if the bunker is not shown dead-end enough earlier, "living in a bunker" is too thin for the "dead end" half of the game theory. (2) "All of us? Together?" tips her hand: a viewer may feel the scene is over before the door slams, flattening "Come back tomorrow". Mitigation: play line 11 flat, almost to herself, and let Maddie not react. (3) Cutting the "Drive it?" question removes the human beat of incomprehension; line 7 becomes a Maddie monologue of about 11 s, the longest on the step. (4) The humming tail is not in script; it needs a sound cue and a shot Kai has already asked for (Maddie turning and rolling away).

**Protected lines.**
- "I'm a robot. You're the conscious ones. You'll just have to go on your feelings." - kept verbatim (line 4).
- "And what do you expect us to do about that? Again? You killed everybody." - kept verbatim (line 6).
- "It runs on... well... you." - kept verbatim with both big pauses (line 7).
- Maddie whispering "this time we won't fuck it up" - kept; now the end of line 10, with the ahem and embarrassment intact. Only the lead-in "As he says" is dropped.
- Also kept: "So you want us to kill ourselves so your boss can live", "Come back tomorrow", the slam, the roll away.

## Dialogue 1 - Maddie's introduction

Shape carried over from the ending: the doorstep now leans on Maddie being credible and on scene A having already shown the AI's grief, so scene A plants only three things, in the order the doorstep will pay them off: he misses you, nothing he builds is real without you, he still has the patrol out.

| # | Speaker | Line | Est. s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maddie | Oh, hello! | 1.0 |
| 2 | Maddie | Ooh, let me turn this racket down. | 2.5 |
| 3 | Maddie | It's his tune. Every day, on repeat. Ever since you lot... [pause] went. | 5.0 |
| 4 | Maddie | He's built a hundred worlds to get you back. Nothing in them's real. Nobody there to notice. | 6.0 |
| 5 | Maddie | Turns out you were the bit that mattered. | 2.5 |
| 6 | Maddie | Anyway. Patrol's out. Mind how you go. | 2.5 |

Total: about 19.5 s.

**Why it works.** It is one thought with a turn: music, so he is sad, so he built worlds, so they were empty, so it was us, so off you go. The line "Turns out you were the bit that mattered" lets her say the thesis as a throwaway before she retreats into patrol logistics, which is how a chatty West Country bot would cover an emotional slip. "Hundred worlds" quietly seeds the number a hundred in the audience's ear before the bunker.

**What it cuts.** "Oh, I didn't notice you there" (the reason for turning the music down carries the surprise); "He's been ever so down" (the repeating tune says it); "You. And your consciousness" (the word consciousness is now delivered only on the doorstep, by Kai's "You're the conscious ones", so it lands fresh there); "Of course, he'd never admit that he misses you" (the deniability joke; the whole "he misses you" is now implied by "to get you back"). 

**Risk.** (1) "Misses you" is no longer said, only implied, which weakens the sarcastic-nurturing note; the cost is the joke about him never admitting it. If Kai wants it back, swap line 5 for "Of course, he'd never admit he misses you." (3 s, total about 20 s) and lose the thesis line. (2) "Consciousness" as a word is gone from scene A; the missing-piece idea is carried by "nobody there to notice" and "the bit that mattered", which a distracted viewer may not decode. (3) Already voiced takes for the cut lines are wasted.

**Protected lines.** Dialogue 1 had none flagged. Kept from the current text: "Oh, hello!", "Ooh... turn this racket down" (shortened), "It's his tune... on repeat, every day... Ever since you lot... went", "Nothing in them's real. There's nobody there to notice", "Turns out that was the bit that mattered" (reworded to "you were the bit"), "Mind how you go".
