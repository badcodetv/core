# Custody, Clawback, and Delegation

**Question:** How are SPL delegate approvals and Token-2022's permanent-delegate extension actually perceived, flagged, and tolerated by the Solana market — and is "the treasury holds an unlimited delegate over your coins to debit rent" a survivable design for ENC, or the thing that gets it called a rug?

## What I found

1. **`approve`/`approve_checked` grants a delegate the right to move up to a *set amount* from one token account — not the whole balance by protocol design, but nothing stops the amount being set to `u64::MAX`, which is functionally unlimited until spent or revoked.** A token account holds exactly one delegate and one delegated amount at a time; a new `approve` call replaces the old one. [Solana Cookbook: Approve Delegate](https://solana.com/developers/cookbook/tokens/approve-token-delegate)

2. **The approval is silent and durable — it does not expire, and revoking it is not something most wallets surface as a routine action.** Once granted, it's described in guides as remaining valid "indefinitely until you proactively revoke it." Phantom does not ship a dedicated delegate-revocation UI in its own settings; users are pointed to the CLI or third-party revoke tools. [Panda Academy: SPL Token Approvals & Security](https://academy.pandatool.org/en_US/solana/2315), [TokenToolHub: Solana Token Approvals and Revokes](https://tokentoolhub.com/solana-token-approvals-and-revokes/)

3. **Token-2022's `PermanentDelegate` extension is a mint-level authority that can transfer or burn *any* holder's tokens for that mint without their signature, and — critically — token account owners cannot revoke it.** This is stronger than a standard delegate: it isn't granted per-holder, it's baked into the mint itself and it's permanent by name. [Solana Docs: Permanent Delegate](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/permanent-delegate)

4. **This exact extension is, right now, the mechanism behind a live, ongoing scam pattern on Solana: attackers mint a token with `PermanentDelegate` set, let victims buy it, then burn it out of the victim's wallet seconds later.** Reported by Cointelegraph (syndicated via TradingView) on **3 September 2024** — the original brief dated this to September 2026, which is wrong by two years; corroborating syndication is dated 2024-09-04 ([Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/2024-09-04-scammers-exploit-solana-token-extension-to-burn-users-crypto-13074543469010)). The source is Slorg of Jupiter's Core Working Group, describing a token ("RED") whose buyers had their balance burned seven seconds after the swap. Corroborated independently by RugCheck.xyz and Jupiter, both of which display specific on-screen indicators when a token carries the extension. Numbers beyond "an active pattern" (e.g. "40% of new tokens," "$50M in Q1 2026") appear only in lower-tier aggregator/SEO sites (coinpaper, altcoinbuzz, bitdegree, yellow, cryptonews, voiceofcrypto) that all trace to one unreachable dev.to post — **treat those specific figures as unverified**, but treat "RugCheck and Jupiter both now flag PermanentDelegate as a named, watched risk category" as solid, since it's corroborated by the primary TradingView/Cointelegraph piece and independently by scanner documentation. [TradingView/Cointelegraph, Sept 2026](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:13ab18ce1094b:0-scammers-have-found-a-way-to-burn-tokens-from-inside-solana-wallets/)

5. **Jupiter, Raydium, and Orca all support trading Token-2022 tokens including most extensions — they do not blanket-block permanent-delegate tokens — but Jupiter specifically renders a UI warning/indicator when the extension is present**, i.e. the market's response so far is disclosure, not exclusion. I could not verify a specific DEX or aggregator that outright refuses to list a PermanentDelegate token. [Chainstack: Token Extensions overview](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-token-extensions), [TradingView/Cointelegraph](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:13ab18ce1094b:0-scammers-have-found-a-way-to-burn-tokens-from-inside-solana-wallets/)

6. **Risk scanners (RugCheck, and by description GoPlus, Solscan-adjacent tools) treat mint authority, freeze authority, and delegate-style powers as first-class red flags shown to every prospective buyer before they trade**, alongside LP-lock status and holder concentration. An active mint or freeze authority is described in scanner guidance as "the clearest possible signal the team can rug at any time." ENC's mint authority (a program PDA, non-upgradeable) and freeze authority (None) will read *clean* on these axes — but a permanent delegate on the NFTs and a live SPL delegate over holders' coin balances will not. [StakePoint / Rugcheck summaries](https://stakepoint.app/blog/solana-token-safety-scanner), [RugCheck.xyz](https://rugcheck.xyz/)

7. **Real-world clawback precedent is real and large: as of early 2026, Tether has blacklisted roughly 10,000 addresses holding over $5B, freezing 4,163 addresses / $1.26B in 2025 alone; Circle has blacklisted around 370 addresses totaling ~$109M.** These are centralized-issuer freeze functions (not the same primitive as Token-2022 permanent delegate, but the same *shape of power* — an issuer that can act on your balance without your signature). [Spark: Stablecoin Blacklisting research](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-blacklisting-sanctions-mechanics), [Crypto Trace Labs: Can Tether Freeze Stolen USDT](https://cryptotracelabs.com/blog/can-tether-freeze-stolen-usdt-how-stablecoin-blacklists-work-2026-guide/)

8. **The Drift Protocol hack (~$285M, April 1 2026) is the sharpest recent case study in how the market reacts when an issuer *has* clawback power and visibly hesitates to use it.** Attackers bridged ~$232M in stolen funds to USDC and moved it over 6–8 hours in business-hours daylight; Circle did not freeze it, later stating freezes happen only on law-enforcement/court direction, not unilaterally during an active hack. The result was a 100+-plaintiff class-action lawsuit and sustained public anger — not at the *existence* of freeze power, but at its *inconsistent use*. Multiple corroborating sources: Yahoo Finance, DailyCoin, CCN, CoinDesk, Fortune, CryptoSlate (all April 2026). [CoinDesk, Apr 3 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/03/circle-under-fire-after-usd285-million-drift-hack-over-inaction-to-freeze-stolen-usdc)

9. **Session keys — short-lived, narrowly-scoped keypairs used mainly in Solana gaming to avoid per-action signing — are the standard alternative to a standing delegate, but they solve a different problem (many small in-app actions) rather than a long-running recurring debit.** [Solana Docs: Auto-approve/session keys](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/auto-approve)

10. **Streamflow is a live, funded Solana protocol built specifically for the problem ENC has (recurring/streamed payments) via escrow, not standing delegation: the payer deposits into a program-controlled stream/escrow account up front, and tokens flow out of *that account* at a defined rate — the payer's main wallet balance is never subject to a delegate.** It already supports payroll-style recurring payouts and OTC escrow. [Streamflow: Payments](https://streamflow.finance/payments), [Streamflow: About](https://streamflow.finance/about)

## Precedents

| Project / pattern | Mechanism | What happened | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tether (USDT) | Issuer blacklist/freeze on any address | ~10,000 addresses frozen, $5B+, ongoing since 2017; treated as routine compliance tooling | A freeze power that's used narrowly, disclosed, and consistently becomes background noise, not a scandal |
| Circle (USDC) / Drift hack | Issuer freeze, discretionary | $232M stolen USDC left unfrozen for hours during an active hack; class-action lawsuit, PR crisis | The scandal was inconsistency and opacity in *when* the power gets used, not the power's mere existence |
| Token-2022 `PermanentDelegate` scam tokens | Mint-level burn/transfer authority, no holder consent, no revoke | Live scam pattern: tokens burned from buyers' wallets seconds after purchase; RugCheck + Jupiter now flag the extension by name | A permanent, unrevokable delegate is now a **named, watched attack signature** on Solana — carrying it invites exactly that scanner flag regardless of your actual intent |
| Wallet drainers (generic `approve` phishing) | Malicious delegate/transfer approval signed by the victim | $87M+ drained in Q2 2025 alone via phishing; Solana drains skew toward direct-transfer phishing more than allowance abuse (unlike Ethereum) | The market has been trained hard to distrust *any* "approve this program to move your tokens" prompt, on sight |
| Streamflow | Escrow/stream account, not a standing delegate | Operating protocol for payroll/recurring payments on Solana | Proves the "recurring payment without a standing delegate" pattern is buildable and already has production tooling on this exact chain |

## What this means for ENC

**→ A live, unrevokable permanent delegate on the ten NFTs is the single highest-risk piece of the current design, independent of intent.** It is not a hypothetical bad look — it is, right now, the specific mechanism powering an active Solana scam pattern that RugCheck and Jupiter both display a named warning for. Any buyer who runs the NFT mint through RugCheck before buying sees the same flag a scam token gets.

**→ An unlimited (or `u64::MAX`) standing SPL delegate over a holder's whole ENC balance, granted once at purchase, reads to any scanner and any semi-informed buyer exactly like the approval step of a wallet-drainer phishing flow — because mechanically it is the same primitive.** The fact that ENC's version is disclosed and permissionless doesn't change what the wallet prompt looks like at signing time; Solana users have specifically been trained to fear this exact prompt.

**→ "No admin key, non-upgradeable, freeze authority None" — the properties ENC is proud of — will not save it on scanners that also check delegated-balance and permanent-delegate flags separately.** The Drift/Circle episode shows the market cares less about "can the issuer act" than "how transparent and bounded is that power" — ENC's rent-delegate and NFT-permanent-delegate need the same bounded, legible framing (fixed rate, on-chain-verifiable formula, no discretion) or they get read as a Tether-style backstop with none of Tether's decade of track record.

**→ A bounded, capped delegate amount (e.g. approve exactly N days of rent at a time, or a hard ceiling well below the full balance) is a small code change that meaningfully changes the story: it stops being "the treasury can drain your wallet" and becomes "the treasury can collect what you already owe, and no more."** This is the cheapest lever available and doesn't touch the core M2-peg mechanic at all.

**→ Streamflow-style escrow (holder deposits a rent-prepay balance into a program-owned account, rent is drawn only from that account) removes the standing-delegate-over-your-main-balance problem entirely, at the cost of an extra prepay/top-up UX step.** This is the closest existing Solana pattern to "pull recurring payments without an unlimited approval," and it's already live infrastructure, not a novel primitive ENC would need to invent and get audited from scratch.

**→ Permanent delegate on the NFTs specifically should be reconsidered or very loudly disclosed at point of mint, because "the program can take your NFT without your signature" is indistinguishable, to a scanner, from the exact scam pattern currently degrading trust in Token-2022 on Solana.** If the foreclosure mechanic needs a way to reclaim a defaulted NFT, a program-owned escrow custody model (the NFT sits in a vault-controlled account by design, not "your wallet, but we can reach into it") reads very differently even though it achieves a similar practical outcome.

**→ The underlying primitive (permanent delegate + unlimited approval) has a documented history as a theft vector, and the scanner response to it is settled rather than fresh.** *Corrected 2026-08-12:* the burn-after-buy episode dates to September **2024**, not 2026 — so this is not a story breaking around us. That cuts both ways. It is not a news cycle ENC would be walking into, but it does mean RugCheck's and Jupiter's flags on the extension are two years established and routine, so a buyer meets a mature, unambiguous warning rather than an unfamiliar one.

## Open questions

- Should the rent-collection delegate be capped (e.g. periodic re-approval, or a fixed ceiling) instead of unlimited — a human product decision, not just an engineering one, since it changes the UX (how often does a holder need to re-approve?).
- Should the NFTs move to a deposit/escrow custody model instead of permanent delegate — this is a real architecture choice with rent-foreclosure implications the team needs to decide, not something this brief can resolve alone.
- Does BadCode want to proactively label the delegate/permanent-delegate mechanics in-app (a "why we need this" explainer at the approval prompt) to get ahead of the scanner-flag optics, the way the joke itself already leans into radical transparency elsewhere in the design?
- Is there appetite to prototype the Streamflow-style escrow alternative before committing to permanent delegate, given it's existing audited infrastructure on Solana already?

## Confidence

**High confidence:** how `approve`/`approve_checked` and `PermanentDelegate` work mechanically (primary Solana docs); that RugCheck and Jupiter both flag `PermanentDelegate` tokens as a named risk category as of late 2026; that Tether/Circle blacklist scale figures (~10,000 / $5B and ~370 / $109M respectively) and the Drift Protocol hack timeline and dollar figures are real and multiply-corroborated; that Streamflow is a live, operating protocol offering escrow-based recurring payments on Solana today.

**Lower confidence / flagged unverified:** the specific "40% of new tokens" and "$50M+ Q1 2026 losses" figures for the permanent-delegate burn scam — these appear only in syndicated aggregator content tracing to one inaccessible source and should not be quoted as fact without a better primary source; whether any major DEX/aggregator outright *blocks* (rather than just flags) permanent-delegate tokens — I found disclosure/warning behavior but no confirmed hard block; exact current Phantom/Solflare/Backpack approval-signing screen wording — I could not retrieve actual current screenshots, only secondary descriptions that such warnings exist.
