# Distribution, Liquidity, and Price Formation for ENC

**Question:** Where does an ENC price come from at all — how does the token reach people, and what does its market look like given ~22B tokens at genesis, 100% treasury-held, with no BadCode allocation and no promised liquidity?

## What I found

1. **Solana's dominant 2025-2026 launch mechanism is the bonding curve, and it works by the deployer/protocol selling supply into the curve.** Pump.fun (2025-2026 activity) and Meteora's Dynamic Bonding Curve (DBC, actively updated through July 2026) both start a token at a virtual price and let buyers push it up a curve until a liquidity threshold is hit, at which point the pool "graduates" to a full AMM (PumpSwap or Meteora DAMM). [Meteora DBC docs](https://docs.meteora.ag/overview/products/dbc/what-is-dbc), [What Is Meteora Crypto — DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/what-is-meteora-dlmm-dynamic-bonding-curve-2026). This mechanism *requires* someone to be selling newly-issued supply for SOL to seed the pool — it structurally conflicts with "the treasury never sells."

2. **Graduation is rare and most launches die.** On pump.fun, roughly **1.4% of launched tokens graduate** to a real AMM pool ([Laika Labs pump.fun guide](https://laikalabs.ai/market-intelligence/pump-fun-tokenomics-guide)). A Solidus Labs study of **7 million+ pump.fun tokens deployed Jan 2024–Mar 2025** (each with ≥5 trades) found only ~97,000 ever held more than $1,000 of liquidity — **98.6% collapsed into worthless pump-and-dumps**, and 12 wallet clusters were responsible for ~20% of all launches and 82% of liquidity drains ([CoinDesk, May 2025](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says), [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-are-rug-pulls-or-fraud-report/)). This is the default outcome for a permissionless Solana launch — recent (2025) and large-sample, not a stale or cherry-picked stat.

3. **"Fair launch" mechanisms exist and are actively used, but don't guarantee price stability.** Balancer-style Liquidity Bootstrapping Pools (LBPs — a Dutch-auction pool that starts token-heavy and drifts to reserve-heavy, deterring snipers) are the standard no-presale precedent ([Balancer LBP primer](https://medium.com/balancer-protocol/a-primer-on-fair-token-launches-and-liquidity-bootstrapping-pools-11bab5ff33a2)). Fjord Foundry runs these on Solana-adjacent chains today; even its own token's 2024 LBP opened at $5.36 and closed at $2.32 in the same sale ([CryptoTimes, Apr 2024](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2024/04/20/fjord-foundry-surpasses-15-million-in-token-pre-sale/)) — fair distribution mechanics still produce large intra-launch price swings. On Solana specifically, **Believe (LAUNCHCOIN, April 2025)** is the current "100% fair-launch, no presale, no team allocation" precedent closest to ENC's ethos ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-is-believe-token-launchpad)) — but it still launches via a curve that sells supply, unlike ENC.

4. **Protocol-owned liquidity, when the protocol itself is both issuer and dominant holder, has already failed once at scale.** OlympusDAO (2021) held **99.5% of its own liquidity** via bonding, paying APYs up to 4,040% funded by continuous new bond sales. When bond demand slowed, the treasury's backing thesis reflexively unwound: OHM fell from an ATH of **$1,334.36 (Nov 2021) to $9.06 (Sep 2022)** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/90146/token-behind-defi-project-olympusdao-falls-crypto-crash), [Keone Hon's writeup](https://keonehd.medium.com/elucidating-the-financial-mechanics-of-olympusdao-eebab7a1502b)). The lesson isn't "protocol-owned supply is bad" — it's that a treasury which is both the sole liquidity provider *and* the counterparty in every trade produces a market with no independent price signal, which is fragile under any stress.

5. **Faucet/airdrop-distributed tokens get dumped, consistently and fast.** A study of 2M+ airdrop addresses found **~64% sell immediately** at the distribution event; 50–70% of airdropped tokens are gone within 30 days; Delphi found dump rates run 4–11 points higher again by day 90 ([BeInCrypto on Delphi](https://beincrypto.com/delphi-airdrops-over-wallet-dump-rate/), [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/when-to-sell-airdrops)). A 2026 Keyrock study found **88% of tokens launched alongside an airdrop have declined in price**, with most of the crash landing in the first 15 days ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/snapshot/keyrock-study-says-most-token-airdrops-crash-after-launch/)). The one consistent mitigating factor cited across sources: recipients hold when the token has a **use case beyond resale** — which is relevant to ENC because the faucet's actual use case ("buy assets that are guaranteed to rise") is a real, distinct claim from most zero-utility airdrops.

6. **A closed-loop currency with no open market is a legitimate, working precedent, not a compromise.** Roblox's Robux and Fortnite's V-Bucks have no sanctioned secondary market at all — value is purely internal (buy cosmetics/access), real-money trading is a ToS violation policed as fraud, and the systems function at enormous scale regardless ([screenwiseapp guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/v-bucks-vs-robux-the-ultimate-in-game-currency-guide), [securityscientist.net](https://www.securityscientist.net/blog/12-questions-and-answers-about-roblox-cross-trading-ban-risk/)). Separately, MMO gold economies (WoW, pre-patch Diablo III) show that a currency purely governed by faucet/sink balance, with no dollar price at all, still needs deliberate design — Diablo III's early gold sinks were too weak relative to faucets and gold inflated until items were unaffordable for new players ([hellozum.com](https://hellozum.com/an-analysis-of-wows-economy-gold-sinks-and-inflation/)) — the failure mode without a market price is *internal* inflation, not price collapse.

## Precedents

| Project | Mechanism | What happened | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pump.fun launches | Bonding curve, deployer sells into curve | 98.6% of 7M+ tokens never reach real liquidity ([Solidus/CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says)) | Permissionless launch ≠ a market; most tokens never get one |
| Balancer LBP (Fjord Foundry, FJO) | Dutch-auction pool, token-heavy → reserve-heavy | Raised $24M but price fell 57% within the sale itself ([CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2024/04/20/fjord-foundry-surpasses-15-million-in-token-pre-sale/)) | Fair-launch mechanics fix *allocation fairness*, not *price stability* |
| OlympusDAO (OHM) | Protocol owns ~99.5% of own liquidity via bonding | ATH $1,334 → $9.06 in 10 months ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/90146/token-behind-defi-project-olympusdao-falls-crypto-crash)) | Treasury-as-sole-counterparty markets are reflexive and fragile |
| Airdrops broadly (2M+ wallets studied) | Free distribution, no purchase | ~64% sold immediately, 50-70% gone in 30 days ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/when-to-sell-airdrops)) | Free tokens with no non-resale use case get dumped, near-universally |
| Roblox Robux / Fortnite V-Bucks | No sanctioned secondary market | Functions at massive scale with zero open price | A market price is optional if the currency's only sanctioned use is internal spend |

## What this means for ENC

**→ ENC's genesis design (no allocation, no admin key, no presale) already matches the "fairest" precedent available (Believe/LAUNCHCOIN, LBPs) — but the design has no launch mechanism at all**, and every real launch mechanism on Solana today (pump.fun, DBC, LBP) works by having *someone* sell newly-issued supply into a pool. If BadCode seeds no pool and sells nothing, there is no mechanism by which ENC gets an initial price — it just sits inert in the treasury until the faucet or an asset sale puts a few tokens in outside hands.

**→ If nobody ever creates a pool, ENC has no market price and doesn't need one** — the Robux/V-Bucks precedent shows a purely closed-loop currency (spend to unlock assets, no resale) works fine at scale. This is arguably the safer design for the satire: no price chart to be judged against, no "did I get rugged" discourse, the joke lives entirely in the on-chain asset-price mechanism and a dashboard, not in a tradeable spot price.

**→ If a market DOES form (and on Solana, someone eventually will spin up a pool for almost any token that has any attention), the float feeding it will be tiny and dump-prone.** The only ENC that ever leaves the treasury is faucet emissions (small, and paying nothing below 50% vault share) plus whatever asset sellers receive. Airdrop research says 50-88% of faucet-style distributions get sold fast. A pool built on that thin, faucet-sourced float is easy to move with pocket change in either direction — good for "don't take this as an investment," bad for "we didn't rug anyone," because thin liquidity gets *read* as manipulation even when it's just math.

**→ The treasury is structurally the OlympusDAO failure mode, not just adjacent to it.** It receives 100% of new mint AND all rent AND all foreclosed NFTs, while its only outflow (the faucet) is throttled to zero below 50% vault share. Over time the treasury's share of total ENC only grows — it becomes the dominant, thinly-traded whale in its own market, exactly the setup that broke OHM. Unlike OHM, ENC's treasury makes no backing promise, so the failure mode isn't a price collapse people feel entitled to be angry about — but it is worth naming explicitly rather than discovering by accident.

**→ A bonding-curve or LBP launch is not free — it requires selling treasury-owned supply, which the current "treasury never sells, no admin discretion" design rules out.** If the team wants a market price on day one, that is a real, distinct decision (how many ENC, sold how, by whom) that hasn't been made yet — it can't be bolted on later without either an allocation carve-out or accepting that price formation is emergent and uncontrolled.

**→ The faucet is the one distribution channel BadCode does control, and airdrop research says its recipients will sell fast if there's nothing else to do with ENC.** The mitigating factor across every source is a non-resale use case — which ENC actually has (buy assets). Whether that's enough to beat the ~64-88% dump base rate is unverified and untestable until there's an actual market.

## Open questions

- Does BadCode want ENC to have an open market price at all, or is the closed-loop (Robux-style) framing actually closer to the intended joke?
- If a market is wanted: who provides the first liquidity, with what ENC (there is no allocation to draw from without amending the "treasury never sells" principle), and is that one-time exception disclosed?
- If no market is wanted: is there a technical or social lever to *discourage* a spontaneous third-party pool (there mostly isn't, on a permissionless chain) — or does the design just accept that someone, eventually, will list it?
- Is the growing treasury share of total supply (the OHM-shaped risk) something worth capping or redistributing over time, or is it acceptable because no backing promise is being made?

## Confidence

**Confident:** the pump.fun/Solidus graduation and rug-rate figures (large, recent, named sample); the OlympusDAO history and price figures; the airdrop dump-rate figures (multiple converging studies); that bonding-curve/LBP mechanisms require selling treasury supply, which conflicts with ENC's current no-sale principle; that Robux/V-Bucks are real, working closed-loop precedents.

**Less confident / unverified:** exact current TVL or volume figures for Meteora DBC or Jupiter Studio specifically (fast-moving, no authoritative number found); whether Believe/LAUNCHCOIN's "100% fair launch" claim has held up post-launch at the wallet-distribution level (only the mechanism, not outcome data, was verified); any claim about what would specifically happen to an ENC pool, since no ENC market exists yet — everything about ENC's own market is extrapolation from these precedents, not observation.
