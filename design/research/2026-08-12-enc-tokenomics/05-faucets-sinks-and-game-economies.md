# Faucets, Sinks, and Game Economies — what real virtual economies teach ENC

**Question:** With a per-epoch faucet pot, a sybil-open welcome grant, ten scarce items, and continuous ~5%/day rent as the only sink, what steady state does game-economy precedent predict for ENC — and is the design's own bet (dormancy is likely, rent forces turnover) supported by the evidence?

## What I found

1. **EVE Online runs faucets/sinks as a managed instrument, not a set-and-forget rule.** CCP employs a chief economist (Dr. Eyjólfur Guðmundsson) and publishes a Monthly Economic Report (MER) naming every faucet (bounties, mission rewards, NPC buy orders) and sink (transaction tax, broker fees, ship insurance, the "asset safety" sink) with month-over-month deltas. November 2025: total money supply 2,614.5 trillion ISK, ~1.1 quadrillion ISK spent in the month (+15% YoY). April 2024: transaction tax alone removed 35.7T ISK; tax + broker fees together, 47T. This is a live-tuned economy with a dedicated team watching the numbers monthly — a standard ENC cannot replicate (no admin key, no economist). [tagn.wordpress.com](https://tagn.wordpress.com/2026/01/12/the-december-2025-eve-online-monthly-economic-report-and-a-summary-of-overall-destruction-in-2025/), [nosygamer.blogspot.com](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2025/12/eve-onlines-november-2025-monthly.html), [eveonline.com](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-april-2026)

2. **Ultima Online's classic failure was a *sink* shortage, not a faucet glut — and it was driven by hoarding, not inflation.** Raph Koster and Zach Simpson's contemporaneous analysis found players banked and hoarded raw materials (wool, ore) rather than circulating them; the game literally ran out of sheep to spawn more wool because none was being consumed. The fix that emerged — the "faucet-drain" framing itself — was to add more *drains* (sinks), not fewer faucets. This is the origin of "faucet/sink" as the standard vocabulary. 2003-era analysis, still cited as canonical. [raphkoster.com](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/did-players-destroy-the-uo-ecology/), [dergigi.com PDF](https://dergigi.com/assets/files/UO-Economics.pdf)

3. **"Mudflation" is the accepted term, and it means item devaluation from power creep, not currency inflation per se.** Coined on Usenet as early as April 1993, popularized during EverQuest's Kunark expansion (2000): new tiers of gear make old "god-tier" loot worthless, independent of the money supply. Standard remedy in practice is item sinks (repair costs, decay, obsolescence by design) plus periodically retiring old content's rewards — not just currency taxes. [muds.fandom.com](https://muds.fandom.com/wiki/Mudflation)

4. **RuneScape's worst inflation events were duplication bugs, not faucet design, and they left permanent scars.** The 7 Nov 2003 RuneScape Classic dupe generated an estimated 110 billion gold in a single day; the long-run legacy is that "party hats," once ultra-rare, are permanently and structurally cheapened as a class. Old School RuneScape suffered a comparable incident in 2018 (a patch bug handed "countless" players max cash stacks; Jagex pulled the game offline ~20 minutes later). Lesson: a single exploit can do more damage than years of faucet/sink mistuning, and the damage (mispriced items) doesn't fully heal. [pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/old-school-runescape-pulled-offline-as-billions-of-gold-appear-out-of-nowhere/), [runescape.fandom.com](https://runescape.fandom.com/wiki/Duplication_glitch)

5. **Play-to-earn economies collapse when the earned token has an open market price and the only sink can't absorb faucet volume.** Axie Infinity's SLP is uncapped supply with breeding as the only real sink; emissions from millions of active players vastly outran breeding demand. SLP fell from $0.39 (Jul 2021) to under $0.004 (mid-2022); daily active players fell from 2.7M to 250K after Feb 2022; cheapest-Axie floor price fell to ~$30 in Jan 2022 as holders dumped. Sky Mavis's Season 20 update cut daily SLP emissions by 56%, but too late to stop the collapse. StepN's GST fell 98% in two months by June 2022 (GMT −81% from ATH); a July 2022 China ban triggered a 74% crash and analysts flagged the classic "death spiral" pattern. Both are ~2021–2022 events, well-documented, not stale in mechanism even if old in date. [coindesk.com](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2022/02/08/axie-infinity-reduces-slp-emissions-to-prevent-collapse), [axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2022/04/12/fall-of-play-to-earn-gaming-p2e-axie-infinity-slp), [cryptoslate.com](https://cryptoslate.com/is-move-to-earn-dead-stepns-gst-token-tumbles-98-in-2-months/)

6. **Diablo III's real-money auction house was shut down (Mar 2014) because it undermined the core reward loop, not because of fraud.** Blizzard's own retrospective: letting players buy best-in-slot items with real money made grinding for loot pointless, "short-circuiting" the game's actual point. It stayed live past that realization only because the feature was printed on the retail box (legal/promise reasons). This is the closest precedent to "a mechanism that's individually sound but kills the reason people play." [gamedeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/why-diablo-3-shut-down-the-auction-house)

7. **Airdrops with no identity check are heavily captured by sybils, even with active mitigation.** A sybil-analysis of the Arbitrum airdrop identified ~148,595 sybil addresses plus 279,328 same-person addresses, together receiving roughly **48% of total distributed tokens** — despite Arbitrum having collaborated with Nansen specifically to filter sybils before distribution. Hop Protocol ran an explicit sybil-detection/disqualification process. These figures come from a third-party analytics writeup (X-explore), not the protocols' own audited numbers — treat as indicative, not exact. 2023-era data. [xangle.io](https://xangle.io/en/research/detail/1192), [github.com/hop-protocol](https://github.com/hop-protocol/hop-airdrop/issues/367)

## Precedents

| Project | Mechanism | What happened | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ultima Online (1997) | Fixed resource pool, weak sinks | Hoarding, resource "banks" ran dry, deflation-by-hoarding | Faucets aren't the risk if there's nowhere for value to leave — sinks are |
| EVE Online (ongoing) | Actively-managed, published faucets/sinks | Stable multi-decade economy, quadrillions ISK, monthly public tuning | Requires an economist and a live dial — ENC has neither |
| RuneScape (2003, 2018) | Duplication exploits | 110B gold in a day; permanent item mispricing | One bug outweighs years of faucet/sink design |
| Axie Infinity (2021–22) | Uncapped SLP, breeding-only sink | SLP −99%, DAUs 2.7M→250K | An earned token with a market price + weak sink = death spiral |
| StepN (2022) | GST/GMT dual-token move-to-earn | GST −98% in 2 months | Same pattern, faster, plus a regulatory shock (China ban) |
| Diablo III RMAH (2012–14) | Real-money item trading | Shut down: killed the core loop | A "sound" mechanism can still be the wrong mechanism for the game's point |
| Arbitrum airdrop (2023) | No-identity claim, ~150K addresses flagged | ~48% of tokens went to sybils/same-person wallets | Any faucet without identity is majority-captured by farmers |

## What this means for ENC

**→ The welcome grant will be farmed, not organically claimed.** With zero identity system, the Arbitrum precedent (~48% sybil capture *with* active filtering) is a floor, not a ceiling, for a design with *no* filtering at all. Assume most of the welcome-grant faucet goes to script wallets, not new genuine holders.

**→ ENC's real risk profile is closer to Ultima Online than EVE or Axie.** It has no continuous item faucet (only ten fixed assets) and a currency faucet tied to M2, not to play activity — so the "too much money chasing too many faucets" collapse mode (Axie, StepN) doesn't map cleanly. The nearer risk is UO's: value gets stuck (hoarded in a handful of wallets or in the vault after foreclosure) with nothing pulling it back into circulation.

**→ 5%/day rent (full value every ~20 days) has no comparable in anything found here.** WoW's gold sinks (repair bills, mount training) and EVE's sinks (tax, broker fees, insurance) are single-digit-percent, transactional, or episodic — never a continuous double-digit-percent-of-asset-value monthly drain on a *held* item. This isn't "supported by precedent" or "refuted by precedent" — it's simply untested at this magnitude in any economy reviewed. Flag as a real unknown, not a validated design choice.

**→ The Diablo III lesson is the sharpest warning, not the airdrop stats.** RMAH wasn't shut down for being exploited — it was shut down because it worked exactly as designed and that undermined the point of playing. Force-buy-at-listed-price plus near-24%-a-month rent could do the same to ENC: technically coherent, but it may turn "hold ENC to buy assets" into "assets are unownable, so nobody bothers," which guts the joke's demonstration (assets beat cash) rather than proving it.

**→ Dormancy is the most evidence-consistent outcome, but the "rent forces turnover" fix is unproven.** No game reviewed pairs *forced sale + continuous high-rate rent* on a small, fixed, non-replenishing item set — this is a genuinely novel combination, not a known-working pattern borrowed from elsewhere.

**→ A single dupe/exploit is a bigger tail risk than the tuned rate.** RuneScape's 2003 and 2018 incidents show mispricing from one bug outlasts years of faucet/sink tuning; the vault/mint PDA and permissionless `sync_m2` should be treated as the highest-value bug-bounty target, since ENC has no admin key to patch price after the fact.

## Open questions

A human must decide: **(1)** whether the welcome grant is worth keeping at all given predictable sybil capture, or should be cut/shrunk since it can't be defended without identity; **(2)** whether ~5%/day rent is a deliberate abandonment-machine (acceptable if dormancy *is* the joke) or needs empirical tuning against actual holder behavior once live; **(3)** whether dormancy of the ten assets is a feature (a monument to an idle emperor's cabinet) or a bug that kills engagement — this is a creative call, not an economic one, and the numbers can't answer it.

## Confidence

High confidence on the historical episodes with named primary/near-primary sources: EVE MER data, Axie SLP price collapse and DAU figures, StepN GST/GMT drawdowns, the Diablo III RMAH shutdown rationale, and the Ultima Online hoarding analysis. Medium confidence on the Arbitrum sybil percentage — sourced from a third-party analytics writeup, not Arbitrum's own audited disclosure, so treat the 48% as indicative order-of-magnitude, not exact. Lowest confidence, and flagged explicitly as such: any claim about what 5%/day rent "should" do to ENC specifically — no comparable game mechanic at that rate was found, so that assessment is extrapolation from adjacent (much gentler) sink designs, not evidence.
