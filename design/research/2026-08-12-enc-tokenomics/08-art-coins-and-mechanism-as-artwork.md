# Art coins and mechanism-as-artwork: closest precedents for ENC

**Question:** What can art projects and joke coins where the mechanism IS the artwork tell us about reception — what made audiences feel the joke land, and what made them feel cheated?

## What I found

1. **Damien Hirst's "The Currency" (2021–2022)** forced a binary economic choice on 10,000 buyers: keep the NFT or exchange it for the physical dot-painting, never both. On the 27 July 2022 deadline, **5,149 chose the physical work, 4,851 chose the NFT** — the physical works tied to the NFT choice were then burned, 1,000 in a public event at Newport Street Gallery in October 2022. [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/damien-hirst-the-currency-results-2155325), [FAD Magazine](https://fadmagazine.com/2022/07/27/damien-hirst-to-burn-4851-artworks-from-the-currency-at-nft-exhibition/), [designboom](https://www.designboom.com/art/damien-hirst-destroys-1000-physical-artworks-the-currency-10-13-2022/). Artnet's own framing: a "resounding preference for traditional art" — the audience's economic *choice itself* became the reviewed artwork, which is the mechanism-as-art move ENC is attempting.

2. **The Currency's reception split cleanly into two channels**, and only one was about the mechanism. Frieze's review engaged the concept seriously but criticized Hirst for not addressing blockchain's environmental cost and called the whole thing "the low water mark in a career built on headlines and brass neck" — critique of *taste and integrity*, not of the mechanism being unfair. [Frieze](https://www.frieze.com/article/damien-hirst-the-currency-2022-review). Separately, a **provenance scandal** broke: at least 1,000 works marketed as painted in 2016 were actually made in 2018–2019, which is what actually triggered "fraud" language in press — a factual misrepresentation, unrelated to the forced-choice mechanic itself. [Surface](https://www.surfacemag.com/articles/damien-hirst-currency-paintings-dates/). **Lesson for ENC: audiences will accept a harsh, binary, artist-designed mechanism as art. What burns trust is a lie about facts, not the rules of the game.**

3. **Nouns DAO** (one Noun auctioned every 24 hours, forever, 100% of ETH to treasury) is the most durable non-predatory mechanism-as-treasury precedent, running since 2021. As of early 2026 the treasury holds **~9,200 ETH (~$31M)**, daily auctions settle around **0.4–0.6 ETH**, and roughly **18% of holders vote** in a typical governance round. [DAOtimes](https://daotimes.com/what-is-nouns-dao-governance-current-state-and-tokenomics-explained/), [Delphi Digital](https://members.delphidigital.io/projects/nouns-dao). But it wasn't friction-free: in **September–November 2023**, disgruntled holders used a new "fork" exit right to pull roughly **$27M** (about half the treasury at the time) out over anger at frivolous spending (a Rose Parade float, $90K on naming rights for a frog species). Three forks in three months cut treasury assets from ~$50M to ~$22M, and the actors who triggered it were largely **arbitrageurs buying Nouns below book value to bet on the exit payout**, not idealists. [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/197400/nouns-fork-disgruntled-nft-holders-exit-27-million-from-treasury), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2023/09/21/nouns-daos-27m-revolt-reveals-toxic-mix-of-money-hungry-traders-and-blockchain-idealists). **Lesson: any built-in exit-with-payout right will be arbitraged by people who never cared about the art — plan for that as an economic actor, not a bad-faith outlier.**

4. **ConstitutionDAO (Nov 2021)** raised ~$47M from **17,437 wallets** to bid on a copy of the US Constitution, lost the Sotheby's auction to Ken Griffin ($43.2M), and had to refund everyone. Refunds were badly designed for Ethereum L1 gas costs: total fees across contributions and refunds ran to roughly **$1–2.5M combined**, and contributors who gave small amounts sometimes lost most or all of their donation to gas just getting it back. [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/nfts-and-web3/constitutiondao-refunds-gas-fees), [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/constitutiondao-aftermath-everyone-very-mad-confused-losing-lots-of-money-fighting-crying-etc/). The organizers then **flip-flopped on whether the leftover PEOPLE token had any purpose** (floating a new "WTP" token, then reversing), which cratered trust and read as chaotic rather than as art. [Coinchapter](https://coinchapter.com/constitutiondao-floats-a-new-token-wtp-giving-governance-rights-to-nothing/). **Lesson: the exit path is part of the artwork's design and needs to be cheap and decided in advance — a good cause with a badly engineered unwind still reads as a mess, and vacillating publicly about what a token means afterward is the single fastest way to torch goodwill.**

5. **Small self-referential/tokenized-artist experiments hold up better than big spectacle pieces.** Simon de la Rouviere's **"This Artwork Is Always On Sale"** (2019, a Harberger-tax NFT where the owner sets their own price and pays a continuous tax that funds the artist) is still cited as a clean, honest demonstration of the mechanism: as of its v2 writeup the artist had earned **~18.4 ETH in patronage**, the piece had changed hands 3 times, and was valued at 240 ETH — a slow, legible, non-viral success. [Outland](https://outland.art/simon-de-la-rouviere-this-artwork-is-always-on-sale/), [simondlr.com](https://home.simondlr.com/posts/this-artwork-is-always-on-sale-v2). Kevin Abosch's **Forever Rose** (Feb 2018, a single ERC-20 token sold for $1M to 10 collectors, proceeds donated to CoderDojo) worked because the stated purpose (charity via absurd valuation) was congruent with the outcome — no one lost money, the "buyers" bought a stunt on purpose. [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/enterprise/coderdojo-foreverrose-gifto-kevin-abosch-cryptocurrency). Sarah Meyohas's **Bitchcoin** (2015, tokens redeemable for slices of her physical prints, later backing *Cloud of Petals*) is now read as a proto-NFT precedent precisely because it never promised speculative upside — it promised art. [Studio Meyohas](https://sarahmeyohas.com/bitchcoin/). terra0's **Flowertokens** (2018) drew "immoral"-style pushback not on financial grounds but ecological/philosophical ones (forcing nature to commodify itself); it ended without scandal when it technically broke (a MetaMask update) and flowers wilted, and the project self-reports the response as "overwhelmingly positive." [Burgh Vivant](https://burghvivant.org/2023/02/12/you-can-buy-yourself-flowertokens-carnegie-museum-of-arts-refractions-series-explains-terra0-with-talia-heiman/), [terra0 Medium](https://terra0.medium.com/when-bloom-the-end-of-flowertokens-project-archiving-and-the-end-of-trading-47d5bf1d379a).

6. **Dogecoin proves "explicitly a joke" is not immunity once real money enters.** Jackson Palmer created it in 2013 specifically to satirize crypto speculation; a decade later he is crypto's loudest insider critic, calling the industry "inherently right-wing, hyper-capitalistic technology built... to amplify the wealth of its proponents." [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonathanponciano/2021/07/14/dogecoin-cofounder-blasts-crypto-and-claims-billionaires-manipulating-markets-heres-why-he-quit-the-joke-memecoin-years-ago/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jackson-palmer-dogecoin-crytocurrency-scam-for-rich-twitter-thread/). His argument isn't that Dogecoin's joke failed to land — it's that **once a joke coin has real liquid value, "it's a joke" stops functioning as a defense** for whoever holds it or promotes it, because the financial incentives take over the meaning regardless of original intent. This is the sharpest cautionary precedent for ENC: intent doesn't survive contact with a liquid market.

7. **Rug-pull accusations in art-adjacent crypto projects that I found are concentrated in projects with concrete unmet promises, not ambiguous mechanism design.** Pixelmon ($70M+ raised, "substandard" delivered art, funds diverted to buying other NFTs instead of building the promised game) and Hay & Mayo's "Vault of Gems" (falsely claimed to be pegged to hard assets it never held) are the clearest examples, and both involved **explicit factual claims about backing or delivery that turned out false**, leading to federal fraud charges in the Hay/Mayo case. [Cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/hype-bad-art-deception-pixelmons-70m-rug-unpacked/), [web3isgoinggreat](https://www.web3isgoinggreat.com/single/hay-and-mayo-rug-pull-indictment). I found **no example** in this research of a project accused of rugging purely for having an aggressive or unusual *mechanism* that was fully disclosed and mathematically true to its own rules — every rug-pull accusation I could verify traces to a broken or false promise about backing, delivery, or control, not to the mechanism itself being harsh.

## Precedents

| project | mechanism | what happened | lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hirst, The Currency | forced binary: NFT or physical, not both, loser burned | 4,851/5,149 split; reviewed seriously as art; provenance date scandal was the actual scandal | harsh disclosed rules ≠ scandal; lying about facts = scandal |
| Nouns DAO | perpetual daily auction into treasury, exit-to-fork right | durable since 2021, $31M treasury 2026; 2023 fork drama pulled $27M via arbitrage of the exit right | any exit-with-payout gets arbitraged by non-believers |
| ConstitutionDAO | crowdfund, refund losers via gas-costly L1 mechanism | $47M raised, lost auction, refunds ate contributors' money in gas, org flip-flopped on token purpose | plan and cost the unwind path before launch; never waver publicly on what a token means |
| de la Rouviere, TAIAOS | Harberger self-assessed tax, continuous patronage | slow legible growth, 18.4 ETH to artist, no scandal | small, honestly-labeled, no promised upside = safest pattern |
| terra0 Flowertokens | tokenized living asset, self-liquidating | philosophical pushback, not financial; ended quietly, no scandal | ecological/philosophical objections ≠ financial betrayal objections |
| Dogecoin | joke currency, real market forms around it | creator now crypto's harshest critic of what it became | "it's a joke" stops protecting you once it's liquid |
| Pixelmon / Hay & Mayo | promised delivery/backing that wasn't real | federal fraud charges, community backlash | false factual claims, not mechanism harshness, is what triggers "rug" |

## What this means for ENC

**→ The forced-buy mechanic is the closest thing to a proven precedent (Hirst), and disclosed harshness held up.** ENC's force-buy-any-asset-at-listed-price with no refusal is structurally similar to Hirst's forced either/or: the audience accepted a rule that sounds brutal on paper because it was total, symmetric, and stated in advance. Keep it that way — never quietly soften it for anyone, including BadCode's own team, or the "no admin key" story breaks the moment someone gets an exception.

**→ Every liquid exit-with-payout mechanism you build will be arbitraged by people who don't care about the art (Nouns).** The faucet, and especially the foreclosure bounty on unpaid rent, are exactly this shape — a payout for triggering an event. Expect bots to farm foreclosures the moment gas/fees make it profitable, not as an edge case but as the base case. That's not a flaw to fix, but it should be *named* in the docs as expected behavior, the way Nouns eventually had to publicly own its fork mechanic being gamed.

**→ ConstitutionDAO's failure mode is the one ENC is most exposed to: the exit path, not the entry path.** Nobody in that story got mad about the crowdfund; they got mad about refunds that gas fees ate alive, and about the team wavering on what the leftover token meant. ENC doesn't have a literal refund path (there's no "give me my money back" button by design), but the equivalent risk is **rent/foreclosure edge cases and the faucet payout being unpredictable or feeling arbitrary** — those need to be as legible and pre-committed as the mint/burn math, or the same "why did I lose money to a process I didn't understand" anger lands on ENC instead.

**→ Dogecoin is the most important counter-example, and it argues against complacency, not against ENC's design.** "This is satire, don't buy it as a bet" is a framing that works at launch and decays as liquidity grows — Palmer's arc shows a joke coin's stated intent has no enforcement power once secondary markets form. ENC should assume that within months of any real trading volume, a meaningful fraction of holders will treat it exactly like a speculative asset regardless of the artist's framing, and the mechanism (not the README) is what will have to keep the joke true. This is arguably the strongest argument *against* the current design if the team's hope is that disclosure alone keeps people from feeling scammed when M2 stalls or falls and the "guaranteed to rise" story pauses.

**→ No precedent found for "rug" accusations sticking to a fully disclosed, self-consistent mechanism — but ENC's story depends on a promise (assets rise measured in ENC) that is not fully self-executing.** Hirst, Nouns, and TAIAOS all have mechanisms that are true by construction (the rules produce the outcome mechanically). ENC's core joke — "hold ENC to buy assets, they're guaranteed to rise" — is only true while M2 keeps rising and the oracle keeps functioning. If M2 ever prints a sustained decline (recession, deflationary shock), ENC's supply burns and asset ENC-prices fall too, which is the one scenario that could make ENC's disclosed mechanism *feel* like a broken promise even though it's operating exactly as designed. This gap between "the joke is guaranteed by math" and "the joke is guaranteed by a real-world variable BadCode doesn't control" is worth stress-testing explicitly, because it's the one place ENC's story diverges from the cleanest precedents (Hirst's binary, Nouns's perpetual auction) which don't depend on an external index continuing to move in one direction.

**→ Provenance/factual honesty is non-negotiable, and cheaper to get right than people assume.** Hirst's actual scandal was misdated paintings, a solvable admin problem that became a credibility crisis because it touched trust in the artist's word. ENC's equivalent exposure is anything where the "no admin key" claim turns out to have an asterisk — a forgotten upgrade authority, a multisig with residual power, an oracle job BadCode could still tamper with. Any of those, discovered later, would do to ENC what the dating scandal did to Hirst: it stops being a debate about the art and becomes a debate about whether BadCode lied.

## Open questions

A human needs to decide: **what ENC's public messaging says will happen if M2 falls for a sustained period** (this is not hypothetical — M2 has had real multi-quarter declines, e.g. 2022–2023) — is a falling-asset-price scenario framed in advance as "the joke cuts both ways" or left undiscussed until it happens live. Also worth a decision: whether the foreclosure-bounty and faucet mechanics should be explicitly documented as "yes, bots will farm this, that's fine" the way Nouns eventually had to own its fork-arbitrage dynamic, versus hoping it doesn't become a story.

## Confidence

High confidence, multiple corroborating sources: Hirst's exchange numbers (4,851/5,149), the ConstitutionDAO raise/refund story, Nouns DAO's 2023 fork/treasury numbers, Dogecoin/Palmer's public statements. Medium confidence: Nouns DAO's *current* (2026) treasury/participation figures, since search results blended cached 2024–2025 data with what claims to be "January 2026" — treat the ~$31M/9,200 ETH/18% figures as approximately right but not verified against a live on-chain query. Lower confidence / could not verify: I found no citable, sourced example of a disclosed-mechanism art-crypto project being credibly "read as a scam" purely for its mechanism (as opposed to a factual misrepresentation) — this is a genuine gap in what I could find, not a confirmed absence, and is stated as such in finding 7 rather than asserted as proven.
