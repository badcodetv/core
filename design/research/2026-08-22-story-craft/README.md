# Story-craft sweep — how to tell a story people finish

Twenty-four research briefs on what makes a story engage a reader, commissioned 2026-08-22 by
Kai as the first step of an adversarial review of *GitPush Origin Master* — and as the permanent
craft base behind the **`story-craft`** skill, the way the ChillPanic harvest sits behind
`suno-prompt`. Twenty briefs were commissioned to a layered scope (craft canon → engagement
mechanics → BadCode's own forms → BadCode's own job: persuading a lecture-allergic reader with a
sarcastic machine narrator); a critic then read all twenty and commissioned four more (21–24) for
what was missing; three under-sourced briefs (15, 17, 19 — the shared web-search budget ran out
mid-sweep) were re-run with fresh search.

**They are raw research, not house policy.** The distilled, graded, ruled layer is
[`docs/story-craft/`](../../../docs/story-craft/README.md). Read a brief for the long version of a
tool; never treat a brief's application list as a rule the toolkit didn't adopt. Every brief was
told to fetch its sources and mark anything from memory *(unverified)*; the critic's grading of
each is in [`critic-notes.md`](./critic-notes.md).

## What the critic found

The full notes are [`critic-notes.md`](./critic-notes.md). In short:

- **Gaps** — four, all commissioned (21–24). Considered and not commissioned: writing the
  working-class UK protagonist without caricature (folded into 24), British sitcom escalation,
  satire's clean failure cases, denouement length, more last-man-on-earth case studies.
- **Contradictions** — sixteen places where briefs assert opposite things (where the one direct
  address goes; transportation as goal vs risk; never state the point vs state it once; hold the
  joke vs earn the tragic turn; flat narrator vs the engine of regret; cliffhangers; plants firing
  in-release; cadence figures an order of magnitude apart; Horatian vs toothless; naming names;
  cold-open on the human vs the narrator; theme sentence first vs discovered; percentage grids).
  **All sixteen are ruled in [`docs/story-craft/principles.md`](../../../docs/story-craft/principles.md#house-rulings-on-contested-advice)** — averaged into nothing, decided, and marked overturnable.
- **Weak briefs** — 17 and 19 (built without search; re-run), 15 (sources never fetched; re-run),
  06 (series-endings section is inference), 14 (off-target on drum & bass specifically), 05 (video
  half listicle-grade), 11 (the numbers are craft-blog folklore), 13 (platform cadence is forum
  anecdote), 20 (short-form rules unverified). Set-wide: ~190 applications and ~280 diagnostics with
  the "beneficiary on-panel" rule restated sixteen times — the toolkit keeps it once, as a gate.
- **Strongest** — 08 (persuasion without sermon — peer-reviewed, on the maker's central problem),
  03 (scene craft — practitioner primary sources throughout), 16 (satire case studies — seven
  practitioner interviews fetched in full); honourable mentions 07 and 12.

## The numbers that must never be cited

Per the critic and brief 07, these are folklore or failed replications and appear in the briefs
only as things to distrust: "stories are 22× more memorable" (untraceable to Bruner); Zak's
oxytocin storytelling claims (the underlying paradigm failed replication); the Zeigarnik effect as
a memory mechanism (2025 meta-analysis: lacks universal validity — the Ovsiankina urge-to-resume is
the robust sibling); every YouTube/TikTok "first N seconds" percentage and the "Stanford study"
(no citation exists); the MrBeast production document's figures (unconfirmed provenance); webtoon
gap pixel ranges and the "drop-off at panel 20" (content-marketing blogs). Keep the shapes, drop
the digits — [`docs/story-craft/evidence.md`](../../../docs/story-craft/evidence.md) holds the graded list.

## 🔴 An operational lesson for the next sweep

**WebSearch has a per-session budget (200 searches) and twenty parallel researchers exhaust it
in the first half-hour.** Briefs 04, 09, 15–17, 19, 20 reported the budget gone mid-run; 17, 19 and
20 never got a search. The re-runs (15, 17, 19) had **no** search either — they were built from
direct `WebFetch` of known URLs (Wikiquote, authors' own sites, Talkhouse, Valve's developer
commentary, Weiland's blog), which is why 17 fetched 30 sources and 15 only 10 and deliberately
left out every title it could not reach (*Primer*, *Dark*, *The Terminator*, *La Jetée*, *Never Let
Me Go*, *Station Eleven*, *The Road*, *On the Beach*, the *1984* appendix, *The Handmaid's Tale*
notes, Briggs, the last-man tradition) rather than cite unfetched summaries. Next time: cap
searches per agent, stagger the launch, or split the sweep across sessions.

## Method

Workflow script: `story-craft-research-sweep` (20 Sonnet researchers in parallel → one critic →
up to 4 gap briefs), then `story-craft-rerun-weak-briefs` (15, 17, 19). Each researcher got the
same context block (who BadCode is, the four forms, the reader, the narrator, the flagship), a
brief-specific spec naming the primary sources to seek, and a fixed output shape (top findings ·
techniques in detail · disagreements · how this applies to BadCode · diagnostic questions ·
graded sources). 25 agents, ~3.6M tokens, 54 minutes; the re-run added three.
