# 01 — Name and logo collisions

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

The exact name "BadCode" is not crowded in music or video: the only real namesakes found are a small luxury-streetwear label (BADCODE, caution hoodies and python jeans), a YouTube handle @badcode whose content I could not read, a one-track punk song, and a TV episode. But the trademark registers were unreachable, so "clear" is unproven. The trope that namesakes lean on is bug/error/glitch-red tech humour, which is the same territory as the current curly-brace C, so the strongest move is away from code punctuation. Bad Company (drum and bass) also shows that a mirrored-letter mark can own a genre.

## Clichés to avoid

- Curly brace or angle brackets standing in for a letter (already ruled out by the founders, and it is the dominant register of every programmer-humour namesake)
- Bug, insect or 'squashed bug' icons (the natural visual pun on 'bad code')
- Red error triangle, red X, 404 or Blue Screen of Death imagery
- Glitch / RGB-split / scan-line text (used by nearly every 'bad code' meme merch design)
- Hazard / caution yellow-and-black stripes (the likely look of the BADCODE fashion brand's 'caution' line, unverified)
- Reptile / python skin patterns (the fashion brand sells python and crocodile jeans)
- Skulls (the stock 'bad' symbol; none found among namesakes but it is the default)
- Mirrored B and C glyphs around a bar (Bad Company owns this in drum and bass)
- Matrix green falling characters and binary strings

- **(medium)** Trademark registers not checked — No result from USPTO, and UK IPO, EUIPO and WIPO were not reached at all. Absence of hits in this report is not clearance.
- **(medium)** Drum and bass BC-glyph territory — Bad Company's ᑐᗴ|ᗱᑕ is an established, well-known mark for the initials BC in D&B. A symmetric BC monogram would echo it and is a bad idea for a record sleeve in the same genre.
- **(low)** Search noise from the generic phrase — 'bad code' is overwhelmingly developer slang, and Person of Interest used it as an episode title. Searchability will always be poor for the words alone, so the mark and a distinct handle carry the load.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The single red pupil on a dead-black square

**Sketch in words:** A square (or full circle for avatars) filled flat near-black, #0a0a0c. In the exact centre, one small solid red disc, #d7261e, diameter about one-eighth of the square's width. Nothing else, no outline, no ring, no highlight. Wordmark 'BADCODE' is optional and sits far below in one line of wide-tracked grotesque capitals, off-white, at about one-sixteenth of the square's height. The glyph is drawn with one stab of a marker.

**Why it fits:** Reads as the Terminator / HAL eye without copying either: no lens ring, no glow, no letter. It is the opposite of every code-punctuation, bug and glitch namesake found, and it echoes our tiny LED points of light in near-black frames. Survives favicon size.

**Weakness:** HAL 9000 and the Terminator eye are the most famous versions of this idea, and generic red-dot logos exist everywhere (record labels, cameras, security apps). It may read as 'a camera' or 'a recording light'. It has no ownable shape, so it needs the wordmark or a consistent frame to be ours.

### The dot that has replaced the O

**Sketch in words:** Set 'BADCODE' in heavy, wide capitals, white on black, but the letter O is missing: in its place stands a solid red disc the height of the caps, with a narrow gap either side. The reader still reads BADCODE. One O only, so the red dot sits mid-word as the unblinking centre.

**Why it fits:** It makes the mark into a word you can read aloud while making the eye the character. It is drawable in one stroke plus letters, and it avoids all brace/bug/glitch tropes. Works as a video ident: the dot lights up last.

**Weakness:** Replacing an O with a dot or eye is a known logo move (many brands do it), and the red dot then looks like a recording button. It does not shrink to a favicon unless we crop to just the dot, which loses the word.

### The vertical blade with a red pip

**Sketch in words:** On a black square, one thin vertical line of cold off-white (blue-white, #cfe6ff), width about one-fiftieth of the square, running from the top edge to about three-quarters of the way down, centred. At its lower end, a single small red disc, same width as three of those lines, sits exactly on the line's tip like a full stop or a lit fuse. Nothing else.

**Why it fits:** It borrows our own house register (one thin vertical blade of cold light in near-black) so it is unmistakably ours, and the red pip is the threatening machine eye. Read another way, it is an exclamation mark or a pin dropped into the world, which is the 'push' in git push. Nothing in the namesake trawl looks like it.

**Weakness:** An exclamation mark and a lit-cigarette or a map pin can all be read from it, so meaning is ambiguous. The thin line vanishes below about 32 pixels, so the favicon needs a thicker version.

### Strikethrough 'BAD' over intact 'CODE'

**Sketch in words:** Two lines, stacked, wide grotesque capitals. Top line 'BAD' in red, with a single thick horizontal red bar through its middle, like a redacted or crossed-out word (bar height about a fifth of the cap height, overshooting by a small margin on each side). Bottom line 'CODE' in white, untouched. Black ground.

**Why it fits:** The joke of the brand is that the badness is being marked up by a future editor: the future AI has struck through the mistake and left the lesson. It ties to the sarcastic, authoritative narrator and the Gazette redaction idea in the coin canon. Instantly legible as a word mark, no icon needed.

**Weakness:** Type-only, so it is less ownable and less of a 'character'. Strike-through can read as 'not bad code', or as an edit rather than a threat. Red on black at small size can fill in and blur.

### The uneven pair: one bright pixel among a dark grid

**Sketch in words:** A 3-by-3 grid of small near-black squares with hairline gaps on a black ground, so the grid is barely visible. Only the centre square is lit solid red, all others matte. Optional: the top-left square has a slight offset, one pixel out of line, as if a single bug. Drawn as nine squares with a marker, one filled in.

**Why it fits:** It says 'one point of light in a monumental machine hall', echoing our LED points, and a grid is neutral and non-cliché compared with braces. The lit centre reads as target, tile and eye at once, so it works as a sticker or stencil.

**Weakness:** It looks like a noughts-and-crosses board, a game-of-life cell or a generic 'app grid' icon. It is close to the plain red dot in idea, so it is only a variant, not a separate concept.
