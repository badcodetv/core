# 13 — What the target reader sees, trusts and distrusts

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

This audience trusts marks that look like a made object with a lineage (a masthead, a crest, a painted banner, a skull on a dubplate), and it has publicly revolted against flat, minimal, corporate-looking redesigns; so BadCode should be a blunt, heavy, single-colour object with an obvious job (a warning plate, a lamp, a stamp) rather than a clever tech symbol, and its red should read as an indicator light, not a party or tabloid colour.

## Findings

- **[verified]** British red-top mastheads are a white title on a red rectangle. The Sun copied the Daily Mirror's red rectangle when Murdoch relaunched it as a tabloid in 1969, and that mimicry gave rise to the term 'red tops'. A secondary source says the Sun's type is Franklin Gothic Heavy italic and unchanged since 1969 (not checked against a primary source). — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_(United_Kingdom)>
- **[verified]** Leeds United's January 2018 crowdsourced minimalist crest (full club name in place of initials, a torso with fist on heart) was mocked by fans and rivals and drew a 24,000-signature petition in the first day (The Drum); other outlets report 70,000 to 100,000+ within days (figures vary by source and date). The club said it had done six months of design work and consulted 10,000+ people, yet it withdrew the crest, reopened consultation and delayed any new badge to 2019/20. — <https://www.thedrum.com/news/2018/01/24/the-damned-united-logo-leeds-scolded-fans-crowdsourced-crest-redesign>
- **[verified]** Leeds' own badge history is chunky and idiosyncratic rather than minimal: coat of arms, owl, 'LUFC' script (1971), the 1973 'Smiley' bubble-lettering badge, a peacock, and finally a shield with a white rose and vertical 'LUFC' lettering since 1998/99. Supporters treat the shield as theirs; the 2018 redesign broke that lineage. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_A.F.C.>
- **[secondary]** Search summaries report that Juventus's 2017 minimalist rebrand was called 'embarrassing' and 'disrespectful' by supporters, and that Atlético Madrid (2024 vote) and Ajax went back to older, more traditional crests after fan dissatisfaction. The stated pattern is that involving fans gives the new design legitimacy. I could not fetch the Dezeen, Creative Bloq or FourFourTwo pieces (403 or truncated). — <https://www.footyheadlines.com/2024/11/new-should-other-clubs-return-to-versions-of-their-badges.html>
- **[verified]** Metalheadz, arguably the most recognisable drum & bass label mark, is a skull wearing headphones: a 1991 design by Darren Bartlett, with the headphones added by Goldie ('the skull symbolised the head, while the phones were music, because music will be here long after we're all dead and gone'). Goldie also said Wolverhampton FC's logo influenced it. It first appeared on a dubplate. RAM Records is also cited as one of the most recognisable D&B logos. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalheadz>
- **[verified]** Durham Miners' Gala banners are hand-painted silk rectangles carrying the lodge name plus figurative images: Methodist and biblical scenes (Good Samaritan, David and Goliath, 'Go ye, and do likewise'), socialist figures, and in one case Marx, Lenin and the hammer and sickle. Attendance peaked above 300,000 in the 1950s-60s and is roughly 100,000 now. This audience's own political heritage speaks in moral pictures with a caption, not abstract marks. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Miners'_Gala>
- **[verified]** Reform UK's identity is a vivid turquoise, a circular badge with a right-facing arrow that looks like a campaign button and points at its own box on a ballot paper, three signature fonts, blocky capitals for emphasis and rigid consistency. Commentators contrast this with Advance UK's 'generic' corporate look and Restore Britain's 'stark, confrontational' one. The lesson is that discipline and a physical, usable object beat cleverness. — <https://plmr.co.uk/2025/07/perception-is-power-the-branding-battle-on-the-populist-right/>
- **[verified]** New Statesman (an opinion piece, January 2026) says Labour's X account used 17 typefaces in about a month, that its mandated Poppins is 'unstylish, unthreatening', and that Reform's consistency reads as stronger. It also says Mamdani and Catherine Connolly won with hand-drawn, culturally specific looks that signal authenticity. This is design-press opinion, not measured audience response. — <https://www.newstatesman.com/politics/labour/2026/01/why-does-labour-look-so-bad>
- **[secondary]** On algorithm and surveillance imagery: Big Brother Watch reports that about three quarters of Housing Benefit claims flagged by a DWP algorithm had no fraud or error, and a Guardian-reported case has pensioners saying they stopped buying lottery tickets for fear of being flagged. This shows the grievance is real and personal (a letter, a lost benefit), not abstract. Foxglove's FOI work found the DWP risk score does not appear to control caseworker decisions, which is a warning against implying 'the algorithm decides' when a named human does. I found no polling on how CCTV or 'all-seeing eye' imagery lands with this audience. — <https://bigbrotherwatch.org.uk/press-coverage/the-guardian-dwp-algorithm-mistakenly-flags-200000-people-for-possible-fraud-and-error/>
- **[secondary]** Class and taste: the Bourdieu-derived line in a search summary is that the working-class 'aesthetic' is a dominated one, defined against elite taste; and Burberry once played down its check after it became tied to working-class 'chav' culture. Fashion commentary also notes middle-class 'cosplay' of working-class style. These are background theory and anecdote, not evidence about logo preference, and I found no polling on class and design taste or on distrust of charity aesthetics. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinction_(sociology)>
- **[*(unverified)*]** Trade and street signage in the UK (van doors, hi-vis, betting-shop fascias, lager cans, pub boards) leans on heavy all-caps type, stencils, hazard yellow, black and red, and function-first legibility at distance. This is my own recall; I did not verify it.

## Precedents

- **The Sun / Daily Mirror / Daily Star mastheads** — White heavy sans title on a solid red rectangle; the Sun's is described as heavy italic. The Sun copied the Mirror's red box in 1969, which created the term 'red top'. **Lesson:** The most-seen masthead form in the target reader's life is a bold word in a solid slab. Red-plus-white is owned by them, so BadCode's red must be a small light, not a field. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_(United_Kingdom)>
- **Leeds United 2018 crest and the fan rebellion** — A minimalist, crowdsourced crest replacing initials with the full club name and a fist-on-heart torso, withdrawn after a petition of tens of thousands of signatures and mockery from rival clubs. **Lesson:** Fans reject marks that look designed by committee, flatten history or explain themselves; a badge must feel inherited, blunt and slightly odd. — <https://www.thedrum.com/news/2018/01/24/the-damned-united-logo-leeds-scolded-fans-crowdsourced-crest-redesign>
- **Leeds 1973 'Smiley' badge and the shield** — Bubble-lettered 1973 badge, and the current shield with white rose and vertical LUFC lettering. **Lesson:** Loved club marks can be odd, chunky and lettered; personality and history beat neatness. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_A.F.C.>
- **Atlético Madrid, Ajax and Juventus reversals** — Minimalised crests (2017 Juventus and Atlético) later reversed or bitterly criticised, per search summaries. **Lesson:** Flat minimalism from a brand agency is a known lightning rod for exactly the football-following audience. — <https://www.footyheadlines.com/2024/11/new-should-other-clubs-return-to-versions-of-their-badges.html>
- **Metalheadz skull-with-headphones** — 1991 skull drawing by Darren Bartlett with headphones added by Goldie, first seen on a dubplate; also influenced by Wolverhampton's wolf-head logo. **Lesson:** The best-loved D&B label mark is a simple face-like character with a one-line story, a tattoo-able shape that started as a cheap stamp on vinyl. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalheadz>
- **Durham Miners' Gala banners** — Hand-painted silk rectangles on a crossbar, each with a lodge name and a moral or political picture (Good Samaritan, Marx, Lenin). **Lesson:** The audience's own tradition of political identity is a name plus one allegorical picture on a physical object, not an abstract logo. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Miners'_Gala>
- **Reform UK circular arrow badge** — Turquoise circle with a right-pointing arrow that looks like a campaign button and doubles as a ballot-paper pointer; three fonts, blocky caps, strict consistency. **Lesson:** A mark that works as a physical button or in a real-world action, applied ruthlessly, beats a clever concept; and turquoise and arrows are now Reform's. — <https://plmr.co.uk/2025/07/perception-is-power-the-branding-battle-on-the-populist-right/>
- **Labour's Poppins-and-17-typefaces look** — Soft geometric sans, inconsistent fonts and gradients on social media, per the New Statesman's opinion piece. **Lesson:** Soft, inconsistent, 'unthreatening' design reads as a department or a party under pressure; BadCode should be the opposite in every respect (hard, single, consistent). — <https://www.newstatesman.com/politics/labour/2026/01/why-does-labour-look-so-bad>

## Clichés to avoid

- Curly brace, angle brackets, slashes, terminal prompt (>_) or any code punctuation standing in for a letter: the tech-startup signal the founders already rejected
- Matrix rain, binary strings, circuit-board traces, glowing neural-net or brain-with-circuits imagery
- Blue or purple gradients, glassy or glowing effects, rounded geometric sans (the 'Poppins' look that reads as government department or app)
- Chrome robot head, humanoid face, or a bare HAL/Terminator eye lifted straight from the films (unoriginal and legally and culturally borrowed)
- Padlock, shield, hexagon, all-seeing-eye pyramid or Palantir-style eye: cybersecurity and conspiracy shorthand
- Thin, minimal, flat 'we simplified it' crest treatments: the exact thing Leeds, Juventus and Atlético fans rejected
- Soft charity look: hand-drawn friendly people, pastel or warm-yellow palettes, lowercase rounded wordmarks
- Party colour codes: Reform turquoise or arrow-in-circle badge, Labour red rose or flat red, Conservative blue, Union Jack
- A straight copy of the red-top masthead (white heavy italic on a red rectangle)
- Rugged workwear cosplay or over-distressed 'blue-collar heritage' styling that reads as middle-class fashion

## Risks

- **(high)** Red confusion: a red mark reads as Labour, as a red-top tabloid (Sun/Mirror/Star), or as a Terminator/HAL copy, and pairing red with blue (the current logo) reads as a party. — The only reliable escape is to make the red a small physical light (an LED or indicator lamp) on black, never a background field or a red-and-blue split. Kai has already raised the party-colour risk in the current logo.
- **(high)** A pure red dot on black is the most generic 'AI is watching' image and is close to HAL 9000 and Terminator. — Needs a second, distinctive element (a housing, a plate, a scale or a wordmark cut) so the dot is an object with a context, not a symbol.
- **(medium)** Surveillance imagery (eye, camera, red recording light) touches a real grievance (benefit-fraud flagging, CCTV) and could be read as BadCode sneering at, or being, the watcher. — The-reader rule: name the decision-maker, not the technology. Foxglove found the DWP score does not appear to drive decisions, so a mark or caption implying 'the algorithm decided' is factually shaky. Frame the light as the warner, not the watcher.
- **(medium)** Fake 'one of ours' signals (a banner pastiche, a fake vintage crest, van-signwriting fonts) can read as costume, since the working-class look is often borrowed by middle-class fashion. — Borrow the function and construction (blunt, legible, cheap to reproduce), not the nostalgia.
- **(medium)** Evidence base for this angle is thin: there is no polling on class and logo taste, no evidence on charity-aesthetic distrust, and the football material is fan-backlash anecdote, not measured preference. — Treat findings as design heuristics to test with real people in the target town, not as proof.
- **(medium)** Any mark that needs fine detail (a crest, a banner) fails at 16px favicon and TikTok avatar size. — The version at avatar size must be one shape and one dot.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### Pilot Plate

**Sketch in words:** A square-cornered near-black plate (radius about 4% of width) filling the avatar, with one red circle (diameter about 22% of the plate width) sitting slightly above centre, offset up-left by a tenth of the width, like an indicator lamp on a machine panel. Under it, BADCODE in heavy, wide, all-caps stencil-cut sans (bridges in the B, D, C, O), off-white, about 12% of plate height, centred. A 1-2px pale grey inset border, as on a factory nameplate. Colours: black #0c0c0d, lamp red, off-white. At favicon size the wordmark drops and it is a black square with one red lamp.

**Why it fits:** Reads as a warning or nameplate on machinery, the same visual family as van stencils, hi-vis and hazard signs this audience sees daily, while the lamp carries the Terminator dot without being an eye. It is also a physical object in the near-black, tiny-LED register the films already use.

**Weakness:** Very close to countless 'record light' and 'power LED' icons and to the HAL eye; it could pass as a hardware brand if the stencil lettering is weak. It needs the lamp to have one distinctive tell (for example a small square notch cut into the plate).

### O-Lamp Wordmark

**Sketch in words:** BADCODE in one line of very heavy, slightly condensed, all-caps sans, tight tracking, off-white on black. The letter O is a solid red disc the same height as the caps, with no counter, so it sits as a lit lamp in the word. The other letters are square-shouldered, with a flat chop across the terminals of the C and E (a cut, not a curve). Lock-up ratio about 5:1 wide, and a stacked version BAD / CODE with the red O centred as the only round shape, for square avatars.

**Why it fits:** The word is the mark: it works like a masthead or lager can, is legible at 30 metres like a van door, and needs no explanation. BADCODE happens to contain exactly one O, so the red dot lives inside the name with no added symbol and reads as the reader's 'spot the red light' game.

**Weakness:** Letter-as-eye substitution is itself a familiar trick (a rounded O as a red eye is common in sci-fi titling), and it is close to a red-top masthead if the letters are italic. It relies on the type being unmistakably heavy and blunt to avoid looking like a tech startup.

### Warning Stamp

**Sketch in words:** A perfect circle ring (thick line, about 8% of diameter) in off-white on black, split at the top by a gap of about 12 degrees, like a stencil or rubber stamp, with a red dot at exactly the centre (diameter about 30% of ring). Around the inside of the ring, spaced caps 'BADCODE  ·  FROM  ·  2034  ·' in stencil type (2034 being the collapse year in canon). One gap in the ring, one dot, no other shapes. Can be pressed as a stamp, sprayed as a stencil or used as a video ident that starts as a ring, then lights the dot.

**Why it fits:** Circle badges are what this audience's political brands already use (Reform's button) and what football clubs use (roundel crests), and a stamp signals authority and 'this is official notice from the future' with the show's dry humour. The gap makes it unmistakably not a party badge or a plain target.

**Weakness:** A circle with a dot is a crosshair and could read as target or sniper, and the ring text is illegible below 64px, so the small version is just ring plus dot, which is generic without the gap.

### Lodge Banner Strip

**Sketch in words:** A tall black rectangle (2:3 portrait) like a banner hanging from a crossbar (a thin off-white horizontal bar at the top with two short cords), a red dot dead centre, BADCODE in heavy caps across the lower third, and a bottom edge of short vertical fringe ticks in off-white. No figure, no scroll. For avatars crop to the dot and crossbar only.

**Why it fits:** Points at the Durham banner tradition (a painted silk rectangle hung on a crossbar, carrying a moral picture and a name) and reframes the Gala's 'we carry our own story' image as a banner from the future. It is the one idea that leans on this audience's own inheritance rather than tech or tabloid.

**Weakness:** Weakest of the four at small size, and easily read as nostalgia or Labour-movement pastiche (or as a Corbynite reference), which conflicts with the-reader's rule not to court the converted left. Try it as a video ident or sleeve device, not the main avatar.
