# 11 — Wordmarks with one deliberate mistake, and a name made of round letters

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

The word itself is a gift: b, a, d, c, o, d, e are all one ring plus a stem, a cut or a bar, so the mark can be a RULE rather than a drawing (the NIN lesson: any bootlegger can reproduce it in any font). The strongest, cheapest rule is to set the word in geometric lowercase and make exactly one ring different, most likely the o as a solid red disc. That gets you the Terminator/HAL dot without a separate symbol. The main threat is collision with the red-disc family (Mobil, Beats, Target, HAL), not with adidas or ABC.

## Findings

- **[verified]** Nine Inch Nails: logo made by Trent Reznor with designer Gary Talpas, first seen in the 1989 'Down in It' video; the second N is reversed. Inspiration was the typography on Talking Heads' Remain in Light (Tibor Kalman). The reversal is a one-letter RULE, not an illustration. — <https://www.nin.wiki/Logo_History>
- **[verified]** Why the NIN reversed N became iconic (The Fader): a convincing bootleg needs only type with every 'n' reversed, so the identity survives any font, any surface, any imagery; the Fader calls it 'democratic, populist, egalitarian'. Also 'uniform line weights, mathematic spacing', symmetrical, legible at any size. Lesson: the best deliberate-mistake marks are rules fans can copy, which suits a Facebook/TikTok audience that makes stickers and memes. — <https://www.thefader.com/2013/09/23/nin-design-artifacts-trent-reznor>
- **[secondary]** ABBA: mirrored B designed by Rune Söderqvist (1976 logo; first mirrored B appeared 1975 with 'Honey, Honey'). It began as an accident when Benny held up a reversed B in a Bravo magazine photo shoot; Söderqvist's rationale was that each B faces each A, two couples. The mistake is iconic because it carries a story. — <https://www.creativebloq.com/features/abba-logo-history>
- **[verified]** Toys R Us: founder Charles Lazarus designed the backwards R so the name looks like a child wrote it. Korn: Jonathan Davis designed the logo; the backwards R was borrowed from Toys R Us, where several band members had worked. Every famous reversed-letter mark has a reason; and by now the reversed-letter trick is a crowded club (NIN, ABBA, Korn, Toys R Us), and a copy-of-a-copy risk for BadCode. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn>
- **[secondary]** Glitch / RGB-split type: glitch art was adopted by brands around 2012, and by the late 2010s gaming brands, music platforms and tech startups had all absorbed the split-channel look. Trade commentary says overused effects date fast and turn to noise at small sizes. Treat glitch and misregistration as the dated option; a static mistake (one reversed or recoloured letter) has lasted decades, animated glitch has not. — <https://99designs.com/blog/design-history-movements/glitch-art-design/>
- **[verified]** Herbert Bayer's Universal alphabet (worked on 1925-1930, at the Bauhaus) is lowercase-only and built from circles, straight lines and simple arcs (also square). It was never cast as real type, and commentators say it suits logos and single words better than long text. That is exactly our use case: one seven-letter word. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Bayer>
- **[verified]** Futura's lowercase: near-circular single-storey 'a' and 'g', low-contrast near-monoline strokes, an 'o' that is almost but not quite a circle; Futura Extra Bold was Stanley Kubrick's favourite typeface. Kubrick's use of Futura (recalled: 2001's titles and the film's whole visual language, where HAL's red eye lives) gives a round-letter BadCode a free 2001/HAL echo. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futura_(typeface)>
- **[verified]** ITC Avant Garde grew out of Herb Lubalin's 1960s-70s Avant Garde magazine logo (developed into a typeface with Tom Carnase, 1970-77) and shipped with 33 alternate characters and ligatures, i.e. the geometric-lowercase look became distinctive by fusing letters that share a stem or ring. A tight ligature is a second distinctiveness move besides colour or reversal. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITC_Avant_Garde>
- **[secondary]** Paul Rand's ABC (1962): lowercase 'abc' inside one black circle. Rand reportedly called it pure geometry, 'four circles, three of which happen to be letters'; one source says the letters are Helvetica-based (I distrust that; typeface attribution unresolved). BadCode's collision trap: a, b, c, d, e are the first five letters of the alphabet, so any badcode wordmark enclosed in a circle will read as an ABC knock-off. — <https://www.logodesign.org/the-history-of-the-abc-logo/>
- **[verified]** adidas has been styled in lowercase since 1949; its recognisable property is the stripes (three-stripe mark bought from Karhu in 1952, Trefoil 1972, three-bar 'Performance' logo 1997 by Peter Moore), not the letters. My inference: heavy lowercase geometric letters alone are not adidas-proprietary; the collision comes from heavy weight plus stripes or bars, so avoid parallel diagonal bars near the word. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas>
- **[secondary]** Braun: raised, enlarged 'A' in the wordmark dates to Will Münch's 1934 design and was retained by Wolfgang Schmittel's 1952 clean-up (rounded letterforms, solid black, wider gaps). One letter changed in scale or height has been a durable identity for 90 years. From memory, the other letters are capitals. — <https://www.logodesignlove.com/braun-logo>
- **[secondary]** The red-disc-in-a-round-letter family already exists: Beats is a lowercase b in a red circle, Mobil's 'o' is red (Pegasus was its symbol; the red o as focal point), and Oculus made its 'O' into a headset shape. None is a Terminator dot, but any red o or red disc in a wordmark will be compared to them. — <https://www.logofav.com/logo/red-letter-b-circle-modern-logo-example-beats>

## Clichés to avoid

- Reversed-letter rock trope as the whole idea (NIN, Korn, ABBA, Toys R Us already own it)
- Glitch / RGB-split / scanline type (2012-2019 trend, now stock templates)
- Underscore cursor, terminal block, or curly/square brackets used as letters (a mirrored c reads as a closing bracket, bringing back the cliche being escaped)
- Lowercase wordmark inside a circle (ABC; and a,b,c,d,e are the first five letters)
- Generic red HAL eye with a lens ring and glow (every AI startup and stock template)
- Friendly rounded 'startup sans' lowercase set at medium weight (reads cute, opposite of threatening)
- Binary or Matrix-green digital rain

## Risks

- **(high)** Round geometric lowercase reads friendly (Futura/Avant Garde/Bauhaus warmth, startup sans) — BadCode is meant to be somewhat threatening. Weight, spacing and colour must supply menace: very tight tracking, hairline or extra-bold extremes, near-black ground. Untested; I cannot see the results.
- **(high)** A solid red o or red disc collides with Mobil, Beats, Target and the HAL/Terminator eye that everyone already imagines — Distinctiveness must come from where the disc sits and how the rest of the word behaves, not the red disc alone.
- **(medium)** Mirrored c reads as a closing bracket — Reintroduces the code-punctuation cliche the founders want to leave, and it joins the crowded reversed-letter club.
- **(medium)** Party-colour reading of red and blue — Repo memory says the earlier red/blue logo raised this and it was never ruled. A single red dot on near-black avoids pairing with blue, which is a point in its favour; not verified with any audience.
- **(medium)** Ring letters clog at avatar and favicon size — Closed bowls (a, b, d, e, o) fill in at 16-32px; at that size the wordmark is unreadable so the mark must reduce to one glyph (disc, or stem plus disc), and that glyph must not be a Beats b.
- **(medium)** Legibility of a deliberate mistake — A word of only round letters with b/d mirror twins is already easy to misread; adding a reversal may tip 'badcode' into 'babcobe'. Needs a quick stranger test.
- **(low)** Second-hand evidence — I could not view any logo; descriptions come from text. ABBA article fetch failed, so ABBA details rely on a search snippet.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The One Red o (a rule, not a drawing)

**Sketch in words:** Set 'badcode' all lowercase in a monoline geometric sans (Futura/Bayer construction), very tight tracking, near-white on near-black. Every letter is a thin ring or ring-with-stem, except the o, which is a solid red disc the same outer diameter as the other rings (red only, roughly #cc2b37). Nothing else is coloured. The avatar is just that disc alone, or the disc plus the stem of the following d, placed dead centre on black. Rule any fan can copy: type it, colour the o.

**Why it fits:** Follows the NIN lesson (a rule that survives any font and is easy to copy for stickers and memes). Gives the Terminator/HAL dot without a literal eye, and the 'o' sits in the middle of the word so it works as a pupil watching the letters. Also happens to be the one round letter whose counter is the whole letter, so filling it is the least invasive edit.

**Weakness:** Collides with the red-disc family (Mobil, Beats, Target, HAL); a solid disc on its own is generic. It only becomes BadCode through the surrounding word and the black ground.

### Blade and Disc (b/d flip)

**Sketch in words:** Avatar mark: one thin pale vertical stem (the 'blade of cold light' from the film register), about six times taller than it is wide, with a red disc whose diameter is about half the stem's height, touching the stem on its right side. That is a lowercase b. In a video ident the disc slides across the stem to the left side and the mark becomes a d, which is the git fork (bad branch, good branch) and the word's own b and d. In stills the wordmark stays the plain ring-and-stem set from idea 1.

**Why it fits:** b and d are the first letter of 'bad' and a letter of 'code', and they are mirror twins in any geometric alphabet, so 'the mistake' is a built-in reversal with a story (one flip = the other timeline) instead of an arbitrary one. The stem echoes the existing near-black plus one thin light-blade look, and the whole thing is two shapes a marker pen can draw in two seconds.

**Weakness:** A lollipop b in red is uncomfortably close to Beats; reads as a plain 'b' until the flip is shown, and static, the flip idea is invisible. Also only works if the audience meets the animated version first.

### The Backwards c that Watches

**Sketch in words:** Same tight monoline lowercase set, but only the c is mirrored so its opening faces left, toward the d before it, and there is a small solid red dot sitting inside the counter of the mirrored c, roughly a quarter of the ring's diameter, slightly off-centre toward the opening. Reads 'bad' + 'ɔ' + 'ode' with a red pupil. Story: the c faces backward because the message comes from the future.

**Why it fits:** Deliberate-mistake logic with a stated reason (time runs backward) like ABBA's two couples or Toys R Us' child's hand. The dot inside the bowl gives the eye-in-a-letter feeling.

**Weakness:** Worst collision profile: reversed letter (NIN/Korn/ABBA/Toys R Us) plus a mirrored c that reads as a closing bracket, bringing the code cliche back. Keep as the fallback or as an ident-only gag, not the primary mark.

### Ring, Gap, Disc

**Sketch in words:** A two-shape monogram for 'co' (from 'code'): on the left an open ring in thin pale line, a c with the gap at the upper right, and on the right a solid red disc of the same diameter, one stroke-width apart, both centred on black. Wordmark stays the plain geometric set, or with the o red as in idea 1. The gap in the ring faces the disc: the c has been 'read' by the o.

**Why it fits:** Reduces the round-letter idea to something that works at 16px as a favicon and reads as 'c' then 'o', the exact centre of the word 'badcode'. Asymmetry (open vs solid) gives it a character a lone circle would lack.

**Weakness:** Two circles evokes Mastercard/Olympics-style generics and a record/loading icon; the code-cliche is avoided but it borders on anonymous. Would need a weight or proportion twist to become ownable.

### Shared Stem Ligature (the Lubalin move)

**Sketch in words:** In the geometric lowercase set, join 'd' and 'c' by letting the d's stem run straight through into the c so the two letters share one vertical line, then let the second 'd' and 'e' touch at the bowl. The o stays red and isolated with clear space either side, so the only unjoined, coloured letter is the o. Line lengths equal, stems slightly taller than ascender norm.

**Why it fits:** Avant Garde and Lubalin became recognisable through ligatures; ligating the black letters while isolating the o makes the o the single object left standing, which reads as a deliberate act rather than a font choice.

**Weakness:** Ligatures can be too fussy at small size and can create accidental misreadings ('bod'). Needs a legibility check and adds design labour compared with idea 1.

## Gaps — what this brief could not verify

All 8 searches were used. I could not view any image, so every mark description is from text. The Creative Bloq ABBA fetch returned only navigation, so ABBA details come from the search snippet. I did not verify: the typeface behind the adidas wordmark; the ABC typeface (sources disagree, one says Helvetica); the exact shape of Bayer's 'a'; the designer and date of Mobil's red 'o'; how often and where Kubrick used Futura in 2001 (only 'favourite typeface' was verified); whether any reversed-letter mark (ABBA etc.) had trademark disputes; whether any wordmark actually puts an eye or dot inside a letter's bowl beyond Beats, Mobil and Oculus (I found no famous case of a pupil dot inside a lowercase bowl, which may mean the space is open or that my searches missed it). Collision claims about adidas, ABC, Adobe and Braun are my reasoning from construction, not from a legal or design-search database. I did not check Adobe's mark at all beyond memory (a red A glyph). The party-colour and near-black-legibility risks are untested with the real audience.
