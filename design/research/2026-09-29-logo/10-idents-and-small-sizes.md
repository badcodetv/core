# 10 — Motion idents, and surviving at tiny sizes

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

The idents that stuck (Channel 4, BBC Two, MTV, HBO, THX) all lock ONE constant behaviour and vary the context, so BadCode should design a behaviour (a thin line arrives, a dot ignites, one low tone) and let the still frame be that behaviour paused, inside the middle ~65% of a circle, with no text (Spotify bans it) and a dot of at least ~14% of the canvas so it survives 16-32 px.

## Findings

- **[verified]** Recurring pattern in the best-documented idents: one constant behaviour or shape, varied context. Channel 4's blocks assemble into a '4' from a single camera position, and that alignment moment persists across the 1982, 2004 and 2015 versions (2015 directed by Jonathan Glazer). BBC Two's 1991 idents put a plain '2' into varied physical scenes (paint, water and so on). BBC One ran the same trick with a balloon (1997-2002), dancers (2002-2006) and circles (2006-2016). The constant is one thing the viewer learns to wait for. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4_idents_and_presentation>
- **[verified]** MTV's 1981 logo was deliberately mutable. Frank Olinsky, Pat Gorman and Patty Rogoff (Manhattan Design, under Fred Seibert) drew an M and scrawled TV over it. The source says the only constants were the general shape and proportions, while colour, pattern and image changed with every ID. Lesson: a mark can be a fixed skeleton plus a changing skin. This suits BadCode, whose storyverse already swaps skins (Story, Theatre, Myth, Game). — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV>
- **[verified]** HBO's static ident is trademarked as a motion-and-sound mark: TV snow, from which the logo emerges then sharpens, with the sound of static 'turning on' resolving to an ascending choir-like bass chord. Read-secondary detail: it dates from 1996, was made by Imaginary Forces, and the audio was composed by Bruce Richmond. Lesson: the ident is a small three-beat story (noise, resolve, hold), and the sound was worth registering as a trademark in its own right. — <http://www.tantalizingtrademarks.com/2015/08/hbo-files-trademarks-for-look-and-sound.html>
- **[verified]** THX Deep Note (James A. Moorer, Lucasfilm, late 1982, debuted at Return of the Jedi in 1983, trademarked 1992) uses 30 voices over seven measures. They start clustered at 200-400 Hz and diverge to a chord spanning three octaves. Gary Rydstrom said it feels loud because the spectrum grows from small to large. It is one gesture (a rise from chaos to order), it is unmistakable, and it survived parody (a 1994 Simpsons gag led THX to commission a remake). A drum & bass act can borrow the principle: a single swelling sub-bass gesture. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Note>
- **[verified]** Sonic-logo memory research (Silas, Robinson, Baker, Harrison, Jacoby and Mullensiefen, Goldsmiths and Cambridge, accepted for APA 2026): 90 real commercial sonic logos, N=1,860. Recognition and sung recall both vary reliably between logos, but melodic complexity helps recognition and hurts recall, so at logo level the two trade off. Simple stepwise melodies are easy to recall but hard to recognise for lack of distinctiveness. Prior work cited there says effective logos are easy to hum, of moderate length, with simple contour and clear tonality. Implication: decide whether the BadCode sound must be recognised (a distinctive timbre or gesture) or hummed (a melody). A timbre or gesture like Deep Note serves an ident. — <https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/eb68da1f-6ea7-4829-be41-d7da46f26aff/content>
- **[secondary]** Vendor and marketing-blog figures on sonic logos (96% higher recall than visuals alone; 43% correct brand attribution, 18% when the name is not in the audio; 2-3 seconds ideal; brand name in the audio makes attribution 9x more effective; a 2020 Bianco study found learned patterns persisted at least 7 weeks) come from agency and blog pages, not primary studies. Treat them as directional only and do not cite them publicly. — <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02650487.2023.2273075>
- **[verified]** Spotify's official artist-image guidelines: avatar minimum 750x750 px, header minimum 2660x1140, gallery minimum 690x500, .jpeg/.png/.gif, max 20 MB. Images may not contain text, advertising or busy backgrounds. So on Spotify the avatar must be a pure symbol with no wordmark. Read-secondary: it displays as a circle on artist pages and in search. — <https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/artist-image-guidelines/>
- **[secondary]** Avatar specs from secondary guides (Sprout Social and others): YouTube 800x800 (98x98 minimum), TikTok 200x200 minimum (another guide says 1080), Instagram 110 display, Facebook 320-400, X 400, Snapchat 320, SoundCloud at least 1000x1000 (2 MB max, circular), Bandcamp 600x600 (2 MB max). Most platforms crop to a circle, so keep detail away from the edges. Upload 1000-1500 px square and design for the smallest displayed size, not the upload size. Exact on-screen sizes in comments and feeds (roughly 24-48 px) are from my memory and were not checked. — <https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-image-sizes-guide/>
- **[*(unverified)*]** CONSTRAINT, circle-crop geometry (arithmetic, not sourced): a circle inscribed in a square keeps the full diameter but loses the corners. The largest square that fits inside is 70.7% of the side. Anything that must not be cut (ends of a line, any lettering) should sit inside the central ~70%, and for safety against platform padding, the key parts of the mark should sit within roughly the central 60-65%. An off-centre dot must still clear the circle edge by at least its own diameter.
- **[secondary]** CONSTRAINT, favicons and dark mode, from secondary guides: 1px lines and gradients are lost at 16x16, so use simple geometric shapes and flat colour. If a stroke flickers between visible and invisible depending on scaling, it is too thin, so make shapes heavier than feels right on the artboard. Thin light strokes vanish on dark tab bars and thin dark strokes on light ones. Test on #ffffff and dark slate such as #0f172a. SVG favicons can recolour via prefers-color-scheme in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, but Safari is inconsistent and needs a PNG/ICO fallback that reads on both light and dark. — <https://faviconbuilder.com/guides/dark-mode-favicons/>
- **[verified]** CONSTRAINT, stroke and dot weight. The one primary number found: Material Design system icons use a 24dp grid with a 2dp stroke (8.3% of the canvas), a 20dp circle keyline and an 18dp square keyline. My own rule of thumb (recalled, not sourced): any line or dot that must survive at 16 px should be at least 1.5 px there, about 9-10% of the canvas. A dot of 14-16% of the canvas is about 2.5 px at 16 px. A 1px hairline at a 32 px avatar is about 3% of the canvas and will alias or vanish. Treat these as design targets to test, not as a specification. — <https://m2.material.io/design/iconography/system-icons.html>

## Precedents

- **Channel 4 blocks** — Nine differently shaped blocks form a '4' (1982, Martin Lambie-Nairn and Colin Robinson). The idents show the blocks aligning into the numeral from one camera position in varied scenes (2004 ambient, 2015 Glazer 'geodes'). **Lesson:** The recognisable event is the alignment moment. The logo is a payoff, not a static picture, and it works because it is one repeatable behaviour. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4_idents_and_presentation>
- **BBC Two 1991 '2' idents** — A plain sans-serif numeral 2 appearing in varied physical scenes (paint, water and so on). Per the source, research said viewers had thought the channel dull and old-fashioned and the idents changed that. **Lesson:** A very plain shape plus a changing world gives freshness without losing recognition. The plainness is what makes it copyable in a marker pen. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_BBC_television_idents>
- **MTV 1981 mutable M** — Rogoff's block M with Olinsky's spray-scrawled TV, re-skinned in colours, patterns and imagery per ID, with only shape and proportion fixed. The launch ident morphed a moon-landing flag into the logo to a guitar jingle. **Lesson:** Fix the skeleton, let the skin mutate. It suits a collective that publishes in several media, but it needs a skeleton this strong. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV>
- **HBO static** — A white line splits open to reveal TV snow (read-secondary), then the logo resolves out of the noise with a static-on sound and a rising choir-like bass chord. Registered as a motion and sound trademark. **Lesson:** The nearest tonal cousin to BadCode: menacing, sonic, and a line-then-reveal grammar. It is also the closest thing to avoid copying too directly. — <http://www.tantalizingtrademarks.com/2015/08/hbo-files-trademarks-for-look-and-sound.html>
- **THX Deep Note** — A sound-only mark: 30 voices sliding from a 200-400 Hz cluster to a three-octave chord over seven measures, accompanied on screen by the THX logo. **Lesson:** One simple gesture (small to huge) has been recognisable for over 40 years. The sound is more famous than the picture, which suits a music-led collective. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Note>
- **Netflix 'ta-dum'** — A short two-beat audio logo attached to the red N; Wikipedia only confirms it as Netflix's audio logo in use since 2014. From memory, it is under 5 seconds with a swelling low-mid hit. **Lesson:** A two-note sound plus a one-colour letter carries a whole streaming service. Details of its maker and length were not verified. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix>
- **A24** — Art-deco-styled wordmark and motion intro created in 2012 by the agency GrandArmy, briefed as 'modern but echoing the golden age of Hollywood'. From memory, the title-card version is often varied per film while the shape stays the same. **Lesson:** Verified only that it is a fixed shape with a motion intro. The per-film variation is from memory, but it supports the MTV lesson that the skeleton stays and the skin moves. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24>
- **Kurzgesagt** — A minimalist Earth: a blue circle with white and green lines and lighter blue halos (per Wikipedia). It is a single circular badge that suits round avatars. **Lesson:** The video-essay channels lean on one simple, round, single-idea mark. The circle already matches how every avatar is cropped. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt>
- **Vsauce** — Original hand-drawn V logo by Nik Guinta. Rebranded in December 2014 by Natasha Jen to a 'grown-up' look, per Wikipedia (I did not view the marks). **Lesson:** Channels can survive a rebrand when the name and format are unchanged, so the first mark does not have to be forever. Details of the marks were not examined. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsauce>

## Clichés to avoid

- A glowing red HAL 9000 or Terminator eye (a red lens with a bright rim and reflection is the single most copied menace image)
- Glitch or chromatic-aberration wordmarks
- Circuit-board traces, binary strings, and green Matrix rain
- Robot heads, skulls with wires, and neural-network node graphs
- Curly braces, angle brackets, semicolons and slash-star code punctuation (the very thing being escaped)
- The power-button symbol, and hexagon or shield tech badges
- Lens-flare pulses and scanning-radar sweeps
- A 'ta-dum' style boom copied too closely from Netflix or HBO's choir-over-static

## Risks

- **(high)** A red dot on black reads as 'recording', 'notification badge' or 'live' at small sizes, not as a threatening intelligence, and Instagram and TikTok already put red dots on avatars. — At 16-32 px a lone red dot has the same silhouette as an unread badge or the YouTube live signal. Give it a distinguishing partner (a line, a ring, or an off-axis position) so it has a shape as well as a colour.
- **(medium)** HAL 9000 and Terminator are so well known that any lone red point risks reading as a homage or copy. — The dot is the trigger, not the mark. What makes it BadCode has to be something specific to the project: the vertical blade of cold light, or the fork, or the behaviour of arrival.
- **(medium)** Near-black avatar on dark-mode UI loses its edge. — TikTok, YouTube and Spotify dark themes are near black. A black disc with a red dot shows only the dot, so the circle crop disappears. A visible thin cold-light edge, or a non-black field, may be needed. Untested.
- **(medium)** Spotify's ban on text, advertising and busy backgrounds in artist images rules out a wordmark avatar there. — The symbol has to carry the name alone and be learnt elsewhere first, which is a slow burn for a new collective.
- **(medium)** Red plus blue reads as party colours in UK politics, and the current curly-brace logo already uses red and blue. This was raised in the project notes, not ruled. — A red-only mark on black avoids it; red and cold-white avoids blue too. The founders' target is a working-class UK reader drifting right, so partisan colour is a real hazard, not a taste question.
- **(low)** Motion-ident research base is thin: the strongest paper measures melodic memory of commercial sonic logos, not whether an ident builds recognition for a small channel. — No study found compares a plain mark to an animated ident for recall. The precedents are large broadcasters with repeated exposure over years, which a small channel will not have.
- **(low)** Sonic logo used as a signature on every video may irritate on repeat, and short-form platforms strip or replace audio. — TikTok and Reels often play a trending sound over the clip. The sound may only ever land in long-form and on Spotify. Unverified but well known from memory.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The Arrival

**Sketch in words:** Square canvas, near-black (#0a0a0c), cropped to a circle. A single red dot (#d0202c or similar) sits dead-centre, diameter 16% of the canvas. One thin vertical line of cold white (#dfe9f2), width 5% of the canvas, runs from the top edge to just above the dot and stops with a gap equal to half the dot's diameter. Nothing else. The line is the vertical blade of light from the house register; the dot is the eye or lens waiting under it. Ident (2 seconds): the line falls from the top in 0.6 s, stops, holds for a beat, then the dot ignites on a rising sub-bass note that swells to a single low hit at 1.4 s and cuts to silence.

**Why it fits:** It is one behaviour, an arrival then an ignition, which matches the Channel 4, HBO and Deep Note pattern. It uses the house look (blade of light, LED point) so it is unmistakably BadCode, and it works in 2 seconds. The still frame is a paused frame of the ident. It is drawable with a marker in two strokes.

**Weakness:** It resembles a lowercase 'i' or an exclamation mark, and at 16 px the gap between line and dot may fill in and make it a vertical bar with a red end, or a pin drop. It also reads as a 'warning' glyph, which is close to the intended meaning but generic.

### The Fork Dot

**Sketch in words:** Near-black circle. A cold-white vertical line rises from the bottom edge to the centre, where it splits into a Y, two equal branches at about 35 degrees from vertical, each ending in a dot. The left dot is red and the right dot is the same size and pale cold white. Line width 5% of the canvas, dots 13%. Total mark occupies the central 60%. Ident: the line climbs, the Y opens, and the left branch's dot ignites red with the low hit while the right one flickers once, then stays dim and silent.

**Why it fits:** It is the project's own metaphor (git fork: bad branch and good branch) reduced to a shape. The bad branch is the red threat, the good branch is the unlit hope, and the narrator's tone (threatening but nurturing) sits in one glyph. Not a code punctuation mark: a road fork.

**Weakness:** A Y with dots is also a git commit graph, Octocat-adjacent and possibly a network-node cliche. If it looks too much like a diagram it will read as a tech logo again, the very thing being escaped. It needs the colour asymmetry (red versus dim) to carry the meaning at small size.

### The Slit

**Sketch in words:** Near-black field. One horizontal hairline of cold white, 4% of canvas thick, spans the full width of the crop, slightly below the middle (at 55% height). A red dot, 15% of the canvas, sits on that line, off-centre at 38% from the left. The line makes the dot look like a sun on a horizon or an eye at the level of the world's edge. Ident: a black frame, the horizontal line draws across in 0.5 s, the dot ignites and holds, and a HBO-like static 'turn-on' hiss resolves to a single sub-bass.

**Why it fits:** It is the most stripped-back version of the house register (thin light, one point of light). Off-centre placement gives it a character that a centred dot lacks, so it does not read as a notification badge. It works as a favicon, sticker and stencil (a line and a hole), and on a record sleeve it looks like a horizon at the end of the world.

**Weakness:** A horizon with a dot reads as a sunset, or as Japan's flag in reverse, and may feel calm not threatening. The line is also crop-sensitive: a circle crop shortens it and the off-centre dot can approach the edge. It is also the closest to the HBO line-then-reveal grammar.

### The Held Breath

**Sketch in words:** A thin ring (cold white, line width 6% of canvas, outer diameter 56%) with a gap of about 40 degrees at the top, like an unfinished O. A red dot (14%) sits centred inside the ring. Near-black field. Ident: the ring draws itself clockwise and stops short of closing; the dot ignites; the gap stays open. The sound is one low tone that hangs on an unresolved note, deliberately not landing. The gap is the point: humanity still has time to close the loop.

**Why it fits:** A ring plus a dot is the strongest shape at 16 px. The gap says 'not too late', which carries the nurturing part of the voice inside the threat. It fills a circular avatar crop naturally. It also works as a bracket-free way of saying 'incomplete code'.

**Weakness:** It looks a lot like the power symbol, a camera lens, or the HAL eye (a ring around a red dot is almost literally HAL's housing). This is the highest risk of the four for the 'copy of someone else's mark' problem, and it needs a distinguishing feature before it is used.
