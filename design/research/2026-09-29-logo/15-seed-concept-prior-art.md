# 15 — Prior-art check on five candidate concepts

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

A bare red dot is the most crowded thing on your list (Leica, RED, HAL, Terminator, Nothing, every recording light), so the dot alone will not make a mark that is unmistakably BadCode. The candidates with clear space are the ones that combine a dot with an off-axis relationship (line plus dot that never touch, a pupil pushed to the edge of a ring). No brand I could find owns an averted-pupil eye or a hair-thin line with a red dot.

## Findings

- **[verified]** Impulse! Records (1961): the wordmark is a heavy, lowercase sans-serif with a trailing exclamation mark that mirrors the lowercase 'i' at the start. Colours are orange, black and white, chosen because no other label used that combination. In the 1960s the logo sat in a white circle with black-and-orange exclamation marks above it. Designer was Fran Scott (now Fran Attaway); art director Robert Flynn. So Impulse! already has the 'i / !' mirror joke, but it is bold, orange, and dependent on the word. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impulse!_Records>
- **[secondary]** Yahoo! keeps its exclamation mark inside its name and tilts it nine degrees 'for whimsy' (2013 redesign, per Marissa Mayer). E! is the letter E plus an exclamation mark underneath. Both are exclamation marks bolted to a word, not stand-alone marks, so neither is a near-neighbour to a hair-thin line with a red dot. Yum! Brands also uses one in its name. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E!>
- **[verified]** A bare, stand-alone exclamation mark is legally and commercially rare but not empty. Chips Ahoy (sold as Pepitos in Latin America) holds a US-registered 3D exclamation-mark mark, and the trade blog notes the USPTO treated it as inherently distinctive while the EU refused Joop!'s attempt to register a stand-alone exclamation mark. Takeaway: the punctuation itself is hard to own; only your particular drawing of it (hair-thin blade, red dot) can be distinctive. — <https://www.duetsblog.com/2009/11/articles/trademarks/branding-exclamations/>
- **[verified]** The exclamation mark is the universal hazard sign (a lone '!' inside a triangle on yellow or white/red). Unlike the brand uses, this is the strongest real 'neighbour' for candidate 1: viewers may read it as 'warning', not as a logo. It also has the programmer nickname 'bang' (shebang, bang paths), which is a genuine BadCode-friendly double meaning for a git-themed project. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclamation_mark>
- **[verified]** The RKO Radio Pictures logo (opening use 1929 to 1959) is a radio tower on top of a rotating Earth globe, sending out lightning bolts and sound waves, with Morse code on the soundtrack. Designed by Paul Detlefsen with Harry Kechnel, filmed by Linwood Dunn; the transmitter was inspired by a Tesla-coil tower in Colorado. It is the canonical 'mast that transmits' mark. The Mast candidate is safe if it drops the globe, the bolts and the waves, but it inherits the 'broadcast signal' association, which actually suits a warning transmitted from the future. — <https://www.avid.wiki/RKO_Radio_Pictures>
- **[verified]** Arqiva runs all UK TV and most UK radio transmitters from about 1,450 sites (Crystal Palace, Emley Moor, Alexandra Palace and so on). I could NOT get a description of its logo, so I cannot say how close a mast mark would be. Aircraft-warning red lights on masts are real, but I did not verify any broadcaster logo that puts a red light on a thin mast. A UK reader will still know masts like Emley Moor by sight, which is a resonance for the Mast, not a conflict. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arqiva>
- **[verified]** The CBS 'Eye' (first broadcast 20 October 1951) was conceived by William Golden from a Pennsylvania Dutch hex sign and a Shaker drawing, and has never been redesigned. It is a stylised eye with concentric rings, essentially a centred, staring eye. Big Brother (UK) uses a different eye logo every series, first a photo of housemate Melanie Hill's real eye, then designs by Daniel Eatock, Hello Charlie and Shop, all meant to say 'Big Brother is watching'. Both establish that a ring-with-pupil eye reads instantly as 'watching', which is CBS and Big Brother territory, and a UK audience knows the Big Brother one best. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_(British_TV_series)>
- **[secondary]** Averted-pupil eye: my search for a brand logo with a sideways pupil returned only stock-art side-eye icons, no brand. Cognitive-science material in the results says straight-gaze eyes are detected faster than averted-gaze ones. So there is probably open ground, but 'no brand found in one search' is not clearance, and the emoji-style 'side-eye' is now mainstream comic vocabulary that could make the mark read as a joke or meme rather than a threat. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stare-in-the-crowd_effect>
- **[verified]** The single red dot is owned culturally by several parties. HAL 9000 is 'a camera lens containing a red and yellow dot' (prop lens about 3 inches; the eye was a Fisheye Nikkor 8 mm f/8). The T-800 has a faint red eye glow when online, and loses it when out of action. Cylon centurions have chevron-shaped red visors. A secondary source says the RED camera company's single-eye logo resembles HAL, though I could not confirm that from RED's own description. Any red-dot-in-black mark will be read as 'HAL / Terminator' first, which matches what the founders 'keep seeing' but means it will not be ownable on the dot alone. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000>
- **[verified]** Leica's red dot is the real legal and visual neighbour for candidates 4 and 5. It is a bright red circle with white script; first appeared on the Leica R3 (1976) and M4-2 (1977) as the 'Leitz' dot, became the Leica dot around 1986 on the M6, and has been the official company logo since 1999. A plain red disc on a black field, or a red disc with lettering, sits very close to it. I found nothing on how aggressively Leica enforces it. Also note the Red Dot Design Award and the Japanese flag as further plain-disc associations. — <https://leicasocietyinternational.org/blog/2020/05/revenge-of-the-red-dot>
- **[secondary]** Red 'o' precedent: Mobil's wordmark famously has a red 'o' in an otherwise blue-and-black name (secondary source; it is a red letter, not a disc). I found NO music or media wordmark that replaces an 'o' with a plain dot or disc in this budget, and my one search for a red-dot D&B label logo returned nothing usable, so 'no precedent found' means only that, not that none exists. From memory (not checked): Nothing (the phone brand) uses dot-matrix type and red accents, and a red circle is also the universal 'record' symbol. — <https://www.inkbotdesign.com/wordmark-logos/>
- **[*(unverified)*]** Search limits: Wikipedia, DuetsBlog and Avid wiki were fetchable; Logopedia (402), Atlas Obscura (403) and one CBS page (404) were not, so the list of stand-alone exclamation logos is incomplete. Recalled but not checked: ThinkPad's 'i' dot glows red as a status light, and YouTube's player uses a red scrubber line with a red playhead dot, which matters for any 'line plus red dot' mark.

## Clichés to avoid

- A bare red disc on black with nothing else: it is Leica, RED, HAL, a REC button and a Japanese flag at once
- A staring, centred eye with a glowing red pupil: it is the Big Brother, CBS, Sauron and Eye of Providence family, and the Providence eye is already the surveillance-conspiracy cliche
- Exclamation mark inside a hazard triangle: reads as a road sign, not a brand
- Radio tower with lightning bolts and sound waves around a globe: that is RKO, near-verbatim
- Orange-and-black with a heavy lowercase word and a trailing '!': that is Impulse!, and a trailing '!' after any word is Yahoo!/Yum!/E!
- Glowing red HUD or Terminator skull imagery, and circuit-board or binary decoration (the founders already reject code punctuation)

## Risks

- **(medium)** Impulse! Records: a lowercase word plus an exclamation mark, especially near the i, is a direct echo of a famous jazz label, and the label is now a Universal imprint that reissues old covers. — Only a problem if the wordmark and the '!' appear together. A stand-alone hair-thin line with a red dot shares nothing with Impulse! beyond the punctuation; keep it away from orange and from any lowercase bold wordmark. I only verified the logo from Wikipedia, not from the trademark register.
- **(medium)** The bare exclamation mark reads as 'warning' or 'error' not 'brand'. — That happens to be on-message (a warning from the future) but a UK viewer on TikTok may see a notification icon or an alert badge. Chips Ahoy holds a 3D exclamation mark registration in the US, so check any trademark use in confectionery-adjacent classes, which is unlikely to matter for BadCode.
- **(medium)** Line plus red dot is also a YouTube progress bar, a map pin and a matchstick. — The Mast and the line-plus-dot Bang are dangerously close to YouTube's red scrubber, and the audience lives on YouTube. Recalled from memory, not verified. Avoid a horizontal line with a red dot on it.
- **(high)** Red dot is too generic to be unmistakable; Leica, RED, HAL, Terminator, Cylon, recording lights. — Candidates 4 and 5 rely on it most. Without a second element (the blade of light, the ring, a placement rule) the mark will not survive being placed next to Leica or a REC button.
- **(low)** Party-colour and hazard reading of red plus blue, and red plus black reading as politically loaded in the UK. — Existing memory notes flag red top / blue bottom party-colour risk on the earlier brace logo. A red dot alone avoids the pair but a red dot on black is also the Terminator/threat palette the founders want. Not researched here.
- **(medium)** Averted-pupil eye may read as the side-eye emoji or a meme, defusing the threat. — Sarcasm is on-voice for BadCode, but the fiction says the narrator is a menacing, authoritative machine. A pupil pushed to the corner can look shy, bored or suspicious. Draw it as a machine (no eyelids, no eyebrow, no white sclera) to keep it from becoming a cartoon.
- **(low)** Search coverage was thin: 8 searches, some pages blocked. — I found no brand with an averted red pupil, a red-dot 'o' in a D&B or media wordmark, or a hair-line-and-dot mark, but that is absence of evidence from a small sample. Before committing, run a trademark search (UK IPO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database) and an image search on the final drawing.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The Bang, Falling

**Sketch in words:** On a square black field, a hair-thin vertical line in cold white-blue (about 1/60 of the field width) running from the top edge to about 62% of the way down. Below it, separated by a gap about three times the dot's diameter, a solid red dot (about 1/12 of the field width) sitting slightly left of the line's axis, roughly one dot-radius off. The line and dot never touch and are not aligned. No wordmark in the mark itself; 'badcode' appears beside it in small lowercase letterspaced type only in wide lockups. Rotated 180 degrees the dot sits over the line, which reads as a lowercase 'i' with the dot mislaid.

**Why it fits:** Pushes away from Impulse! (heavy, orange, word-attached), Yahoo! and E! (punctuation stuck to a word) by being a hair line, a cold blade of light and an off-axis dot. Echoes 'bang' from git and shell and is the house register (one thin blade, one LED). The gap plus offset is what makes it a mistake, and BadCode's whole subject is a mistake humans have made.

**Weakness:** Still reads as an error or warning icon at 16px, and the offset dot may look like a printing fault. The blade is so thin it disappears at favicon size, so a favicon version needs a thicker line.

### The Mast, Light Adrift

**Sketch in words:** A thin vertical line rising from the bottom edge of a black square, slightly right of centre, stopping at about 70% height with a tiny horizontal foot or nothing. A red dot floats above the tip at a distance of one dot-diameter, hovering a little higher than any real mast light would sit. Add no tower lattice, no waves, no bolts, no globe. For a wide lockup, draw the line from the baseline of the word 'badcode', standing where the ascender of the 'd' is, so the mast IS a letter stroke.

**Why it fits:** Distances it from RKO (globe, bolts, waves) and from Arqiva-style corporate towers. A red light on a mast at night is a real thing a working-class UK viewer sees on the hill above town, which makes 'a transmission from the future' literal.

**Weakness:** Reads as a lollipop, a match, a pin or a lowercase i. The line-plus-red-dot shape is also the nearest neighbour to the Bang, so choose one. Sits near YouTube's red playhead if drawn horizontally, so keep it strictly vertical.

### The Slit

**Sketch in words:** Not a ring. A thin horizontal slit, like a letterbox, about 5:1 wide to tall, in cold grey on black, no eyelid curve and no lashes. Inside it, a red dot slightly taller than the slit, pushed to the far right end so it overflows the top and bottom edges, as if the pupil is looking sideways at something off-frame. Optionally the slit narrows by a few pixels in the animation, like a sarcastic squint.

**Why it fits:** Keeps the 'watching' idea but escapes the CBS/Big Brother ring-and-pupil family entirely, and escapes the emoji side-eye by having no face parts. A machine that looks away from you is the sarcastic-but-authoritative narrator: it does not need to stare because it already knows how it ends.

**Weakness:** At small sizes this reads as a loading bar or slider knob, and it could be mistaken for a YouTube-style progress bar. It needs the dot to be clearly larger than the slit to stay an eye.

### Standby, Not Centred

**Sketch in words:** A black square. A single small red dot placed on the lower-right third intersection (about 2/3 across, 2/3 down), diameter about 1/16 of the field. Directly above it, one vertical hairline in cold light, offset one dot-diameter to the LEFT, running from the top edge to the dot's own height, stopping exactly level with the dot's centre. Animated, the dot breathes (fades 100 to 30 per cent over about 4 seconds) while the hairline never moves.

**Why it fits:** Adds the missing second element to the plain red dot, so it stops being Leica or a REC button and becomes 'the standby light beside a blade of light', tied to the house near-black register. The breathing gives it a heartbeat for a 2-second ident.

**Weakness:** Static, it is still a dot on black and the hairline vanishes at avatar size, so the mark depends on animation and on the black field, and cannot stand alone on a light sleeve or sticker without being drawn on a black rectangle.

### badcode, Eye in the O

**Sketch in words:** Lowercase 'badcode' in a plain, slightly condensed geometric sans, tightly spaced, all one colour (off-white). The 'o' is a thin ring, its stroke matching the other letters, but with a red dot inside about one third the ring's diameter, pushed hard up and to the right so it almost touches the ring. Nothing else is red. The rest of the letters carry no decoration.

**Why it fits:** Merges candidates 3 and 5 so it avoids Mobil (a solid red letter) and Leica (a solid red disc with lettering) by being an outline ring with a small off-centre pupil. It is a wordmark, so it works for a record sleeve, and the 'o' can be cropped out alone as the avatar (the ring with the pupil).

**Weakness:** The ring-with-pupil 'o' is an eye-in-a-letter trope (like the 'Google' eyes joke and many tech logos), and at avatar size a small dot inside a ring can vanish or look like a target or bullseye. Also, the word 'badcode' has only one 'o' and it falls late in the word, so the cropped avatar loses the 'bad' and the 'code' meaning.
