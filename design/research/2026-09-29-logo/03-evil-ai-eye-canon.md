# 03 — The menacing machine eye in fiction

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 7 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

HAL's eye is not a drawing of an eye. It is a real camera lens lit red from behind, sat alone in a blank black slab, and most of its menace comes from stillness, voice and context. The Cylon and KITT scanners are the same mechanism with opposite moods. So shape alone never carries menace. BadCode's mark should be a device with behaviour (a lens, a light, a way of moving), not a picture of an eye, and it must be visibly not HAL's centred red dot on a black square.

## Findings

- **[verified]** HAL's on-screen 'eye' is a lightly modified Nikkor 8mm f/8 circular fisheye still-camera lens with a red R60 filter screwed on the back and a light shone through it. It sits in a faceplate made of four bare aluminium bars mitred at 45 degrees, satin-black panel, a grey speaker grille and a HAL 9000 decal. Lens ring is about 96mm across. The white tungsten light behind a red filter looks different from a red LED. The menace starts from a real object, a camera, meaning surveillance, with visible aperture blades and curved glass reflections. — <https://kosmofoto.com/2019/09/nikon-fisheye-hal-9000-2001/>
- **[verified]** The prop details come from a fan reverse-engineering site: overall faceplate 346 x 116mm, satin black panel, grey grille, bare aluminium edges. The whole mark is one circle, one colour, a black slab and a grille. The proportion is tall and narrow, not a square. — <https://sites.google.com/site/theageofplastic3d/hal-9000s-faceplates/ii-ingredients-of-the-faceplate>
- **[verified]** HAL's logotype (white outline Letraset Grotesque 9 on blue and black) was probably finalised by Harry Lange and was deliberately made to look like IBM or Honeywell corporate branding. So HAL's brand is banal and corporate next to its terrifying eye. That gap is part of the effect. HAL also shows almost nothing else: no face, no body, a bland mid-Atlantic voice (Douglas Rain, recorded in about a day and a half). — <https://sites.google.com/site/theageofplastic3d/the-hal-9000-logotype>
- **[secondary]** The Cylon Centurion scanner (1978) was a custom electronic eye of 32 bulbs swept left to right in a repeating pattern, with a warbling sound added in post-production. KITT's scanner in Knight Rider (same creator, Glen A. Larson) is the same idea: a red light sweeping along a slit. KITT's pilot reused the Cylon warble, then the sound was redesigned deeper and less robotic. Same mechanism, one is a killer and one is a friendly car. The effect is almost entirely motion plus sound, and the valence comes from voice and context, not the shape. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KITT>
- **[verified]** The Terminator's red eye glow is a persistent franchise trait meaning 'online'. The glow going out is used to show the machine is out of action. So the red dot works as a status light: on means it is watching, off means it is dead. Stan Winston designed the endoskeleton with Cameron. Brad Fiedel's score was built on a mechanical heartbeat in an odd 13/16 metre. Much of the menace is in the sound. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_(character)>
- **[verified]** Skynet itself is 'rarely depicted visually'. Its first on-screen face came only in Terminator Salvation (2009), and it is played by an actor. The most feared AI in the franchise has almost no mark, which suggests absence and delegation (its agents carry the eyes) is stronger than a logo. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skynet_(Terminator)>
- **[verified]** GLaDOS is a hanging tangle of wires and machinery with a yellow-eyed core, hanging upside-down from the ceiling. Her threat is carried mainly by Ellen McLain's voice, made to sound synthesised while keeping emotional inflection, and by passive-aggressive humour rather than a shape. AUTO in WALL-E was a conscious HAL homage. He hangs from the ceiling like a spider, is voiced with Apple's MacInTalk text-to-speech, and the film uses 2001's music (Blue Danube, Also sprach Zarathustra). The homage is done through hanging posture, voice and music, not by copying the lens. SHODAN uses green eyes and a glitching, stuttering voice; the growing glitches were inspired by HAL's degradation. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUTO_(WALL-E)>
- **[verified]** GERTY in Moon (2009) is a smiley-face display plus a camera eye on a ceiling rail, voiced by Kevin Spacey with HAL as the influence. It inverts HAL: friendly surface, quiet complicity underneath. A friendly-looking face on a corporate machine is itself an established menace device, so a smiling, sarcastic BadCode is a real option as well as the red dot. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_(2009_film)>
- **[verified]** Tolkien's Eye of Sauron is 'rimmed with fire, but was itself glazed, yellow as a cat's, watchful and intent, and the black slit of its pupil opened on a pit, a window into nothing.' Jackson's films made it a disembodied flaming eye. The threat is a black slit, not a red circle: an absence. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron>
- **[secondary]** In Person of Interest the Machine and Samaritan were designed by CoSA VFX as point-of-view overlays. Restricted areas are marked by concentric red lines and a red orbit, and aircraft carry white triangles. The intelligence has no face at all: it is a way of seeing, made of thin red lines, small marker shapes and labels. I could not fetch CoSA's own page (certificate error), so details rest on a search snippet. — <https://www.cosavfx.com/flashback-friday/flashback-friday-poi/>
- **[secondary]** Legal position, as far as I could find: I could not find a registered trademark for the HAL 9000 eye or a HAL 9000 mark held by Warner Bros. The HAL word marks the search surfaced belong to unrelated companies. Warner-licensed HAL replicas do exist (Master Replicas), so the character is treated as licensable IP. I found no report of anyone being challenged over a HAL-like red eye. Fan-made HAL eye SVGs sit on Wikimedia Commons under CC licences. The RED camera company's single-eye logo is described by press as resembling HAL, with no dispute found. For Terminator, the TERMINATOR word mark is registered (StudioCanal Image S.A.), and a snippet says T1, THE TERMINATOR and 'any depiction of Endoskeleton' are trademarks claimed by Studiocanal, so a chrome skull is the riskiest thing to draw. Absence of a found dispute is not clearance, and I did not check UK or EU registers. — <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HAL9000_red_camera_eye.svg>
- **[secondary]** Portal and GLaDOS pay homage by changing the light colour (a single fisheye seen with a blue light in one source, yellow in the core), and by changing the body. AUTO changes posture and adds spider-like hanging. GERTY changes the interface (smiley). None copies the red-lens-in-black-slab. The homage pattern is: keep one HAL trait (single unblinking light, calm voice), change colour, body and context. — <https://www.thephoblographer.com/2020/07/14/hal-9000-how-this-nikon-fisheye-lens-inspired-hollywood-and-culture/>

## Precedents

- **HAL 9000 (2001: A Space Odyssey, 1968)** — A circular fisheye lens, lit red with a yellowish centre, in a satin-black tall faceplate with a grey grille and a small blue-and-black corporate decal. No face. The lens shows curved reflections and visible aperture blades. **Lesson:** One real object, one light, total stillness, and a corporate label give a machine that is more frightening for being bland. It is the most-copied mark in this space, so a centred red circle in a dark square will read as HAL. — <https://kosmofoto.com/2019/09/nikon-fisheye-hal-9000-2001/>
- **Terminator T-800 (1984)** — Two small red lights in an exposed metal skull; the glow is a status signal, on means active. The point-of-view display is red-tinted with text readouts (from memory, not verified). **Lesson:** A red dot means 'on and hunting'. Pairing it with a skull adds a heavily claimed, over-used image; the light is the strong part, the skull is the cliche and the legal risk. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_(character)>
- **Cylon Centurion and KITT scanner (1978 / 1982)** — A red light sweeping left to right along a horizontal slit (32 bulbs on the Cylon), with a warbling sound. KITT uses the same idea. **Lesson:** Motion plus sound is the effect. The same mechanism serves villain and hero, so context decides. A sweeping bar is also the most cliched 'AI' image after the red dot. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KITT>
- **GLaDOS (Portal, 2007)** — A yellow-eyed core on a hanging cluster of wires and machinery, with coloured personality-core spheres attached. **Lesson:** The threat is voice and humour, passive-aggressive and polite. The visual is an unusual body, not a copy of HAL. This is the closest tonal match to BadCode's sarcastic, nurturing menace. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLaDOS>
- **AUTO (WALL-E, 2008)** — An autopilot in the ship's wheel, hanging like a spider, with a red eye (red detail from memory, not verified), voiced by MacInTalk. **Lesson:** Homage by posture, voice and borrowed music, so audiences recognise HAL without a copied shape. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUTO_(WALL-E)>
- **GERTY (Moon, 2009)** — A smiley-face display and a camera eye on a ceiling rail. **Lesson:** A friendly face on a corporate machine is a legitimate menace device and is not HAL-like at all. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_(2009_film)>
- **SHODAN (System Shock, 1994/1999)** — A silver face with green eyes and green lips with wires extending, plus a glitching, stuttering voice. **Lesson:** Deliberate corruption (glitch, stutter) is a different threat register from HAL's calm. It is also a cliche on YouTube already. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHODAN>
- **Eye of Sauron (Tolkien / Jackson)** — A flame-rimmed, cat-yellow eye with a black vertical slit pupil, a window into nothing. **Lesson:** A pupil that is a hole is scarier than one that glows. But a fiery vertical-slit eye is a well-guarded property, so avoid the flame rim. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron>
- **Person of Interest: the Machine and Samaritan (2011-16)** — No face, only point-of-view overlays: red concentric lines, an orbit, white triangles and small labels over live footage. **Lesson:** An intelligence can be a way of seeing. Thin lines and tags applied to real footage read as surveillance, which suits BadCode's video-first channels. — <https://www.cosavfx.com/flashback-friday/flashback-friday-poi/>
- **RED Digital Cinema logo** — A single-eye style logo which press describes as a stark resemblance to HAL 9000 and the Nikon 8mm lens. **Lesson:** A HAL-like single eye has been used as a real company logo for years with no dispute I could find. Legal risk from the eye alone looks low, but it is crowded and unoriginal. — <https://www.thephoblographer.com/2020/07/14/hal-9000-how-this-nikon-fisheye-lens-inspired-hollywood-and-culture/>

## Clichés to avoid

- A centred red circle with a yellow or orange centre on a black rectangle (HAL, verbatim).
- Two glowing red eyes in a chrome skull or robot face (Terminator, and Studiocanal claims depictions of the endoskeleton).
- A red bar sweeping left to right (Cylon/KITT scanner) as the whole logo.
- A wired or circuit-board face, a brain made of circuits, or a green digital-rain look (SHODAN, Matrix).
- A flame-rimmed vertical-slit eye (Sauron) or an eye in a triangle or pyramid (Illuminati or Big Brother clip art).
- Glitch or stutter text as the identity: it is already the default 'dangerous AI' YouTube look.
- Curly braces, angle brackets, cursors or terminal prompts (the cliche the founders already rejected).
- A red dot alone on black, which at small sizes reads as a notification badge or a live or record indicator.

## Risks

- **(high)** Reading as HAL: a centred red round light on a dark ground will be recognised as HAL 9000 by older viewers and named as such by everyone else. — Nobody I found has been challenged over it, but originality is the founders' stated requirement. Differentiate by geometry, placement, colour or behaviour, not by tweaks to a red circle.
- **(medium)** Trademark and copyright: I found no HAL 9000 registration, but I did not search UK, EU or copyright-office records, and a search snippet says Studiocanal claims endoskeleton depictions. Avoid skulls and chrome endoskeleton shapes. Check UK IPO and EUIPO for the final mark before committing. — This is research, not legal advice. Absence of a found dispute is not clearance.
- **(medium)** Colour collision: red on top of the team's previously picked red/blue brace mark (from repo memory, #cc2b37 and #2696d4) plus a red dot raises the 'party colours' risk already noted for the target UK reader, and red also reads as an alarm or a live badge in feeds. — Test the eye in an off-red (deep amber, red-orange) as well as red, and check it beside UK party palettes. Recalled from repo memory, not verified on the web.
- **(medium)** Small-size failure: a detailed lens, reflection or catchlight vanishes at favicon and avatar size and leaves a plain dot. — Any secondary detail must be optional; the mark must still be distinct as a single flat shape at 16px.
- **(low)** Audience reference gap: HAL is a 1968 film. Many working-class UK viewers on TikTok will know Terminator or GLaDOS-style tropes better than HAL, and the mark must work with no reference at all. — Not verified; a judgement from general knowledge.
- **(medium)** Menace without warmth: pure threat contradicts the brand voice, which is sarcastic and nurturing underneath. A cold eye alone tells the story's dark half only. — GLaDOS and GERTY show that wit, politeness and a friendly surface can carry menace better than a stare.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The Side-Eye

**Sketch in words:** A black disc (circle) with a thin ring of dark grey around it. Inside sits one small red dot about one quarter of the disc's diameter, pushed hard against the right-hand edge of the disc, at 3 o'clock, so the eye is looking sideways at the wordmark placed to its right. No highlights. Wordmark in plain lowercase 'badcode' set close to the disc. In the video ident the dot slowly slides from centre to the right edge, holds for a second, then the wordmark lights.

**Why it fits:** Gaze direction is character. A machine looking sideways at you or at the work reads as watching and judging with dry contempt, which is the sarcastic-narrator voice, and it is not HAL, whose eye stares straight out. It is drawable in three pen strokes.

**Weakness:** A sideways glance can read as cute or comic, or as a shifty emoji. The menace has to come from the ident's motion and voice, and it still uses a red dot in a dark circle, close to HAL at thumbnail size.

### The Blade Pupil

**Sketch in words:** A solid black circle with no outline. Inside it one thin vertical slit of light, red-orange, about one twelfth of the circle's width and about 60 percent of its height, dead centre. Nothing else. On video the slit is a blade of light that opens outward into a dot (a blink) and closes again.

**Why it fits:** BadCode's register is one thin vertical blade of cold light in near-black, and the Sauron precedent says a pupil that is a slit or hole is scarier than a glowing ball. The mark recycles the house image, not HAL's lens.

**Weakness:** It reads as a power or pause slot, a cat's eye or Sauron's pupil, and dropping the flame rim is essential. A red slit on black can also look like a battery or notification bar at 16px.

### Record, Lens, Recording

**Sketch in words:** A black circle like a vinyl record with about three thin dark-grey concentric grooves near the edge. At the centre, where the record label would be, one solid red dot about one fifth of the diameter, with no hole through it. The red dot is a lens, the REC dot on a camera, and the spindle of a record in one shape.

**Why it fits:** The three uses are a record sleeve (music), a recording light (surveillance, filmed evidence) and a lens (the watching machine). One shape carries the music and the watching, and reads at any size because the disc is a simple silhouette.

**Weakness:** It can look like a generic vinyl or 'live' icon, and a red dot in the centre of a black circle is HAL's arrangement again unless the grooves stay visible and distinctive. It risks looking like a record label rather than an AI.

### The Catchlight From The Future

**Sketch in words:** A black circle. A red dot, about one third of the diameter, slightly below centre. On the dot's upper-right edge sits one tiny cold blue-white vertical sliver (about one tenth of the dot's height and a hair wide), like a reflection. Nothing else. The sliver is the 'blade of light' from the house register, seen reflected in the machine's eye.

**Why it fits:** A reflection is what makes a lens look alive and looking at you, and the blue sliver ties the eye to the existing visual language and to the red-and-blue palette; the reflected light is the future watching the present.

**Weakness:** The sliver is lost at favicon size, leaving a plain red dot in a black circle, which is HAL. It also adds a second colour to a mark that is meant to be one flat shape.

### The Empty Socket

**Sketch in words:** A plain square-ish rounded tile in near-black. In the tile a circle outline only, in dark grey, empty except for a very small red dot resting on its lower rim at 6 o'clock, like a pupil that has dropped or a light left on. The wordmark sits below, centred.

**Why it fits:** The negative space carries the idea (the machine has gone and only a light is left), which fits BadCode being a message left from a timeline that ended. It is quiet and hardly a HAL copy.

**Weakness:** It is subtle, so it may read as a loading spinner or an empty checkbox, and the threat is weak without the ident's sound.
