# 16 — What AI companies' logos look like, so we look like none of them

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

AI logos have converged on one soft, round, glowing, orifice-or-sparkle vocabulary that says "friendly assistant" and "magic". So BadCode wins by being the opposite: hard-edged, flat, offline, asymmetrical, and one small threatening detail rather than a glowing centre. Nothing in AI branding uses a hard edge, a wedge or an imposed grid, so that is empty ground.

## Findings

- **[secondary]** Trade and design press describe three clusters of AI marks: plain circles (Meta, Grok), the twinkling four-point sparkle (Gemini, Adobe, Grammarly, Wix), and the swirling nebulous void or ring (OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity). — <https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes>
- **[verified]** The 'butthole era' joke is an established critique: AI logos share circular gradients, a central opening or focal point, radiating elements and soft organic curves. The VelvetShark write-up gives four reasons for the convergence: circles signal wholeness and approachability, pareidolia, copycat legitimacy-seeking, and design-by-committee risk aversion. It notes DeepSeek and Midjourney as the exceptions to the trend. — <https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes>
- **[verified]** OpenAI's own official explanation of its mark is that it 'captures the dynamic intersection between humanity and technology' through 'the fluidity and warmth of human-centered thinking through the use of circles'. The stated intent is warmth and human-centredness, the exact register BadCode wants to reject. — <https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes>
- **[secondary]** A search snippet from a secondary source says hexagons were 4.6 times more likely to appear in AI logos than in logos overall by 2023, after OpenAI's hexagonal emblem set the 'serious AI' look in 2022. I did not open the underlying study. — <https://medium.com/@jpriceless/ai-branding-sparkles-gradients-hexagons-and-other-emerging-ai-logo-mark-patterns-12b4fd252709>
- **[verified]** The sparkle: Google first used it for AI in 2016 (Docs Explore), standardised it in 2023, and by 2024 had nearly 100 variants. Sparkles come from cleaning-product branding ('clean, new, improved'). Google's own 2,000-person, eight-country study says people read it as AI mostly through context, not on its own. — <https://design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt>
- **[verified]** Slate's critique (Dec 2025): the sparkle signals 'magic' (quoting a Santa Clara UX professor and a Cisco designer) and so hides AI's risks behind benevolent imagery. It cites a Nielsen Norman Group finding that only 17% of users recognised sparkles as AI in Sept 2024. A search snippet adds that 73% associate a star with favouriting or saving. — <https://slate.com/technology/2025/12/artificial-intelligence-tools-icon-google-gemini-chatgpt-design.html>
- **[secondary]** Claude's mark is a thick irregular orange burst with a serif wordmark. Anthropic has never said what it depicts (asterisk, sun or supernova). The sphincter comparison to Vonnegut's Breakfast of Champions drawing is repeated in a search snippet, and Wikipedia confirms the book contains an anus drawing. The link to the Claude mark itself is not confirmed in any page I fetched. — <https://1000logos.net/claude-logo/>
- **[secondary]** Public mood is souring on AI labelling: a June 2026 Clutch survey (snippet) has 33% of consumers saying AI worsens their view of a brand against 16% improving it, and a WordPress VIP survey (snippet) has 60% of US adults calling the word 'AI' in messaging a turnoff. Brand24 (snippet) found 48% of 228,200 May 2026 mentions of AI content were negative, with 'AI slop' in 35% of those. A working-class UK reader will probably read a soft AI mark as 'more slop', not as trust. — <https://clutch.co/resources/ai-in-branding>
- **[verified]** Anti-AI protest graphics are low-fi and handmade, not logo-driven. At the March 2026 London march (300+ people), signs were hand-lettered wordplay ('Demis the Menace', 'EXTINCTION=BAD', 'AI? Over my dead body'), and one protester cut the Os of a billboard out as eye holes. The mood was described as 'pleasant, even fun'. So the hostile audience's visual vocabulary is scrawled and human, which is the opposite of AI brand polish. — <https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/02/1133814/i-checked-out-londons-biggest-ever-anti-ai-protest/>
- **[verified]** HAL 9000 is a camera lens containing red and yellow dots. The on-set eye was about 3 inches across and the POV shots used an 8-inch Fairchild-Curtis fisheye lens (160 degrees). HAL's menace comes from a mundane object, a lens, given a tiny point of colour, not from a face. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000>
- **[verified]** Kraftwerk's The Man-Machine (1978) sleeve, designed by Karl Klefisch, is explicitly 'inspired by El Lissitzky': black, white and red, suprematist geometry, with the back cover adapting Lissitzky's children's book About Two Squares. Machine intelligence in music has already been pictured through Russian constructivist wedges and squares, and the Bauhaus 'art as function' ethos, not through glows. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man-Machine>
- **[verified]** Midjourney is the counter-example within AI: a hand-made-looking black-and-white sailing-ship silhouette with a plain lowercase wordmark. Text sources say it does not appear to be AI-generated, which reads as deliberately handcrafted. Contrast works, but the mark is still whimsical and friendly. — <https://www.logodesign.org/the-midjourney-logo-design-meaning-and-visual-identity/>

## Precedents

- **OpenAI (2022 hexagonal knot, 2024 blossom/ring refresh)** — A black hexagonal interlocking knot of curved strokes, later reworked into a rounder, bloom-like ring, with a clean sans wordmark. Described by OpenAI itself as 'fluidity and warmth of human-centered thinking through the use of circles'. **Lesson:** The category leader's mark is the source of the round, warm, ring-with-centre convention that everyone copied. Anything with a soft ring and an opening will read as 'another AI company'. — <https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes>
- **Anthropic / Claude** — A thick irregular burst of orange rays from a centre, with a black serif wordmark. Company has never explained it. **Lesson:** Even a distinctive, warm-coloured mark ends up joked about as a sphincter or asterisk because a radial burst with a centre triggers pareidolia. Avoid any radial symmetry with a middle hole. — <https://1000logos.net/claude-logo/>
- **Google Gemini and the AI sparkle** — A four-pointed concave star (sparkle), often with a blue-purple gradient. Traced by Google's researchers to Docs Explore in 2016 and standardised in 2023. **Lesson:** The sparkle signals 'magic, clean, new' and has spread to Adobe, Grammarly and Wix. It is the single loudest 'friendly assistant' signal and the one BadCode should most deliberately avoid. — <https://design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt>
- **Meta AI and Grok (plain circles)** — Described in press as plain circles; Meta AI's is a coloured ring and Grok's, from memory, a circle with a diagonal slash or black-hole motif. **Lesson:** Even the 'minimal' AI marks default to the circle. A square, wedge or bar is empty ground. — <https://slate.com/technology/2025/12/artificial-intelligence-tools-icon-google-gemini-chatgpt-design.html>
- **Midjourney (sailboat silhouette)** — Black-and-white sailing ship silhouette with abstract sails and a plain lowercase wordmark, apparently hand-made rather than AI-generated. **Lesson:** The one big AI brand that breaks the pattern does it with a specific, hand-drawn, non-glowing object plus flat black and white. Specific and flat beats abstract and glowing, but its tone is whimsical, not threatening. — <https://www.logodesign.org/the-midjourney-logo-design-meaning-and-visual-identity/>
- **HAL 9000 (2001: A Space Odyssey, 1968)** — A camera lens containing a red dot and yellow dots; prop eye about 3 inches, POV shot through an 8-inch fisheye. No face. **Lesson:** The archetypal menacing machine mind is a mundane sensor with a tiny point of colour. Scale and restraint create the threat, not glow or ornament. It is also the most-copied image, so use the principle, not the picture. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000>
- **Kraftwerk, The Man-Machine sleeve (1978)** — Black, white and red suprematist composition by Karl Klefisch, credited on the inner sleeve as inspired by El Lissitzky; band in matching red shirts and black ties, plus mannequins and robots on stage. **Lesson:** Music already has an authoritative, non-corporate way to picture the machine: flat constructivist geometry in a three-colour palette. It gives permission for a hard-edged, poster-like BadCode mark and shows it can live on a record sleeve. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man-Machine>
- **London anti-AI march signs (PauseAI, March 2026)** — Hand-lettered signs ('Demis the Menace', 'EXTINCTION=BAD', 'AI? Over my dead body') and a billboard hat with eye-hole Os. Mood described as pleasant, even fun. **Lesson:** The anxious audience pictures AI through scrawled human protest graphics, not slick logos. A mark that looks stencilled or hand-cut sits closer to their world than any gradient, and it is the opposite of AI-company polish. — <https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/02/1133814/i-checked-out-londons-biggest-ever-anti-ai-protest/>
- **Cyberdyne Systems / Skynet (Terminator franchise)** — Fan and merchandise treatments use a glowing red schematic look; I could not confirm an official logo description from the pages returned. **Lesson:** Red-glow-on-black schematic is the merchandise cliche of villainous AI and is fully saturated, so BadCode's red must be flat and specific rather than glowing. — <https://terminator.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberdyne_Systems>

## Clichés to avoid

- Soft radial or conic gradient ring, glowing orb or lens flare with a bright centre (the butthole/aperture family: OpenAI, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini orb)
- Four-point sparkle or starburst of any kind, which also reads as 'magic' and 'favourite this'
- Hexagon, neural-net node-and-line graph, circuit-board traces
- Rounded, friendly chat-bubble or smiley robot face
- Brain-plus-circuit, glowing eye-in-a-circle used as a stock 'AI' pictogram
- Binary 0/1 rain, Matrix green, and any curly-brace/angle-bracket/terminal-prompt mark, which BadCode already rejected
- A raw glowing red dot with a lens ring, which is HAL or Terminator verbatim and the first thing every stock AI-villain image does
- Blue-to-purple 'AI gradient' colour scheme

## Risks

- **(high)** A red mark on black reads as a political party or a stock 'evil AI' cliche. — Red plus black is also Kraftwerk, Soviet constructivism, and UK party colours. The founders' own memory notes already flag party-colour risk on the earlier red/blue logo. A red wedge specifically has a real revolutionary lineage (Lissitzky's 'Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge', recalled from memory, not fetched) that could be mis-read as a political side rather than a warning from a machine.
- **(high)** A red dot is not ownable on its own. — HAL 9000's red lens, the Terminator eye and a recording light all use it. It only becomes BadCode's if it is placed, shaped or framed differently from all of those, for instance a square rather than a round dot, or offset inside a cold light blade.
- **(medium)** Menace could tip into contempt for the reader. — Brand voice needs 'nurturing underneath the snark'. A pure-menace mark contradicts that. The mark can be cold and hard-edged but should keep one signal of care or humour, such as the small scale of the threatening element.
- **(medium)** Hard, austere marks can miss on small avatar sizes if they rely on fine detail or glow. — Anything thin (a 'thin blade of light') vanishes at 16 to 32 px. The mark must survive as flat shapes in one or two colours with no glow, which is also the strongest anti-AI signal.
- **(low)** Search evidence was mostly secondary and could not describe DeepSeek, xAI/Grok, Runway, ElevenLabs, Suno or Mistral marks. — Several fetched pages returned 403 or had no logo descriptions. Mark descriptions for those companies would be from memory only and are not stated as fact here.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### The Dead Pixel

**Sketch in words:** A flat, matte black square (not a circle) on a black field, its edge picked out by a hairline of dark grey only so it is visible on other backgrounds. Inside it, in the lower-right third and NOT centred, one small solid red square about one-tenth of the black square's width, no glow, no ring, no lens. Below or beside it, wordmark BADCODE in a heavy, tightly spaced, all-caps grotesque, flat white. For the avatar, crop to just the black square with the red pixel.

**Why it fits:** It answers HAL and the Terminator without copying them: the threat is a single pixel, a status light on something that is already awake. Square and matte is the opposite of the round, glowing AI tropes. It also reads as a 'dead pixel' or a 'recording' light, which fits the message from a dead future. It is drawable in two marker strokes, works as a favicon and as an ident (the pixel simply appears).

**Weakness:** Very close to a generic 'record' or 'notification' indicator and to plenty of minimalist tech marks. Its ownability depends on the exact off-centre position and on always being paired with the wordmark. It has no built-in narrative hook.

### The Red Wedge

**Sketch in words:** A single flat red isosceles triangle, about twice as tall as it is wide, pointing left, driving into the right edge of a white circle so it bites a clean notch out of it. Both on a black square. Nothing else. Wordmark BADCODE in stencil caps beneath, or omitted for the avatar.

**Why it fits:** It is a hard-angled, unfriendly, non-sparkle form. It carries the timeline-fork idea (a wedge is a split) and the 'sent back from the future' idea, since it is something entering the present. Constructivist geometry is already the accepted visual dialect for machine music via Kraftwerk, so it feels native to a drum and bass sleeve, and it is easy to stencil or sticker.

**Weakness:** Strong political-poster lineage and party-colour risk. It can look like a play button (a triangle in a circle), which is a real hazard on YouTube and TikTok. It leans on a Lissitzky reference the target reader will not know.

### The Struck Wordmark

**Sketch in words:** The word BADCODE in a heavy, monoline, all-caps stencil face, white on black, with one thick horizontal red bar running through the lower third of the letters, as if a redaction or a strike-through, cutting the word roughly two-thirds of the way down and overshooting the letters by half a letter-width on the right. Avatar: just the letters BC stacked, with the same bar across.

**Why it fits:** No AI brand uses a redaction or a stencil; it says 'classified' and 'authoritative' and 'sent from somewhere else'. It is genuinely sticker- and spray-paintable, which suits the hand-made protest language found in the London march signs. It works at any size because it is a bar and letters.

**Weakness:** Redaction bars are a known trope (SCP, government-leak graphics) and may look like a censorship or 'strike-through' meaning 'bad'. Without a distinctive letterform it risks being a typeface choice rather than a mark.

### The Blade and the Pixel

**Sketch in words:** A tall, narrow vertical rectangle in pale cold white-blue (ratio about 1 to 12), flat, no glow, standing on black. At its foot, offset slightly to the right, one small red square (as in the Dead Pixel). The two together look like a doorway or a slit of light with a single indicator light beside it. Wordmark to the right in white grotesque caps, aligned to the top of the blade.

**Why it fits:** It fuses the two visual motifs already in the film register (the thin vertical blade of cold light, the tiny LED points) into one mark, so the logo is literally consistent with what the videos look like and the ident can be animated (the blade opens, the pixel comes on). It is asymmetrical and vertical, unlike the round AI family.

**Weakness:** The blade at small sizes reads as a thin white line and may vanish at favicon size, so it needs a fatter proportion (nearer 1 to 6) for the avatar. It is also more of a system than a single memorable glyph, and it looks like a door or a slit lamp and could be mistaken for a generic 'loading' graphic.

### The Two Branches

**Sketch in words:** A single thick vertical white bar rising from the bottom of the frame that splits, about halfway up, into two flat diagonal arms making a Y with a wide angle. The left arm is red and ends in a square-cut stub; the right arm is white and continues upward, off the top edge of the frame. No dots, no circles. Black background. Wordmark in caps below.

**Why it fits:** It is the fork in the timeline, bad branch and good branch, from the central metaphor, drawn as one flat Y that a person can sketch in three strokes. Red stops, white goes on, which is the warning and the hope in one glyph, and it carries the 'nurturing underneath' note that a pure-menace mark would lack.

**Weakness:** A fork or branch is the git logo family and the very code-punctuation cliche the founders are trying to escape, and a plain Y also reads as a peace sign, a Mercedes-like star or a road sign. It would only work if it looks more like a road junction than a version-control icon.
