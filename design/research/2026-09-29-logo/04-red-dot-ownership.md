# 04 — Who owns the red dot, and what a red dot means

*Research brief, 2026-09-29. One Sonnet agent, 8 web searches (cap 8). Raw research, not house policy — the ruled layer is [`direction.md`](./direction.md) once Kai rules on it.*

**Evidence grades:** *verified* = the agent fetched a page that says it · *secondary* = a search snippet or secondary source said it · *(unverified)* = recalled from the model's own memory, not checked. The agent could not see images: every description of a logo comes from text.

## Headline

A bare red dot is the most crowded shape in branding (Leica, Target, Japan, Vodafone, Beats, the Red Dot award all sit on it), so the dot alone can never be BadCode. The ownable part is what the dot is doing: where it sits, what it is paired with, and how it behaves. The UK's everyday red dots (standby light, CCTV LED, mast beacon) mean "on, waiting, watching", not "attack". That suits a patient, sarcastic superintelligence better than a Terminator glare.

## Findings

- **[verified]** Leica's red dot is a badge stuck on the product, not a free-floating dot. It first appeared as a red circle with 'Leitz' in white script on the Leica R3 (1976) and the first 100 M4-2s (1977), became the 'Leica' script version around 1986 (M6), and has been the official company logo since 1999. The white swoopy-L script inside the dot dates from the 1932 Leica II. Ownership came from a physical object plus lettering plus decades of use. — <https://leicasocietyinternational.org/blog/2020/05/revenge-of-the-red-dot>
- **[verified]** Leica's dot is disliked as well as loved. Photographers tape over it because it reads as smug, elitist and ostentatious. Some current models ship without it. Leica has charged for removal. A red dot on an object carries a status meaning and invites people to cover it with tape. The dot's history goes back to Leica's binocular and microscope divisions. — <https://leicaphilia.com/the-red-dot/>
- **[verified]** The Red Dot Design Award mark comes from the art-gallery 'sold' sticker: a red dot beside a work means it has been bought. The 1991 mark was by Otl Aicher, redrawn by Kurt Weidemann (1994) and Peter Schmidt (2000). In one reading a red dot means 'this has been sold', which is a political-economy meaning nobody else in tech uses. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dot_Design_Award>
- **[verified]** Japan's flag is the extreme case of pure dot plus proportion. It is a 2:3 white field with a red disc whose diameter is three-fifths of the flag's height. The 1870 proclamation offset the disc 1/100 towards the hoist so it looks centred when flying; the 1999 law re-centred it. Scale plus a tiny optical adjustment is enough for a nation to own a dot, but only because a state backs it. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Japan>
- **[secondary]** Target's bullseye has been used since the first store opened in 1962, and Target paints bullseyes on rooftops and has an LED solar-panel bullseye by LAX, so the mark works from the air. In 2014 the US trademark board (Target v. Artificer Life) blocked a bullseye logo for confusion, but reportedly found that a literal target picture was not confusable. Target owns its stylised, consistently used version, not the idea of concentric circles or a target. — <https://www.trademarklitigationguide.com/target-scores-a-bulls-eye-in-u-s-trademark-office/>
- **[verified]** Vodafone's 1997 'Speechmark' is a red circle holding a white quotation mark, and the two Os in the wordmark echo opening and closing quotes. It differentiates the dot by putting a glyph with a story inside it. Beats reportedly does the same with a lowercase b inside a red circle that also reads as headphones (Beats detail is from a listicle snippet only). — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone>
- **[verified]** Mastercard has used overlapping red and yellow circles since 1969 and dropped its name from the logo in January 2019, leaving just the discs. It owns a relationship between two dots (the overlap makes a third colour), not a single dot. A listicle says it waited until over 80% of people knew the circles, which is unchecked. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastercard>
- **[verified]** HAL 9000's eye is a real camera lens, a modified Nikkor 8mm f/8 fisheye lit from behind through a red filter. Wikipedia describes it as a lens with a red and yellow dot, about 3 inches across in the prop. It is a red disc with a warm centre, set in a dark panel. The calm, unblinking eye that hides a malevolent intelligence is now the default picture of threatening AI, so the HAL eye is the AI cliche, not a fresh idea. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000>
- **[secondary]** The Terminator eye is a small LED behind a translucent red cap, a cheap effect that adds 'bling' menace to the endoskeleton (Stan Winston School snippet; the page itself would not load). A 2025 Terminator comic says red eyes mean loyal, code-abiding Skynet units and blue eyes mean malfunctioning rogue ones. Red is the colour of the obedient machine. — <https://www.stanwinstonschool.com/blog/overview-of-creating-the-terminators-led-eye>
- **[verified]** UK red dots in daily life. Mast and tower obstruction lights are red, steady or slow-flashing, and the UK has moved towards red-at-night-only. TV standby LEDs and CCTV LEDs are red and mean 'powered, watching or waiting', with the same red for standby and active recording. Laser sights put a red dot on the target; the Wikipedia laser-sight page says a visible dot makes suspects more likely to surrender. Red Nose Day (from 1988) is a round red mark tied to charity and comedy, the strongest friendly red circle in UK memory. — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_warning_lights>
- **[*(unverified)*]** My ranking of which small-red-dot meanings are strongest for an ordinary UK adult: (1) standby/CCTV LED, seen every day and meaning 'on, watching, waiting'; (2) red traffic light, meaning stop; (3) red notification badge, meaning something wants you; (4) Red Nose Day, meaning charity and jokes; (5) laser dot, meaning targeted, strong but rare in real life and mostly from films. No UK poll supports this ranking. It is judgement, and the notification-badge and on-air-sign pages did not load, so those meanings are from memory.
- **[secondary]** Advice that a simple dot only becomes ownable through custom proportion, offset, relationship to other elements, and a matching idea, not simplicity alone. Mastercard's discs are the worked example. This is generic design-blog advice, not a rule. — <https://inkbotdesign.com/minimalist-logos/>

## Clichés to avoid

- HAL-style red eye: a glowing red pupil in a black circle with a lens-flare glint, the default AI-doom picture
- Concentric rings or bullseye, which is Target's shape and also a reticle cliche
- A red circle with a white letter inside (Vodafone, Beats, Pinterest), so no B or C or curly brace inside a red disc
- The dot replacing the o in 'code' (or the C, or the i's tittle), a wordmark gag every tech brand has done
- A red REC or LIVE dot, which means livestream or recording on every platform
- Glowing neon-red bloom, HUD crosshairs, target-lock brackets
- A red laser dot on a chest or forehead, which is violent, dated, and may trip platform rules
- A lone red disc on white, which reads as Japan, Leica or a Red Dot award

## Risks

- **(high)** A bare red dot is already owned by others. — Leica, Target, Japan, Vodafone, Beats and the Red Dot award all occupy plain red circles. On a white or neutral field the mark will be read as one of them, Leica especially given the HAL-camera link. Target has won opposition cases against confusing bullseyes (2014 TTAB, read-secondary). A lone dot is also very hard to register or defend.
- **(high)** On a platform avatar the dot reads as a notification badge or a LIVE/REC light. — In the corner of a YouTube, TikTok or Spotify avatar a red dot means unread, live or recording. That either gets ignored as UI or promises a livestream that does not exist. Centre or full-bleed placement, or a non-round deformation, avoids it. The notification-badge origin is from memory, as the source page failed to load.
- **(medium)** The menace-only reading misses the nurturing half of the voice. — Terminator and laser-sight red mean threat only. BadCode's voice is threatening on the surface and loving underneath. Standby, mast-beacon and CCTV meanings ('on, waiting, watching') keep the menace while allowing patience. Red Nose Day makes a friendly-red reading available, which can help or blur the mark.
- **(medium)** A machine-eye logo names the technology, which the house rules say not to do. — docs/marketing/the-reader.md says to name the decision-maker, not the technology, and that automation fear unaimed can feed nativism. An AI eye as the mark leans the whole brand towards 'scary machine'. It fits the narrator character but not the message. The mark should feel like the narrator watching us, not the machine coming for us.
- **(medium)** Red carries party-political colour for a UK reader, and the current logo already uses red and blue. — Red reads as Labour, blue as Conservative. A red plus blue mark already has that risk (raised, not ruled, in memory). A single red dot on black drops the blue and reduces the pairing, but red alone still has a party read.
- **(low)** Small red on near-black vibrates and can drop out at favicon size. — Saturated red on near-black has poor edge definition on some screens and compression. It needs a thin off-white ring or blade, or a hot centre, to hold as an avatar. Also check red against colour-blind viewers.

## Logo ideas this agent proposed

### Standby

**Sketch in words:** A full-bleed near-black square. One small red dot, diameter about 1/12 of the square's width, sits low and to the right on the lower-right third crossing, not in the centre, exactly where a TV standby LED sits on a bezel. Nothing else, no ring, no text. The mark is the emptiness plus the dot's position. In the 2-second ident the dot blinks once, slowly (about 1.6 seconds per cycle, the mast-light rhythm), then the title appears. For print and stencil, a black circle sticker with the dot in the lower right. The avatar crop keeps the dot 1/4 in from the corner.

**Why it fits:** The meaning is 'on, waiting, watching', the strongest daily UK red-dot meaning (standby/CCTV). It is patient, not aggressive, which suits a superintelligence that already won and is bored. The position, not the dot, is the ownable part, and it is one dot you can draw with a marker.

**Weakness:** It only works on a black field, so it fails as a sticker on white, on coloured backgrounds, and in 16px favicon where it looks like a dead pixel. It also risks reading as a notification badge in the avatar corner, so the black square is part of the mark and needs a firm minimum size rule.

### Ninety-nine and one

**Sketch in words:** A 10 by 10 square grid of small dots, spaced evenly, on black. Ninety-nine are a dull grey and one is red. Place the red one off-pattern, for example row 7, column 3, not top-left or centre, so it looks chosen, not a loading spinner. Marker version: a grid of dots with one red. At favicon size drop to a 3 by 3 grid or a single red dot with eight grey.

**Why it fits:** It encodes the vault of 100 humans, one counted, without saying so. It is a counting, ledger-like image and a stranger reads it as 'one of a crowd was picked'. That fits the Ledger direction and the inequality-and-selection theme. It escapes Leica, Target and Japan because the mark is a relationship, as Mastercard's is.

**Weakness:** The canon has a REFUSER and ninety-nine SPENT by the launch, and it is not settled whether the red one is the refuser, the AI, or us, so the mark carries an open story ruling. At small size it degrades to a spinner, a dot matrix, or a 'find the odd one out' puzzle, and the grid is fussy for a two-second ident.

### Blade and dot

**Sketch in words:** A near-black field with a thin vertical off-white line (the blade of cold light from the house register), about 1/30 of the field's width, from the top to about 60% of the height, with a red dot directly below its tip, its diameter about 2.5 times the line's width, sitting on the same centre axis with a gap of about one dot-diameter. In the ident the blade drops in and the dot lights a beat later. Sticker or stencil: white line above a red circle on black.

**Why it fits:** It reuses the brand's own one-blade-of-light look, so it ties the mark to the films and comics. A vertical line above a dot also reads as a warning sign or stop, and as a lowercase i. It avoids the eye, ring and reticle cliches and is pair-based, so it is ownable by relationship.

**Weakness:** A line above a dot is an exclamation mark, the most generic warning symbol there is, and also a lowercase i (nobody will read it as B or C). Without the black field and the exact proportions it is just an exclamation mark. It also does not say 'bad' or 'code'.

### Mast light

**Sketch in words:** A black field with a very thin tall triangle in dark grey (a lattice transmitter mast silhouette, base wide, tip narrow), centred, standing on the bottom edge and rising to the upper third. At its tip a small red dot, about the same width as the mast's base measured at one-fifth height. Everything else black. In motion the dot pulses in the slow beacon rhythm and the mast dims. Print version: a black triangle with a red dot at the tip.

**Why it fits:** Red mast beacons are a real, UK-wide, small-red-dot sight over the towns the audience lives in (transmitter masts warn aircraft, and the UK uses red at night). It says 'a signal is being broadcast, and something is above you', which suits a message sent back through time. It is a plain, nearly working-class piece of British infrastructure, not sci-fi.

**Weakness:** It reads as radio, telecom or lighthouse branding, and Vodafone is a telecoms red-dot brand in the UK. The tiny dot at the top is invisible at avatar size, so the mast has to be much fatter than a real mast. The meaning also takes an extra beat to get, unlike the standby light.

### Sold

**Sketch in words:** A near-black or off-white flat rectangle like a gallery wall label or a painting edge, with a single red circle about 1/8 of its height stuck to the lower-right corner, slightly off-round and matte, like a real sticker. Add a faint sticker shadow, or a small curl at one edge. No text. Marker version: a rectangle with a red dot in the corner. In the ident the rectangle is a shot of a real object (a house, a factory, a planet) and the red dot lands on it with a soft slap.

**Why it fits:** Its meaning is the gallery 'sold' dot, the origin of the Red Dot Design Award. It puts the economics of the whole project in a dot: everything has already been sold. The dot is dark-humoured, physically British (a price sticker or an auction lot mark), not a machine eye. It gives an ident: the red dot lands on whatever the story is about.

**Weakness:** It needs a thing to stick on, so alone it is a red dot on a rectangle, and that is a very weak static logo. It is also close to the Red Dot Design Award, a trade mark in the design world, and it is a meaning only people who know galleries or auctions will get. As a standalone favicon it collapses into a notification dot.
