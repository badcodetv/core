# 13 - Register and genre grammar

## 1. The short answer

- Genre grammar is a set of learned conventions (lighting key, lens choice, angle, camera stability), not a natural law — an audience reads "noir" or "horror" because it has seen those codes before, not because the codes are inherently menacing.
- To make architecture read as **oppressive** rather than merely large: put a human figure in frame at a scale that is visibly, uncomfortably small against it.
- Symmetry plus a single, hard, motivated light source in a mostly-dark frame is the fastest route to "monumental and inhuman" — it reads as designed-to-dominate, not lived-in.
- Deep, converging perspective (a corridor, a colonnade, a line of columns) receding into shadow reads as institutional power in both Soviet monumentalism and Deakins' Blade Runner 2049 interiors — same grammar, opposite politics.
- For **documentary/archive** authenticity: visible imperfection reads as truth. Grain, unstable framing, uneven exposure, and an unmotivated cut all signal "this was recorded, not staged" — even when, as film theorists point out, all of those things can be faked.
- Sync-sound-era observational documentary (cinéma vérité) sells authenticity partly through the *absence* of polish: no reframing to catch the "perfect" composition, camera visibly reacting a beat late.
- Beautifying suffering is a real, named ethical trap in documentary theory — a subject filmed too beautifully can read as spectacle rather than testimony, and theorists split hard on whether that's ever acceptable.
- Noir = low-key lighting + skewed/extreme angles + deep focus, all descended from German Expressionism; horror borrows noir's underexposure and adds withheld information (frame edges empty, negative space that "could" contain something); sci-fi trends the opposite way — bright, sharp edge-to-edge, wide lenses that make space itself feel vast and abstract.
- For BadCode specifically: monumental/brutalist register survives our constraints almost completely (it's a framing and lighting problem, solvable in a single still or an 8-second static-camera clip). Documentary register survives partially — the *visual* grain/wobble/exposure codes transfer to a generated clip; the codes that come from *lived process* (unrehearsed reaction, sync sound, an editor cutting real found footage) do not, and faking them sits on real ethical terrain because our film is about a real, true subject (money creation).

## 2. The findings

**Human-scale contrast is the mechanism, not size itself.** — Grade **p**. Alexey Kozhenkov's brutalist photo series *Spaces for Winds* is built around lone human figures looking up at massive concrete buildings specifically to "emphasize the sense of scale" — the building isn't legible as oppressive until a body gives it a ruler. (ArchDaily, summarizing Kozhenkov's series) — [archdaily.com/994163](https://www.archdaily.com/994163/colour-composition-and-scale-analysing-brutalist-photography)

**Roger Deakins deliberately avoided obvious light sources in Blade Runner 2049's monumental interiors.** — Grade **p** (single named practitioner, industry-press paraphrase, primary interview not independently re-verified this sweep — the direct ASC article 403'd). Deakins is reported saying some of the sets were "very severe, just square walls with no windows or obvious light sources," and that he wanted the interiors of "this huge, monolithic building" to always carry the feeling of moving sunlight — achieved via a water-as-ceiling caustic-light rig. — reported via multiple trade sources summarizing Deakins/Villeneuve interviews *(unverified in sweep — primary ASC article blocked, secondary aggregation used)* — [theasc.com/article/deakins-blade-runner-2049](https://theasc.com/article/deakins-blade-runner-2049/), [variety.com Deakins interview](https://variety.com/2017/artisans/awards/blade-runner-2049-roger-deakins-interview-1202579857/)

**Blade Runner 2049's architecture was explicitly built on Brutalism, and Deakins used its geometry to frame the protagonist as dwarfed.** — Grade **p**. Brutalist architecture (specifically London examples) was the stated key visual reference; harsh geometric buildings were used to create "frames within frames," with the architecture reading as more imposing precisely when it dwarfs the character in the same shot. — StudioBinder analysis of Deakins/Villeneuve's stated approach — [studiobinder.com/blog/blade-runner-2049-cinematography-analysis](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/blade-runner-2049-cinematography-analysis/)

**Brutalist architecture's "cold, oppressive" reputation is itself a critical judgment, not a physical property — the same buildings photograph as beautiful when the frame emphasizes texture and shadow instead of scale.** — Grade **p (mixed with A-adjacent architectural criticism)**. Brutalism has been "continuously criticized" for monumentality and "a perceived lack of care for human scale," dismissed by critics as cold "concrete monsters" — but photographers like Peter Chadwick and Simon Phipps reframe the same buildings as an "expressive language" of raw concrete, light and shadow, without the oppressive read. This is evidence that the *feeling* (oppressive vs. beautiful) comes from the photographic choices (scale reference, human presence, angle) layered on top of the architecture, not the architecture alone. — ArchDaily / architecturecourses.org — [archdaily.com/994163](https://www.archdaily.com/994163/colour-composition-and-scale-analysing-brutalist-photography), [architecturecourses.org](https://www.architecturecourses.org/learn/distinctive-characteristics-brutalist-architecture)

**Soviet monumentalism used deep perspective and scale explicitly as ideological instruments, and the visual grammar is functionally identical to the sci-fi-dystopian register.** — Grade **P** (institutional/historical consensus, multiple academic sources agree on function even if framed differently). Lenin's "monumental propaganda" strategy treated visual monumental art as core to propagating revolutionary ideas; Stalinist architecture from the late 1930s shifted to "stark, imposing exteriors meant to communicate the overwhelming strength and power of the regime"; Moscow Metro station photography used "deep perspective to emphasize the curving and spacious passageways," described as functioning as "monuments, ideological markers endowed with an almost mystical aura by their positioning in space." — academic summary via Wikipedia "Monumental propaganda," Oxford thesis on Stalinist monumental art, Nailya Alexander Gallery exhibition notes — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_propaganda), [ora.ox.ac.uk Stalinist Monumental Art](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:99e691d0-9f67-455a-8dbd-9bf34c79ea01/files/m13597041ea3fdd482e5fa3699db062f4), [nailyaalexandergallery.com](https://www.nailyaalexandergallery.com/exhibitions/the-art-of-photography-and-architecture-in-soviet-russia-1920s-1930s)

**Tarkovsky's Stalker builds monumentality out of duration and stillness as much as scale — extremely long takes (some over three minutes), forward dollies into space, and compositions that grow more visually crowded as dread increases.** — Grade **p**. The film's shot-length distribution shows medium and long-duration shots filling out the graph considerably more than typical cinema of its era; Tarkovsky photographs the human head as "monumental: sculptural and philosophical," and one signature composition shows a lone figure dwarfed in a tunnel lined with stalagmites/stalactites. — nostalghia.com (Filippo Schillaci academic paper on Tarkovsky's shot-duration patterns), cinephiliabeyond.org — [nostalghia.com PDF](http://www.nostalghia.com/TheTopics/Tarkovskij-for-cinemetrics.pdf), [cinephiliabeyond.org/stalker](https://cinephiliabeyond.org/stalker/)

**Documentary "authenticity" markers are a designed code, not raw capture — natural lighting, on-location sound, and visible imperfection are choices that signal truth, and the code can be faked.** — Grade **A (mixed)** on the theoretical claim (widely held in film theory, but the specific sourcing here is a course-summary tier, not a named peer-reviewed study; treat as well-established academic consensus rather than a single citable study). Cinéma vérité's hallmark grammar — grain, unstable frames, abrupt cuts — is explicitly noted as reproducible by fiction, which is described as "a fundamental paradox" of the mode: the very thing meant to guarantee truthfulness is a style, separable from the truth-claim. — Fiveable Film Theory course notes (student-facing summary, not primary — treat as **p**-adjacent secondary synthesis, not full A), Backstage.com — [fiveable.me/key-terms/cinema-verite](https://fiveable.me/key-terms/introduction-to-film-theory/cinema-verite), [backstage.com cinéma vérité guide](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/what-is-cinema-verite-examples-75673/)

**Aestheticizing poverty and suffering in documentary is a live, named ethical controversy with no resolved consensus — some theorists hold the documentarian's obligation is to the true, not the beautiful, and beautification is treated as a form of "aesthetic violence."** — Grade **A (mixed)** — this is contested academic territory, not settled fact, and is reported as such. Named case: photographer Alejandro Chaskielberg's drought-in-Africa images, criticized for being "beautifully lit... reminiscent of advertising photography" while documenting hardship, used as a touchstone in the ethics-of-aesthetics debate; a FIELD journal piece frames Walker Evans/James Agee's *Let Us Now Praise Famous Men* explicitly through the lens of "aesthetic violence" done to its poor subjects; researchers on *Rich Hill* (2014) find its visual/sound style "aestheticizes poverty... obscuring class dynamics." — Open College of the Arts, FIELD journal, ResearchGate (Rich Hill study) — [oca.ac.uk/weareoca/photography/the-ethics-of-aesthetics](https://www.oca.ac.uk/weareoca/photography/the-ethics-of-aesthetics/), [field-journal.com Breaking and Entering](https://field-journal.com/issue-25/breaking-and-entering-poverty-and-aesthetic-violence-in-let-us-now-praise-famous-men/), [researchgate.net Rich Hill study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318674990_Aestheticising_rural_poverty_representations_of_white_working_class_youth_in_the_documentary_Rich_Hill_2014)

**Noir's visual grammar (low-key/chiaroscuro lighting, skewed and extreme angles, deep focus) descends directly from 1920s German Expressionism, carried to Hollywood by émigré directors and cinematographers.** — Grade **P** (broad, uncontested film-history consensus, multiple secondary sources agree, though none cited here is a single named landmark academic text — treat as strong practitioner/historical consensus rather than a single hard citation). Deep focus specifically lets foreground and background stay simultaneously sharp so the two planes can interact meaningfully within one composition — a compositional tool, not just a lighting mood. — NFI, First Draft Filmworks, lowthroes.com — [nfi.edu/film-noir](https://www.nfi.edu/film-noir/), [firstdraftfilmworks.com noir cinematography](https://firstdraftfilmworks.com/blog/cinematography-film-noir-techniques-legacy/)

**Horror and sci-fi sit at opposite exposure extremes as a genre-signaling convention: horror trends dark/underexposed with detail-starved shadow (negative space that could conceal something), sci-fi trends bright/overexposed and sharp edge-to-edge with wide lenses that make space read as vast and abstract.** — Grade **p** (single-source framing, practitioner blog, not cross-verified against a second named DP this sweep — flag as *unverified in sweep*, plausible but not independently corroborated). Cites Spielberg's stated preference for the 21mm focal length to produce this "vast, almost abstract" sci-fi look. — tylerwilliamsdp.com (working DP's blog) — [tylerwilliamsdp.com/blog/the-cinematography-of-fear](https://www.tylerwilliamsdp.com/blog/the-cinematography-of-fear)

## 3. What survives our constraints

**Fully survives (still image or single static/simple-move 8-second clip, no crew needed):**
- Human-scale contrast — put one small figure in a vast frame. Pure framing decision, works in a single still.
- Symmetry + single motivated light in a dark field. Pure lighting/composition decision.
- Deep converging perspective (corridors, colonnades, rows of anything receding into dark) — a framing and set/scene-design decision, achievable in one generated still.
- The noir/sci-fi/horror lighting-key distinctions (low-key vs. bright/sharp vs. dark/detail-starved) — all lighting and exposure decisions, fully controllable per-shot with no actor or blocking dependency.
- Grain, slight frame instability, imperfect/off-center framing as documentary-authenticity signals — these are texture and framing choices that transfer directly onto a generated clip or a still.

**Survives partially:**
- Deakins' "moving sunlight through no obvious source" trick — the *look* (soft moving highlights on severe geometry) can be approximated in a still or clip, but the underlying rig (a physical caustic-light ceiling) is set-dependent craft; we'd be faking the light behavior, not building it, which is fine for a still but means we're choosing an effect, not solving a lighting problem.
- Tarkovsky's duration-driven monumentality — a very long static or near-static take is achievable within an 8-second clip only in miniature; the *feeling* Stalker builds through three-minute holds cannot be replicated at 8 seconds, so treat this as "slow held shot," not "Tarkovskian duration."
- Cinéma vérité's specific codes (grain, wobble, abrupt cut) transfer visually. But vérité's true authenticity claim rests on the footage being unrehearsed, reactive, and really-happened — that part cannot survive a generated clip, because a generated clip has no real event behind it. Using vérité's *visual* codes on invented footage is the aestheticization trap named in section 2, sharpened: **we would be manufacturing the signal of "this really happened" for something that didn't.** That is worth flagging explicitly to Jack/Kai before it's used on real subject matter like money creation — the ethical debate in section 2 exists precisely because visual authenticity and factual authenticity can come apart, and this project's job is factual argument, not a vibe.

**Does not survive at all:**
- Directed performance, blocking across a shooting day, coverage (multiple angles of one real event to cut between) — all of noir/horror's classic "build tension across a scene" craft assumes a crew and continuity across takes. We get one generated shot at a time with no guaranteed continuity between attempts.
- Archive footage's grammar of genuinely aged, physically degraded film stock and period-accurate sync-sound recording quality — approximable as a texture/grain effect, but if the footage is meant to read as *real archive of a real event* (rather than "documentary-styled" original footage), that is a `find-footage` sourcing problem, not a look problem, per this repo's lane rules.

## 4. Contested

- **Deakins' "practical, in-camera" instinct vs. the invented-caustic-light source.** Deakins and Villeneuve are reported as committed to shooting "everything practically... to give greater detail and weight to the imagery," yet the signature caustic water-ceiling effect is itself an engineered fake light source dressed as motivated light. This isn't a disagreement between two practitioners so much as an internal tension worth naming: "practical" in top-tier VFX-era cinematography doesn't mean "unstyled," it means "built physically, then filmed straight." For a shop with no set to build, this is worth sitting with rather than resolving — we can fake the *result* but not the *discipline* that produced it. *(unverified in sweep — inference from secondary sourcing, not a stated practitioner debate)*
- **Documentary theory: is the aesthetic ever legitimate, or always a compromise of the true?** Named split in section 2's sources — one camp (per the OCA/FIELD framing) holds the documentarian's obligation is to truth over beauty, treating aestheticization as a debt owed to the subject; another reading (the "bourgeois viewer" argument summarized from the suffering-aestheticization literature) holds that a beautified image can still do ethical work by forcing a privileged viewer to confront their own position relative to the depicted poverty. Neither side is named to a single individual in what surfaced this sweep — treat this as a live academic fault line to flag for MMT rather than a settled house position. *(unverified in sweep — no single named theorist pinned to either side this pass; worth a deeper follow-up before the documentary short locks its visual approach)*

## 5. Myths - never cite

- **"Brutalism is inherently oppressive architecture."** Not supported — the same buildings are shot as beautiful by photographers who choose texture/shadow over scale-contrast framing (section 2). The oppression reads from the photographic choice, not a property of poured concrete.
- **"Handheld/grainy footage is inherently more truthful."** Directly contradicted by the film-theory sources themselves — the codes are stylistically reproducible by scripted fiction, which is the "fundamental paradox" named in section 2. Never assert grain = truth without the caveat that it is a designed signal.
- **"Cinéma vérité captures reality unmediated."** The movement's own definition already concedes intervention — vérité "blends naturalistic observation with... deliberate interaction with subjects." It was never claimed to be unmediated by its own practitioners; treat any framing of it as "raw truth capture" as a myth.

## 6. Vocabulary worth having

| Term | Plain-English gloss | What it buys you |
|---|---|---|
| Human-scale reference | A person (or person-sized object) in frame that lets the eye judge size | Turns "big building" into "building that could crush you" |
| Chiaroscuro | Strong light-vs-dark contrast, little midtone | Signals moral ambiguity/menace (noir/horror) in one lighting choice |
| Deep focus | Foreground and background both stay sharp | Lets two things in a frame argue with each other without cutting |
| Negative space | Large areas of frame deliberately left empty/dark | Reads as threat (horror) or as scale/isolation (monumental) depending on context |
| Motivated light | Light that appears to come from a source visible or implied in the scene | Keeps a stylized shot feeling "real" instead of stagey |
| Converging perspective | Parallel lines (corridors, columns) that visually rush to a vanishing point | Fastest way to signal institutional power/depth in a single still |
| Diegetic imperfection | Camera flaws (wobble, late reframe, grain) that imply a real operator reacting in real time | The core authenticity signal in documentary/archive footage |
| Aestheticization (of suffering/poverty) | Making hardship look beautiful through lighting/composition/color | A named ethical trap — can read as spectacle instead of testimony |
