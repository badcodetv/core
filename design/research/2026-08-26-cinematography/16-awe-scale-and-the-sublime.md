# 16 - Awe, scale and the sublime

## 1. The short answer

- Awe has a real, standard, empirically-derived definition: **perceived vastness + a "need for
  accommodation"** (the experience does not fit your existing mental categories, so your
  concept of the world has to update). This is Keltner & Haidt's model, and it is what the
  house register is actually built to trigger — not just "big things look impressive." Grade **A
  (mixed)**: the two-appraisal model is the standard citation and widely used, but Keltner &
  Haidt's own 2003 paper is theoretical/prototype-based, not itself an experiment.
- Awe is not always positive. **Threat-based awe** — the same vastness-plus-accommodation
  structure but appraised as dangerous or uncontrollable (a tornado, a wrathful god, a
  terrorist attack) — is a documented, distinct variant, found in roughly 1 in 5 naturally
  reported awe experiences. **Grade A (mixed)**, single core study (Gordon et al. 2017) with
  follow-up replication/extension work, not yet a large multi-lab literature. This is the
  variant BadCode's narrator embodies: awed by its own catastrophe, not uplifted by it.
- Awe reliably shrinks the felt self relative to the world (the **"small self" effect**) and
  this measurably increases prosocial behaviour and reduces entitlement. **Grade A**: Piff et
  al. 2015, five studies, N=2,078, a mix of lab induction, field induction (a literal grove of
  tall trees) and a national survey — one of the better-replicated results in the whole awe
  literature.
- Awe is popularly claimed to slow or expand subjective time. **This is contested, not
  settled — grade A (mixed), do not state it as flat fact.** One well-cited study (Rudd,
  Vohs & Aaker 2012) found awe expands felt time availability; a later attempted replication
  (Stamkou & colleagues, published in *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*) found
  **no effect** of induced awe on implicit or explicit time perception, and a newer study finds
  the effect runs through perceived *vastness of space*, not awe generically. Treat "awe makes
  a moment feel longer" as a live, disputed finding, not a fact to build a shot list on.
- Burke's 1757 aesthetics predates the psychology literature by 250 years and describes our
  register almost exactly: the **sublime** (vastness, obscurity, darkness, power, terror at a
  safe distance) is a distinct and *greater* passion than the **beautiful** (smallness,
  smoothness, delicacy). Burke specifically names **obscurity and darkness** as instruments of
  the sublime — an unseen threat frightens more than a visible one. **Grade A** as intellectual-
  history fact (this is what the text says and is uncontested as exegesis), not an empirical
  claim about brains.
- Kant split the sublime into two kinds worth keeping separate: the **mathematical sublime**
  (overwhelmed by sheer size/magnitude — a server hall stretching past the edge of the frame)
  and the **dynamical sublime** (overwhelmed by a force that could destroy you, viewed from a
  position of safety — the machine that could crush you, but is filmed from where it can't
  reach you). BadCode's near-black monumental-machine shots are reaching for the dynamical
  sublime specifically: power you are safe from *right now*.
- On screen, awe is produced less by an object's absolute size than by **scale contrast made
  legible** — something human-sized in the frame that lets the eye compute how big the rest of
  it is — and by **withheld duration**: a slow reveal that makes the viewer's understanding
  visibly lag the image, rather than a cut that shows the whole thing at once. This is
  **practitioner-grade (p)** craft wisdom (named examples below), not a controlled study, but it
  follows directly from the "need for accommodation" appraisal: if the frame explains itself
  instantly, there is nothing left to accommodate.
- What kills awe on screen, by the same logic: **fast cutting** (no time to fail to assimilate
  the image before the next one arrives), **showing the whole object in one clean establishing
  shot** (nothing is withheld), **over-explaining in narration or score** (the meaning is handed
  to you, so there's no gap for the mind to fill), and **excess detail/business in frame**
  (competes with the vastness cue). Grade **p** — this is standard editing-craft reasoning
  extended from the accommodation model, not a cited finding.
- Fascist-monumental and BadCode-monumental use the *same shot grammar* for awe (see brief 13);
  what separates critique from celebration is almost entirely **who the vastness belongs to and
  who it dwarfs** — see §4.

## 2. The findings

**Awe = perceived vastness + need for accommodation, with five secondary appraisals (threat,
beauty, exceptional ability, virtue, the supernatural) that flavour the emotion positive or
negative.** — Grade **A (mixed)**. Keltner & Haidt propose this as the prototype structure of
awe from a review of religious, aesthetic and political awe experiences across cultures; it is
the field's standard citation but is itself conceptual/theoretical, not a controlled experiment.
Keltner, D. & Haidt, J. (2003), "Approaching awe, a moral, spiritual, and aesthetic emotion,"
*Cognition and Emotion*, 17(2). — [tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699930302297](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699930302297), summary via [greatergood.berkeley.edu (Shiota PDF discussing the model)](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/shiota.2007.pdf).

**Awe induces a "small self" — self-diminishment relative to something vast — which mediates
increased ethical decision-making, generosity and reduced entitlement, and reduced individual
self-focus more broadly.** — Grade **A**. Five studies (lab induction via video/nature scenes,
field induction standing among a grove of tall trees, and a national representative survey),
N=2,078. This is the strongest-replicated result in the corpus for this brief. Piff, P.K.,
Dietze, P., Feinberg, M., Stancato, D.M. & Keltner, D. (2015), "Awe, the Small Self, and
Prosocial Behavior," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. —
[apa.org PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-pspi0000018.pdf),
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25984788/).

**A negative, threat-based variant of awe exists as a distinct cluster, not just "awe plus
fear."** — Grade **A (mixed)**, single core study plus follow-up theoretical/empirical
extensions, so treat as directionally solid but not yet a mature multi-lab literature. Gordon
et al. (2017) sampled natural awe experiences (tornadoes, terrorist attacks, a wrathful-god
experience) and found ~20% clustered as threat-based: lower perceived self-control and
certainty, higher perceived situational control by the object/force, and elevated fear,
distinct in structure from positive awe. A named follow-up (Danvers & colleagues, "The Curious
Case of Threat-Awe: A Theoretical and Empirical Reconceptualization") pushes back on parts of
the original framing — the two do not fully agree on how clean the positive/threat split is
(see §4). Gordon, A.M. et al. (2017), "The Dark Side of the Sublime: Distinguishing a
Threat-Based Variant of Awe," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. —
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27929301/),
[ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354647759_The_curious_case_of_threat-awe_A_theoretical_and_empirical_reconceptualization).

**Awe expands subjective felt time — contested, not settled.** — Grade **A (mixed)**, live
contradiction inside the literature. Rudd, Vohs & Aaker (2012) found induced awe led people to
feel time was more plentiful and to prefer experiences over material goods. A later attempt
using implicit and explicit time-perception measures (not self-report of "time felt
available") found induced awe had **no effect** on time perception — a direct non-replication
of the mechanism, though not of the original self-report finding. A more recent study reframes
it: perceived *vastness of the space itself* predicts longer duration estimates, with awe as
the mediating variable, rather than awe alone doing the work. Do not build a claim like "hold
the shot longer because awe slows time for the viewer" on this — say instead that duration
correlates with awe, not that awe reliably manufactures duration. Rudd, M., Vohs, K.D. & Aaker,
J. (2012), "Awe Expands People's Perception of Time, Alters Decision Making, and Enhances
Well-Being," *Psychological Science*. — [bauer.uh.edu PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/mrrudd/download/AweExpandsTimeAvailability.pdf);
non-replication: [Springer, *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-019-01924-z);
vastness-mediation: [Springer, "The perceived duration of vast spaces is mediated by awe"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-022-02542-y).

**Burke: the sublime is produced by terror experienced at a safe distance, and obscurity/
darkness are its most reliable instruments — a clearly seen threat is less sublime than a
half-seen one.** — Grade **A** as textual/historical fact. Burke's 1757 *A Philosophical
Enquiry into the Origin of Our Ideas of the Sublime and Beautiful* draws beauty (smallness,
smoothness, delicacy, evoking love and relaxation) against the sublime (power, vastness,
obscurity, evoking terror and "delightful horror") and states that danger must be felt as
*possible but not present*. — Wikipedia summary of the primary text: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Philosophical_Enquiry...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Philosophical_Enquiry_into_the_Origin_of_Our_Ideas_of_the_Sublime_and_Beautiful),
secondary overview: [Philosophy Now, "Burke, Kant and the Sublime"](https://philosophynow.org/issues/11/Burke_Kant_and_the_Sublime).

**Kant: two distinct sublimes — the mathematical (overwhelmed by magnitude, engaging
theoretical reason) and the dynamical (overwhelmed by a destructive force, engaging practical/
moral reason, but felt from safety).** — Grade **A** as exegesis of the *Critique of Judgment*
(1790). Kant locates the sublime feeling not in the object but in the mind's recognition of its
own capacity to conceive of a totality (mathematical) or to morally withstand a force it could
not physically withstand (dynamical) — sublimity is "in the mind," not in the storm or the
building itself. — [1000wordphilosophy.com, "Immanuel Kant's Theory of the Sublime"](https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2023/11/11/kants-theory-of-the-sublime/),
[Philosophy Now overview](https://philosophynow.org/issues/11/Burke_Kant_and_the_Sublime).

**Kubrick's one-point-perspective corridors (2001's Discovery One, The Shining's Overlook)
use dead-centre symmetry and a vanishing point to make a human figure read as small and
"almost irrelevant" against the geometry, and critics read the device as coding technology and
institutional space as cold, controlling and indifferent.** — Grade **p**. This is
practitioner/critical film-writing consensus (multiple outlets making the same observation
independently, but no single named cinematographer's own words are quoted on *why*, and no
controlled study exists) rather than a cited empirical claim. — [Filmmaker Magazine, "The
'One-Point Perspective' in Stanley Kubrick's Work"](https://filmmakermagazine.com/85083-the-one-point-perspective-in-stanley-kubricks-work/),
[Far Out Magazine on Kubrick's one-point scenes](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/stanley-kubrick-one-point-perspective-scenes/).

**Human-scale reference is the mechanism that turns raw size into legible vastness** — a
building only reads as awesome/oppressive once a body in frame gives the eye a ruler; the
same architecture photographed without a scale reference reads as abstract pattern, not scale.
— Grade **p**, single named case (Alexey Kozhenkov's brutalist photo series, discussed in
brief 13 already, not re-argued here — see that brief's §2 for the citation).

**Tarkovsky builds monumentality out of duration as much as size: extremely long, near-static
takes (several minutes) that let dread and scale accumulate rather than announcing themselves
in a single wide shot; this is described by film scholars as "liturgical" rather than merely
slow.** — Grade **p**. Consistent with the accommodation model (more time = more room for the
mind to fail to assimilate the image) but this specific causal link is our inference, not a
claim any cited source makes about Tarkovsky directly. — [robertcmorton.com, "Discover the
Mesmerising World of Slow Cinema"](https://www.robertcmorton.com/the-art-of-slow-cinema/),
already-cited Tarkovsky shot-duration analysis in brief 13.

## 3. What survives our constraints

**Survives in a still image, fully:**
- Human-scale contrast (a small figure or human-scaled object against a vast dark field) — this
  is a pure composition/lighting decision, zero motion required, and is the single most
  load-bearing awe device we have.
- Obscurity/darkness as instrument of the sublime (Burke) — near-black with one thin light is
  literally the recipe; a still frame holds this indefinitely.
- The dynamical sublime's "safe distance" framing — the viewer is always at a safe distance from
  a still image by definition, so this one is free.
- Kubrick-style dead-centre one-point symmetry — a single frame, no movement needed.

**Survives partially in an 8-second clip:**
- The slow reveal (withholding the full scale until partway through the shot) survives in
  miniature: a slow push-in or a figure walking further into a vast space over 8 seconds can
  still visibly *add* information the first frame didn't have, which is the mechanism the
  "need for accommodation" appraisal actually requires. But 8 seconds is short enough that the
  reveal has to be simple and single-beat — one camera move, one new fact learned — not a
  multi-stage discovery.
- Threat-based/dynamical awe (the machine that could crush you) survives if the frame itself
  states the danger (scale, a hard edge, motion toward camera) rather than relying on narrative
  build-up we don't have time for.

**Dies, or needs a real film set / long-form duration:**
- Tarkovsky's multi-minute accumulation of dread through duration — the *feeling* a three-minute
  static take earns cannot be compressed into 8 seconds; treat any BadCode "slow held shot" as a
  gesture toward this tradition, not a replication of it.
- The disputed "awe expands felt time" effect (§2) is unproven even in ideal lab conditions with
  minutes of induction; there is no basis for expecting it from an 8-second clip, so don't design
  around it.
- Multi-stage scale reveals (small detail → medium → full vastness, classically done by a slow
  dolly or crane move across 20–40 seconds) don't fit in 8 seconds; compress to one stage only.

## 4. Contested

**Is threat-based awe a genuinely separate cluster, or just awe with a fear coefficient
added?** Gordon et al. (2017) argue for a distinct threat-based cluster with its own appraisal
signature (lower self-control/certainty, higher situational control by the threat, more fear).
A named follow-up reconceptualization ("The Curious Case of Threat-Awe," Danvers and
colleagues) pushes back on how cleanly this splits from ordinary fear or from positive awe,
arguing the theoretical and empirical picture is messier than a clean two-cluster model. Both
papers agree the phenomenon (awe that feels dangerous) is real; they disagree on whether it's a
structurally distinct variant of awe or a blend that needs a different model. For BadCode: this
matters less for craft (we're clearly reaching for the threat/dynamical end regardless of the
theoretical resolution) than for how confidently we can cite "threat-awe" as an established
category — cite it as a documented phenomenon, not a fully settled taxonomy.

**Does awe expand subjective time, or not?** Rudd, Vohs & Aaker (2012) say yes (self-report).
A later study using implicit/explicit time-perception tasks found no effect. Neither team is
disputing the other by name in what this sweep found, but the results directly contradict each
other on the same question using different methods — treat this as an open empirical question,
not resolved in either direction, and never state "the shot will feel longer because of awe" as
settled fact.

## 5. Myths - never cite

- **"Awe is always a positive, uplifting emotion."** False — roughly a fifth of naturally
  occurring awe experiences are threat-based (Gordon et al. 2017), and Burke's founding account
  of the sublime is built entirely on terror, not uplift.
- **"Bigger is more awesome, full stop."** Not supported as a simple size claim — the evidence
  points to *legible* vastness (with a scale reference and something the mind can't
  immediately assimilate) producing awe, not raw dimensions. An enormous object shot without any
  human-scale reference or context reads as abstract, not vast.
- **"Awe reliably makes time feel like it's slowing down, so hold the shot."** Contested at
  best (§2, §4) — one study supports it, one directly failed to reproduce the effect on time
  perception specifically. Don't cite it as a design justification.
- **"Symmetrical monumental framing = fascist aesthetics, full stop."** Wrong as stated — the
  same one-point-perspective, symmetrical-vastness grammar that reads as authoritarian in
  Riefenstahl or Soviet propaganda is also Kubrick's tool for making technology feel cold and
  indifferent, and Deakins' tool for making *Blade Runner 2049*'s corporate power feel dead
  inside (brief 13). The grammar alone doesn't carry the politics — who the shot dwarfs and
  whose gaze the frame adopts does (this brief does not fully resolve that question; it is a
  live BadCode design risk flagged by the critic under gap 3, not answered here).

## 6. Vocabulary worth having

| term | plain-English gloss | what it buys you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vastness | Something reads as much bigger than you and the things you're used to (can be physical or conceptual/status) | The trigger condition for awe — nothing else in this table works without it |
| Need for accommodation | The image doesn't fit your existing mental categories, so your understanding has to update mid-experience | The actual mechanism of awe, distinct from mere surprise; explains why slow reveals beat instant full reveals |
| Small self | Awe measurably shrinks the felt importance of the individual relative to the vast thing | The psychological payoff of a human-scale-contrast shot — not just "looks cool," it's the device that produces humility/connection in the viewer |
| Threat-based / dynamical awe | The vast thing is dangerous, not benign, but you are watching from safety | Names exactly what BadCode's narrator feels about its own catastrophe — awed, not just horrified |
| Mathematical sublime (Kant) | Overwhelmed purely by size/magnitude, no danger involved | Distinguishes a server-hall-stretching-to-infinity shot from a menacing-machine shot — same vastness, different emotional colour |
| Dynamical sublime (Kant) | Overwhelmed by a force that could destroy you, felt from a position of safety | The precise label for "monumental machine that could crush a human, shot so the viewer is not actually at risk" |
| Obscurity (Burke) | Deliberately not showing everything — darkness, shadow, partial reveal | The house near-black palette isn't just mood, it's the single most reliable sublime-instrument on record since 1757 |
| One-point perspective | A frame built around a single vanishing point, everything converging to dead centre | Makes a human figure read as small against architecture with zero extra effects |
| Scale reference | A human or human-sized object placed in frame specifically to let the eye judge size | Turns an abstract vast shape into a legible "this could crush you" |

## 7. Open questions

- Does the "need for accommodation" appraisal actually require *narrative* novelty (the viewer
  learning something new about the story), or does *pure visual* novelty (an unfamiliar shape or
  scale, no story content) suffice on its own? No source found in this sweep separates these
  two, and it matters directly for whether a static, story-neutral establishing shot can carry
  awe by itself.
- Is there any published research on awe and *editing rhythm* specifically (cut frequency,
  shot length) rather than on real-world/VR/lab stimuli? This sweep found none — the
  "fast cutting kills awe" claim in §1 is inference from the accommodation model, not a finding
  about film editing, and should be tested against actual audience response before being
  treated as more than a working hypothesis.
- Gap 3 (fascist-aesthetic risk) is adjacent to this brief but explicitly out of scope here —
  this brief establishes that the *same grammar* serves both awe-as-critique and awe-as-
  propaganda (§5), but does not attempt to resolve when BadCode's use tips into the latter. That
  remains gap 3's job.
- No source in this sweep addressed whether *generated* (AI) imagery can produce the "need for
  accommodation" appraisal at all, given that a generative model's vastness is synthetic rather
  than a photograph of something that really is that big — an open and BadCode-specific question
  no cinematography or psychology literature has reason to have asked yet.
