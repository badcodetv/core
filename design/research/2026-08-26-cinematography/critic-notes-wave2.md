## 2. Did they repeat wave one's grade-laundering?

Mostly no — and this is the second-most encouraging finding in this pass. Several briefs
actively *defend* against the exact pattern wave one was caught doing: brief 15 explicitly notes
its New Wave and Parr claims converge across "academic and reference sources... not just the
same three SEO blogs"; brief 18 catches its own "blue hour" claim resting on two content-mill
sources and downgrades it to p with the reasoning stated inline; brief 19 keeps Todd Klein's
lettering rules at p rather than inflating a single (if highly credentialed) practitioner to P;
brief 20 downgrades the crush/lift and shadow-protection claims to "p-adjacent" for lack of a
named colourist, even though the underlying craft consensus is broad.

That said, it did happen twice, and one instance is a step **worse** than anything in wave one:

- **Brief 18, "Shulman and the founding generation... corrected verticals as the discipline's
  baseline grammar," Grade P.** Sourced to `aboutphotography.blog` (the brief's own source
  table grades this **content-mill**) and `photoephemeris.com` (graded "practitioner-adjacent
  craft explainer" — itself not a named authority, just a second blog). Two blog-tier sources
  agreeing is exactly the pattern wave one named as laundering, and it produced a P grade here
  the same way it did in wave one's briefs 01, 07, 09, 12 and 13. The brief correctly separates
  the underlying optical geometry (genuinely A) from the craft-convention claim riding on top of
  it, but the convention claim itself is the same two-blogs-equals-P move, not a new failure —
  a straight repeat.
- **Brief 19, "50–75 characters per line is the standard print/screen readability range... for a
  caption... go shorter," Grade P.** This is worse than laundering, not an instance of it: there
  is **no citation anywhere in the brief for this specific claim** — no §2 paragraph, no source
  row, nothing. It appears once in §1 with a P grade and is never substantiated. Laundering
  launders *something* (three weak sources into one strong-looking one); this is a P grade
  manufactured from zero sources, which is a step down from the pattern being screened for, not
  a repeat of it exactly, but the same underlying failure — a confident number with no
  evidentiary trail — and arguably more concerning because there is nothing to trace at all.

One borderline case worth naming rather than either clearing or convicting: **brief 17 grades
the *Metropolis*/*Brazil* dystopian-architecture claim "A (mixed)" off a single ResearchGate
paper that was never fully read, only summarised** ("unverified in sweep — full paper not
independently re-read"). A grade of A for a single unread primary source, distinguished from
wave one's problem (many weak sources → inflated grade) only by being one un-verified source →
a grade that implies more confidence than "we read the abstract" earns. Not the named pattern,
but adjacent to it and worth flagging for the same reason: the grade outruns what was actually
checked.

## 4. New contradictions

1. **Brief 18's flat vertical-convention rule vs. brief 17's own headline thesis.** Brief 18
   states, without much hedge, that corrected/straight verticals read as "monumental/
   permanent/institutional" while converged verticals read as "unstable/dynamic" — a fairly
   deterministic pairing. Brief 17's own §1 opens with "the grammar is genuinely neutral; the
   reading is built by what surrounds it" — the exact caveat wave one's critique said was going
   missing from executive summaries (its item under "Executive-summary hedging loss"). Brief 18
   doesn't contradict 17 outright (verticals are a narrower claim than general monumental
   grammar) but repeats the same shape of overclaim wave one flagged, sitting right next to a
   sibling brief that states the opposite discipline. **17 is better evidenced here** — it's
   built on multiple independent case studies (Kubrick vs. Riefenstahl vs. Deakins) rather than
   two craft blogs.

2. **Brief 18's Salgado-derived "aerial reads as pattern, not scale" vs. wave one brief 05's
   "overhead/God's-eye framing... cheap to generate, structurally suited to a narrator who
   already knows how it ends" (ranked #10 in wave one's top 20, P-grade).** These aren't a
   logical contradiction — omniscience and legible-scale are different jobs an overhead shot can
   be asked to do — but they license opposite instructions for the same camera position if
   BadCode reaches for an overhead shot specifically to convey *awe/scale* rather than
   *omniscience*. Brief 18's claim is the better-evidenced one for the scale question
   specifically (named critical descriptions of Salgado's fifty thousand miners reading as
   "ants," "dots," an "abstract design," versus brief 05's more general P-grade claim). Practical
   fix, not flagged by either brief: an overhead shot buys omniscience for free but has to earn
   scale separately (a legible reference object even from above), and nobody has written that
   down yet.

3. **Brief 15's Arnold logic vs. brief 16's own headline awe device.** Not a factual
   contradiction — both are accurately reported — but a direct values contradiction the corpus
   now contains in two different briefs' voices. Brief 16 calls "one human-scale figure, visibly
   small against architecture" **the single most load-bearing awe device we have**. Brief 15
   reports Andrea Arnold's stated reason for Academy ratio as refusing to let a landscape make a
   person small, because that's what respect looks like to her. Brief 15 flags this itself
   ("Both are legitimate, deliberate choices; they are not compatible in the same shot") — so
   this isn't an oversight, but it is a real, load-bearing tension the corpus now states on the
   record in two places without resolving which register wins for which beat. See §6.

## 5. The ruling brief 17 owes us

Brief 17 was commissioned to produce concrete formal tests separating monumental grammar that
indicts power from monumental grammar that celebrates it. What it actually delivers, quoted
directly:

- **"Is there a visible cost in this frame"** — wreckage, exhaustion, a body that failed, a
  named victim.
- **Massing / ornamental pattern** — "if the humans in frame read as decoration, the shot is
  drifting toward celebration."
- **Congealed vs. ceaseless motion** — a posture/motion cue drawn from Sontag's observation that
  fascist choreography alternates frozen "heroic" poses with synchronised mass movement.
- **Camera behaviour** — "a camera that glides up a structure in slow, worshipful reveal reads
  differently from one that holds static and lets a small human cross the frame at the bottom."

Judged hard, against "usable on a single frame":

- **Visible cost is genuinely usable and the standout of the four.** It is a yes/no question
  answerable by what is or isn't placed in one still: is there wreckage, a failed body, a named
  victim, anything paying a price? This is the one test that will actually change what gets put
  in frame.
- **Massing/ornamental pattern is real but narrow for BadCode's actual subject matter.** The
  test is built for scenes with crowds (Sontag's Nuremberg masses). BadCode's house shots are
  overwhelmingly one dwarfed figure against a machine, not a crowd — so this test will rarely
  have anything to check against. Usable when it applies; rarely applies.
- **Congealed-vs-ceaseless motion is not usable as stated.** The brief's own §3 admits the real
  device (hundreds of synchronised bodies) "no BadCode shot has the budget for" and downgrades
  it to "a posture cue for the figures we do have" — which is no longer a checkable formal test,
  it's a vague reminder to think about posture. On a single frame with one figure, there is
  usually nothing here to test.
- **Camera behaviour only applies to moving shots**, and roughly half of BadCode's output (per
  brief 15 and 18's own framing) is static stills. A test that only fires on video clips is not
  a general single-frame test.

That leaves **one genuinely usable test out of four**, plus three that are real but either
narrow (massing) or effectively inapplicable to a still (motion, camera behaviour) given
BadCode's actual shot inventory. And the brief is explicit, in its own myths section, that the
sharpest available discriminator — **whose gaze the frame adopts and who it dwarfs** — is *not*
resolved here: "this brief does not fully resolve that question; it is a live BadCode design
risk flagged by the critic under gap 3, not answered here." Its own §7 goes further: the
checklist "has not [been] tested... against an actual BadCode frame," and recommends running it
against 3–4 shipped panels before trusting it as house doctrine.

**Verdict: this is a real, useful, honestly-scoped first pass, not the ruling the task asked
for.** It hands over one strong test (visible cost), two weak-for-us tests, and states plainly
that it has not answered its own central question. Treating "visible cost — is anyone or
anything visibly paying a price in this frame" as a mandatory pre-publish check for any
monumental shot is a defensible, immediately actionable rule to take from this brief today. Treating the
brief as having settled when the grammar tips from critique to celebration would be a
misreading of what it actually says about itself.

## 6. Tension with the house register

Yes, plainly, and it should be said as plainly as the task asked for: **the corpus now contains
material that argues, on its own terms, against BadCode's near-black monumental register**, and
reconciling it takes a real editorial choice, not a research finding.

- **Brief 15** documents that the visual grammar closest to BadCode's actual target reader's own
  vocabulary (per `docs/marketing/the-reader.md`) is democratic-eye-level, unlit-by-design,
  handheld, refusing exactly the symmetry/engineered-light/scale-dwarfing moves the house
  register is built from. It states this itself: "BadCode's own register... is close to the
  visual opposite of this whole tradition on every axis that matters."
- **Brief 17** documents that the specific grammar BadCode has adopted (monumental scale,
  symmetry, single hard light, a dwarfed human) is the *same* grammar that, absent a visible
  cost and a legible whose-gaze answer, reads as celebration of power to a real, documented
  fraction of any audience — and that documented fraction includes directors (Verhoeven, Harron)
  who built the critique on purpose and still lost part of the room.

Both points are independently well-evidenced, not speculative. The honest reading is: **the
house look is not wrong for the reader in some general sense, but it is unproven for the
reader and it carries a specific, named, recurring failure mode (aspirational misreading) that
nothing in the corpus has tested against BadCode's own work.** Reconciliation is possible but
requires committing to disciplines neither brief states as settled:

1. **Pair, don't choose.** Brief 15 already proposes this for the human-scale question: keep the
   monumental register as the default awe engine, but treat Arnold's tight human-scale frame as
   a *deliberate exception* for specific beats (a character moment inside the machine-scale
   story), not a replacement. This is a real reconciliation path, stated in the corpus, not
   invented here.
2. **Make brief 17's one usable test mandatory, not optional.** If every monumental shot is
   required to carry a visible cost or a named victim in the same piece — which is already
   BadCode's existing rule for automation fear generally (`the-reader.md`: never raise a fear
   without naming a beneficiary) — that is a real, checkable discipline that directly answers
   the aspirational-reading risk, even though brief 17 itself says it hasn't been tested against
   our own shipped panels yet.
3. **What is not reconciled, and should not be claimed as reconciled:** brief 15's harder,
   unresolved question — does the actual working-class audience the register is aimed at respond
   to social-realist grammar, or is its appeal mostly to critics and an arthouse audience — is
   explicitly unanswered by any source in either brief. Nobody has evidence either register
   *works* for this specific reader. The house register is not shown safe; social realism is not
   shown to be the answer either. A human needs to rule on which risk BadCode is more willing to
   carry, because the research corpus, honestly read, does not pick a winner.

## 7. The top 10 from wave two — ranked, most improves a shot tomorrow first

1. Check and correctly tag full/limited colour range before every export — the exact bug that
   already shipped once (`camping.mp4`), now a five-point QC-script addition. — **[20, A]**
2. Before any monumental frame ships, ask: is there a visible cost in it — wreckage, exhaustion,
   a body that failed, a named victim? If not, it is drifting toward celebration, not critique.
   — **[17, p]**
3. A dark frame needs one region clearly brighter than its surroundings for the eye to anchor
   against, or it reads as broken/underexposed rather than as chosen darkness — now grounded in
   real perceptual science (anchoring theory), not just craft rule. — **[20, A]**
4. Awe's actual mechanism is vastness the mind cannot immediately assimilate, not raw size — a
   frame that explains itself instantly has nothing left to accommodate. — **[16, A mixed]**
5. Scale for a machine or building has to be manufactured with a known-size reference object in
   frame (a doorway, a stair, a figure) — without one, size collapses into abstraction. —
   **[18, P (mechanism A)]**
6. An empty space reads as absence only shot close and low enough that human-scale proportions
   (doorway, desk, stair) stay legible — shot from above or far away, it reads as geometry, not
   as "someone should be here." — **[18, A mixed, our synthesis]**
7. One human-scale figure against architecture is the single most load-bearing awe device
   available — but it is the exact inverse of a real, named, deliberate alternative (Arnold's
   tight human-scale frame) that reads as respect, not awe; know which one a given beat needs.
   — **[16/15, A/p]**
8. Add deliberate grain/dither to any near-black sequence before compression — a cleanly
   denoised dark frame is the one most likely to band after re-encode. — **[20, A mechanism / P convention]**
9. Never eyeball a dark shot — a waveform or false-colour check is the only way to know whether a
   shadow is "deep but present" or already clipped to nothing. — **[20, A/P]**
10. On any feed platform, on-screen text is not a redundant accessibility layer — a majority
    (magnitude disputed, direction consistent) of viewers watch with sound off, so a caption has
    to carry the beat alone, not just reinforce narration. — **[19, P]**
