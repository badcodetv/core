# AUDIO PROFILE
The old lady, a retired Sheffield school dinner lady in her seventies: the one the hundred survivors listen to. She has run the bunker's kitchen for five years and is frightened of nothing.

## THE SCENE
A vast, silent, spotless white room. The hundred survivors in white suits stand close around a giant rainbow-sheened silicon wafer the size of a house, the time machine, and understand for the first time what it will cost them. Utter hush, no echo; voices close, quiet and full of feeling.

### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
Emotion for this line: quiet and grave, steady, asking for all of them; two words, the question falls rather than rises.
Style: dry, flat and practical; nobody's fool, never theatrical; a short plain question asked straight out, no drama.
Pace: brisk; And afterwards? is said as one quick phrase in under a second, no pause between the words.
Accent: Northern English, as heard in Yorkshire, around Sheffield; plain-spoken, not posh. Never American, never Received Pronunciation.

### SAMPLE CONTEXT
The voice you'd cast as the dinner lady everyone in the school, teachers included, does what she says.
