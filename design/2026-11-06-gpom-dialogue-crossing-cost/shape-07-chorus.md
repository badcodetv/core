# Shape 07: The chorus

Side characters voice the doubts, so Maddie does not have to. The old lady asks the practical questions (what does it run on, do we wake up), Sandra asks the frightened one, Derek asks the one nobody wants to say, and Maddie only answers. She gives the shortest true reply each time, kindly, with "he" as the source. In the goodbye, the old lady nudges the pair and then the couple get the last two words.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |

| MADDIE | No, my lovely. You don't. | 2.0 |
| [silence over the empty chair grid] | | 1.5 |
| DEREK | And him? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | He goes. Only him. He's just data, see. | 2.7 |
| OLD LADY | Anyone want out? [silence] | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | Then drink your tea, Derek. | 1.8 |
| **Total** | | **24.3** |

**Why it works.** The doubts come from the humans and the answers from Maddie, so the cost lands as Q&A with no speech. Each fact sits in its own short exchange. "Anyone want out?" and the silence give the hundred their consent. Sandra's "drink your tea" turns the end into an ordinary British gesture and sets up the mug that stops steaming.

**Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back..." and "it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it".

**Risk.** Four voices in 25 s could blur on TTS unless the old lady is distinct. The joke is attributed to the boss, which may blur whose joke it is. "Anyone want out?" is close to a stated moral. The silence beat must be kept. "My loves" / "my lovely" may read twee.

**Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OLD LADY | Oh, go on, you two. | 1.5 |
| SANDRA | Seat down? | 1.0 |
| [beat, hands tighten] | | 1.5 |
| DEREK | Always. | 1.0 |
| SANDRA | Liar. | 0.8 |

| **Total** | | **7.3** |

**Why it works.** The old lady is the chorus's last push, saying what the audience feels, then nothing more. "Seat down?" is a joke, a question and a goodbye; "Always" is a small lie told with love; "Liar" is Sandra letting him have it. Nobody mentions after.

**Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?".

**Risk.** "Liar" could read as bitter if harsh. "Always" is ambiguous. Opaque if the seat row is not planted. The old lady's line is a little twee and could be dropped.

**Protected lines.** None.
