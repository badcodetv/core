# Shape 10: Ending first, plant backwards

I wrote the last ten seconds first: the final words two married people say, and the one thing that has to be true when Maddie says "Boss..." to an empty room, which is that nobody tricked them and nobody is coming back. Then I worked backwards and planted only what that ending needs: the tea (so it can stop steaming), the seat (so the last line pays off), and the plain fact that only he goes. Everything else was cut. The humans never explain themselves; the bravery is a cuppa and a jab.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | It's the time machine. It can only send him. He's just data, see. | 4.0 |
| MADDIE | It runs on you. All hundred of you, all at once. | 3.2 |
| SANDRA | Runs on us how? | 1.5 |
| MADDIE | Everything you are. It'll feel a bit like dying. Mostly like nodding off. Then you don't wake up. Only he goes. | 6.0 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.2 |
| MADDIE | Not much. Less than the last five years, he says. | 3.2 |
| DEREK | Right. [beat] Do we get a cuppa first? | 2.5 |
| MADDIE | [warm] Course you do, love. | 1.6 |
| **Total** | | **24.2** |

- **Why it works.** The cost is said once, plainly, in Maddie's care register, with "dying" named and softened in the same breath ("mostly like nodding off"), so the audience cannot later say they were not told and nothing is grotesque. Sandra asks the question everyone is thinking and gets the dark joke; Derek's response to learning he will not wake up is to ask for tea. That is the bravery: flat, practical, British. The tea is the plant, because the scene's last image is a mug that stops steaming. "Only he goes" is the sentence the whole scene turns on, and it is the shortest.
- **Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back, to before it all went wrong." (false now). "And it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it." (implies they travel, and states a moral aim). Any hint of an afterwards.
- **Risk.** "A bit like dying" plus "you don't wake up" in one line is heavy for TTS; if Maddie's read tips into solemn it loses the warmth and Kai's "not grotesque" line is crossed. "He's just data, see" could read as glib about the boss. Derek's tea line comes a beat after the worst news, so it needs a real pause or it reads as a gag rather than as coping. "Less than the last five years" is a joke about their lives, which risks contempt for the people; the delivery should be Maddie repeating the boss's dry line kindly, not mocking.
- **Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim as the opener (Derek).

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SANDRA | [quietly, not looking] Seat down? | 1.6 |
| DEREK | [beat] Seat down, love. Always was. | 2.6 |
| SANDRA | [a breath, almost a laugh] Liar. | 1.8 |
| **Total** | | **6.0** |

- **Why it works.** This is the ending written first. Their last exchange is the row they have had for thirty years, and for once he concedes it and she knows it is a lie and takes it anyway; "Liar" is the last word either says, and it is love. No reunion, no future tense, no "see you", no "ready?" Then silence and the lights going out, so Maddie's "Boss..." lands on a room that has nobody left in it to answer her. The seat row is only a joke if it was set up, so Dialogue 1's cuppa and the established row do the rest.
- **Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" (implies an afterwards). "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?" (the answer confirms the afterwards, and "ready" makes it a speech about courage).
- **Risk.** It leans entirely on the audience already knowing the seat row from earlier scenes; a new viewer may only get "bickering couple". It is also very short, so if the shot is 10s the silence is doing 4s of work and must be held, not scored over. Derek lying out loud to comfort her could read as sentimental if the actor overplays it.
- **Protected lines.** None. "Seat down?" survives from Claude's candidate; the rest is new.
