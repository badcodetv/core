# Shape 05: The other side wins

The humans set the terms and Maddie is the one who gives way. Sandra and Derek will not be managed, so they refuse the soft version. They make Maddie drop the dressed-up wording and say what the machine does to them, and she concedes it and admits the boss never tried it. The last concession goes to Derek, who gives up the toilet-seat row on the final beat.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | It's the time machine. It runs on you. All hundred of you, all at once. | 3.5 |
| SANDRA | Don't dress it up, love. What does it do to us? | 2.5 |
| MADDIE | [small, straight] He wanted me to be gentle. Straight, then. It's like nodding off. A bit like dying. You won't wake up. | 5.5 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | He says not much. He's never actually tried it. | 2.8 |
| DEREK | Less than the last five years. | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | Just him, then? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | Just him. He's only data, see. The rest of us can't go. | 3.0 |
| DEREK | Tell him to get it right. | 1.8 |
| **Total** | | **24.1** |

**Why it works.** "Don't dress it up" gives Maddie permission to be blunt, so the plain statement comes from the humans' demand. Maddie's concession is also a care move. Her admission that the boss has never tried it keeps her honest and small. Derek taking the dark joke off her means the humans are the funny ones. "Tell him to get it right" is a practical note to the one going. "Only him" is stated twice.

**Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back..." and "it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it".

**Risk.** "A bit like dying" aloud may be blunt, though Kai asked for it. "He's only data, see" may be over-folksy. "The rest of us can't go" puts Maddie among the left-behind, which may be wrong in canon; swap for "The rest can't." Near the top of the range; cutting "Just him, then?"/"Just him." brings it to about 20 s.

**Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SANDRA | Go on. Admit it. You never once left it up. | 3.0 |
| DEREK | [a long pause] Never once. | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | [quiet] Knew it. | 1.0 |
| DEREK | Thirty years, I put it down. | 2.2 |
| **Total** | | **8.2** |

**Why it works.** Sandra sets the terms and Derek concedes the one thing he has fought over for thirty years. Giving it up is how he says the love out loud, sideways. Derek's last line is about the past, so nothing is implied to come after.

**Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?".

**Risk.** Only pays off if the audience knows the row. "Thirty years, I put it down" could be heard as a boast. The pause before "Never once" needs room. Sweet, with no sting; some may find it too neat. "Seat down?"/"Seat down." from the candidate list are not used.

**Protected lines.** None.
