# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

Every beat is carried by the fewest words that still land it. Maddie states the cost once, in plain short sentences. One euphemism ("nodding off") does the work of dying. Sandra asks the one question. The answer is the joke. Nobody comments on what they just heard. The humans take it flatly, and the silence between lines carries the weight. Spoken seconds are estimates at a slow, warm narration pace and include the beats.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.2 |
| MADDIE | The time machine. It runs on you. All hundred of you, all at once. | 4.0 |
| MADDIE | He goes back. Only him. [beat] You'll go to sleep, my loves. Nodding off. You won't wake up. | 6.0 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.3 |
| MADDIE | Less than the last five years. | 2.3 |
| DEREK | [beat] Right. | 1.5 |
| MADDIE | I'm sorry. | 1.2 |
| **Total** | | **17.5** |

**Why it works.** Every required beat is present once: it runs on them, all at once, only he goes back, sleep and nodding off, they won't wake up, and Sandra asks "Will it hurt?" Maddie never says "die". "Nodding off" followed by "won't wake up" lets the listener supply the word. The joke answers the only question anyone asks. Derek's "Right." is his whole reaction. "I'm sorry." is the only warmth added.

**Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back, to before it all went wrong" (implied survival) and "And it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it" (explained the plan, carried no cost).

**Risk.** About 17.5 s against a target of 18 to 25 s. "Sleep" and "nodding off" together can read as reassurance. "Less than the last five years" may land as Sandra's joke rather than Maddie's. Without "it can only send data", a viewer who skipped earlier scenes may not follow why only he goes.

**Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim as Derek's opener.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SANDRA | Seat down? | 1.3 |
| DEREK | [beat] Seat down. | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | [beat] Liar. | 1.5 |
| DEREK | [hand squeeze, quietly] Always was. | 2.0 |
| **Total** | | **6.8** |

**Why it works.** The toilet-seat row is the whole marriage in two words. She calls him a liar, which is a jab and a tease. His reply is flat, affectionate and a little ambiguous. No future tense, no reunion, no "see you".

**Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" (implies both are there afterwards) and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?".

**Risk.** "Liar" may read as an accusation; the read must be warm. "Always was" could be heard as Derek admitting he always left the seat up (deliberate ambiguity, may be muddy). 6.8 s is at the bottom of target. Needs the seat gag set up earlier.

**Protected lines.** None.
