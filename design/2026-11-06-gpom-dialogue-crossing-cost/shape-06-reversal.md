# Shape 06: Reversal, the listener gets there first

Maddie is never allowed to finish the terrible sentence. Each time she starts toward it ("it has to run on something...", "less than the..."), a human completes it for her, flatly, as if reading a bill. The audience hears the cost from the people paying it, not from the machine, so Maddie only has to nod and be kind. Nobody names the thing: "Us", "we don't wake up", "last five years". In the goodbye, Derek begins the sentence every couple dreads and Sandra shuts it down with the row, so love is said by refusing to say it.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | It's the time machine. It only sends data back. Only him. But it has to run on something, and he said... [trails off] | 6.0 |
| SANDRA | Us. | 0.6 |
| MADDIE | [small] All hundred of you, love. At once. | 2.2 |
| DEREK | And we don't wake up. | 1.6 |
| MADDIE | No. Just nodding off. Lights out. | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | Not much. Less than the... | 1.8 |
| SANDRA | Last five years. Yes, go on. | 1.8 |
| DEREK | Right. Any chance of a brew first? | 2.3 |
| MADDIE | Kettle's on. | 0.9 |
| **Total** | | **21.2** |

- **Why it works.** The humans work it out one beat ahead of Maddie, three times, so the audience watches them take the news in real time and the bravery is the speed and flatness of it. Maddie's job shrinks to warmth: "love", "lights out", the kettle. Death is never said; the euphemism ("nodding off") is hers and the understatement ("Us.") is theirs. "Only him" lands early and plainly, so the film cannot be read as any of them surviving. The brew is the practical-Brit exit: no speech, just the next small thing.
- **Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back" and "before it all went wrong" go entirely; "help you avoid it" goes (the stakes are in the next scene, and no moral is stated). The old Maddie exposition paragraph is replaced by a trailing half-sentence.
- **Risk.** Heavy reliance on interruption and trailing off: TTS tends to give Maddie a clean falling cadence, so "and he said..." and "Less than the..." need an unfinished-rise direction or they sound like complete sentences and Sandra talks over nothing. The overlap needs the timeline to cut Sandra in tight. "Last five years" as a joke may read as Sandra's line, not Maddie's, which departs from Kai's candidate (same words, different mouth). "Kettle's on" might read as too cosy; cut it and end on Derek if so. Running a touch long if pauses are generous.
- **Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim as Derek's opener.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | Sandra. I've always... | 1.8 |
| SANDRA | I know. Seat down. | 1.6 |
| DEREK | [beat] ...Seat down. | 1.6 |
| **Total** | | **5.0** (about 7 with pauses) |

- **Why it works.** Derek starts the sentence people say at the end, and Sandra gets there before him and trades it for the toilet-seat row. She has heard the whole speech already, after thirty years, so he doesn't have to make it. His echo is the "I love you" and the "me too" in two words. Nothing implies a later: no future tense, no "see you", no "are you ready".
- **Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?" both go; the afterlife implication and the readiness check are the exact problems Kai named.
- **Risk.** At 5 s it is under the 6-10 s target, so it needs held silence over the hands (a long beat after "Seat down" before the first light goes out), or the cut feels rushed. "I know" could read as Sandra being cold if Sandra's delivery isn't soft on the second beat. "Seat down" depends on the row having been set up earlier in the film; if it hasn't, it is just an odd line.
- **Protected lines.** None.
