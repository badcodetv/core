# Shape 08: The place is the argument

Every line leans on one physical detail of the room, so nobody has to state the cost. In Dialogue 1 the detail is the chairs: Maddie says "count the chairs", and Derek's count lands on exactly enough, which proves the machine runs on them. No chair is left over for the boss. In Dialogue 2 the detail is the gap between the two chairs, and the hands close it.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | It's the time machine. Count the chairs, love. | 2.8 |
| DEREK | [counting under his breath] ...ninety-nine. A hundred. | 3.0 |
| MADDIE | It runs on you. All of you, all at once. That's what the chairs are for. | 4.2 |
| MADDIE | Like nodding off in front of the telly. You won't wake up. | 3.4 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | Less than the last five years, love. | 2.2 |
| MADDIE | There's no chair for him. He's only data. He's the one going. | 3.4 |
| DEREK | [a beat, flat] Right. | 1.0 |
| **Total** | | **22.0** |

**Why it works.** The cost is one thing you can see: a hundred chairs for a machine that needs a hundred people. Derek does the sum himself. The chairs make "only he goes" concrete, because there is no spare seat. "Nodding off in front of the telly" is the warm British euphemism, delivered as plain fact. Derek's flat "Right." is the bravery.

**Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back", "to before it all went wrong", and "it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it".

**Risk.** "Count the chairs" needs about 3 s of held empty chair grid. Derek's counting may not voice well; may need a murmured "...hundred" only. The joke in Maddie's mouth could sound cruel if not soft. "He's only data" is the thesis line and could land cold; may need to move before the joke.

**Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SANDRA | [reaching across] Gap's too wide, these chairs. | 2.2 |
| DEREK | [reaching, hands meet] There. | 1.0 |
| SANDRA | Seat down? | 0.9 |
| DEREK | Seat down. | 0.9 |
| **Total** | | **5.0** |

**Why it works.** The gap between the chairs is where the two of them are, and closing it is the whole scene. The seat row is thirty years in two words. Derek giving in is how he says it. No "love", no "ready", no afterwards.

**Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?".

**Risk.** At 5 s it is under target; needs a held silence after the last line (could be the strongest part or dead air). "Seat down" lands only if the row is set up. "Gap's too wide" may be on the nose if the shot already shows the gap.

**Protected lines.** None.
