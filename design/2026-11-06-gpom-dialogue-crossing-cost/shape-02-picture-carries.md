# Shape 02: The picture carries it

I leaned on shots the audience has already seen and cut every line those shots already say. The wafer (d3), the overhead chair grid (d4), "They sit" (d6) and the two hands (d7) show the scale, the emptiness and the love, so the words only carry what a picture can't: the machine runs on them, it's like dropping off, they don't wake up, and only he goes. Everything else is silence. I relied on the d4 shot (overhead chip-grid, then the old lady's mug) to deliver the "so many of us" weight without Maddie counting it.

## Dialogue 1: The wafer: what it costs

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEREK | What is it? | 1.1 |
| MADDIE | [soft, straight] It's the time machine. It runs on you. All hundred, all at once. | 4.0 |
| (hold) | [wafer hum, nobody speaks; slow push on the wafer] | 1.5 |
| SANDRA | Will it hurt? | 1.0 |
| MADDIE | Not much. It's like dropping off. Less than the last five years. | 3.5 |
| (hold) | [beat; Derek looks at the floor] | 1.0 |
| DEREK | And then? | 0.8 |
| MADDIE | You don't wake up. Only he goes. He's only data. | 3.2 |
| (hold) | [cut to the overhead chair grid; Maddie's beacon dims a touch] | 1.5 |
| SANDRA | Right. Kettle on, then. | 2.0 |
| **Total** | | **19.6** |

**Why it works.** The wafer is already huge on screen. Each human gets one short question and takes the answer without comment. "Dropping off" and "don't wake up" carry it in warm British terms. Four holds do the grieving. "Kettle on, then" is the bravery, flat and practical, and hands on to d4's "Milk, two sugars" and the old lady's mug.

**Cuts from current.** "It'll send all of you back..." and "it'll send him too, so he can help you avoid it"; the explanation of why the plan exists.

**Risk.** Maddie's "Less than the last five years" may read as flip; could go to Sandra as a retort to "Not much." About 4 s of near-silence over a static wafer may feel slow without push-in and hum. "Kettle on, then" may sound deflecting. "Dropping off" is less than Kai asked for ("a little like dying"); option: add "a bit like dying" (about 1 s).

**Protected lines.** "What is it?" survives verbatim, Derek's, first line.

## Dialogue 2: The goodbye: two hands

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (hold) | [two hands across the gap; tea steaming on a side table; 2 s of room tone] | 2.0 |
| SANDRA | [barely] Seat down? | 1.0 |
| (hold) | [Derek's hand closes on hers] | 2.0 |
| DEREK | [gruff] Seat down. | 1.0 |
| (hold) | [her thumb moves once; nothing more] | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **7.5** |

**Why it works.** The d7 shot does all the love, so the words don't touch it. Derek answers with the thing he has refused to do for thirty years, without comment. Nobody says "after", "see you", "love you" or "ready". The scene ends on a thumb moving.

**Cuts from current.** "Does this mean I get to hang out with non-grumpy Derek?" and "Yes, love. Are you ready for this?".

**Risk.** Leans on the audience remembering the seat row. Derek conceding could play as a gag; the delivery must be a long soft beat. Relies on d3 having done "no afterwards". The 2 s opening hold must not be filled by a music swell.

**Protected lines.** None.
