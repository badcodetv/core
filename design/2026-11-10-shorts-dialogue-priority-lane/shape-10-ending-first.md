# Shape 10: Ending first, the button that feels inevitable

I wrote the last five seconds before anything else, asking for a button that lands the gap between the two vehicles without a word of explanation, then worked backwards so every earlier line is a setup for it and nothing else. Each version below has its own ending, pushed to an extreme (a caption over a still-stuck ambulance; a reporter who knows how this ends; silence). The punchline of each is marked **[PUNCHLINE]**.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

**Version A: the good-news caption (ending: the ambulance is still stuck)**

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [flat, regional, rain on the mic] Police have stopped this ambulance at a red light. A car is coming through. | 4.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, motorcade, seething] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| REPORTER | [to camera, same flat voice, ambulance still held behind him] We'll bring you more as we get it. | 2.5 |
| CAPTION | [white meme text, freeze on the ambulance, blue lights still turning] **[PUNCHLINE]** BREAKING: THE RASPBERRY HAS BEEN FOUND | 0 |
| Total |  | 8.0 spoken, ~20 with picture |

- **Why it works.** The caption is good news delivered over bad news, and the viewer's eye does the sum: the freeze holds on the ambulance, still waiting, while the text celebrates a jam. Nobody says whose time counts; the picture is the only thing that disagrees with the caption. The reporter's lines are pure "and now the local news" dryness, so the gap between the tone and the scene is the joke.
- **Cuts from current.** Nothing spoken is lost except the unwritten reporter placeholder; the reporter now speaks 18 words in total.
- **Risk.** The caption is a sight gag, so if the ambulance is not visibly still held in frame 15-20 s it reads as a non-sequitur. "Found" may be too cute for a Jack who wants a sharper word ("RESOLVED", "SORTED").
- **Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." kept verbatim, on its own beat.

**Version B: the reporter who knows how this ends (ending: back to camera, no caption)**

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [straight to camera, blue light on the balaclava] Behind me, an ambulance. Ahead of it, a red light. Coming through it, a man in a hurry. | 5.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, furious] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| REPORTER | [same dead calm, ambulance finally pulling away behind] In my timeline, this is where the jam starts. **[PUNCHLINE]** Not on the road. | 4.0 |
| Total |  | 10.5 spoken, ~20 with picture |

- **Why it works.** The reporter is the future narrator in disguise: certainty without a moral. "The jam starts" and the cut to wet empty road tells you something is about to go wrong without telling you what. The twist ("not on the road") hints at the real jam without defining it.
- **Cuts from current.** Drops the caption entirely; the reporter carries the button.
- **Risk.** Close to a stated moral. If "not on the road" feels like it explains, cut to "In my timeline, this is where the jam starts." and stop. Also over the 12-14 s limit if spoken slowly.
- **Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." verbatim.

**Version C: the paramedic (ending: the smallest possible line, from the wrong person)**

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [deadpan, regional] The road is closed to everyone but the most important person in the city. | 3.5 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| PARAMEDIC | [leaning out of the window, to the outrider, mild, almost polite] **[PUNCHLINE]** Is it long? | 1.0 |
| CAPTION | [lower third, regional-news style] LIVE: RASPBERRY | 0 |
| Total |  | 6.0 spoken, ~20 with picture |

- **Why it works.** The paramedic's tiny, polite question is the only human line in the film and it asks about the wrong thing, which lets the viewer fill in the stakes. The minister's trivial fury and the paramedic's patience are the two ends of the gap. "Live: raspberry" gives the regional-news register a last twist.
- **Cuts from current.** The reporter's explanation is shortened to one sentence; the minister's line is untouched.
- **Risk.** The paramedic asking "Is it long?" might be read as flippant about a patient; the delivery has to be weary and kind, never a joke at the paramedic's expense. Needs a third lip-synced voice.
- **Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." verbatim.
