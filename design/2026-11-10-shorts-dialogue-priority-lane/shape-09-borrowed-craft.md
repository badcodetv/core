# Shape 09: Borrowed craft from UK TV satire

I took the compression tricks of British broadcast satire and applied them as mechanics, not as imitation. The Day Today and Brass Eye: total gravitas applied to something trivial, and the on-screen graphic or ticker as a second voice that carries the joke the presenter will not. Look Around You: flat, instructional, passive-voice euphemism where the reporter describes the outrage as a logistics matter. Wipe: one sarcastic caption doing the work of a paragraph. Pushed to the extreme: the reporter never acknowledges the ambulance as a problem and treats the minister's jam as the only news story; the gap between the two vehicles is carried entirely by who gets the gravitas.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [straight to camera, ambulance idling red-lit behind, outrider's hand up] "Traffic on the A40 is being managed this evening, to keep an important journey moving." | 5.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, back seat, motorcade sweeping through] "Lemon curd. I said raspberry." **(PUNCHLINE 1, protected line)** | 2.5 |
| CAPTION | [lower-third, news-ticker style, over the ambulance finally pulling away in the rain] "RASPBERRY SHORTAGE: LATEST" **(PUNCHLINE 2, the meme)** | 0 |
| REPORTER | [back to camera, unmoved] "We will bring you more on the jam as it develops." **(PUNCHLINE 3, the button)** | 3.0 |
| **Total** | 24 words spoken | **10.5** |

Alternate buttons to swap in (same slot):
- REPORTER [deadpan]: "Our thoughts are with the toast." (Punchline: the toast; 2 s.)
- CAPTION only, reporter silent, held on her face: "MINISTER 'DEEPLY CONCERNED'" over the ambulance's tail lights. (0 s spoken; the Day Today move of letting the graphic do the lying.)
- REPORTER: "Anyone with information on the lemon curd should contact the police." (Punchline: police as the jam's guardians; 3.5 s.)

**Why it works.** The Day Today and Brass Eye ran on authority applied to the wrong object: the presenter sounds exactly as serious about nonsense as about a death, and the audience supplies the outrage. Here the reporter's opening line is Look Around You in miniature, a calm passive-voice description ("is being managed", "an important journey") that is technically true and never names who or what is held. The minister then spends the film's only emotion on a condiment. The caption is the second voice: it adopts the grammar of breaking news ("LATEST") for the jam, and the reporter's final promise of "more on the jam as it develops" lets the audience complete the comparison with the ambulance themselves. Nobody says whose time counts; the reporter simply gives all her gravitas to the wrong thing, in the voice of UK regional news.

**Cuts from current.** Any explanation of the ambulance (patient, urgency, paramedic line) goes: the picture carries it. No narrator knowing-how-it-ends line; the reporter stays inside the news voice for this one. The outrider and paramedic get no speech.

**Risk.** "Important journey" could read as the reporter endorsing the motorcade, so the delivery must be bland, not approving; a wink kills it. The caption has to be readable in under two seconds on a phone. "Jam" is a double meaning (traffic and lemon curd's neighbour) that may be too cute, with raspberry jam implied rather than stated; if it confuses, swap to "We will bring you more as it develops." Total is about 10.5 s of speech, so there is spare room for a beat of silence on the ambulance, which is probably where the real laugh is.

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, the last three words of the minister's line; "Lemon curd." is added in front as the set-up, since the line alone has nothing to answer.
