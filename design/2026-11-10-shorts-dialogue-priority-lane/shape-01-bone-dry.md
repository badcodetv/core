# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

I cut to the fewest words that still carry every beat: one reporter line, the protected minister line, one caption, and nothing else. Beat 1 gets no words at all (the ambulance and the raised glove do it); the reporter says only what a regional bulletin would say, with the awful thing buried in a bureaucratic verb; the minister is untouched; the button is a caption in news-ticker voice that treats the trivial matter as the emergency, so the gap lands without a word of explanation. Every line is short enough to mouth in one breath, and the silences are doing as much work as the speech.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (beat 1, 0-5 s) | [no speech: rain, siren held low, the outrider's glove] | 0 |
| REPORTER | [straight to camera, rain on the mic] Traffic is being managed. | 2 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, back seat, furious] I said raspberry. **<- PUNCHLINE (protected)** | 1.5 |
| CAPTION | [white block capitals, news ticker, 0 s] BREAKING: LEMON CURD INCIDENT. SERIOUS. | 0 |
| REPORTER | [back to camera, not turning round, ambulance still behind him] More as we get it. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | 14 words spoken, 3 lines | **~5 s speech, ~20 s picture** |

Alternative reporter openers, same slot, pick one: "Roads in the area are being cleared." (2.5 s) / "Delays are expected." (1.5 s, driest) / "Everything is under control." (2 s, a future-knower's lie).

**Why it works.** Total spoken time is about 5 s in a 20 s short, so the picture carries the rest: the held ambulance, the balaclava reporter's blank authority, the motorcade. "Traffic is being managed" is true and monstrous at once, so nobody needs the moral; the viewer does the sum. "I said raspberry" is the punchline, because it arrives after the ambulance has been made to wait, and the caption twists the knife by using emergency language on the jam. "More as we get it" is a pure UK regional sign-off, and delivered while the ambulance is still stuck behind him it is the second laugh. Nobody names a party, a politician or the point, and nobody sneers at the viewer.

**Cuts from current.** Any reporter exposition (who, where, why the road is shut); any paramedic or outrider line; any explicit contrast between the vehicles; any line from the minister beyond the protected one (no "lemon curd" spoken: it appears only in the caption, so the joke is read, not heard); any reporter commentary on the minister.

**Risk.** It may be too bare: a viewer who does not clock that the motorcade is a politician's will lose the button, and 5 s of speech leaves long silent stretches that need strong picture, sound and ticker design to hold attention. "Lemon curd" never being spoken means the caption has to be on screen long enough to read (hold 2.5 s minimum). "Serious" in the caption comes close to editorialising; if Jack finds it too pointed, drop it to "BREAKING: LEMON CURD INCIDENT." Reporter is not given a knowing-from-the-future flavour in this shape on purpose; that is the first thing to add back if the short feels like a generic satire.

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, said on the phone by the minister in the back seat, and is the punchline of this version.
