# Shape 08: The place is the argument

I picked one physical detail of the junction and let it do the arguing, so the reporter never explains anything: she just reads the street out like regional news. Version A uses the blue-and-white sign on the pole ("Give way to emergency vehicles"), B uses the red light's own countdown, C uses the queue and the blue light on the wet road. The reporter describes the place with total deadpan, the minister supplies the trivial fury, and the caption is the street furniture itself replying.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

**Version A: the sign** (detail: a council sign on the pole above the ambulance)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [to camera, rain, straight] Behind me, a junction. Above me, a sign. [beat] The sign says emergency vehicles have right of way. | 5.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, seething] I said raspberry. | 1.8 |
| REPORTER | [back to camera, not turning] **The sign has been reminded of its position.** | 2.5 |
| CAPTION | [sign, lit by blue, small council font] "GIVE WAY TO EMERGENCY VEHICLES (except)" | 0 |
| **Total** | about 24 words | **9.3 s** |

**Version B: the red light** (detail: the pedestrian-crossing style countdown, stuck, the ambulance waiting on it)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [to camera] This light has been red for ninety seconds. It is not broken. It has been asked to stay red. | 5.0 |
| PARAMEDIC | [through the windscreen, flat] Is that him? | 1.2 |
| MINISTER | [phone, motorcade] I said raspberry. | 1.8 |
| REPORTER | [light turns green behind her] **And it's green. Lovely. It was only ever a question of who was coming.** | 3.5 |
| CAPTION | none | 0 |
| **Total** | about 36 words | **11.5 s** |

**Version C: the queue** (detail: one ambulance, blue lights on, at the front of a line of ordinary cars, all of them waiting politely)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [to camera] Tonight's queue is eleven cars long, and one ambulance. Nobody is hooting. Britain, ladies and gentlemen. | 5.0 |
| MINISTER | [phone] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| REPORTER | [flat, as the motorcade passes behind her] **Eleven cars, one ambulance, and a lemon curd emergency.** | 3.0 |
| CAPTION | "LEMON CURD INCIDENT: 1 / PATIENT: PENDING" | 0 |
| **Total** | about 31 words | **9.5 s** |

**Why it works.** The joke is already in the junction; we only have to point the camera at it. A sign that says one thing while the street does another (A), a light that obeys the wrong person (B) and a polite British queue (C) each carry the whole argument without anyone saying whose time counts. The reporter treats the street as the news item and the minister's lemon curd as the only real emergency, so the gap between the two vehicles is felt, not stated. Lines are short and mouth-able, and the spoken total sits well inside 12-14 s, leaving room for the 5 s picture beats. Punchlines are in bold: A "reminded of its position", B "only ever a question of who was coming" (the closest to saying the point, cut it if Jack finds it too near), C "a lemon curd emergency".

**Cuts from current.** Any reporter line that characterises the minister or the police. No narration of the ambulance's cargo; the paramedic is used once at most. The caption in A and C is the street's own signage, not a meme text card, so it needs no punchline of its own.

**Risk.** B's last line leans on the moral and could tip into saying the point aloud; A depends on viewers reading a small sign in a night shot, so the reporter has to say "sign" first or it is lost. C's "Britain, ladies and gentlemen" is a mild sneer at the viewer and may need cutting. The captions need the image to be sharp enough to read. In all three the reporter has to stay straight; if she winks, it collapses.

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim in all three versions, always as the minister's only line, after the reporter's setup and before her button.
