# Shape 04: Reporting from the future

The BadCode reporter is a correspondent who has already seen how this ends, and treats a red light on a wet road as a historical landmark, in the flat, grave cadence of UK regional news. All the dread is in the reporter's certainty; the picture does the rest. The ambulance is never mentioned as the victim and nobody states a moral. The reporter's authority is the joke, and the minister's trivial fury is what the future turns out to be about.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAPTION | [lower third, beat 2, regional-news style] BADCODE NEWS. LIVE FROM 2041. | 0 |
| REPORTER | [grave, straight to camera, ambulance blue light flickering behind] This junction will be in the history books. Not for the ambulance. For what comes through next. | 6.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, seething, back seat] I said raspberry. | 2.0 |
| REPORTER | [back to camera, not a flicker] He will chair the inquiry into this. It will be about the lemon curd. **(PUNCHLINE: "It will be about the lemon curd.")** | 4.5 |
| **Total** | about 29 words | **12.5** |

- **Why it works.** The future-correspondent device lets the reporter be certain without lecturing: knowing how it ends is the authority, so no moral is needed. "Not for the ambulance" tells the viewer where the weight is without saying whose time counts. The minister's raspberry line is pure bathos against that gravity, and the button pays it off by making the trivial grievance the thing history remembers, with the gap between the two vehicles left for the viewer to do the sums. Alternate buttons, same slot: "[beat] He was right about the lemon curd, as it happens." / "Lemon curd. Remember the lemon curd. Everything else is a footnote." / CAPTION only, after a held silent shot of the ambulance: "AMBULANCE: DELAYED. LEMON CURD: PENDING."
- **Cuts from current.** No paramedic or outrider line (beat 1 stays picture only, siren and rain). The reporter's opening is a single sentence plus one fragment. All explanation of the minister's job, party or the ambulance's cargo is gone. The caption exists only as the 0 s lower third.
- **Risk.** "Not for the ambulance" is the closest line to naming the point; if Jack finds it too pointed, swap to "Not for who was waiting. For who wasn't." or cut to just "This junction will be in the history books." The minister's "inquiry" tag implies a future that is fictional, so it must read as the reporter's knowledge, not a claim of fact. Possible overlap with another shape's button. Lip-sync on the long second reporter line needs a late-not-early placement; 4.5 s is tight for 14 words.
- **Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, unchanged, as the minister's only line (the "[on the phone]" direction is kept as a bracket).
