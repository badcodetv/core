# Shape 06: Reversal, the viewer gets there first

The reporter starts to explain and is never allowed to finish. Every line is built so the audience completes the sentence in their head a beat before the speaker could, and the speaker is then cut off, overtaken or flatly carries on. The point (whose time counts) is never said by anyone: the reporter sets out the facts like regional news, the sentence that would state the moral is interrupted by the motorcade, and the button treats the viewer's conclusion as already reached. Pushed to the extreme: the reporter says as little as possible and the film does the talking.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [straight to camera, ambulance and outrider behind, blue light on his balaclava] Behind me, an ambulance, held at a red light by police. The road ahead is completely empty. They are waiting for-- | 7.0 |
| MINISTER | [motorcade siren swallows the reporter; back seat, on the phone] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| REPORTER | [back on camera, motorcade tail lights passing behind him, not a flicker] **Sorry. Where was I?** *(PUNCHLINE)* | 1.5 |
| CAPTION | [lower third, regional TV font, 0 s spoken] LIVE: Lemon curd situation developing | 0 |
| **Total** | spoken ~10 s; ~20 s with picture-only holds (outrider hand, siren sweep, the ambulance still waiting at the end) | **~10** |

Alternate buttons (swap in for the last REPORTER line and caption):

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REPORTER | [to camera, ambulance still stopped behind him] **Back to the studio.** *(PUNCHLINE, ALT A)* | 1.0 |
| PARAMEDIC | [through the window, to nobody, tired] **Do we have to turn the siren off, or...?** *(PUNCHLINE, ALT B, he asks the outrider if he may stop being urgent)* | 2.5 |
| CAPTION | [meme caption over the still-waiting ambulance] **LEMON CURD: 1  PATIENT: STILL PENDING** *(PUNCHLINE, ALT C, silent)* | 0 |

**Why it works.** The first line hands the viewer every fact (ambulance, red light, empty road) and then stops mid-clause on "waiting for", so the audience supplies the ending themselves, which is far stronger than being told. The motorcade arrives like the answer, and the minister's trivial fury proves it without a word of moral. The reporter's "Sorry. Where was I?" lands the reversal: the one person who knows how this ends acts as if he lost his place, and the audience is already ahead of him. Deadpan authority becomes the joke, not the explanation.

**Cuts from current.** Any reporter line that names the minister, the gap or the cost of the delay. Any caption that explains. "Whose time counts" is never stated. The caption becomes a regional-news red herring rather than the meme explanation.

**Risk.** If the viewer does not complete "waiting for--" the short reads as a gag with no target; the empty road must be clearly visible in beat 1. The cut-off needs sound design (siren over his voice) or it looks like a mistake. "Where was I?" can read as a weak joke if the reporter performs it with any wink; it only works played flat. Alt B is the most human but risks sympathy for the paramedic overshadowing the satire.

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, delivered as the answer to the interrupted sentence.
