# Shape 03: The logic said plainly, like a traffic report

Approach: the reporter does not comment, joke or judge. She reads the situation out as a regional TV traffic bulletin: two vehicles, two statuses, one flat reason, in the cadence of "the M6 is closed northbound". The absurdity is carried by the arithmetic of the sentences, never by an adjective. The minister's protected line sits in the middle as the only emotion in the piece, and the button is a delay report that measures the wrong thing with total authority. The point (whose time counts) is never said.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAPTION | [lower-third, 0 s, over beat 1] LIVE: A-ROAD, 11.47PM | 0 |
| REPORTER | [to camera, rain on the mic, beat 2] Traffic update. The ambulance is held at a red light. The minister is not. Police confirm the minister is running late. | 7.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, beat 3, motorcade passing behind glass] I said raspberry. | 1.5 |
| REPORTER | [beat 4, straight back to camera, same tone] Ambulance delay: four minutes. Raspberry delay: ongoing. **[PUNCHLINE: "Raspberry delay: ongoing."]** Back to the studio. | 4.5 |
| **Total** | 36 words | **13.0** |

**Why it works.** Traffic-report grammar does the sneering for her: "held" against "is not", then a reason that is a complete non-reason delivered as procedure ("running late"). The ambulance gets a status and the minister gets a status and the viewer does the sum. The raspberry line then arrives as a perfect, unexplained piece of evidence, and the button reuses the report format to put a four-minute medical delay and an "ongoing" jam shortage on the same ledger, so the gap between the vehicles lands without one word about priorities. "Back to the studio" keeps her utterly straight and gives the picture a beat to hold on the ambulance's blue lights. Alternative punchlines to swap in if Jack wants a different button: "Motorists are advised to be patient. The minister is not." / "The ambulance has been told to expect a priority lane. It is not the priority." / "Normal service will resume. The minister's lemon curd has been escalated." / caption only, 15 s: ESTIMATED DELAY: AMBULANCE 4 MIN / JAM: UNKNOWN.

**Cuts from current.** Nothing in the current script is lost: "I said raspberry." stays verbatim. The empty reporter slot is filled with a bulletin rather than a rant; no outrider or paramedic line (the picture carries them), and the caption is a place-and-time strap only, so the meme space is left for Jack.

**Risk.** Too dry: with no joke in the beat-2 line it depends on delivery, so the TTS voice must be flat regional-news, not comedic. "Police confirm the minister is running late" edges toward stating the moral by implication; if Jack finds it too pointed, cut to "The minister is not." and let the raspberry do the work. The button's "Raspberry delay" assumes the audience heard the minister's line clearly over the motorcade. Four minutes of ambulance delay is an invented figure and should stay vague if the short is posted as a real incident (it is a fictional one: no real politician or party is named).

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, delivered on the phone with no pause or trim.
