# Shape 05: The other side wins

Every line is the winning side setting the terms and the reporter agreeing to them with total professional calm. The outrider names the rule in five words, the reporter (a straight regional newsreader) ratifies it as reasonable, the minister supplies the trivial fury, and the button is the reporter apologising to the wrong party. The reporter never objects, never wonders, never says whose time counts; the concession does all the work. Pushed to the extreme: the reporter is more deferential than the outrider.

## Dialogue 1: Priority Lane: the whole short

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OUTRIDER | [hand up, not looking at the ambulance] Hold there. Someone important's coming. | 2.5 |
| REPORTER | [straight to camera, rain, ambulance behind] Police have asked the ambulance to wait. We think that's reasonable. | 4.0 |
| MINISTER | [on the phone, back seat] No. I said raspberry. | 2.0 |
| REPORTER | [same shot, same face] **We would like to apologise to the minister for the delay.** | 3.0 |
| CAPTION | [meme text over the ambulance's blue lights] LEMON CURD INCIDENT: DEVELOPING | 0 |
| **Total** | about 27 words | **11.5** |

(Rest of the 20 s is picture: the hand, the wet road, the motorcade, the held beat on the reporter's face. Punchline is the bolded reporter line; the minister's "I said raspberry" is the setup it pays off, and the caption is the optional second sting.)

Alternate punchlines, swap in for the bold line:
- REPORTER: "In fairness, he did say raspberry." (reporter sides with the minister's grievance)
- REPORTER: "The ambulance has been very understanding." (sarcasm-free, so the absurdity is entirely in the delivery)
- OUTRIDER, as a final cut-in: "Nobody told him they had lemon curd, did they, love?" (outrider concedes nothing, reporter nods)

**Why it works.** The gap between the two vehicles is never described: it lives in the distance between "reasonable" and "raspberry". Because the reporter agrees with everyone in authority, the viewer is left to do the disagreeing, which is the only way the point (whose time counts) gets made without being said. The regional-news register is the joke carrier: a deadpan "we think that's reasonable" is funnier and more dangerous than outrage, and it never sneers at the viewer or names a party. The apology flips the expected button (reporter finally criticises) into the maximum concession.

**Cuts from current.** The reporter has no knowing-future-narrator line, no stated stakes and no moral; every explanation is gone. The caption is no longer the carrier of the meme, only a garnish. The paramedic never speaks.

**Risk.** Too much deference can read as the BadCode reporter genuinely endorsing the motorcade, so the delivery must carry a hair of authority-from-the-future (a held beat before "reasonable") or the satire dies. The outrider line is a little on the nose ("someone important"); if Jack finds it explains too much, cut it and let the raised hand do it, which shifts the opening to picture only. Four speaker turns in 11 s is tight for lip-sync, though each line is short.

**Protected lines.** "I said raspberry." survives verbatim, with only "No." added in front as a cue for the phone call; drop the "No." if Jack wants it pure.
