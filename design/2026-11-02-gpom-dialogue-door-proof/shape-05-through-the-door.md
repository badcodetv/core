# Shape 05: Through the door

The steel door is the instrument. Nobody on either side ever hears the other cleanly: Maddie's tannoy arrives inside as a clipped, squealing bark; the old lady is heard outside as a muffled voice through a foot of Cold War steel. The one clear voice on each side is the one we SEE, so the cut itself is the half-and-half. Whatever is muffled is written in full but needn't be intelligible, which lets the refusal be short, and the proof is not announced: it is heard through the door and seen only as light on faces.

- **Why it works.** The door does the compression. The refusal is split in two, so the old lady needs one clear line to be seen as stern and grieving, and the second, longer line can be buried under steel and still land because we already know what she means. The tannoy turns the apology into a joke at Maddie's expense (cheerful volume, wrong register) and then she switches it off and the sincerity arrives at human size, which is the whole turn of the scene: she is heard as a machine, then as a visitor. Nobody argues; the question "how could I win your trust?" is met with silence, so the humans' decision is shown as the monitor coming on and the door opening, never said. The replay is the answer, and it is delivered as sound through a wall and light on faces, which keeps it wordless, short and about them, not about her.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes entirely. No Sandra and Derek, no crowd murmur lines, no explanation of what the boss is or why. Maddie does not describe the replay, and the old lady never concedes.
- **Risk.** Muffled audio can read as a sound-mix mistake rather than a device if the first tannoy line (clipped, squealing inside) does not establish the rule; the cut to outside needs a visible door in frame. The old lady's second line may be unintelligible on purpose, so if a reader cannot tell she refused, the first clear line must carry the whole refusal. "Right. Have a look at this, then." makes Maddie the one who triggers the monitor, which is slightly less mysterious than the monitor coming on by itself; if that feels like explaining, cut it and let the question hang for four seconds. The existing lip-synced take covers only "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved."; the muffled second line needs no lip-sync because we are outside.
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." (line 1), "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." (line 3, reuses the existing take), "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." (line 4, verbatim but muffled), "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." (line 5), "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." (line 6), "How could I win your trust?" (line 7). Kai's "yeah" and "The thing is," were dropped from lines 5 and 6 for time.
