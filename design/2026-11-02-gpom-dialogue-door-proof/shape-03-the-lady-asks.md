# Shape 03: The lady asks

The other side sets the terms. Maddie never offers the proof; she asks how to win their trust (her protected line), and the old lady answers with a single demand: she heard something huge at her door and she wants to see it. Trust is extracted, not sold. Maddie only concedes and obeys, so the replay is the humans' idea and the scene needs no pitch. Every line turns something, and the lady gets the last word.

**Why it works.** The humans are never persuaded by anything Maddie says; they are persuaded by something they themselves insisted on seeing, which is the only way a grieving woman in charge of a hundred people could believably budge in 35 seconds. "Show me" is also the push-back the old doorstep talk never had: she turns Maddie's open question into a condition, so the scene has a single decision and the replay is its answer, not a lecture. The lady's "Derek. The bolts." gives the decision without anyone saying "we trust you", and Derek, handed the job, supplies the comic release that the stand-down needs after the dread. Half and half does its work: the seen line is the bare protected grievance, the muffled line is the refusal, and the clear "Show me" is the only moment the door seems to open, which is what the audience feels before it physically does.

**Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes entirely; the negotiation is now the bridge. Nothing about the AI's feelings, the time machine or why it happened is added; Maddie's whole case is five protected-ish sentences and one obedient "Hang on."

**Risk.** (1) "Show me" can read as Maddie being led by the nose; play her "Hang on." as relieved, not defeated. (2) The lady knowing something "came to our door" depends on the humans having heard the kill bots arrive through the earth; scene A must keep the rumble audible (or add a single low thump on the door). (3) The monitors-come-alive method needs one new plate of a dead CCTV bank and a footage insert that matches scene A's stand-down angle. (4) The tannoy "And, you know. All of it." is the only apology and it must carry the sincerity from her high-street intro; if it lands as flippant, the lady's second line loses its sting. (5) Roughly 38 s with the replay; if it runs long, drop "Hang on." first, then shave the final door beat.

**Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim. "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." opens the scene, followed by a short new apology tail. "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." is the seen, clear take (existing lip-sync reused). "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." is the muffled take, unchanged. "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." and "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." and "How could I win your trust?" run in Maddie's order, with the "Yeah," from Kai's note dropped for time; restore it as a one-word lead-in if the budget allows.
