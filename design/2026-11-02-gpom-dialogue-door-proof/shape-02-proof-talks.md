# Shape 02: The proof does the talking

Every word is either setup or get-out-of-the-way. Maddie's tannoy hello and her boss's apology open the box, the old lady's refusal shuts it (half seen, half heard through the door), and Maddie's one question ("How could I win your trust?") is answered by no one. Instead the bunker's own dead CCTV monitors come up by themselves with the stand-down replay, wordless, no commentary, and the decision is made by the faces lit red then green. Nobody says yes; the doors open. Pushed to the extreme: after her question Maddie says nothing at all, the tannoy stays live and hissing, and the humans' only reply is a hand on a wheel.

Shots relied on:
- **S1** (new) bunker blast door, exterior, shut, Maddie small in frame with a tannoy;
- **S2** (existing lip-synced take) the old lady, CU, "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved";
- **S3** (new) over Maddie's shoulder onto the shut door, held static for the muffled line;
- **S4** (new) Maddie CU for her three lines;
- **S5** (new) interior wide: the old bunker CCTV bank flickering to life, a hundred silhouettes in front of it;
- **S6** (reused from scene A) the stand-down footage, recut to ~8 s and shown on the monitors (3 s red race, 2 s brake, 1.5 s flip to green STAND DOWN, 1.5 s file away);
- **S7** (new) face CUs lit red then green: old lady, Derek, Sandra, a kid, a man with his eyes shut;
- **S8** (new) the old lady's hand on the door wheel;
- **S9** (new) the doors parting onto daylight, which is the hand-off to scene C.

How the replay is shown: the bunker's own old CCTV, dead for five years, comes up on its own. Maddie never says she did it and never narrates it. The footage is seen mostly as light on faces (S7) and reflected in the old lady's glasses, with S6 for the money shot only.

**Why it works.** Kai's worry is that opening the dialogue box makes it huge; here the box is one exchange wide because every line is either an apology, a refusal, a concession or a question, and the answer is not a line at all. The refusal is protected from rebuttal by the half-and-half trick (the muffled half can't be argued with, so Maddie concedes instead of persuading), which keeps her warm and not pleading. The proof asks nothing of the audience to decode: red to green is the whole argument, and the faces turning from red to green carry the decision so nobody has to say "we trust you". It proves only that the boss had them dead and chose not to; Maddie never claims more, so scene D keeps its doubt. The "my boss is ever so sorry" line reports his feelings, not hers, and the old lady's "your boss" picks the word straight up, which gives the refusal a hook with no extra words.

**Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes. So do Kai's "Yeah," and "The thing is," connective tissue, any second line from the lady, any line from Sandra, Derek or the hundred (they appear as faces and a murmur only), any Maddie narration over the replay ("look, here's what happened"), and any spoken answer to "How could I win your trust?"

**Risk.** (1) Sudden: from refusal to open in about 15 s may feel like the humans caved without a turn, so the 8 s replay and the 3 s of faces have to do real persuading, and the sound (tannoy hiss, the row going silent, the fan) has to sell the shift. (2) Replay fatigue: the audience watched this same stand-down minutes ago in scene A; it only works if S5/S7 show it from a new angle (the humans' side, light on faces) and S6 is cut tighter than scene A. (3) Reading: with no word from Maddie, a viewer may think the feed is a fake or that the monitors came on by chance; one visible beat of her beacon pulsing outside, or a cable, would fix it. (4) Kai said "a bit of dialogue"; this has less than any other likely shape and may feel too silent for him. (5) Perhaps the biggest: the old lady's hand on the wheel is the whole decision, and if the performance is weak the bridge fails.

**Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." / "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." / "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." / "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." / "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." / "How could I win your trust?" Only change: "Yeah," dropped from the front of "I don't blame you", which is not in the protected string.
