# Shape 10: Ending first, the proof is only ever seen on faces

I wrote the last ten seconds first and let them decide everything before them. The ending is eight wordless seconds in which we never see the stand-down footage itself, only its light on the hundred faces and reflected in the old lady's glasses: red, racing, a brake, green. Then the doors open on Maddie small in the daylight, so C's "Go on. I promise you'll be okay." is the first thing anyone says after the proof. Working backwards, the humans never get a line that concedes anything, because the conceding has to be the picture. That leaves only what the proof is the answer to: a hello, a grief, a refusal, and a question. Every word earns its place by making the silence afterwards the answer.

- **Why it works.** The humans do not say yes, and Maddie does not win a debate. The question "How could I win your trust?" is answered by eight seconds in which a hundred people watch the thing that nearly killed them lose its nerve, and we watch them watch. Showing only the light keeps it cheap (no extra shot of the footage beyond what scene A already shot, run through a CRT glow and a red-to-green grade), keeps it from being explained, and puts the audience in the humans' seat: we are the ones reading faces to see what they decide. The half-and-half split makes the refusal a one-two: the grief is seen once, the stubbornness is heard through steel, which also lets it be longer and rougher without a second lip-sync shot.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes entirely, along with any doorstep explaining of who the boss is, why he is sorry, or what the plan is. Maddie never says "my boss" in this bridge at all: the old lady does it for her, so the word first arrives as an accusation and the proof answers it. No Sandra and Derek banter, no murmur of the hundred.
- **Risk.** Eight wordless seconds on faces is long, and if the faces are not distinct and well lit it just reads as a pause. It depends on the reflected-light shot being gorgeous: the lady's glasses, three or four named faces, the red-to-green change landing on cue. Also, "And, you know. Everything." is the one added line; if the apology feels thin it may need a beat more, but adding words is what grows the box. And because nobody inside speaks after the refusal, a viewer could read the opening as the humans being overpowered rather than choosing; the lady's glasses going green and one held breath have to carry the choice.
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." / "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." / "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." / "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." / "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." / "How could I win your trust?" Only addition is "And, you know. Everything." as the apology for how things have gone, and "How could I win your trust?" joins the previous sentence in one breath.
