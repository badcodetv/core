# Shape 07: The chorus, a hundred doubts through the door

The hundred behind the door are one animal with a hundred mouths. Neither lead argues at length: the old lady gets only her two protected lines, Maddie gets only hers, and everything else the humans feel arrives as overlapping whispered fragments ("Mine." "Don't." "It's talking.") that cut under and across the leads, so resistance is heard as a crowd rather than a debate. The crowd stops dead for the replay, and its silence is the answer.

- **Why it works.** The chorus carries the length of the argument without anyone arguing: one old woman's line is a verdict, and a hundred fragments under it make it a room. Overlap lets a lot of doubt fit in a second of clock, since three people saying "Mine." takes one beat. The muffled half and half hides the old lady's second line behind the door, so it is felt rather than followed, and the crowd echoing "everybody" does the repetition for her. Nobody says "we trust you"; the chorus simply goes silent for the replay, which is the whole decision, and the opening door is the only reply. Sandra's "Derek, shush." keeps one human, comic breath in the grief.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes, along with any explanation of why they open the door. No Maddie pleading, no second round of objections, no old-lady speech beyond the two protected lines.
- **Risk.** Overlapping whispers can turn to mush and cost intelligibility; the lead lines must sit clear and the chorus needs a low mix with at most three distinct voices, not a hundred. Eight bodies of murmur can also read as a crowd of extras mumbling, and the 1 s chorus beats could be cut by an editor and nothing would show, so they have to be cast as specific voices (a man, a girl, an old man). The "Derek, shush." joke could undercut the grief; it can be dropped for 1 s. The tannoy "everything" beat may feel too soft before the old lady's hit. And the chorus gives the humans a collective mind that scene C may not carry through (they follow her out as individuals).
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." (opens the tannoy line, "And the... everything." is an addition after it); "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved."; "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody."; "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks."; "We've got a way we might be able to fix this."; "How could I win your trust?" Only the old lady's second line is treated (muffled through the door), not altered.
