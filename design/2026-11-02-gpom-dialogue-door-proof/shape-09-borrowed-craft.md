# Shape 09: Borrowed craft (concede, ask, then let the image answer)

I looked at how films compress "why would they trust the enemy" and took five techniques (below), then pushed them to the extreme: Maddie only concedes and asks, never argues or explains; the old lady is the whole hundred in one face; the proof is the only answer, and it arrives as a colour change on faces (red to green) with no line spoken after it. Every spoken line either hurts or turns the scene, and the scene ends on a bolt, not a sentence.

**Research: how others do it (from memory, described not quoted).**
- *The Day the Earth Stood Still* (1951): Klaatu's proof is a demonstration of power used with restraint (the world's machines stop, nobody is hurt). Trust comes from what a dangerous thing chose NOT to do.
- *Terminator 2*: Sarah Connor is won over by watching the machine hold back (it disables people without killing them) and by seeing it in front of her son, not by anything it says. Restraint shown, not claimed.
- *Arrival*: Louise takes off the protective suit in the chamber. The offer is vulnerability, made first, and nobody argues it. Trust is a risk taken in front of the other side.
- *WALL-E*: the humans on the ship are moved by footage of Earth and a living plant, with almost no talk. The image does the persuading; faces change in silence.
- *The Iron Giant* and *E.T.*: the child decides, then the adults catch up. A single small person stands in for the crowd's decision; the crowd's doubt is voiced by one stern figure, then dropped on a visual beat.
- *Paddington*: the family's turn happens through one doubter being overruled by the least expected person in a couple of lines. The "yes" is a half-sentence, never a speech.

**Techniques extracted.** (1) Proof by restraint: show the costly thing the stronger party did not do. (2) Concede first: the one who agrees with the objection ends the argument. (3) Ask, don't assert: handing the question to the other side gives them the agency, so the decision feels theirs. (4) One face for the crowd: the hundred is a murmur; one stern person carries the resistance and the turn. (5) Wordless turn: the decision is a change in light or a mechanical sound, never a stated "OK".

- **Why it works.** Each lesson is borrowed and compressed: Maddie concedes the objection before the lady has finished making it (so there is no argument to drag on), then hands her the question, "How could I win your trust?" (so the lady, not Maddie, owns the decision), and the answer is restraint shown, not claimed: the machine had them dead and stood down. The decision itself is wordless: red light turns to green on a hundred faces and a bolt moves, nobody says "we trust you". The lady is the whole hundred, seen once and then heard through steel, which halves her talk and lets her grief stay hard without a debate. Using the bunker's own dead monitors means the evidence comes through their kit, not from the visitor, so it reads as theirs. "My boss" in Maddie's second line is the match that sets off the lady's protected line, so the resistance has a cause on screen.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes entirely. Cut also: any explanation of what the boss is doing, any "I promise" before the doors open (scene C owns it), Sandra and Derek's bickering beyond the held arm, and every line after the replay.
- **Risk.** (1) The lady's two refusal lines (3 and 4) say nearly the same thing; if it reads as repetition, the muffled line is the one to shave (see below). (2) The apology is short and carries the sincerity by sound (the volume drop) rather than words; if the earlier intro does not carry it, it may feel thin. (3) The replay is only as good as the footage being recognisable as the same stand-down; it needs the red-to-green flip to read at a glance, and 8 s of near-silence can feel long if the faces are not strong. (4) Nothing is said about whether the plan works, which is right for scene D but means this bridge leans on the audience granting that restraint is enough.
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." (line 2); "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." (line 4, existing lip-sync take, seen); "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." (line 6, muffled; if it needs to be shorter, keep it whole in the take but cut the audible mix so only "...no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." is clear, and drop "There's" under the door thud); "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks."; "We've got a way we might be able to fix this."; "How could I win your trust?" (split across two takes to leave a beat, but unaltered).
