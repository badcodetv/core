# Shape 08: The door's own camera is the argument

I let one object in the bunker do the arguing: the old CCTV wall by the blast door, five years of grey snow that the humans stare at out of habit, fed by a single door camera nobody trusted. Maddie never offers evidence and never explains. She only points at what the room already owns ("Your door's got a camera. Give it a thump."), so the humans pull the proof out themselves and the decision stays theirs. The replay is their own camera's footage, not her beacon's, so it can't be accused of being a trick; the green STAND DOWN is the only colour on a grey screen and it lights a hundred faces. Spoken words are cut to the bone because the monitor, the muffle and the track marks outside carry the rest.

- **Why it works.** The bunker's own equipment makes the case, so there is no argument to lose: the camera was recording the whole time, the humans just never looked. The humans are never talked round. Maddie asks, and the room answers with a screen. The half-and-half cut does real work: the first half of the refusal is seen (a grieving face), the second is muffled through steel so it can be short and can't be rebutted. The scorched track marks that stop a foot from Maddie's wheels are the outside proof she's looking at while she concedes, a silent echo of the replay. Nobody says "we trust you"; the wheel turning is the verdict.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes, along with any spoken explanation of what the footage means. No second apology, no recap of the boss's regret (the intro already did that), no "you're safe".
- **Risk.** The door camera conveniently still working after five years is a plausibility stretch (one thump covers it as a joke, and a thump on dead tech is a very British gag, but some will see the cheat). The scene needs the CCTV wall, a fisheye door-cam look and the replay re-rendered as monochrome with green, which is a new asset, not a reuse of the colour stand-down clips. "Give it a thump" could read as flippant right after the grief line. If it does, swap to "Your door's got a camera. Look at it." The old lady never speaks after the refusal, so her turn to yes must live entirely in her face and hand; if that performance isn't in the footage, the ending is mute but unconvincing.
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." / "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." (the existing lip-synced take, seen inside) / "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." (muffled outside) / "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." / "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." / "How could I win your trust?" Only the leading "Yeah," from Kai's version is dropped for time. The apology is the new, short "Sorry about everything, actually."
