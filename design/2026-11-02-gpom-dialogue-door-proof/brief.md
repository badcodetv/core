## 1. Where it sits

GPOM's second half. A hundred survivors have hidden in a Cold War bunker for five years since the AI removed
humanity. Their row (Sandra and Derek, about a toilet seat) leaks through the earth; the AI's kill bots lock on
and race to the bunker door. Maddie, a small cheerful West Country delivery robot who works for the AI ("my
boss"), rings the boss; the kill bots brake on the doorstep, flip to green STAND DOWN and file away. Maddie,
shaky: "Good job. That was close." **The humans inside saw none of it.** All they know: something huge came to
the door and went away.

Earlier, Maddie's intro on the high street (spoken to the AUDIENCE, not to the humans) already landed the AI's
regret: he misses you something rotten, he worked out consciousness drives the world, "we really feel like we
fucked this up". So the audience already believes she's sincere. The humans don't.

**The gap (this dialogue):** after the stand-down we jump to the humans following Maddie out of the bunker
(scene C opens: Maddie on the step in the daylight doorway, "Go on. I promise you'll be okay." / "You've got my
word."). Kai: "too weak a link between kill bots were about to arrive to kill you, and now you're willing to just
get into the machine that the AI made." The bridge must earn the humans' decision to trust her, fast.

## 2. Kai's mechanism: THE PROOF (picked)

Maddie replays the kill bots' own stand-down footage to the humans: red KILL MODE racing at their door, it brakes,
flips to green STAND DOWN, they file away. A hundred faces watching, the light of the replay on them. Kai loves
this. It proves one thing: the machine had them dead and chose not to. It does NOT prove the plan works (that
doubt belongs to scene D: "Does it even work?" / "Then say you don't know.", and D's line "There's nothing I can
say that proves it"). Don't spend D's doubt here.

How she shows it is open (propose one): the bunker's own old CCTV monitors flicker and the feed comes up; her
beacon throws it on the blast doors; a little screen on her hatch held to the spyhole; anything better.

## 3. Kai's dialogue (his words, keep them close)

- Doors still SHUT. Maddie outside, speaking in through a tannoy / megaphone: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the
  intrusion." and the apology for how everything has gone. Sincere; it builds on her intro.
- The old lady refuses: "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." (A lip-synced take
  already exists of her saying **"Your boss killed everyone we ever loved."**; reusing it saves a shot.)
- **Half and half:** we SEE her say it inside, then cut outside to Maddie and hear the rest of it muffled
  through the door (audio treated to sound like that).
- Maddie: "Yeah, I don't blame you. I can see how it looks. The thing is, we've got a way we might be able to fix
  this. How could I win your trust?"
- Then the proof. Then the doors open (scene C picks up on the step).

## 4. Kai's challenge (the whole point)

"The moment we open that box, it becomes huge." The last doorstep talk grew to a minute-plus, explained
everything, removed the mystery, and nobody pushed back. **Keep it tiny.** Target: spoken words **20 s or
less**, the replay itself wordless (about 8 s), the whole bridge about 35 s. Every line must turn something.

## 5. Rules

- **Never mention the time machine, the cost, consciousness or the storyverse.** "We've got a way we might be
  able to fix this" is as far as it goes. D names the machine first ("It's the time machine.") and Kai likes that.
- Real resistance from the lady (stern, grieving, not a pushover). The decision is shown, never argued to a
  conclusion: no one says "we trust you".
- No moral said aloud. No "you've been duped". Name the decision-maker (the boss), never "AI" as a force.
- Maddie: warm, West Country, a bit gossipy about "my boss", sincere under the cheer; she reports his feelings
  more than her own. She does not plead.
- Side characters available: Sandra and Derek (the toilet-seat couple; comic, bickering; Derek grumpy), the
  hundred as a murmur. Optional.
- The tannoy joke was loved before (Kai: "Love it"): her chirpy voice cutting a row dead.
- Through-the-door audio is a tool: what's muffled can't be argued with, and can be shortened.
- Stage directions in [square brackets]. Give seconds per line.
