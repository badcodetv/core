# Shape 04: Comedy first, the tannoy cuts the row dead

The jokes carry the tension and keep it short. We open on Sandra and Derek mid-row (the toilet seat has not been settled in five years), and Maddie's chirpy tannoy lands on top of it, bad timing as a weapon. Every line either gets a laugh or turns the door; the old lady is the one person not laughing, so her refusal hits harder for the clowning round it. The apology is sincere but arrives like a customer-service announcement. The proof is wordless and plays on the bunker's own dead CCTV monitors, so nobody has to explain it.

- **Why it works.** The comedy is the tension valve: the audience laughs at Sandra and Derek, then the old lady drops the temperature to zero with a line nobody can joke over, and the laugh dies in the room the way it would in life. Because the humour ends where the grief begins, the refusal reads as real resistance instead of a speed bump. The tannoy and "Derek. Door." bracket the scene: a chirpy voice interrupting a petty row, and a petty-row voice ending the standoff. Nobody says "we trust you"; the decision is a married woman telling her husband to open a door. The proof does the persuading, so no one has to talk themselves round, and the stand-down is shown in the bunker's own CCTV so the humans see it with the kit they already own.
- **Cuts from current.** The black card "Some negotiation later." goes entirely. No time machine, no cost, no explaining, no second round of argument, no Maddie pleading.
- **Risk.** Derek's seat joke could sour the apology before it lands (the audience may be laughing when "Sorry about the intrusion" should be sincere): mitigated by Maddie's cheer slipping on "And the rest of it. Sorry." The row may also read as filler if the audience does not remember the seat from scene A. "Derek. Door." could feel like the humans caved too easily (Sandra instead of the old lady deciding): if so, give the last word to the old lady, a single nod, no line. The "half and half" cut needs the seen shot and the muffled audio to be clearly the same speaker.
- **Protected lines.** All six survive verbatim: "Oh, hello, humans. Sorry about the intrusion." / "Your boss killed everyone we ever loved." / "There's no way we're going anywhere with you. You killed everybody." / "I don't blame you. I can see how it looks." / "We've got a way we might be able to fix this." / "How could I win your trust?" Only the stage direction and the apology's tail ("And the rest of it. Sorry.") are new.
