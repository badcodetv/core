# Shape 07: The chorus: his butler, his accountant and a donor testify

Every other voice in the short is a witness for the appeal. The narrator shrinks to a soft-voiced framer at the top and the bottom; the middle is handed to a butler, an accountant and a donor, each speaking to camera in the grave tone of a charity case study, each treating "only £100,000" as a tragedy. The name is invented (Rupert Ashgrove-Vane, "Rupert" on the voiceover) and points at no real chancellor. The only statistic is the checked one, £39,039 (ONS, 2025 median full-time pay), and it arrives on screen after the chorus has finished. Pushed to the extreme: nobody ever says the moral, and the witnesses never break.

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [sad piano, slow motion, black and white] This is Rupert. | 1.5 |
| BUTLER | [hands folded, looking at the floor] He hasn't been the same since the second wine fridge broke. | 3.5 |
| ACCOUNTANT | [shaking her head] Rupert only earns £100,000 a year. We've tried everything. | 3.5 |
| DONOR | [wiping an eye, kind, sincere] I give three pounds a month. It's not much. But to Rupert, it's... [beat] a Chablis. | 4.5 |
| NARRATOR | For just £3 a month, you could help Rupert keep his wine at the correct temperature. | 4 |
| SCREEN | [freeze frame, piano stops] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 a year (ONS, 2025) | 0 (held 2 s, no voice) |
| BUTLER | [into the silence, quietly] Sir will want the fridge by Friday. | 2.5 |
| **Total** |  | **19.5** (about 20 s voice, 2 s held card) |

**Alternates, same slots (buttons for Jack to pick):**

- Donor, sharper: "I'm on thirty-nine thousand myself, but somebody has to do something for Rupert." [CHECK: works only if we keep the donor as a median earner. It makes the donor the fool. Prefer the Chablis line.] (5 s)
- Accountant: "We've cut back. He's down to one car and a driver who also drives the car." (4 s)
- Final button, narrator: "Rupert thanks you. His butler thanks you. His butler's wine fridge thanks you." (4 s, replaces the butler button)
- Final button, silent: hold the card; the butler's soft cough, then the sad piano restarts. (0 s)
- Cold open, 1 s: the standard charity-ad title card "RUPERT'S STORY" over piano, then straight to the Butler.

**Why it works.** The sad charity advert always runs on testimony: the carer, the neighbour, the one-line quote from the person who "sees it every day". Handing that to a butler, an accountant and a tearful donor makes the format recognisable inside two seconds without a word of explanation, and each witness is a different rung of the comfortable class defending the chancellor's comfort, so the target is the decision-maker and his circle, not working people. The donor is kind and sincere, so no one is mocked for caring; the joke is what they are being asked to care about. Because the chorus plays it dead straight, the median card lands as a silence the audience fills themselves, and the butler's last line stings by being the one voice that never noticed the card.

**Cuts from current.** The narrator's two long sentences are split among four voices; "afford a second wine fridge" becomes the butler's grief (the fridge breaking) and the narrator's tidied-up ask (keep his wine at the correct temperature). "Jeremy" becomes "Rupert". Nothing from the SCREEN card is cut.

**Risk.** Four voices in 20 seconds means four voice casts and four lip or caption treatments on a phone short, which is expensive and can sound like a sketch, not an advert. The butler and accountant are fiction-class stereotypes (a hair from "posh people bad"); keep them dignified, never sneering. "Rupert Ashgrove-Vane" must be checked against living MPs before locking [CHECK]. The sincere donor can read as mocking people who give to charity; keep her warm and don't make her a fool. The £100,000 figure comes from the source quote ("£100k a year doesn't go far"), not a verified chancellor salary, so we never say it is Rupert's real pay, only what he "earns" in the joke.

**Protected lines.** None protected. The slate draft survives in spirit: "This is Jeremy. Jeremy only earns £100,000 a year." becomes the narrator opener plus the accountant's line (the invented name replaces Jeremy); "For just £3 a month, you could help him afford a second wine fridge" becomes the narrator's ask, with "wine fridge" moved into the butler's first line. The SCREEN card text is unchanged.
