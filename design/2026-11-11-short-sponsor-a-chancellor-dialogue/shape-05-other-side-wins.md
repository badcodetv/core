# Shape 05: The other side wins (the chancellor pleads his own case, sincerely)

The chancellor takes the microphone himself, in the sad-piano charity format, and argues for his own hardship with complete sincerity: no winking, no villain voice, a decent man who has talked himself into believing it. He is so reasonable that for two seconds we nearly nod. The median-pay card then cuts the plea off mid-breath, and a short narrator button lands the sting. The name is invented (Sir Rupert Aldersgate-Finch, "Rupert" for the ad); the £100,000 is his own claim, and the only fact on screen is £39,039 (ONS, 2025 median full-time pay). **Why it works.** The first two seconds are the format: monochrome, piano, a caption, a soft voice. Letting him speak for himself is the extreme: a sincere man is funnier and nastier than a narrator mocking him, because the audience hears the rationalisation in real time and feels themselves almost agree ("it doesn't go far" is exactly what people say about their own money). The median card is then the turn with no lecture needed; the contrast does the work. The button is mild and nurturing-underneath: the contempt is for the plea, not for people.
