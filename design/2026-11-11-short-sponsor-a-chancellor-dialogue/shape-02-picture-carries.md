# Shape 02: the picture carries it

The pictures do the sad-advert work, so the voice only says what a picture cannot: the number, the name, the price. Every shot I lean on is named in square brackets, and any line that merely described one was cut. "Jeremy" is renamed to an invented Hugo (Jack: swap freely, any name that is not a real chancellor). The only statistic is the checked £39,039; the £100,000 is the quoted salary, not a claimed fact about a real person.

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [SHOT A: b&w slow-mo close-up, a man in a suit alone at the end of a vast marble table, a wine glass held in both hands like a bowl of soup; sad piano] This is Hugo. | 1.5 |
| NARRATOR | [SHOT A, push in on his lip trembling] Hugo only earns £100,000 a year. | 3 |
| NARRATOR | [SHOT B: close-up of ONE wine fridge, door open, light humming, a single drop of condensation running down the glass] He has one. | 1.5 |
| NARRATOR | [SHOT C: the empty alcove beside it, dust in a shaft of light; the camera lingers] £3 a month. | 1.5 |
| SCREEN | [piano cuts dead; FREEZE on Hugo's face] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 (ONS, April 2025). Two full seconds, no voice. | 0 |
| NARRATOR | [SHOT D: the fridge light clicks off in the empty alcove, or Hugo toasts the camera, tiny nod] Hugo thanks you. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | | **~9 voice + 2 silent card = ~11 s** |

Buttons for Jack (swap the last row; each is 1-2 s):
- "Hugo thanks you." (above; the polite sting)
- SCREEN: "Please give generously." (the charity sign-off, said by nobody)
- NARRATOR: "It's not much. It's not nothing. It's two fridges." (extra 3 s, still in budget)
- Silence: end on the freeze frame alone and let the platform loop it (the extreme version: the button is the loop).

**Why it works.** The format is recognised from the first frame, not the first word: black and white, slow motion, piano, the close-up on a trembling lip, so I spend no words on "charity advert". "He has one" and "£3 a month" are fragments the images finish: a fridge with condensation reads as tears, and an empty alcove reads as a gap in his life, which is the whole absurd hardship without a single adjective. The silent card is the turn: the music stops and the real median sits on his face. Nothing says who is struggling; the viewer does the sum, which is the no-lecture rule met by omission.

**Cuts from current.** "second wine fridge" becomes the visual pair of one fridge plus the empty alcove, so "second" and "wine" are never spoken. "you could help him afford" goes (the appeal's grammar is implied by "£3 a month" over the alcove). The line "Jeremy only earns..." loses its second sentence structure and keeps "only". The name "Jeremy" is replaced.

**Risk.** It lives or dies on shots B and C landing without text; if the alcove reads as just a room, "£3 a month" loses its referent and the joke goes dead, so the stills must be checked first. At about 9 s of voice it is under the 10 s target, so Jack may want one more beat. "Hugo" might still be read as a pointed nod to a real chancellor, so check against current office-holders before locking. The £100,000 figure is a quoted salary, not a claim about any real person; keep the quote card off screen unless it is sourced [CHECK]. The turn is a bare number with no framing, which relies on the audience knowing it is the typical wage; the "(ONS, April 2025)" label carries that.

**Protected lines.** None. The slate draft's "£3 a month" and "only earns £100,000 a year" survive near verbatim; "second wine fridge" survives only as picture.
