# Shape 08: The place is the argument

Every detail of hardship is a room. The camera never shows Crispin's face, only where he lives: a grey slow push through a vast kitchen to a wine fridge, then the second wall where the second fridge would go. The narrator never calls him rich and never explains; the charity-advert grammar ("This is... he lives here...") does the work while the place contradicts the sad voice. The invented name is Crispin, and nobody real is named or shown.

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [soft, sad piano, black and white, slow push in on a gate] This is Crispin. | 1.5 |
| NARRATOR | [the house, then a kitchen the size of a bus depot] This is where Crispin lives. | 2.5 |
| NARRATOR | Crispin only earns a hundred thousand pounds a year. | 3.0 |
| NARRATOR | [one wine fridge, humming, lit like a shrine] This is Crispin's wine fridge. It is full. | 3.0 |
| NARRATOR | [pan to an empty patch of wall] This is the space where a second one should be. For three pounds a month, you can fill it. | 4.5 |
| SCREEN | [freeze frame, music cuts] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 a year (ONS, April 2025) | 0.0 (2 s held, no voice) |
| NARRATOR | [warm, flat, a beat late] Please. Give what you can. [BUTTON A] He'd do the same for you. [pause] He wouldn't. | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **17.5 voice (+2 s card)** |

Button options for Jack (swap the last narrator line):
- A (above): "Please. Give what you can. He'd do the same for you. He wouldn't." Tone: sweet, then cold.
- B: "The fridge is full, Crispin. The rest of us are on the second shelf." Quiet and bitter, aims at the comfort gap.
- C: "Crispin's wall is empty. Your wall is somebody else's. Give generously." Sting about the landlord; keep only if the reader is left feeling seen, not blamed.
- D: "Sponsor a chancellor today. [beat] Somebody has to." Shortest, driest.

**Why it works.** The format is recognised in two seconds from the piano, the grey and "This is Crispin". Every line is a place: gate, kitchen, fridge, empty wall. The audience supplies the verdict while the narrator stays sincere, so the argument is carried by what is on screen and not by any stated moral. The £100,000 line gets a flat delivery, and the empty wall (hardship as a gap for a second fridge) is the absurd image. The median card then lands as a plain fact with no commentary, and the button is the only joke that speaks.

**Cuts from current.** "Jeremy" (points at a real chancellor) becomes Crispin. "You could help him afford a second wine fridge" becomes a space on a wall, so the fridge is shown, not said. The narrator is allowed one more line after the card than the draft had, because the sting needs a landing.

**Risk.** The visual does half the work, and the short fails if the kitchen shots read as ordinary stock; they need to be absurdly big. "Crispin" may still sound like a posh stereotype; the contempt must stay on the comfort, not on class. The £100,000 figure is the slate's quoted salary, so keep it as Crispin's invented pay and do not present it as any real office-holder's pay [CHECK before it is attributed to "a chancellor"]. Button A's "He wouldn't" is the sharpest and could read as a personal attack; B or D is safer. About 17.5 s of voice is near the 20 s ceiling, so trim "It is full." if the cut runs long.

**Protected lines.** None. The only fact on screen, £39,039 (ONS, April 2025), is kept exactly as checked.
