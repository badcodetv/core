# Shape 10: Ending first, the button that gets shared

I wrote the button before anything else: the last line a viewer repeats to a friend. Then I worked backwards, so every earlier line is there only to load that button, and anything that does not load it was cut. The invented name is Rupert (no real chancellor is called Rupert). The only fact on screen is the checked £39,039 (ONS, April 2025). The aim stays on the comfort of the decision-maker, never on the viewer.

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [soft, slow, wobbling piano, black and white] This is Rupert. | 1.5 |
| NARRATOR | Rupert only earns £100,000 a year. | 2.5 |
| NARRATOR | [beat, Rupert gazes into an empty wine fridge] Tonight, his second wine fridge stands empty. | 3.5 |
| NARRATOR | For just £3 a month, you can help. | 2.0 |
| SCREEN | [freeze frame, piano stops, 2s of silence] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 a year (ONS, April 2025) | 0 voice (2.5 hold) |
| NARRATOR | [gentler than before] Or keep your £3. You're going to need it. | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **12.5 voice (about 15 with the hold)** |

**Why it works.** The button is the whole short. "Or keep your £3" flips the charity ask in the one place the format cannot survive, the donor is told to say no, and it is kind (the nurturing note under the snark) while the contempt stays on the decision-makers. The £3 is planted in line 4 so the last line pays it off. Silence before the card does the turning, so no moral is stated. Button options, strongest first: (1) "Or keep your £3. You're going to need it." (2) "Please give generously. He never has." (3) "Rupert has been struggling for a long time. Mostly with the concept." (4) "Please give generously. Somebody in this country has to." (5, no voice) the card holds and nothing is said. Dead line to cut if 20s is tight: "Tonight, his second wine fridge stands empty."

**Cuts from current.** "Jeremy" becomes Rupert. "you could help him afford" is shortened to "you can help", and the wine fridge moves into a visual line so the ask stays short. The median card stays, with the month added to the source. The button is new.

**Risk.** "You're going to need it" could read as a lecture or as pity for the viewer, so deliver it warm, not smug. "Second wine fridge" is an invented detail, as is "Rupert", so it must read as satire and not a claim about a real person. £100,000 is the quote's figure, not a checked statistic, so if the on-screen quote is used, attribute it as the source quote. Nothing about the median should say who earns it, just the number.

**Protected lines.** None. The slate's "only earns £100,000 a year", the £3 a month, the wine fridge and the £39,039 card all survive in spirit; only the name and the order changed.
