# Shape 04: From the future, the narrator hijacks the advert

The charity advert runs straight for the first beat so it is recognised in two seconds, then the BadCode narrator (a superintelligence from 2054 that watched this go wrong) cuts through the piano, drops the soft voice, and answers the appeal with what it knows. The median-pay card is the turn; the button is the narrator's total-authority gallows line about the future. The chancellor is invented: "Crispin" (named as a charity-advert first name, no surname, no real chancellor).

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR (soft, slow, sad piano, black and white) | This is Crispin. Crispin is a Chancellor. | 3.0 |
| NARRATOR (soft) | He only earns a hundred thousand pounds a year. | 3.0 |
| NARRATOR (soft) | For just three pounds a month, you could help Crispin afford a second wine fridge. | 5.0 |
| NARRATOR (same voice, now flat and certain) [piano cuts dead; one-second silence] | Sorry. I'm calling from 2054. I've read the file. | 3.0 |
| SCREEN | [freeze frame, held 2 s, no voice] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 a year (ONS, April 2025) | 0 |
| NARRATOR (warm, dry) | Crispin will be fine. In 2054, the fridges survive. | 3.0 |
| **Total** | | **17.0** |

Alternative buttons, same slot (about 3 s each), for Jack to swap in:

- "Crispin will be fine. He's always fine. Look after yourself instead." (care register; most nurturing)
- "I can't send money back. I can send the warning. This is it." (breaks the advert's own ask)
- "Don't sponsor Crispin. Sponsor yourself. [beat] Please. I've seen the fridges." (silliest)
- Silence on the card, then one soft piano note and a SCREEN end card: "Appeal closed." (no voice button, 0 s)

**Why it works.** The first twelve seconds are a faithful charity advert (voice, piano, black and white, "for just £3 a month"), so the format lands in two seconds and the absurd hardship does the joke on its own: a Chancellor on a hundred grand needing a second wine fridge. Then the piano dies and the narrator breaks in as the one voice that knows how the story ends, which gives the median-pay card an authority the bare text lacks and makes the turn a hijack rather than a caption. The button stays in the BadCode register: certain, dark, and aimed at the comfort of the decision-maker ("the fridges survive"), with the future doing the sting so no moral is stated and nobody is told they were fooled.

**Cuts from current.** "This is Jeremy" becomes "This is Crispin" (invented name). "Jeremy only earns £100,000 a year" is stretched to the full charity cadence and gains "Chancellor" up front so the target is the office, not a person. "You could help him afford" becomes "you could help Crispin afford" (the name repeated, as the real adverts do). The SCREEN card stays but gains "a year" and the month, and the narrator now speaks over the turn rather than leaving it silent. Added: the 2054 interruption and a button.

**Risk.** (1) "2054" and the future-narrator lean may feel like BadCode lore a cold viewer doesn't know; it still reads as a funny robotic aside, but the plain "From the future" line is the first thing Jack may cut, leaving the shape as a straight parody with a silent card. (2) "In 2054, the fridges survive" implies a bad future with no evidence on screen; it is fiction in the narrator's voice, not a claimed statistic, but Jack should confirm he is happy with it. (3) Tonal drop from soft to flat voice needs the voice direction to carry it (a second AI Studio profile or a sharp tone change); if the voice can't shift, use the 3 s interruption as SCREEN text only. (4) At 17 s it sits at the upper end; cut line 2 to land at about 14 s. (5) The £39,039 figure is the only checked fact; "April 2025" is from the brief and matches the slate note, but should be re-read against the ONS release before the card is cut. No other statistics appear.

**Protected lines.** None protected. The slate's three beats all survive in altered form: the "only earns" line (now "a hundred thousand pounds", spoken out for the ear), the "£3 a month ... second wine fridge" ask (kept almost verbatim, name swapped), and the median-pay card (kept as the turn, wording extended with "a year" and "April").
