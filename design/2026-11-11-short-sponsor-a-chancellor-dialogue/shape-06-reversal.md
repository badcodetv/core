# Shape 06: Reversal (the turn arrives before the narrator expects it)

The viewer gets the joke in the first three seconds (charity advert, sad piano, a man on a salary no charity would touch), so the short does not hold the median-pay card back as a closing reveal. It hits at about 4s, the piano dies, and the narrator then carries on with the appeal in the same soft voice as if nothing happened. The sting moves from the card to the narrator's refusal to notice it. The name is invented (Crispin) and the only statistic is the checked one (£39,039, ONS April 2025).

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [slow, soft, sad piano, black and white, a man alone in a vast kitchen] This is Crispin. Crispin earns a hundred thousand pounds a year. | 4.5 |
| SCREEN | [piano cuts dead, FREEZE on Crispin's face] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 a year (ONS, April 2025) | 2.0 (silence) |
| NARRATOR | [same voice, piano creeps back, entirely unbothered] For just three pounds a month, you could help Crispin afford a second wine fridge. | 4.5 |
| NARRATOR | [tiny beat] Crispin says thank you. | 1.5 |
| SCREEN | [last frame, charity-style] SPONSOR A CHANCELLOR | 1.0 |
| **Total** | | **13.5 (voice 10.5)** |

- **Why it works.** The first line is already the whole premise: everyone knows this advert and everyone knows what a hundred thousand sounds like, so the viewer is ahead of the narrator by second three. Cutting to the median card there rewards that instinct instead of making them wait for permission. The piano dying is the hard edge. Then the narrator resuming the appeal as though nothing happened is the actual joke: the card is the fact, the carrying-on is the system. No stated moral, and the viewer's own £39,039 sits next to the ask. Alternate buttons for Jack to pick between: (a) "Crispin says thank you." (cleanest, charity-advert cadence); (b) "Crispin says thank you. He has already forgotten your name." (adds the contempt-for-distance note); (c) "Please give. Crispin is counting on you, mostly for the fridge." (more explicit, less good); (d) no spoken button, only the SPONSOR A CHANCELLOR card with the piano resolving (the most silent and the most extreme version, voice drops to 9s).
- **Cuts from current.** "only" in "Jeremy only earns" (the card now does the "only" work, and the order does the irony); "a month" stays but "you could help him" shortens to the advert's own phrasing; the card moves from the end to the middle; the real name Jeremy goes.
- **Risk.** Putting the card at 4s may deflate the second half: if the viewer has already got it, "second wine fridge" has to be funny by itself (it is the absurd detail, but it is carrying more weight). Resuming the appeal could read as lazy if the voice does not stay perfectly warm, so the performance must not wink. "Crispin" could still feel like a posh-name stereotype aimed at a person rather than at the office; keep it a first name only, no surname, no party colours, no resemblance to any real chancellor. [CHECK] that no serving or recent chancellor is called Crispin, and that the ONS figure is quoted exactly as "median full-time gross annual pay, April 2025".
- **Protected lines.** None. The slate lines survive in spirit: "earns £100,000 a year" and "second wine fridge" are intact; "Jeremy" is replaced by the invented name; "For just £3 a month" is spoken as "three pounds a month".
