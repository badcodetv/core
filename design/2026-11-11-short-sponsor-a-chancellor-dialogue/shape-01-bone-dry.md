# Shape 01: Bone-dry minimum

Fewest words that still carry every beat: format, hardship, turn, button. I let the charity-advert grammar (sad piano, slow-motion, soft voice, "£3 a month") do the recognising so the words do not have to, cut every adjective, and let the silence after the median card be the longest thing in the short. The invented name is "Hugo" (a posh, unmistakably not-a-real-chancellor name; check it still points at nobody before it is voiced).

## Dialogue 1: The appeal (the whole short)

| Speaker | Line | Est. s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NARRATOR | [sad piano, slow-motion, soft] This is Hugo. Hugo earns £100,000. | 3.5 |
| NARRATOR | A year. [beat] He needs a second wine fridge. £3 a month. | 4.0 |
| SCREEN | [freeze frame, piano stops dead, 2 s of silence] Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 (ONS, 2025) | 0 |
| NARRATOR | [softest, dry] Please give generously. | 1.5 |
| **Total** | 3 spoken lines, 14 words after the name | **9.0 voice (about 13 s runtime with the beat and the 2 s silence)** |

Button alternatives for Jack (swap for the last line, each about 1.5 s): "Hugo thanks you." / "Hugo is counting on you." / "Hugo, for now, is coping." / no line at all: cut to black on the card.

- **Why it works.** The piano and the "£3 a month" are recognised inside two seconds, so no words are spent explaining the format. "Hugo earns £100,000" then "A year" splits the figure into a flat statement and a second, tiny, aggrieved correction: the narrator sounds like she is reading hardship, and the number refuses to be hardship. The wine fridge is the only specific, so it carries all the absurdity. The turn is the card alone, with the audio pulled out from under it: the viewer does the comparison themselves, no moral stated, and the "please give generously" button then asks the working-class viewer to keep playing along, which stings because the card is still on screen. Aim stays on the decision-maker's comfort, never on working people.
- **Cuts from current.** "only" (the idea now lives in the tone, not the word); "you could help him afford" (replaced by the bare ask); "a second wine fridge" becomes "a second wine fridge" kept but with its set-up shortened; the sentence "For just" goes. Nothing is added except the button and a name swap (Jeremy to Hugo).
- **Risk.** (1) Too short: about 9 s of voice is under the 10 s target, so it relies on the piano, silence and the card to reach 10-20 s of runtime; if Jack wants strictly 10 s of voice, restore "only" and "you could help" (adds about 2 s). (2) Losing "only" removes the self-pity that is the actual joke in the quoted line ("£100k doesn't go far"), so the delivery must supply it (a small catch before "A year"). (3) The dry button may read as plain sarcasm at the viewer unless the voice is warm and sincere. (4) "Hugo" must be checked against any real chancellor or minister before it is voiced. (5) Only the £39,039 figure is verified (ONS, April 2025 median full-time pay); the £100,000 and £3 are the joke's own numbers, not claims, and nothing else statistical is asserted. No other [CHECK] items.
- **Protected lines.** None protected. The slate's SCREEN card ("Median UK full-time pay: £39,039 (ONS, 2025)") survives word for word, because the figure is the one verified fact; "Jeremy" is changed to "Hugo" on purpose (real-person risk).
