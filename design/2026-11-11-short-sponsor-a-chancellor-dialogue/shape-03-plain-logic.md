# Shape 03: The logic said plainly

The narrator never emotes and never jokes. He reasons the numbers out loud like an auditor reading a charity's accounts, in the soft sad-advert voice, one step at a time: here is the man, here is his income, here is what we are asking you to cover, here is the other number. Every line is arithmetic or a plain statement of fact, so the absurdity does the work and the point is never stated. The only checked figure is £39,039 (ONS, April 2025, median full-time pay); the £100,000 is the slate's own figure; anything else derived is marked [CHECK]. Invented name throughout: Sir Rupert Pemberton-Vale [name check: confirm no real chancellor or minister shares it].

Tighter 15s cut (drop the "we have checked" line and the thirty-six-pounds line): lines 1, 3, SCREEN, "Sir Rupert has two and a half of them", button = about 14s.

**Why it works.** The sad-advert voice makes the format recognisable in two seconds, and then the narrator simply does sums, which is exactly what the format hides: charity adverts never show you the maths of who is giving to whom. "We have checked" is the auditor's deadpan; "thirty-six pounds a year" makes the ask feel tiny and rational before the median lands next to it. "Sir Rupert has two and a half of them" is a plain ratio with no adjective, so it reads as fact rather than a lecture, and it aims at the man's comfort, not at anyone's job or wages. No moral is stated: the narrator just asks politely to be paid.

**Cuts from current.** "Only earns" becomes a flat "earns" (the word "only" is the joke's lie, so the logic version removes it and lets "That is not enough" carry the claim). "Help him afford" becomes "can help him afford" with the fee spelled out as a yearly sum. The median card is kept but moved after the arithmetic, with the ratio line added as the spoken turn.

**Risk.** Too many numbers for a 9:16 phone viewer, who may drop at the sum; the ratio line is arithmetic on a slate figure (£100,000 is the quote's number, not a checked salary for any real office), so keep the ratio only if Jack accepts it as comparison rather than claim [CHECK]. The tone is dry to the point of flat: without a visual gag in the first two seconds (the window, the garden, the coat) it can feel like an audit. "Neither will you" risks sounding like it blames the viewer; swap it if so.

**Protected lines.** None. Swap-in buttons for Jack:
- "Please give generously." (plain, the current favourite)
- "He will not notice. Neither will you." (stings both ways, riskiest)
- "Sir Rupert thanks you. He will not know it was you." (the charity-advert thank-you, turned)
- "Sponsor a chancellor today. Somebody has to." (closest to the BadCode voice)
- Silence: piano restarts over the freeze frame, no line at all.
