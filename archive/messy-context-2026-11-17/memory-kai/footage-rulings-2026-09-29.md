Four rulings Kai gave on 2026-09-29 during the Magic Money Tree footage pass. They apply to every
film, not just that one.

1. **"Expired Crown copyright films are usable, 100%."** British government films made before
   1 June 1957 (Ministry of Information, Crown Film Unit, Central Office of Information) are green,
   **picture only**. The soundtracks are separately authored and still in copyright.
2. **"£170 for one clip is too much."** He declined a single Alamy still at £170 even though it sat
   inside the £200 push limit.
3. **Agents may use a browser on paid sites: "Yeah, of course."**
4. **No photograph of a named living person where none is free; show newspaper headlines instead**
   ("surely we'll be able to show screenshots of the newspaper stories about it").

**Why:** he wants real British footage in the films and accepts expired-copyright reasoning as a
class ([[footage-sourcing-lane]]); he is very price sensitive and the budget is for a whole film;
vendor sites block scripts so a browser is the only way to read a price or a clause.

**How to apply:** treat pre-1957 Crown films as green without re-asking, always strip audio, take
files from archive.org or Commons and never from the BFI or IWM. Price any spend against "is this
one item worth most of the film's budget", not against the £200 ceiling; a spend is still a stop
gate. In a browser: own tab, no log-in, no trial, no purchase, leave other sessions' tabs alone
([[concurrent-browser-sessions]]). For headline screenshots: crop out the paper's photograph, credit
the paper and date on screen, and prefer headlines that state a court finding or the person's own
words; attribute anything that is the paper's own allegation. See [[mmt-documentary-cut]].
