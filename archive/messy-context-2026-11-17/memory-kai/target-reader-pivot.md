2026-08-15, after the 24-agent anti-AI research: Kai ruled the target audience is **not** anti-AI
advocates but "the majority of people that would help affect normal societal change" — specifically
a working-class UK reader drifting right of centre because the economy is failing them. A Fable
adversarial review followed (4 more research agents on this reader), producing:

- `docs/marketing/the-reader.md` — the persona + ten evidence-backed writing rules (hero-not-pupil;
  never "duped"; proportionality-not-denial on immigration; name the decision not the technology;
  never raise automation fear without the beneficiary on-panel; perspective-getting; sarcasm
  diagnoses / care re-aims; music-video-first).
- `docs/misc/2026-08-15-target-reader-research.md` — sourced evidence annex (Ipsos/MAC/OBR/Ada
  Lovelace/Kalla-Broockman/Wu 2022 etc.), including §7's list of what nobody has measured.
- `docs/using-ai.md` re-scoped: it is **armour, not the campaign** — disclosure split into a
  one-line in-voice layer on artifacts + a full account page; register rule reframed as
  defendant-with-composure, not contrition.

Load-bearing findings to not re-litigate: AI is absent from the target reader's issue list (their
machine fear is the welfare algorithm, 44%→59%); disclosure is a 94% supermajority demand (armour
stays); anger raised about automation gets re-targeted at partisan-licensed outgroups unless the
beneficiary is named in the same piece (Wu 2022); "immigration costs you nothing" is not
evidence-supportable (MAC: −5% at 5th wage percentile over 24 yrs, +4.4% at 90th) — proportionality,
never denial. The "blame-the-AI = blame-the-immigrant" symmetry claim is untested and stays out of
copy; the evidenced form is "automation anxiety is feedstock for nativism."
