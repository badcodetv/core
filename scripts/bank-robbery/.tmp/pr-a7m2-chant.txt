Close among a crowd behind a steel barrier at dusk, rain falling. At the front a woman in her thirties in a wet black raincoat, dark curly hair stuck to her forehead, holds a white megaphone up to her face with both hands, its mouthpiece covering her mouth and chin, the round bell pointing across the barrier straight at the camera. Beside her, each one a different person: a tall Black man in his twenties in a grey hoodie under a denim jacket, short beard; a grey-haired woman in her sixties in a purple anorak and round glasses, pointing; a stocky South Asian man in his forties in a navy work fleece, thick moustache, both hands on the barrier; a skinny teenage girl in a yellow cagoule with a red fringe; a heavy man in his fifties with a ginger beard and a tweed flat cap. Everyone further back is hooded, under umbrellas or turned away, faces lost in rain and shadow. Camera low on the other side of the barrier, the out-of-focus yellow shoulder of a police jacket in the lower left foreground. Behind them, the columned front of an old bank. Light: one orange sodium street lamp above and behind them, the white glare of a police floodlight from the right on their faces. Rain in the light. No flags, no coloured smoke, no readable text.
