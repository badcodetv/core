## 4. What we should be doing that we're not

The chief of staff keeps this list honest. Each line is a gap, not a task; when one becomes work,
it moves into §3.

- **Publishing anything at all.** Everything above exists to close this one.
- **Owning an audience.** No mailing list yet, so every follower belongs to a platform.
- **Talking back.** Nobody is replying to anyone, because nothing is out. When it is, see §7.
- **A face and a voice that come back.** The research says channels that break out have one
  recurring character. The balaclava host is that, and doesn't exist yet.
- **Showing the fix, not just the fault.** Of 30 comic shorts, one (`20-the-good-half`) shows what
  better looks like. The reader needs hope as well as anger.
- **Somewhere to send people.** The website isn't live and doesn't carry the logo yet.
- **Jack's eyes on the plan and the logo.** Both are Kai's picks so far.

## 6. Who it's for, what we say, how we sound

**The reader** (ruled 15 August 2026, [`../the-reader.md`](../the-reader.md)): a working-class UK
adult drifting right because the economy failed them. In one line they'd recognise: *you were right
that it is rigged; you were told the wrong story about who rigged it.* Every piece passes the reader's
ten rules. The four that bite hardest: never "you've been duped"; name the decision-maker, never the
technology; never raise automation fear without naming who benefits; never repeat the lie to refute it.
**Second audience:** drum & bass listeners, reached through the music.

**What we say:** BadCode is a superintelligence from a future that already went wrong. It tells you,
in stories and drum & bass, which choices caused it, and that they were choices. Under everything:
**"we can't afford it" is a decision, and someone benefits from it.**

**How we sound:** the narrator from the future: certain, dark, funny, on the reader's side
([`../../voice.md`](../../voice.md)). On AI: honest when asked, never the headline
([`../../using-ai.md`](../../using-ai.md)). Real people and real footage only inside Kai's rulings.
When something goes wrong (a claim, a takedown, a pile-on): stop posting that piece, tell both of
you, don't dispute past the first step without both of you saying so.

## 7. Talking back (the human part)

Ruled by Kai on 15 October: marketing is not a machine running in the background. People reply, and
somebody has to be there when they do. The chief of staff can prepare, sort and draft; **only Kai and
Jack talk to people.**

- **Just after a post goes out:** be there, and answer the first comments.
- **On a day something's gone out:** one pass through new comments on every channel. Answer what
  deserves it; ignore the trolls; never argue with a person.
- **Now and then:** answer one comment as a video, with the narrator replying to a real person.
  It's a free episode.
- **When you ask:** the chief of staff hands over a comment digest (the best comments, the questions
  asked more than once, anything that needs a ruling).
- **Every reply to the mailing list** gets a human answer.

**How we answer:** in voice, on the account, aimed at the money, never at the person. **As
ourselves, signed** (`— Kai` / `— Jack`), when someone asks who made it or whether it's AI. Never
"you've been duped", never mocking a voter. When it turns nasty: stop replying on that thread and
tell the other person. The chief of staff drafts replies; it never sends one.
