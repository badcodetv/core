## 2. The one goal for 90 days

> **Publish something every week for 12 weeks in a row, and start a mailing list.**

Not followers, not views, not money. A habit. Every later goal (the 500-subscriber YouTube gate,
the member page, the album) sits on top of this one, and none of them can happen without it.

Measures, in this order (from [`../plan.md`](../plan.md) §9):

1. **Weeks in a row with something published** — target 12 of 12.
2. **Mailing-list sign-ups** — the only audience no platform can take away. A guess at a target: 100.
3. **Followers on the two main channels** — a guess at a target: 500 on YouTube (the first money gate,
   see [`../profit.md`](../profit.md)).
4. **Shares and sends** — is the voice landing?
5. **Views** — context only.

The targets in 2 and 3 are guesses. The research says a new channel starts small (median first
Short about 1,360 views in 30 days, see [`../money-for-something-growth.md`](../money-for-something-growth.md)).
After four weeks the analyst replaces the guesses with real numbers.

## 3. Who it is for

**The reader** (ruled 15 August 2026, [`../the-reader.md`](../the-reader.md)): a working-class UK
adult drifting right because the economy failed them. Not the converted left. Not anti-AI activists.

In one line they would recognise: *you were right that it is rigged; you were told the wrong story
about who rigged it.*

Every piece passes the reader's ten rules before it goes out. The four that bite hardest in a
weekly show: never "you've been duped"; name the decision-maker, never the technology; never raise
automation fear without naming who benefits; never repeat the lie to refute it.

**Second audience:** drum & bass listeners, reached through the music, not the politics.

## 4. What we say (positioning)

> BadCode is a superintelligence from a future that already went wrong. It has seen how this ends.
> It tells you, in stories and drum & bass, which choices caused it — and that they were choices.

The money message under everything: **"we can't afford it" is a decision, and someone benefits from it.**

## 9a. The human part — talking back

Ruled by Kai on 15 October: marketing is not a machine running in the background. People reply,
and somebody has to be in the room when they do. The chief of staff can prepare, sort and draft;
**only Kai and Jack can talk to people.**

| When | What | Who | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First hour after every post** | Be there. Answer the first comments; early replies tell the platform the post is alive | whoever posted | 15 min |
| **Each evening on a posting day** | One pass through new comments on every channel. Answer what deserves it; ignore the trolls; never argue with a person | split by channel (owed, see below) | 20 min |
| **Thursday** | **One comment answered as a video:** the narrator replies to a real comment. It's a free episode, and it shows a person was heard | Kai writes, Jack cuts | 30 min |
| **Thursday** | Two messages to accounts that already have the audience (see [`../money-for-something-reach.md`](../money-for-something-reach.md) §5). AI is declared in the first line | Kai | 15 min |
| **Friday** | The chief of staff hands over the **comment digest**: the five best comments, the questions asked more than once, anything that needs a ruling. That feeds next week's episode | chief of staff → both | 10 min to read |
| **Each email** | Reply to every reply to "Transmissions" (the mailing list). Early on there are few, and each one is a person who chose to hear from us | Kai or Jack | as needed |

**How we answer:**

- **In voice, on the account.** The narrator's certainty and dark humour, aimed at the money and never at
  the person. ([`../../voice.md`](../../voice.md))
- **As ourselves when it matters.** If someone asks who made it, how, or whether it's AI, a human
  answers honestly and signs it (`— Kai` / `— Jack`), using [`../../using-ai.md`](../../using-ai.md).
- **Never** "you've been duped", never mocking a voter, never repeating a false claim to rebut it
  (the reader's rules, [`../the-reader.md`](../the-reader.md)).
- **When it turns nasty:** stop replying on that thread and tell the other person. Don't go past
  one reply without both of you agreeing.

The chief of staff can draft replies for you to change and send. It never sends one.

## 10. Rules the chief of staff holds you to

1. **Something finished goes out before anything new is researched.** A new idea is parked in one line.
2. **No more than three decisions at a time.** Ranked by deadline.
3. **A missed week is logged, not hidden.** The streak is the goal.
4. **Cut what does not work at week 8.** Any format with no sign-ups or shares by then is stopped.
5. **Nothing is published without a human yes**, until both of you change this rule in `log.md`.
