## 1. Facebook as a main channel

**Why:** it reaches 93% of UK online adults and is the biggest social platform for news, 39% (Ofcom, read). It is
where [the reader](the-reader.md) is. By social class: not found, so not claimed.

**How Facebook works now:**

- Every video on Facebook is a Reel, any length (since June 2025, read).
- About a third of what people see in the feed is from accounts they do not follow (Meta's own US report, read).
  That is how a page with no followers gets seen at all. No UK figure exists.
- **Posts with a link barely travel:** about 2% of feed views are on posts with a link, and Meta has tested a cap
  of two link posts a month for pages (read). So the video goes up directly, and **the link goes in the first comment**.
- Meta's own advice (primary): no links in the caption, few capitals, five hashtags or fewer, no other app's
  watermark, nothing that looks re-used. Very short videos "of little value" are discouraged.
- Most people watch without sound in public (a US survey, read). The shorts carry every word on screen.
- **A Page, not a profile**, because two people need to run it.

**To have ready:** the Page (name, picture from `docs/brand/logo/`, one line of bio in his voice), the eight shorts,
the full episode as its own upload, the ten picture posts.

**Not known:** whether sharing a video into local Facebook groups helps or is punished. Each group sets its own
rules. Ask the group's admin first, one group at a time.

## 2. An email list from day one

**Why:** a post reaches about 2 to 4% of the people who follow an account (read). An email reaches the inbox. Four
platform rule changes in this research alone rewrote creators' terms in 2026.

- **Tool:** Kit (free to 10,000) or Substack (free, takes 10% of paid). Both from comparison sites; check the
  price page on the day.
- **Name:** "Transmissions" (already in `plan.md` §3.8).
- **What it sends:** the episode, the morning it goes out, with one line from him. Nothing else.
- **Where the sign-up link can go:** YouTube channel page; Facebook first comment; TikTok bio (sources disagree on
  whether a new account gets a bio link; unverified); the site.
- **Paperwork:** UK data rules apply to any list. The £52 yearly fee has an exemption for marketing-only lists; take
  the regulator's online check (primary for the exemption list).
- **How many viewers sign up:** nobody has published a figure.

## 3. The host as a reply

An eight-second clip of him answering a politician's own post.

- 🔴 **Not on X, unless we pay.** X ranks replies by subscription tier and now lifts replies from accounts that
  follow each other. A free reply under a minister's post is buried by design (read).
- **Do it on Facebook, Bluesky and Threads.** Threads and Instagram put political posts back into recommendations
  in January 2025 (read).
- **Different words every time.** Repeating the same reply trips spam filters (unverified).
- **On TikTok** a video reply can only answer a comment on our own video.
- **No evidence either way** that replying under big accounts grows a small one. Try it for a month and count.
- **Needs a ruling:** every one of these names a living person. Kai's call.

**To have ready:** a folder of his shortest lines as eight-second files. The October episode already has: "In other
news." · "Moving on." · "Onwards." · "Just fucking with you." · "No spoilers."

## 4. One filmed stunt on Budget day (28 October)

**The legal version:** a person on the pavement near Parliament holding a board that says "Nurses: £0". Film it.
The film is the thing; the board is the prop.

- Holding a placard or wearing a costume is **not** banned in the controlled areas round Parliament (the Act
  itself, primary).
- **No megaphone, no speaker** there. Record any sound somewhere else.
- **Stand on the pavement, not the grass** of Parliament Square (the grass has needed permission; older source).
- **Whitehall is outside both controlled areas.**
- Police can still put conditions on a one-person protest.
- **Precedent:** Steve Bray got his sign into the back of about a hundred live television interviews. When the BBC
  built a platform, he used a longer pole (read).
- 🔴 **Not a projection** (planning permission; four Led By Donkeys arrests in September 2025), **not posters on
  walls** (flyposting, up to £2,500), **not chalk on the road** (an offence to mark the highway).
- An advert van is £625 to £995 a day. Out of budget.
- The Home Office's guidance on Parliament Square was saved during research but could not be read here; **a person
  should read it before anyone stands there.**

## 5. Sending it to people who already have the audience

- **Gary's Economics:** 1.6 million subscribers and no weekly show since July 2026. The audience is there and
  unserved. That is a reason to make the show, not a person to pester.
- **Nurses' unions:** the RCN is on Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Bluesky, TikTok and YouTube, and left X in 2024.
  Unison runs many Facebook pages by sector (read).
- 🔴 **Say it is made with AI in the first line of every message, and sign it with a name.** Campaign groups have
  had to back away in public from AI pictures on partners' pages (read), and unions are campaigning against
  generative AI. An honest first line loses fewer friends than a discovered one.
- **Offer the file itself**, not a link, so they can upload it directly.
- **Two messages a week, written one at a time.** No list blast.
- **Not found:** follower counts, anyone's rule on sharing outside work, or a documented case of a small maker
  being picked up this way.
