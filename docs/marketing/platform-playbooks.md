## 1. Cross-platform facts that shape everything (✅ Verified)

- **Posting more buys lottery tickets, not linear growth.** Buffer's 11.4M-post TikTok study
  (Oct 2025): vs once-weekly, 2–5 posts/week → ~17% more mean views/post, 6–10 → ~29%,
  11+ → ~34% — but **median views stay flat**; the lift comes entirely from more chances at a
  viral outlier. Consistent volume matters because of the tail, not the average.
  [buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok]
- **AI-made content is not banned anywhere we operate — but spam, impersonation, and
  non-disclosure are the enforcement targets.** Spotify: "all music is treated equally,
  regardless of the tools used to make it." YouTube: an AI disclosure label "alone does not
  change recommendation distribution or monetization." Lean into being openly AI-made (it's
  our identity anyway); never mass-produce or hide it.
- **React/commentary content is legally safest when our commentary dominates.** Fair use (US
  §107) favors substantive criticism/analysis; "group viewing session" reaction content
  likely fails (*Hosseinzadeh v. Klein*). Credits and "no infringement intended" disclaimers
  provide zero protection. Automated systems can't assess fair use — lawful uses get claimed
  anyway.

## 2. TikTok — the cold-start engine

**✅ Verified:**
- FYP reach is largely **follower-independent** — new accounts can land outlier reach from
  zero. This is the single strongest cold-start surface for us, and the frequency findings
  hold across all account sizes.
- Cadence: **2–5 posts/week is the most efficient band** (best gain per additional post);
  more is better only if quality holds.

**◻ Provisional (trend-scout to verify):**
- Format: 9:16, subtitles always, hook in the first 1–2 seconds (cold-open on the strongest
  image/line — never a title card), 15–45s sweet spot for satellites.
- Cold start: duets/stitches on videos in the theme's conversation are platform-sanctioned
  react mechanics and expose us to the source video's audience — this is the funnel strategy's
  native home. Reply to comments with follow-up videos. Niche hashtags (2–4) still help
  classification; broad ones don't.
- Our DnB is an asset: original sounds that others can use are a TikTok growth surface.

## 3. YouTube (+ Shorts) — the core library

**✅ Verified — copyright reality for react/funnel content:**
- **Content ID matches clips anecdotally under 10 seconds**; practitioners keep third-party
  clips under ~5–10s. ~90%+ of claims are auto-monetized by the rightsholder (they take the
  ad revenue, video stays up); <1% of claims are disputed even though 60–70% of disputes
  resolve for the uploader (2024–2025 YouTube transparency data).
- **Two parallel systems — don't confuse them.** Content ID claim = revenue redirect/block,
  no strike, disputable in Studio. DMCA takedown = legal notice, copyright **strike**;
  3 strikes in a rolling 90 days = channel subject to termination. Escalating a Content ID
  dispute can provoke a scheduled DMCA takedown (7-day pressure window). Rule: dispute
  calmly when clearly fair use; never escalate to appeal/counter-notification without human
  (Kai/Jack) sign-off — that's where channel-level risk lives.
- **AI labeling (May 2026):** YouTube auto-applies an AI label when it detects significant
  photorealistic AI content, regardless of self-disclosure; labels are **permanent** for
  content made with YouTube's own tools (Veo, Dream Screen) or carrying C2PA
  fully-generative metadata. The label itself carries no recommendation/monetization
  penalty — but the separate "inauthentic content" policy (Jul 2025) can demote deceptive or
  mass-produced AI content. Always self-disclose; it's on-brand. **Open question we must
  test:** whether Flow/Veo-pipeline video used outside YouTube's tools triggers the
  permanent label class (our stylized comic art may not read as "photorealistic" at all).

**Practical consequences for us (✅-derived):**
- React/funnel videos on YouTube: our narration/analysis must dominate; third-party clips
  short and fragmented; expect monetization claims as a cost of doing business (we're not
  monetizing early anyway — the funnel click is the payoff); score with our own tracks.
- Prefer TikTok/X native mechanics (duets, quote-posts) for reaction; YouTube gets the
  heavily-transformed versions.

**◻ Provisional:**
- Long-form (narrated comics, music videos): title/describe for search ("evergreen search"
  strategy — §3.10 of the plan); custom thumbnails in the brand register; end screens to the
  next core.
- Shorts: same 9:16 cuts as TikTok; Shorts and long-form audiences barely overlap — treat
  Shorts as discovery, long-form as home. Cadence ◻: 2–4 Shorts/week + long-form as released.

## 4. Spotify — distribution with disclosure

**✅ Verified:**
- **No AI ban**; enforcement targets spam. The autumn-2025 spam filter tags and
  **de-recommends** uploaders doing: mass uploads, duplicates with altered metadata, SEO
  manipulation, ~30-second royalty-farming tracks. 75M+ spammy tracks removed in the year to
  Sept 2025. Rules for us: release deliberately (singles, not Suno dumps), no filler, no
  gaming titles, full-length tracks only.
- **DDEX AI credits are live** (beta Apr 2026, tens of thousands of daily submissions):
  declare where/how AI was used (vocals, instrumentation, post-production) via the
  distributor — DistroKid implemented first; CD Baby, Believe, RouteNote, Soundrop et al.
  support it. **Disclose honestly on every release.**

**◻ Provisional:**
- Distributor choice: DistroKid (first with DDEX AI credits) fits; verify current AI-content
  terms at signup.
- Playlist pitching via Spotify for Artists (submit ≥7 days pre-release), pre-save campaigns,
  Canvas visuals from comic art. Editorial playlisting for a new AI-assisted DnB act is a
  long shot; algorithmic (Release Radar/Discover Weekly) flows from saves + completion rate —
  another reason to avoid the spam bucket. *(Playlist-pitching specifics did not survive
  verification — trend-scout task.)*

## 5. SoundCloud — scene credibility, with a hard constraint

**✅ Verified:**
- **Hosting ≠ distribution.** AI-assisted tracks may be uploaded/hosted (conditionally), but
  SoundCloud for Artists will only approve **distribution or monetization** of AI-assisted
  content made with its official AI partners (Fadr, Soundful, Voice-Swap, Tuney, Tutti,
  AlBeatz, TwoShot, Starmony, ACE Studio, Lemonaide, Hook). **Suno is NOT on the list** — so
  our Suno-made tracks can live on SoundCloud as hosted uploads but cannot currently be
  distributed/monetized through SoundCloud for Artists. Rights burden sits with the uploader
  and SoundCloud verifies before approving.

**Consequence:** SoundCloud = community/scene presence only (hosted tracks, reposts, DnB
crowd); Spotify-and-co distribution goes via a DDEX-supporting distributor (§4). Re-check the
partner list quarterly — it moves.

**◻ Provisional:** repost-exchange within the DnB community, tagging (drum & bass / neurofunk /
174), free-download gates for remix stems as a collab hook.

## 10. UK/EU legal note (open question — resolve before scaling react content)

All verified fair-use findings are **US doctrine**. The UK has the narrower **fair dealing**
regime (specific purposes: criticism/review, quotation, news reporting, parody/pastiche —
with acknowledgment requirements), and the EU AI Act's transparency provisions add AI-content
disclosure duties. As a UK-flavoured brand this needs a proper look before react content
scales past stage-1 human approval. Until then: stage-1 gating + rules §8 of the plan
(commentary dominates, minimal clips, platform-native mechanics) keep us conservative.
