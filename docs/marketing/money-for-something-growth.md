## What the research changes

- **A new channel starts small and Shorts do not snowball.** Median first Short: about 1,360 views in 30
  days; first long video: about 180. The best of the first ten Shorts: about 8,800. More cuts means more
  chances (vidIQ, July 2026, read).
- **The closest thing to us is the Crewkerne Gazette**: two anonymous people, AI, UK, right-leaning, a claimed
  150 million views from fast song parodies about the week's scandal, a new video "in 40 minutes". Only about
  97,000 followers from all of that. No left-leaning equivalent was found (read).
- **First money gate on YouTube: 500 subscribers**, 3 uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 watch hours or 3 million
  Shorts views. It opens memberships and Super Thanks, not adverts (YouTube help page, primary).
- **YouTube's 2025 "inauthentic content" rule does not ban this.** Its page lists "using AI to visualise a
  character and story you invented" as fine. It bans templated, interchangeable videos, and AI personas posing
  as human experts on politics. The host must stay obviously a character (primary).
- **Bandcamp banned AI-made music on 13 January 2026**, and TuneCore blocks Suno's current models. DistroKid
  is the working route. This matters for the album, not for this show (read).
- **Patreon takes 10%** on new pages, and from 1 November 2026 Apple takes up to 30% on sign-ups made in the
  iPhone app. Ko-fi takes 0% on tips (read).
- **About 1 in 100 followers pays, at best.** No audited figure exists. Novara lives on about 15,000 small
  donors (read).
- **Facebook reaches 93% of UK online adults and is the biggest social platform for news (39%).** By social
  class: not found.
- **One file fits everywhere at 61 to 180 seconds.** TikTok's payment scheme needs videos over a minute
  (third-party guides only). Upload a clean file to each platform; watermarked reposts are ranked lower.

## Other ways (second pass, 12 October)

Jack: "find other ways". A second research run on routes the first one skipped. Same marking: **(read)** is
secondary reporting; **(primary)** means the page itself was read; **(unverified)** means do not rely on it.

### Money that is not from followers

- **Cameo, as the host.** Anyone can join now, no follower minimum, and a puppet character has reportedly taken
  over $600,000 there, so made-up characters are allowed. "The host roasts your boss" at a set price. Cameo keeps
  25 to 30%. Its rule on AI-made characters was not found (read).
- **AI film competitions with cash.** Runway's festival pays $50,000 for first place, films of 3 to 15 minutes;
  2026 is closed, 2027 not announced (primary). Chroma Awards Season 2 is listed as free to enter, closing
  31 December 2026, $175,000 in prizes: all three unverified. A 3-minute episode already fits.
- **BFI money is open to AI work.** Its guidance (28 January 2026): "We do not prohibit the use of AI... in the
  projects we support"; you must declare it. The short film fund is £5,000 to £25,000, fiction only, next round
  spring 2027 (primary for the AI line, read for the fund).
- **Arts Council England, Developing Your Creative Practice:** £2,000 to £12,000 to one person, no match
  funding. Next round not announced. Its position on AI-made work was not found (read).
- **Lipman-Miliband Trust:** up to £3,000 for socialist education, has paid for films and podcasts, takes
  unincorporated groups. Closed for review until 2027 (primary).
- **Joseph Rowntree Reform Trust:** up to £10,000 at any time, and "money in politics" is on its list. It says
  it is unlikely to fund films and wants a host organisation, so ask before applying (primary).
- **Not worth it yet:** crowdfunding (the big raises had an audience first, and declaring AI measurably hurts
  on Kickstarter); paid work for unions (their own campaigns are against generative AI); tool-company
  ambassador schemes (the channel becomes an advert for the tool).

### Ways to be seen that are not the four video apps

- **A page on our own site: "How many nurses is that?"** Type in a number from the news, get it in nurses' pay
  rises. One person's "My Little Crony" (2020) was written up by three outlets and cited in Parliament; no
  traffic figure exists for it (read). We already have the site and the stat cards.
- **Pitch that page, not the video, to the press.** Left Foot Forward takes pitches at
  editor@leftfootforward.org, subject starting "Pitch:", for a 500 to 750 word written piece (read). The Poke
  and The London Economic: no stated way in.
- **The show as a podcast.** A quarter of UK adults listen weekly. The audio already exists. Spotify pays only
  after 2,000 hours and 1,000 listeners in 30 days, so this is for reach (primary for the threshold).
- **A WhatsApp-sized file.** 90% of UK online adults use WhatsApp. A small vertical file with the joke in the
  first seconds, made to be forwarded. A WhatsApp Channel on its own will not be found by anyone (read).
- **A four-page newspaper.** 50 copies delivered for £88, 100 for £141 (Newspaper Club, primary). The episode's
  numbers in print, left in cafes and union branches. It also gives a crowdfunder or a member something to hold.
- **A screening in a community hall.** No licence needed if it is not for profit, 500 people or fewer, between
  08:00 and 23:00, with written consent. A pub uses its own licence (read).
- **Reddit:** reaches about 60% of UK online adults, but the UK politics boards' rules on self-promotion and AI
  could not be read. Check each board's rules before posting.
