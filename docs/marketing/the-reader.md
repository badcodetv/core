## The portrait

Working class, UK, in a town that has seen better times — **decline, not poverty, is the wound**.
The economy has worked against them since 2008 and they know it. They are drifting right of centre
— toward the one party offering them an enemy — not because they read a manifesto but because
their status keeps falling and somebody finally sounded angry on their behalf. Top issues:
immigration and the economy — *in that order, and they arrived at the first via the second*. They
trust almost nobody: not politicians, not the news (35% and falling). They are on Facebook,
YouTube and TikTok, not on web-comic sites.

**On AI they are not who r/antiai is.** AI isn't on their issues list at all. Their machine fear
is being **judged and defrauded** — the welfare algorithm, the loan decision, the scam call, the
deepfake — not the provenance of an album cover. They can't tell AI art from drawn art and don't
rank the question. They do, near-universally, hate being *deceived* about it — which is why the
disclosure practice stays.

**What they already believe that we also believe:** the game is rigged; the gains go up; the
people responsible never pay. They have even accepted public ownership — the party they're
drifting toward pledged 50% public ownership of utilities. **The contest is over which structure
gets blamed, not whether structure matters.**

## The theory of the reader (load-bearing)

Their **diagnosis is right**: something real is being taken from them — wages, housing, standing,
the future their parents had. What's curated is the **culprit list**. Our work contests the list,
never the diagnosis. And it never, ever tells them they've been fooled — "you've been duped" is
"you're stupid" with a citation, it supplies the exact guilt that *drives* scapegoating, and the
one book written from inside this audience was written to correct precisely that frame.

## The rules (each evidenced in the research file)

1. **Hero, not pupil.** The reader appears in our stories as the protagonist wronged by the
   system (Bob), never as the audience being taught. Test every story against this.
2. **Never "manipulated," never "duped," never a lecture.** Show the trick being run *on someone
   else* — inoculation works when it demonstrates the technique on a third party and dies when it
   accuses the viewer.
3. **Attack the story about the country, not their wage maths.** The drift is sociotropic and
   status-driven. Fact-checking their beliefs moves nothing; a better story about the same wound
   can.
4. **Proportionality, never denial, on immigration.** The costs at the bottom are real and small
   (−5% at the 5th wage percentile over 24 years; housing where nothing gets built) and dwarfed by
   austerity, sterling and the planning system — one currency move outweighed twelve years of free
   movement. Denial gaslights; proportionality re-aims.
5. **Name the decision, not the technology. Injustice, not inevitability.** "The machines are
   coming" produces fatalism and a slow-it-down reflex; "a man chose this, and it's unjust"
   produces political demand. Every automation beat names a chooser and a beneficiary.
6. **Never raise the temperature without aiming the flame.** Raising automation anxiety *unaimed*
   feeds nativism — anger is conserved and re-targeted at whoever the reader's politics already
   licenses. So the beneficiary is **on-panel** in the same piece, every time: character carries
   the feeling (episodic), the mechanism carries the blame (thematic). Both layers, always.
7. **Perspective-getting.** Let the reader *receive* a first-person account in the character's own
   words. Don't prompt them to imagine being someone else, and don't have the narrator testify at
   them.
8. **Write for the conversation afterwards.** Broadcast story changes what feels sayable in the
   room, not private belief. The deliverable is the argument in the pub, the share, the comment —
   design the piece to be quotable *into* that argument.
9. **Sarcasm diagnoses; care re-aims.** The snark dial is for "the system is rigged" (it breeds
   cynicism, which this reader already has in surplus). The blame-reattribution beat — "here is
   who" — lands in the care register, played straight. This is voice.md's two-step with an
   evidence base.
10. **Never repeat the lie to refute it.** Don't restate the anti-immigrant claim to knock it
    down; familiarity strengthens it. And lean on our structural advantage: the effective
    messenger is the one whose interests cut against its message — **a machine warning you about
    the machine owners is the unlikely messenger, perfected.**

## Where to meet them

Music first (a 4-minute music video is the best-evidenced format in the entire persuasion
literature, and it works *hardest* on high-authoritarian viewers); serialised story second
(the one national-scale precedent for shifting low-income audiences is a serialised drama);
Facebook, YouTube and TikTok as channels — Facebook is this reader's biggest social news source
and is currently our lowest-priority mirror. The scroll-comic site is the terminus, not the door.

## Honest limits

- Expected effect sizes are small (d ≈ 0.04–0.08 durable, at best). The bet is cumulative
  presence, not conversion events.
- This persona is a **north star, not a fence**: it picks which stories get made and how blame is
  aimed; it does not mean every piece cosplays a demographic. Pandering reads instantly and reads
  as contempt.
- The claim "anti-AI politics and anti-immigrant politics are the same manoeuvre" is **untested
  and stays out of copy** — the evidenced version is "automation anxiety is feedstock for
  nativism, and political entrepreneurs are harvesting it." Never deploy either as a defence of
  our own AI use, and never compare artists to xenophobes.
