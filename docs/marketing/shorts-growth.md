# Shorts growth: what makes them spread, and how they feed the films

Synthesis of ten research reports, 11 November 2026. Every claim carries a label: **(official)** platform help, blog or named staff; **(study)** a large dataset or a peer-reviewed paper; **(case)** a named creator or a press account; **(hearsay)** vendor blogs and unsourced figures. Anything with no source behind it has been left out. Where this doc changes `shorts-factory.md` §4 it says so with **[updates §4]** or **[contradicts §4]**.

## 1. In one paragraph

A short grows a channel by being finished and forwarded, not by being seen. Each platform shows a new short to a small test audience and widens it only if people stay, watch most of it and act on it (sends on Instagram, shares on TikTok, "chose to view" on YouTube). So the first second has to state the contradiction, the short must be no longer than the joke, and the ending has to give people a reason to send it. Shorts are reach, not the subscriber engine. Expect most of them to land near 1,000 views and about one in five to pass 10,000 (study, vidIQ 2026), and expect only a trickle of viewers to cross to the film. The links work in fewer places than §4 says: a pinned comment on a YouTube Short cannot be tapped. What makes the funnel work is the Related Video link, a spoken and on-screen badcode.tv, one link in bio that goes straight to one page, and one free offer in the narrator's voice that is worth an email address. For us the edge is authorship. In a feed full of AI slop, a named series with a specific point, written words and our own music is what reads as made by someone. The Instagram political throttle and UK TikTok moderation are the two reach risks to plan around.

**[updates §4]** First-frame view counting is confirmed for Shorts from 31 March 2025 (official). The extension to all formats from 24 August 2026 appears only in smaller outlets and could not be checked against YouTube Help (hearsay).

## 3. What makes a short spread

**Hook: state the claim in frame one.** (study) Metricool's 5.6M-video study (Sept 2025) puts average TikTok watch time at 3.75 s. Most viewers are gone inside the hook window. The Rules should put "can't afford a bed" on screen, burnt in, at 0 s. Hook shapes in §4 still stand (bold claim, curiosity gap, micro-story open). The specific figures in §4 (70% decide in 3 s, 2.2x views for 70-85% hook retention) are single-vendor hearsay. **[contradicts §4: regrade to hearsay]**

**Length: as long as the idea, no padding.** (study, conflicting) Inflow, 5,400 Shorts: 50-60 s earned about 22x the views of under 10 s. A 2026 faceless-channel set: 28-35 s had the best average percentage viewed (about 78%). vidIQ's 35-billion-view study: 13 s and 60 s did best. Buffer, 1.1M TikToks (Mar 2025): longer had higher median reach, with 60 s+ beating 30-60 s by 43%. YouTube's product lead says there is no ideal length (official). **[contradicts §4]** "30-60 s is THE band" is one correlational study, and 60 s is not a ceiling on TikTok. Keep The Rules at about 32 s and run our own length buckets (about 15 / 30 / 55 s).

**Loop: cheap to test, unproven.** (hearsay) Average percentage viewed can pass 100% when people replay (study and official definitions agree on the mechanics). No platform says it weights replays extra. Try one cut of The Rules that ends on the tear and opens on the same image, and compare it with a normal cut.

**Captions: keep them, drop the 83%.** (hearsay) Sound-off figures run at about 58-62% for TikTok and 65-70% for Reels in vendor roundups. The "80% more likely to finish" line is a 2019 survey. **[contradicts §4]** Replace "83% muted" with "roughly 60-70%, unverified". Design for both states: the caption carries the joke at 0 s, and the first sound hit earns the unmute.

**Sound: make our own drop the signature.** (hearsay) Trending audio vs original audio figures conflict and none is rigorous. Our tracks are original and owned. Make the 3-5 s audio hook (the hymn, the drop) the series' recurring signature rather than chasing trends.

**Emotion: anger at a hypocrisy, delivered as comedy.** (study) Cheng and Li (Computers in Human Behavior, 2025, 578k political TikToks): negativity and incivility drive comments and shares; criticising your own side drew shares from both camps. German 2025 election preprint: same pattern (study, provisional). CU Boulder 2026: a week of political TikTok changed moods, not minds (study). Under 10% of 2024 US candidate videos used humour (study), so dark comedy is an uncrowded lane. **[contradicts §4]** The "political frustration gets 37% more comments than humour" figure was not corroborated anywhere. Drop it.

**Sends: write one line people would forward.** (official, Instagram) "Send this to whoever told you there's no money" is the one ask the platform's own metric supports.

**Search words: same plain words in speech, screen and caption.** (case, Hootsuite 2025; hearsay on OCR) "homeless", "tax havens", "bed". One to three topical hashtags at most. YouTube titles matter for Search and Suggested but cannot rescue a short people swipe past (official).

**Cover frame: pick it on purpose.** (official) The thumbnail is a creator-picked frame, probably weighted less than for long-form.

## 4. The funnel

The path is short, then follow, then the film, the site or the email list. Each step loses most people, so every step needs a concrete next thing.

**YouTube**
- **Related Video link: the one official clickable path.** (official, live help page) Set it in Studio after upload (Content > the Short > Related video > Save). It needs advanced feature access (phone verification) and the target must be our own public or unlisted video. Schedulers cannot set it (official, Vizard help), so it is a manual step on every short.
- **Pinned comments and descriptions are not clickable on Shorts**, and have not been since August 2023 (official). **[contradicts §4]** "Pin a comment with the link, the only reliable path" is wrong for YouTube. A pinned comment is still useful as text naming the film, and for a source note.
- **Say the film's title aloud in the last 2-3 seconds** ("the full story is called...") and show badcode.tv on screen (case, OutlierKit 2026).
- **Website and email links** go in the channel About links (up to 14) and in the long films' descriptions and end screens, where links work (official).
- **Playlists:** one per story, holding its Shorts and the film. YouTube has added Shorts series with episodes and sequential playback (case, trade press 2026; check in Studio).
- **Test & Compare does not work on Shorts** (official). Use it on the films the shorts point to: up to three titles or thumbnails.

**TikTok**
- **Bio link:** a clickable website needs about 1,000 followers on a personal account, or a Business account at once (hearsay, vendor blogs; no TikTok help page found). A Business account loses part of the music library, which costs us little because our music is our own. Check in-app first.
- Until then, put badcode.tv as plain text in the bio and a pinned comment.
- Expect 1-3% of **profile visitors** (not viewers) to tap the bio link (hearsay). TikTok's in-app browser loses people, so the page must be fast and do one job.

**Instagram and Facebook**
- Captions do not link. The routes are the bio link, Story link stickers, and badcode.tv on screen (hearsay). Whether Reels have a link sticker is a phased rollout: check on the live account.
- **Comment-to-DM** (ManyChat and similar) runs on Meta's Private Replies API: one reply per comment within 7 days (hearsay, vendor summaries). Viral posts are reported to fire only a fraction of DMs (hearsay, about 3,000 DMs from 15,000 comments). Test it on one post, keep link in bio as the fallback, and verify against Meta's own docs.
- **Facebook Reels:** use Instagram's native "Share to Facebook" button. It is near-free reach to an older UK audience, and Meta's content-protection tool covers that route (official, Nov 2025). Review the numbers after four weeks.

**One landing page, one offer.** Every platform points at one page on badcode.tv: the film, then the email signup. One URL with a source tag per platform (`?src=yt`, `tt`, `ig`) so we can see which platform brings signups (case pattern, beehiiv case studies). A bare "subscribe" converts close to zero (hearsay). The lists that grew from short video all had a free thing on offer and the same link on every post (case: LA Raver 16k, Ali Abassi about 30k in six months; survivor stories, mostly published by beehiiv). Ours needs designing: for example a five-email series sent "from the future", or a one-page printable of "the rules". It must be in the narrator's voice and never sound like "you've been duped".

**Realistic numbers.** None of these is platform data, so treat them all as provisional.
- Shorts to the long film: about 0.04% typical, 0.5% with a Related link plus a spoken call to action (hearsay, AIR, no method published). "A few hundred per 100,000 views" (case, OutlierKit).
- Views per subscriber: about 505 for Shorts against about 106 for long-form (hearsay, vidIQ secondhand).
- Views to email: 0.1-0.5% (hearsay, vendor blogs). Plan on 0.1%: a 50,000-view short gives 50-250 signups at best.
- **[updates §4]** §4's 0.1% / 0.3% bands are the same order of magnitude and remain unsourced. Measure our own from the first 10 shorts.

**Channel mix.** In an 18,000-channel study, mixing Shorts with long-form went with more subscribers in business and education, and fewer in gaming, beauty and entertainment at scale. Above 55% Shorts, long-form suffered, and a sudden swing toward Shorts did worse (study, AIR May 2026, correlational). Keep one channel and avoid becoming Shorts-only. No source recommended a separate Shorts channel (hearsay).

**UK consent rules for the email form** (official, ICO PECR guidance, fetched 11 Nov 2026)
- Consent must be a positive action: no pre-ticked box, no "by continuing you agree".
- Name BadCode and say what we will send and how often ("films and songs, about weekly, unsubscribe any time").
- Do not bundle consent into terms. Each channel (email, SMS) needs separate consent.
- Keep records: timestamp, source page, the wording version shown.
- Unsubscribing must be easy. A privacy policy page must exist before launch.
- The soft opt-in does not cover a newsletter signup. Use explicit consent only.
- Double opt-in is not required by the ICO text but proves consent and removes fake addresses. Turn it on.
- Someone who DM'd a keyword has not consented to email unless the DM flow asked.

**Newsletter tool.** Kit free up to 10,000 subscribers, no automated sequences; beehiiv free up to 2,500; Substack free with no cap, on their domain (hearsay, aggregators Oct-Nov 2026). Check each vendor's DPA and UK transfer terms before choosing; the aggregators did not cover them.

## 5. Political and satirical shorts

**What worked for comparable creators**
- **Crewkerne Gazette** (case, Spectator Dec 2025): two people, AI visuals, their own lyrics, UK politics. One rap reached about 11M views and the Sun's front page, from a following of about 97k. Speed to a news event beat polish, and press and TV pickup outran platform reach. It names real politicians, so check defamation before borrowing the approach.
- **Reform UK on TikTok** (case, Prospect, April 2024 data): about 80 posts against Labour's 296 for roughly eight times the engagement. The craft: one script repeated, a clear character, staccato text reveals, polish. Borrow the craft; do not borrow the punching down.
- **Gary Stevenson** (case, 2025-26): about 1.3M to 1.65M YouTube subscribers. He credits a book and a publicist, not a shorts trick. His tone, bleak then "this can be fixed", is close to ours. The lesson is to find an amplifier outside the algorithm.
- **UK parties 2024** (study, 887 TikToks and Reels, Media and Communication 2026): TikTok gave far more reach and engagement than Reels, and parties used more humour and memes there.
- **Policy beats party** (hearsay, one search snippet): UK TikToks criticising a specific policy had a median of about 7,400 plays against about 1,700 for those criticising a party. Test this in our first 10 shorts; do not quote it.
- **Reuters Institute UK 2025** (official): the scale belongs to a neutral fast-news explainer (News Daddy, about 17M), not to the partisan camps. A position that names the decision-maker and belongs to neither camp is open, but nobody has measured working-class reach for it.

**What gets throttled**
- **Instagram and Threads** do not recommend political content to non-followers by default ("laws, elections, or social topics") (official, updated May 2025). Followers still see it. Check Account Status after the first 3-5 posts. On Instagram the comedy should read first and the policy second. Accountable Tech measured a large fall in reach for advocacy accounts (study, 2024, five accounts, advocacy publisher); the "65%" headline is a linear projection, so do not quote it.
- **TikTok** allows organic political content (paid political ads are banned). Misinformation is what makes a video ineligible for the For You feed (official, NY AG filings 2025-26). So every number on screen needs a source note in the scene folder.
- **UK moderation:** TikTok removed Reform UK videos in May 2026, then reinstated them and called it an error (case). Ofcom is investigating TikTok on child safety (official, Jul 2026). Masked figures tearing up rules could be reported. Frame it as a policy argument, not a call to action. Keep a backup of every short and an appeal note ready.
- **YouTube** loosened ad rules for discussed sensitive topics in January 2026 (study, Tubefilter). Demonetisation is a low risk, and ads are not the goal.

**Fit with the reader rules.** Our reader gets politics from the algorithm, not from friends (hearsay, study via secondary). So the cold-start feeds are the only door in. Outrage drives engagement but does not persuade (study). Aim the anger at an institution or a hypocrisy (a bed versus tax havens), name the decision, and let the funnel do the persuading. Never sneer at the viewer. Being visibly not a party and not for hire is a trust asset now that parties pay influencer agencies (hearsay, New Statesman Dec 2025).

## 6. AI-made shorts

**Policy**
- **YouTube:** since 15 July 2025, "inauthentic content" (templated, mass-produced, "same situation, same outcome", generic AI without original perspective) is ineligible for monetisation. It is not an AI ban and not a documented reach penalty (official). Realistic synthetic content must be disclosed at upload; clearly stylised content need not be; Veo made with YouTube's own tools is auto-labelled; the label itself does not affect ranking (official, 2024, revisions since unverified). Mohan's January 2026 letter made reducing AI slop a priority (official).
- **TikTok:** label realistic AI. Since November 2025 users can turn AI content down with a slider. TikTok reads C2PA credentials and invisible watermarks (official).
- **Instagram:** "We do not throttle the reach of AI content just for being AI content" (official, Mosseri). The reach cut targets unlabelled accounts featuring an AI person (official, 2026, secondary coverage). No primary source for an "originality score" was found.

**[contradicts §4]** "AI-made content is not penalised anywhere we operate" should read: not penalised by policy, but with a small audience-side cost (TikTok slider; disclosure costs about 7-8% of likes in a Journal of Consumer Research study, through perceived effort, study 2025-26), and a monetisation risk if it looks mass-produced. **[contradicts §4]** The "10+ reposts in 30 days" rule is a rumour; the official 30 April 2026 wording gives no number (official vs hearsay).

**Audience.** On one fresh account, about 21% of the first 500 YouTube Shorts were AI slop (study, Kapwing, one account, interested publisher). Mass-produced AI political videos get huge views and get banned (case, Forbes, Explosive Media terminated). AI music has charted, and the backlash came more from artists than listeners (case, Xania Monet 2025).

**How to read as authored, not slop.** No study defines this, so it is our hypothesis to test (hearsay):
- A named series title and the narrator's voice in the first seconds.
- One specific claim, sourced.
- Human-written words and our own music, credited.
- Vary the structure and target between shorts so the series never looks like one template.
- No real person's face or voice in AI footage.
- Show the effort: an occasional "how we made it" follow-up.
- Label honestly. Bio line: "written by people, made with AI".

Before anything is monetised, confirm from Suno's own terms that every track was made on a commercial-use plan, and log the plan in its recipe card (hearsay on terms, Suno faces label lawsuits).

## 7. Cadence and testing for two people

**Frequency.** Buffer, 11.4M TikTok posts (study, Oct 2025): against one a week, 2-5 a week gives +17% mean views, 11+ gives +34%, and the median stays flat at about 500. The first step up carries most of the gain. **[updates §4]** This is TikTok-only evidence. No Shorts or Reels frequency study exists; Metricool and Hootsuite guidance is 3-5 a week with no reach data behind it (study, hearsay). §8's "near the Shorts 1-2/day guidance" is wrong: the guidance is 3-5 a week.

**A realistic week**
- **Monday:** batch-cut five shorts in one sitting. Review last week's log (30 minutes).
- **Tue to Sat:** one short a day, uploaded natively to each platform, each with its own cut and hook text, never a watermarked file. Set YouTube's Related Video by hand.
- **Reactive slot:** keep one of the five free for a news hook, made within 24 hours with the existing lyric and Suno workflow (case, Crewkerne).
- If time is short, three a week is defensible. Do not chase 11+.

**Series.** Run two or three named series (The Rules plus one or two more), with consistent naming ("The Rules #1") and a recurring on-screen tag and sound. TikTok playlists need about 10k followers (hearsay), so naming does the job until then.

**Weekly log, one row per short**
- Hook text, series, length, names policy, party or person.
- YouTube: shown in feed, viewed vs swiped, average % viewed, average view duration, engaged views, subs gained, Related Video clicks if Studio shows them.
- TikTok: average % watched, shares, profile visits.
- Instagram: skip rate, sends per reach, watch time.
- Funnel: landing-page visits and signups by `?src=`.
- AI-label state, and any removal or appeal.

**When to judge**
- A single short: at 48-72 hours, against our own median.
- A series or hook style: after 10-12 shorts, on the best one and the share above 10,000, not the average (study, vidIQ 2026: median about 1,300 views at uploads 1, 5 and 10 alike, so practice alone does not lift the median; change hooks and formats on purpose). Then keep, rework or drop.
- The channel: after about 20 shorts.

**Tests worth running early:** length buckets; one looped cut; writing-forward vs spectacle-forward cuts; policy vs party targets; Instagram Trial Reels for hooks once past 1,000 followers (hearsay on threshold); three or four YouTube Collab/Remix replies beside news clips, rights checked first.

**An amplifier.** Build a list of about 30 UK targets (small newsletters, union comms, campaign groups, podcasts, video reporters). When a short matches a live story, send a pitch of under 200 words with a link, never an attachment (hearsay, Muck Rack via Change.org).

## 9. Myths and weak evidence

- **"Pin a comment with the link on every Short."** Not clickable on YouTube Shorts since 2023 (official). Instagram captions do not link either.
- **Swipe-away and retention thresholds** (under 25%, over 40% broken, 70% viral, 80% kept at 3 s). Single vendor sites, and they conflict (hearsay).
- **"The first hour decides; no slow burn."** No primary source.
- **"30-60 s is the band; 22x views."** One correlational study; others peak at 13 s, 28-35 s or 60 s+ (study, conflicting).
- **"83% watched muted."** Vendor roundups say about 60-70% on TikTok and Reels.
- **"Political frustration gets 37% more comments than humour."** Not found anywhere.
- **Instagram "originality score" and "10 reposts in 30 days".** No primary source; the official rule is qualitative.
- **"Sends weigh 3-5x likes."** Blog claim, not Mosseri.
- **Series multipliers** (67%, 2-3x, cliffhangers 3-5x). Vendor blogs.
- **"Shorts subscribers have 68% lower long-form CTR."** Original study not found.
- **Accountable Tech's "65% reach decline".** A linear projection from five accounts.
- **"Engaged views drive ranking."** Not confirmed by YouTube.
- **TikTok "follower-independent cold start"** (§4). No primary source; some accounts describe followers in the first batch (hearsay). Weak either way.
- **Where reports disagreed:** Related Video link click-through (0.05% in one report, 0.5% with a call to action in another); comment-to-DM rate limits (750 vs 200 an hour); TikTok live age (16+ vs 18+); YouTube membership threshold (500 vs 1,000 subscribers); Metricool's free post cap (20, 50 or 100).
