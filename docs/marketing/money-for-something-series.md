## What the research says about a weekly topical show

- **Speed is most of it.** News videos on YouTube lose half their first day's views every seven hours or so (one
  study, read). Michael Spicer filmed "The Room Next Door" the same evening as the interview; Munya Chawawa posts
  within hours. An episode about last week's news has missed most of its audience.
- **One device people can name and ask for.** Every case with numbers has one: "the Room Next Door treatment", the
  "Honest Government Ad". Ours is the man who has already seen it. (The pattern is my reading of the cases.)
- **The topic goes in the title, not an episode number.** Nobody has measured this; every case does it.
- **Weekly burns people out.** Gary Stevenson stopped his weekly videos in July 2026 for his health, leaving 1.6
  million subscribers with no weekly show on our exact subject. The Juice Media, the closest model to us, posts about
  every seven weeks, not weekly (primary).
- **A two-slot week works**: Some More News posts the main show on Tuesday and a companion on Friday (read).
- **Midweek is best on Facebook** (two large studies agree on the days, disagree on the hour; neither is UK only).
- **The clip is the show.** The Mash Report had about 800,000 television viewers and one clip with 27 million
  Facebook views (read).

## The format, fixed

- **Length:** three minutes or under. Twelve beats was the first one; nine is tighter.
- **Opens cold on the news.** No hello. (Jack, 10 October.)
- **One refrain a show.** October's was the nurses.
- **One straight beat** where he names who is doing well, with no joke on it.
- **One line from the future** per beat at most, thrown away.
- **One sincere line at the end**, then the tree.
- **Sign-off, if Jack wants it:** "See you next week. I've already seen it. It's worse." (his, from the unused pile).
- **Title:** the topic, in plain words, no swear word (a strong one in a title removes adverts; primary).
  Thumbnail: the host and one number.

## The song of the week

**Why:** the fast, sendable song about this week's story is the format with the best evidence in UK AI satire (the
Crewkerne Gazette, right-leaning, a claimed 150 million views). Nobody is doing it from our side. We already make the music.

**What it is:** 30 to 45 seconds. One hook, taken from the episode's refrain, in words a person would say in the
pub. Our own genres. Jack's words (so the lyric is ours to register; see [profit](profit.md)).

**How it gets out, in this order:**

1. **As a video first, the same week.** Posted as a Short, a Reel and a TikTok, the tune becomes a sound other
   people can put under their own video. No distributor can do this in time: Spotify takes 2 to 5 days, TikTok and
   Instagram anywhere from days to weeks (read; DistroKid's own page could not be read).
2. **One full-length song a month to the streaming services**, the month's best, through DistroKid with the AI
   credit filled in. Never the weekly short ones: Spotify's filter punishes "artificially short tracks" and mass
   uploads (read).
3. **Once, at Christmas or at an election, a chart push.** The Kunts reached number 5 twice on about £10, by
   pushing every fan into one chart week and by being unplayable on radio, so the ban was the story (read). That
   needs fans first.

**Rules that bite:**

- 🔴 **Suno (from 3 September 2026, primary):** a song can only be used for money if it was **downloaded on a paid
  plan**. Pro has 20 downloads a month; a weekly song uses 4 or 5. Free-plan songs can never be sold. Remixes
  cannot be sold on any plan.
- Downloads are done by a person, never by code (repo rule).
- **Label it as AI** where the platform asks. TikTok banned an anonymous AI rapper's account this year; YouTube
  labelled it (primary, the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, March 2026).
- 🔴 **The press already has a story about "anonymous AI political music in the UK"**, and it is a far-right one
  ("Danny Bones"). We are not anonymous and say so: names on it, AI declared, as `docs/using-ai.md` already sets out.
- No real singer's voice, no real song's tune.

**Making it:** `suno-prompt` writes the boxes, `suno-automation` loads them and runs the pair. The Camping rounds
already found what works for a rapped vocal; start there, not from nothing. The host's own lines over one of our
beats is the cheaper version and needs no new song.

**First three hooks to try** (the words are Jack's to write; these are only the subjects):
"there's no money" · the expat line · "nurses are still fucked, by the way".
