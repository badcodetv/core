**(primary)** the page itself was read · **(read)** secondary reporting only · **(unverified)** one weak source,

## The short version

- **Nothing pays much below about 10,000 engaged followers.** Followers first, money second, but set the money
  up on day one so it is there when they arrive.
- **The money small creators actually make is direct**: members at about £2.50 each a month, tips, prints.
  Advert money on short videos is pennies.
- **Paying members are far fewer than I said on the first pass.** Seven real pages measured today: paid
  members are roughly 0.03% to 0.6% of YouTube subscribers (subscriber counts from memory, so unverified).
  Plan on 1 in 500, not 1 in 100.
- **Three traps that are ours in particular:**
  1. A 3-minute episode cannot carry adverts in the middle (YouTube needs 8 minutes), and builds watch hours slowly.
  2. Suno's terms changed on 3 September 2026: commercial use needs a **download made on a paid plan**, downloads
     are capped (Pro 20 a month, Premier 60), and anything made on the free plan can never be sold (primary).
  3. HMRC's £1,000 tax-free trading allowance **does not apply to partnership income**. Two people sharing one
     project's income are probably a partnership. Get advice before the first pound (primary for the rule; the
     "probably a partnership" is my reading).

- So the member offer for Money For Something, cheapest to keep up first:
  1. the episode a day early;
  2. the rude cut (rude only; a line that fails the libel check stays out everywhere, a paywall is still publishing);
  3. Jack's rough lines beside the final ones (already in the joke bank);
  4. a vote on next week's target;
  5. a name in the credits;
  6. the making-of.
- **Ask in every episode, out loud, in his voice.** The pages that earn all do.

## Shirts, prints and paper

- **A printed-to-order shirt** costs £8.92 to make at Printful UK (primary). Sold at £22 it leaves about £8 if
  we pay postage, £11 if the buyer does (my sums).
- **A poster** costs £6.63 to make; sold at £12 it leaves £3 to £5.
- **Cold War Steve** (UK collage satirist, primary): calendar £16.99, A2 poster £18, limited editions £50 to £250,
  several sold out. **The limited print is the best margin found.** Whether buyers pay that for AI pictures is unknown.
- **Private Eye Annual:** £12.99 (read). An end-of-year "Money For Something annual" is the same idea.
- **Newspaper Club:** 50 copies of a four-page paper for £88 (primary).
- Printify allows AI designs if they are not passed off as hand-made (primary via search). Other printers' rules on
  political designs: not found.
- If we link to our own shop, the UK advertising rules count that as an advert: label it.

## Adverts and sponsors

- **Swearing (YouTube, primary):** a strong swear word in the **title or thumbnail** means no adverts; a mild one
  means limited adverts. Since July 2025 swearing in the first seconds of the video itself is fine. So: swear in
  the show, never in the title.
- **Politics is not on YouTube's "controversial issues" list.** War talk without graphic footage keeps full adverts.
- **Satire is not a free pass:** "stating your comedic intent is not sufficient" (primary).
- **AI:** the rule is against templated, mass-made video. A weekly AI show is the pattern a reviewer looks at, so
  each episode must be plainly different: new jokes, new pictures, a human's name on it.
- **Sponsors** in news and politics pay about $12 to $25 per thousand views. At the median video in the 10,000 to
  50,000 subscriber range that is $7 to $15 (read). Ground News and VPNs buy this kind of channel.
- **UK rule (ASA, primary):** say "Ad" at the start. "Sponsored", "gifted" and "affiliate" are not enough.

## Music money

- **Suno:** see trap 2 above. A weekly song is 4 or 5 downloads a month out of 20 on Pro. Remixes cannot be sold
  on any plan. Suno gives no promise that copyright exists in what it makes (primary).
- **Downloads are only ever done by a person** (repo rule), so each one is a decision.
- **PRS:** a song made only by a prompt cannot be registered; a song with a human's own words can (read). **Jack's
  lyrics are the part we own for certain.**
- **Spotify:** a track earns nothing under 1,000 plays in a year; about $3 per thousand after. Its filter punishes
  "artificially short tracks" and mass uploads (read). So send **one full-length song a month**, never a weekly 30 seconds.
- **Bandcamp bans AI music; TuneCore blocks Suno's older models; DistroKid takes it with the AI credit filled in** (read).
- **Has a joke song ever made money here?** The Kunts' Boris Johnson single sold 45,119 and then 53,000 in a week,
  number 5 both times, on a campaign said to cost £10. They had fans already (read).

## Money that does not need followers

Saved from the second pass; details and marks are in [the growth list](money-for-something-growth.md).

- **Cameo as the host.** Open to anyone; a puppet has earned over $600,000 there. Its rule on AI characters: not found.
- **AI film competitions.** Runway's: $50,000 first prize, 3 to 15 minutes, 2026 closed. Chroma Awards: listed as
  free, closing 31 December 2026, $175,000 in prizes; all three unverified.
- **BFI:** does not prohibit AI in work it funds; must be declared. £5,000 to £25,000, fiction only, spring 2027.
- **Arts Council England:** £2,000 to £12,000 to one person. Next round not announced; view on AI work not found.
- **Lipman-Miliband Trust:** up to £3,000; closed until 2027.
- **Joseph Rowntree Reform Trust:** up to £10,000 at any time; rarely funds films; ask first.
- **Paid video for clients:** social clips £150 to £500, explainers £1,000 to £8,000 (vendor price lists, read).
  Unions are campaigning against generative AI, so they are unlikely buyers.

## Tax and paperwork

- Platforms have reported sellers' income to HMRC since January 2024, above 30 sales or about £1,700 a year (primary).
- VAT starts at £90,000 a year. Patreon and Gumroad charge and pay members' VAT themselves (read).
- A limited company is not worth its costs at this size (general practice, no source).
- A mailing list: the data protection fee is £52 a year, with an exemption for those who only keep data for
  marketing. Take the regulator's own online check before relying on it (primary for the exemption list).

## What stops small creators earning anything

My reading of the research; nobody has ranked these.

1. **They never reach the platforms' gates.** Short videos build watch hours slowly.
2. **They wait for the platform to pay instead of asking the audience.** The only small-creator money in this
   data is direct.
3. **They find a rule too late.** Ours would be: a swear word in a title, the Suno download rule, a free-plan song
   that can never be sold, and the tax allowance.

## Order to do it in

1. **Now, before anything is posted:** Ko-fi page, an email list, one shop with two shirts. All free. Nothing goes
   live until Kai agrees.
2. **From the first episode:** the ask, out loud, every week.
3. **At 500 subscribers:** YouTube memberships and Super Thanks.
4. **At about 1,000 followers:** the member page with the six things above.
5. **At Christmas:** one printed thing (annual or calendar) and one limited print.
6. **Once a month:** one full-length song to the streaming services.
7. **When a video passes about 5,000 views:** write to Ground News.
