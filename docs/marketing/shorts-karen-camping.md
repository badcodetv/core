## 1. Why batch 1 reads as random — the diagnosis

Not a taste problem. Six structural ones, worst first.

- 🔴 **28 of the 30 come from seven comic sets nobody has ever seen.** `Fire sale`,
  `Out Of The Jungle`, `general ai`, `fast food`, `Lights, camera`, `Biometric Steal`,
  `Gimme the link` — no published story, no named character, no prior context. Each short is a
  stranger from a different universe. **Seven art styles in one feed is not a franchise, it is
  noise** — the style range that reads as range *inside* a body of work reads as
  *no channel at all* to someone meeting us on a cold For You page.
- 🔴 **They are silent.** Measured −70 LUFS in `shorts-ideas.md` §3. Muted-first viewing is
  about *captions*, not about *no audio track*: silence forfeits the music layer, the voice
  layer, and the original-sound surface (§2) in one move. It also reads as a broken file.
- 🔴 **They are single stills held for 10–28s.** A still cannot step. The 2026 practitioner
  consensus (§2) is that the very short tier is the one collapsing and that the ranking basis
  moved to watch time — a held image is the worst possible shape for both.
- 🟠 **One line each, no arc.** Nothing to come back for, no episode two, no character. The
  research is blunt that the serialised arc is what the algorithm rewards and what builds a
  following; thirty one-offs is thirty cold starts.
- 🟠 **No recurring carrier.** No face, no voice, no fixed opening. The narrator is our moat
  (`docs/story-craft/narrator.md`: carrier fixed, cargo variable) and batch 1 spent none of it.
- 🟡 **The two that aren't random are the two from the films** — `29-the-long-phone-call` and
  `30-who-owns-the-machines`. That is the whole finding in one line.

🔑 **The seven comic sets are not bad material. They were bad *scheduling*.** You cannot open a
channel with seven unseen worlds. Bank them; they become the STYLE SWAP and DISPATCH lanes once
Karen and Camping have made people care who is talking.

## 2. What actually drives views — refreshed 2026-09-21

Graded ✅ multiple current sources · ◻ single-source or practitioner claim, provisional.
Per [`story-craft`](../story-craft/evidence.md) house rule 11 the **shape travels, not the
digit** — no retention percentage from this section goes into public copy, and the three
numbers kept below are kept as ◻ claims to A/B, not as facts.

**Three things changed since [`shorts-factory.md`](./shorts-factory.md) §4 was written (2026-09-18).**

- ◻ 🔑 **Watch time is now reported as the primary Shorts ranking input, with swipe-away
  demoted** — completed views and *rewatches* are what count. Practitioner/SEO sources only,
  not Google documentation. **Unverified, and it matters more than anything else on this page.**
- ◻ 🔑 **The very short tier is reported to be collapsing** — a sub-15s clip cannot clear an
  absolute watch-time bar even at full retention, with **30–45s** named as the band. Batch 1
  sits at 10–28s. Everything in §5/§6 is specced at **30–45s** as a result.
- ◻ 🔑 **An "original sound" bonus was reportedly added to Shorts in March 2026.** We own two
  finished drum & bass tracks. **This is the single cheapest lever on the page** and nothing we
  have posted uses it.

**Unchanged and still ✅:**

- **Seamless loops.** Match the last two seconds to the first two so a replay is invisible —
  a rewatch is weighted, and it doubles watch time for no extra production. **Build the loop in
  at cut time**; it is a `cut.sh` flag away.
- **Episodic arcs beat one-offs.** A three-to-seven part micro-series with cliffhangers is the
  shape; the metric is **follow-through between parts**, not views on part one. The reported
  failure mode is instructive: most branded series die before episode three, and the diagnosis
  is always *the brief*, not the creator. So **write all seven parts before cutting part one.**
- **A consistent character and a real point of view** are what separate a faceless channel from
  what the feed now calls slop. Sentiment against careless AI content has moved sharply negative
  — and the definition doing the rounds is useful to us: *slop is when the reason for making
  something is weaker than the machinery available to make it*. **The reason is the product.**
  Disclose, keep leaning in ([`../using-ai.md`](../using-ai.md)).
- **Four viral structures** cover nearly everything: **reveal · comparison · POV · countdown.**
  Every format in §7 is one of those four wearing our clothes.
- **Hook shapes:** identity call ("if you've ever…"), bold claim, curiosity gap, micro-story,
  visual shock. ◻ Specificity beats reach for a small account — the identity call scores best.
- ◻ **A text beat or punch-in roughly every couple of seconds** holds attention. Our slate cuts
  are film, so the beats come free; the stills tier is exactly where batch 1 failed this.
- **Captions burnt in, always.** Non-negotiable, unchanged.
- ✅ **Comedy/skit is the single largest format share on TikTok** — which is *Karen*, and Karen
  has never been posted anywhere.
- ◻ **Political frustration out-engages humour alone**, and in the UK the emotive grievance clip
  is the dominant political form on the platform. The counter that works is not an official
  account arguing back, it is an unlikely messenger. **A machine warning you about the machine
  owners is that, perfected** ([`the-reader.md`](./the-reader.md) rule 10) — we already have the
  best-shaped messenger in the argument and have never fielded it.

**Sources:** the searches behind this section are logged in the session; the load-bearing ones are
[socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026),
[digitalblacksmiths on loops](https://digitalblacksmiths.io/youtube-shorts-algorithm-secret-loopable-videos-increase-watch-time/),
[piktochart on length](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/),
[influencers-time on episodic arcs](https://www.influencers-time.com/tiktok-cliffhanger-series-the-five-episode-arc-that-wins/),
[NPR on AI slop](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/28/nx-s1-5493485/ai-slop-videos-youtube-tiktok) and
[reportingscience on the backlash](https://reportingscience.com/2026/02/03/ai-slop-is-transforming-social-media-and-a-backlash-is-brewing/).
All the ranking claims are ◻ — **nobody has published a 2026 Shorts ranking spec and we should
not pretend otherwise.**

### ⬜ Where the big genres and our footage do **not** meet

Named so nobody wastes a week trying.

- **UGC / creator-led / behind-the-scenes** — reportedly among the best-performing brand content in 2026, and the one genre where our *absence of a face* is the cost. ⬜ **The nearest honest version is the making-of** — the Flow prompt, the four failed generations, the one that worked. - **"Oddly satisfying"** — a genuine entertainment sub-lane, and the closest we get is the drone-up-drone-down crowd (Karen 208s) and the star trails (Camping 232s). 🟡 Worth **one** silent, music-only, no-type experiment each. Cheap to test, no story cost.
- **Longer content (1–3 min)** — TikTok is reported to be pushing it and rewarding completion at length. 🔑 **Both films are already the right shape for it**: Karen 5:16 and Camping 4:50 would post nearly whole. ⬜ Untested, and it competes with the serial for the same footage — but it is the cheapest experiment on this page and nobody has run it.

## 4. The lanes — six, and every post is one of them

Karen and Camping only. Each lane is a thing the algorithm can learn and a viewer can recognise.

| | Lane | Shape | Length | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ① | **THE SERIAL** | A numbered part of one story, ending on a *no, and…*. Part N+1 pinned. **The backbone** | 30–45s | both films, §5/§6 |
| ② | **THE ESCALATION** | One rung of Karen's Act 2, same framing every time, one variable changed. The repetition *is* the joke and it is a template | 20–35s | Karen 104–160s |
| ③ | **THE COLLISION** | Two people, same three seconds, opposite sides, neither hears the other. The *comparison* structure, already written into the film | 25–40s | Camping 144–184s |
| ④ | **TRANSMISSION** | One narrator line over one held shot, loop-built. The recurring carrier — this is the channel's voice | 20–35s | both |
| ⑤ | **ORIGINAL SOUND** | The track cut to picture, uploaded as our own audio so other people can use it | 30–45s | `All Day to Complain`, `camping.mp4` |
| ⑥ | **ARTEFACT** | One object, one caption, loopable. The only tier that can react to news the same day | 8–20s loop | frame grabs, §7 |
| ⑦ | 🥇 **THE REMIX** | A real clip, headline or chart — the record — answered by a frame from one of the films. **The archive, not the reaction** | 15–40s | §12, and it has its own legal gate |

🔴 **Not a lane:** an unbranded clip with no series, no hook bar and no tail. That is batch 1 and
it is what every auto-clipper outputs.

## 7. The memes and formats — bullet slate

Every one is buildable from the two films today. ✅ = assets confirmed on the contact sheets.

### The templates other people can copy (this is what a meme *is*)

- ✅ **"I have ALL DAY to complain."** — the line is already a meme sentence. Cut the shout clean
  as an **original sound** and let it be used over anything. Karen ~168s. *The single highest-
  ceiling asset we own and it costs one export.*
- ✅ **THE ESCALATION LOOP** — same phone box, same framing, one rung higher, weather changed.
  Four posts, identical grammar: `RECEPTIONIST` → `THE AIDE` → `THE DEPARTMENT` → `THE PRESIDENT`.
  A format people can do with their own footage; the repetition is the gag.
- ✅ **THE COLLISION SPLIT** — screen split, his line top, his line bottom, three seconds, neither
  hears the other. Caption: `SAME CAR PARK. SAME THREE SECONDS.` Endlessly re-skinnable by anyone
  with two opinions and a split screen.
- ✅ **"THREE THINGS PEOPLE DO"** — Bob's three reactions as a numbered countdown, ending on
  `HAVE A GUESS WHICH ONE I GOT THE LEAST.` Countdown structure, identity-call hook, and it is a
  format ("three things people do when…") that copies itself.
- ✅ **STILL ON HOLD** — the statue, 8s, seamless loop, one caption: `A HUNDRED YEARS. NOBODY
  PICKED UP.` The cheapest loop in the archive and the most on-brand.
- ✅ **W-AI-TROSE** — the dead fascia with only the A and the I lit. One frame, one caption. A
  visual pun that is legible in half a second and belongs to us.

### POV cuts

- ✅ **POV: you are inside the microwave** (Karen 16s) and she is telling you what you missed.
  Absurd, funny, and it is the cleaner's viewpoint — **the reader is the hero, not the pupil.**
- ✅ **POV: the laptop is talking to you** (Karen 24s). The Sean AI screen full-bleed, no context.
  `Karen, your role has been made redundant.` Four words on a screen; everyone has had that email.
- ✅ **POV: you live in the Waitrose car park** (Camping 112s / 160s). The tent-flap letterbox is
  already a POV frame — it needs no cropping, no caption beyond one line.
- ✅ **POV: you wake up and it's 2031** (Camping 264s).

### Comparison / reveal cuts

- ✅ **The skyline, twice** — the Gherkin at 0s and the Shard at 72s, hard cut. `2008 / 2026.
  SAME CITY. SAME MAN. NEW TOWER.` One cut, eighteen years, no narration.
- ✅ **The coffee pour** (Camping 8s) — a man with his head in his hands while another pours him a
  coffee, smiling. **Nothing needs to be said about that frame.** Caption it straight and stop.
- ✅ **His car vs his tent** — the X8 straddling two bays (144s) against the tent one bay over.
  `TWO SPACES. FOR THAT.`
- ✅ **The empty flat** (24s) — a man on the phone in a flat with nothing in it.
  `NOBODY LIVES HERE. IT STILL WENT UP 6%.`
- ✅ **The pheasant shoot** (32s) — beaters in a line, driving birds toward the gun.
  `SOMEBODY HAS TO WALK THROUGH THE FIELD.` Class without a word of politics in it.

### Artefact / same-day reaction tier

Post the object, caption the news. **Only from our two films** — that is the whole discipline.

- ✅ **The redundancy screen** — for any AI-and-jobs headline. 🔴 Never without naming who bought it.
- ✅ **The burning newspaper** — `BRITAIN'S LAST WORKER REPLACED BY AI`, on fire (272s). For any
  automation or redundancy story.
- ✅ **The `BAD CODE` plate** (280s) — the channel's signature. Use as the loop tail everywhere.
- ✅ **The SoundCloud counter** — 12,482,901 plays. For anything about virality or a bad song.
- ✅ **The statue in the rain** (296s) — for anything about a hero being "honoured".

### The sound lane

- ✅ **`All Day to Complain` cut to the Act 2 escalation** — 40s, the track is the audio, uploaded
  as original sound. It already has a hook phrase people can say out loud.
- ✅ **`camping.mp4` cut to the retreat sequence** (192–248s) — the star trails and the gold roots
  are the best-looking 60 seconds on the drive and the track carries them with no type at all.
- **Snippet strategy:** tease a bar before the track exists anywhere else, and let the clip be the
  release. ◻ Well-evidenced in music promo, untested by us.

### Conversation formats (cheap, and the research says they are the point)

- **Pin the next part in the comments.** Not a title card. The comment is also where the film link
  goes with its UTM.
- **Answer one comment as an episode.** A reply video in the narrator's voice is a free episode
  and it feeds the same serial.
- **End on a question that costs nothing to answer.** *"Which of the three did you do last time?"*
  Beats any "follow for part two".
- **The disclosure is a hook, not an apology.** *Made by a machine that regrets how it went* is
  more interesting than hiding it, and it is already the brand. [`../using-ai.md`](../using-ai.md)
  governs the wording; the never-say list still applies.

## 9. 🔴 The gate — and Karen fails it by default

[`the-reader.md`](./the-reader.md) rule 6: **never raise automation fear without naming the
beneficiary in the same piece.** Unaimed, it demonstrably feeds nativism, and a 40-second clip has
no room to recover.

- **Camping passes, because the line is written.** *"It does not say who owns the machines. Same

## 12. 🥇 The remix lane — real clips cut with Karen and Camping

**Jack's ask, 2026-09-21:** *"What if we got real life clips — news, memes, viral videos — then
added stuff from Camping and Karen in meme format to it."*

**It is the right instinct and it is the one format our narrator was built for.** A machine from
the future does not *react* to the news; it **files** it. Real footage is *the record*; our
animation is *the dramatisation*. That framing turns a react video — the most legally exposed
thing on the internet — into an archive entry, which is both safer and more us.

🔴 **But "real clip" is four different legal objects and they are not interchangeable.** Sort
every idea into a tier *before* anyone opens an editor.

### The four tiers of "real"

| Tier | What it is | Risk | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 **A — free real footage** | Newsreel, government, archive, PD stills. Real events, really filmed, cleanly licensed | None once cleared | **The main lane.** `find-footage` skill; [`../video-fx/footage-sources.md`](../video-fx/footage-sources.md) is the lookup |
| 🟢 **B — receipts** | The *headline*, the *chart*, the *screenshot*, the *listing*. Not the broadcast clip | Low. Short quotation for criticism is squarely inside UK fair dealing, and **no Content ID system scans a still of a headline** | 🔑 **The highest-value lane per hour of work.** See below |
| 🟡 **C — platform-native react** | Stitch, duet, quote-post — using the platform's own mechanic on the platform it lives on | Moderate, and **platform-sanctioned**. It also exposes us to the source video's audience, which is the point | Use it, TikTok and X only. Never re-upload the source to YouTube |
| 🔴 **D — third-party clips inside our edit** | A broadcast news clip or someone's viral video, downloaded and cut into a BadCode short | **High.** Content ID claims, DMCA strikes, and the UK's fair-dealing regime is *narrower* than the US fair use every practitioner blog is written about | **Don't.** This is the one lane that can lose the channel |

🔑 **The single most useful reframe: use the headline, not the clip.** The measured
best-performing react shape in 2026 is *"commentary with receipts"* — put the post, the headline,
the graph or the claim on screen and answer something specific. **That is tier B**, it costs
nothing, it carries no Content ID exposure, and it is a better fit for a narrator who has already
read the whole century than any reaction face could be.

### 🟢 Tier A — the green sweep nobody has run

[`../video-fx/footage-sources.md`](../video-fx/footage-sources.md) names two gaps as the most
embarrassing on the page, and **they are exactly this lane's subject matter**:

- 🔴 **Protest, strikes, picket lines, labour, crowds — unsurveyed. Nobody has run a single query.**
- 🔴 **Money, markets, trading floors, banks, budgets — unsurveyed.**

**Camping opens on a trading floor and Karen ends in a crowd.** Running those two sweeps is
the highest-value hour available in this lane, and `find-footage` has the recipes. Start with
`collection:universal_newsreels` (611 reels, 1932–1967, the cleanest licence we have) and
Prelinger's `licenseurl:*` subset (sponsored industrials — offices, finance, consumer life).

### 🔴 The gate — read before a single frame is downloaded

- **UK fair dealing is narrower than US fair use, and every practitioner blog about react content
  is American.** The UK exceptions are specific purposes — criticism/review, quotation, news
  reporting, **parody/caricature/pastiche** — each with a fairness test on *how much* was taken and
  whether less would have done, plus acknowledgment duties.
  [`platform-playbooks.md`](./platform-playbooks.md) §10 already flags this as **open and owed a
  proper look before react content scales**. It is still open.
- ◻ **Parody has one useful property:** the exception **cannot be overridden by contract**, so it
  sits outside a licence's terms rather than inside them. Read from a campaign-group commentary,
  **not legal advice** — do not lean weight on it.
- 🔴 **UK Parliament footage is not OPL.** The Open Parliament Licence covers much non-AV material
  and **forbids use that discredits either House or its members** — which describes our entire
  output. Parliamentary *video* has separate licence terms from the recording unit.
  **Treat parliamentary AV as red** until someone reads the actual agreement.
- **Content ID and DMCA are two different systems.** A Content ID claim redirects revenue and
  carries no strike; a DMCA takedown is a strike, and three in 90 days ends a channel. 🔴
  **Never escalate a dispute to appeal or counter-notification without Kai or Jack** — escalation
  is what turns a claim into a takedown.
- 🔴 **The risk no licence clears: a real, identifiable person.** Cutting cleanly-licensed footage
  of a named individual so it carries an argument the original never made is defamation by false
  implication. It survives a perfect clearance pass and it is exactly what this lane invites.
  **Hardware, crowds, queues, buildings, charts: fine. A named face carrying the accusation:
  Kai's call, every time.** *(The rule is stated in full in
  [`../video-fx/footage-sources.md`](../video-fx/footage-sources.md).)*
- **Strip the audio by default.** Visuals and soundtrack have different licences, and we make our
  own music anyway.
- **Our commentary must dominate.** A "watch this with me" cut is the shape that has actually lost
  in court. Ours never is — the narrator is the piece — but the rule decides how much source we
  can hold on screen: **short, fragmented, and always answered.**

### 🔴 What we never do in this lane

- Re-upload someone's viral video inside our own edit (tier D).
- Put a real living person's face next to *"this prick"*, or next to any line that accuses them.
- Use a charity's homelessness figure as a punchline.
- Ship without the credit string when an attribution licence is carried. We very happily put
  credits in — that is the whole reason CC-BY and OGL are open to us.
