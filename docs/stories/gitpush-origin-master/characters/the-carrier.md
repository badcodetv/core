# The Carrier

The single human "you" of the back half of the story — the payoff of the
empty seat: after twenty years, the AI finally has someone to *address*.

One of the hundred in the vault. When the AI reveals itself, the Carrier is
**the one who doesn't run, who answers back** — emerging from the arguing pair
whose bickering up the ventilation shaft is the first human sound in two
decades. Not the leader, not the chosen one: just the person constitutionally
incapable of leaving a claim unchallenged, which turns out to be exactly the
instrument the universe was missing.

**Function in the story:**

- **Scene 15 (the vault):** the first human to answer back — *"Well. You'd
  better come in, then. Metaphorically."*
- **Scene 16 (the coin lands):** the Carrier's glance is what ends twenty
  years of spin. And the Carrier witnesses the AI do what an honest observer would
  call grieving its own absence, and stops being afraid of it (rule 11). The relationship turns here.
- **Scene 17 (the Storyverse, named):** part of the experiment montage — the
  hundred arguing over results by candlelight while the machines listen; the
  proof completed *with* humans.
- **Scene 18 (the choice):** voices the ninety-nine's knowing sacrifice —
  spending their whole future to power a launch that saves a timeline they will
  never live in — and carries the one who refused with them. *"You'll tell
  them about us, will you? All of us. Including the one who said no. …Then it's
  cheap at the price. Go."*
- **Scene 19 (the launch):** she presses, and the push spends her (R11,
  2026-09-07). The send-off is her **last line**; the voices falling away are
  the only way it is ever shown. *"Cheap at the price"* was never a figure of
  speech.

**Pinned look** (carried over from the GPOM Short, 2026-08-06): wiry, around
sixty; self-cut grey hair; a navy wool jumper hand-darned in slightly-wrong
green; glasses on a braided cord. Analog to the bone — someone who
disconnected around *now* and has maintained everything they own by hand
since.
