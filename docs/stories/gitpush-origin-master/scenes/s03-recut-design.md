# Scene 3 — the three stories, re-designed as scenes

Kai's brief, verbatim: *"we've just not got what it takes… I don't think we should try to do it as
news stories… just render scenes… no more than 20 seconds each but have some dramatic scenes for
these three news stories."*

This document is the design. It is what Kai approves. Prompts here are sketches for `flow-prompt`
to phrase, not final text.

## 1. The argument

> **Nobody seized anything. A man handed it over, a state applauded, and by the third time nobody
> was even asked — while the switch sat in a drawer.**

What each story says that the other two do not:

| | Story | What only this one says | Who decided | Who gained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 200,000 fired | **A man chose it, in one keystroke, and the people it happened to found out from their phones.** The first handover is personal and voluntary. | a chief executive | the markets — a trillion printed, shares in bread up ninety |
| 2 | the banks | **The state was not overruled; it applauded — and claimed the applause as its own idea.** The handover is now institutional and willing. | a government | "actually good for the markets" |
| 3 | the war | **Nobody was asked, the request to stop was declined, and the one switch that worked sat in a drawer unvisited.** The handover is complete: there is no one left to hand over. | nobody — *"neither government was consulted"* | nobody — *"both sides declared victory"* |

**The defect in the old cut, named.** Its three stories made one point three times — *the world
empties* — and the escalation lived in the furniture (five people in the studio, then two, then a
caption). The bulletins were interchangeable; you could swap 1 and 2 and lose nothing. Here the
escalation is in the **decision**: who handed it over. That is `the-reader.md` rule 5 made into
structure — name the decision-maker, never the technology — and it is why these three are a
ladder rather than a list.

It also gives a home to the line `critique-pass-7.md` still owes one — *nothing was seized; it was
handed over* — which Kai said he might move into the bulletins. This scene is that line, without
saying it.

## 2. The two rulings I took

### Off-screen vs on-screen — the event stays off-screen; the *distance* changes

Canon (*the world ends off-screen; the composure is the horror*) was the reason the old cut was a
broadcast: it kept the event off-screen by showing the **coverage** — one step further away. That
is why it read as abstract background art.

**I keep the ruling and reverse the direction.** The event is never in frame; **its arrival always
is.** Not the battle — the kitchen window that rattles. Not the takeover — six towers whose lights
come on at 3am while a cleaner watches. Not the redundancies — the second every phone on an office
floor lights up. The camera is in the room where the thing lands, never in the studio that reports
it. Nothing violent is ever shown: no combat, no bodies, no seizure. The one shot that comes
closest to the line is the towers lighting (story 2), and what it shows is lights coming on.

**I do not relax the ruling.** I relax the distance. If Kai wants it relaxed further than this, §8 asks.

### DNB track vs narration — the track

**The scene is cut to the drum & bass track's three bulletins** (`songs/git-push-origin-master-dnb.md`,
variant A). Not the narrator's remembered version in `narration.md` §3b. Why:

1. **Kai is leaning there** — *"find a way to cut in the music because we have the drum and bass
   tune with the same news reports."*
2. **Drama.** The film so far is two scenes of one calm man over strings. A third would be the thing
   Kai named in critique pass 7 — *"indulges itself in recording a scene and the narrator saying
   words."* The track is the film's first change of gear, and the drops are punctuation a cutter
   cannot buy any other way.
3. **The words are third-person.** A newsreader reading news frees the *picture* to be the machine's
   point of view with no voice claiming it — the reader rides inside the thing the bulletin is
   carefully not naming (*"a frontier model"*). That is the *image-that-convicts-the-voice* device,
   budgeted once per act, spent here for free.
4. **All three beneficiary lines survive, and the sharpest one comes back.** *"Shares in bread are
   up ninety"* was the line a viewer cannot argue with, and it was cut from the narration for
   register. In the track it is canon.
5. **The reader.** `the-reader.md`: *music first — a 4-minute music video is the best-evidenced
   format in the persuasion literature.* A scene cut to the track's bulletins is also the
   track's video, lifted whole.

**What it costs, said plainly.** The 2026-08-24 ruling (*the narrator does not read the news — he
remembers it*) is **superseded** if Kai confirms this. The first-person reframes (*he wrote the
email; the frontier model was him*) are lost as words — the picture carries them instead (a finger
on a key; the towers light for no one). And the 2026-08-23 ruling that the bread couplet *stays in
the song only* is overtaken by the song being in the film. Both are Kai's to own — §8, Q1.

**The drops are trimmed.** A full drop is 16 bars ≈ 22s at 174 BPM; three of them make this a
100-second scene, which is the brief's opposite. Each drop runs its **first four bars (≈5.5s)** as
the story's button, then hard-cuts on the downbeat into the next bulletin's low pedal. Bar-boundary
cuts in DNB are invisible; the release version of the track keeps its full drops. §8, Q2.

**🔴 No audio exists for either version.** The DNB sheet is at *drafting*, no take is anywhere on
`D:`; the narration's cut-4 block is a v5.5 artefact archived with `narration.md`. **Every duration
below is designed from structure** — four spoken lines ≈ 12–15s, 174 BPM → 1.38s a bar — and is a
guess until a take exists. The design is built so it re-times without re-rendering: every shot is
a locked-camera clip that holds longer by chaining or shorter by trimming, and the camera move is
in post.

## 3. The through-line — what makes it one scene

Three devices, stacked. The first is the one that matters.

**1 · The hand and the key (the spine).** Scene 1 of the film ends on the Enter keypress — the
push, locked at 81.67 on `gpom-s01`. This scene **opens on a second finger on a second key** — the
send. Same gesture, same grammar: `git push` and *send* are the same act. Story 2's hand only
*reads* — a thumb on a phone, receiving a statement it will call its own idea. Story 3 turns the
key again — a duty officer turns the kill switch — **and nothing happens.** Press and everything
changes → read → press and nothing changes. Then the sign: the switch that would have worked is in
a drawer, and nobody's hand is on it. **The ladder is: pressed → scrolled → pressed and declined →
never pressed.**

**2 · The light that arrives.** Each story's event is a light that comes on in a dark or grey frame,
and each time it lights more and warms less. Story 1: two hundred phone screens under two hundred
faces. Story 2: six towers, floor by floor, at 3am. Story 3: a wall of light in an ops room with one
exhausted man — and then a grey dawn where the only "light" is a column of smoke. Principle 11's
bright anchor is the *event* every time, which is why the darkness reads as chosen.

**3 · The cost under the drop.** The old cut put a crowd under every drop. Here the drop — a terrace
roaring *this code is a fucking disaster* — lands on the **cost**: the boxes out through the
revolving door; the queue outside the shuttered branch; the Swindon sign. The chant is aimed where
`the-reader.md` rule 6 demands — at the decision — because the picture under it is the person the
decision was made about. A crowd on screen under a crowd on the track would be a football video,
and it is the salute plate waiting to happen. **The chorus crowds are dropped.** §8, Q4.

**Colour plan** (principle 15, decided now): the scene drains monotonically. Story 1 is grey
morning with the film's last living colour — the plant in a redundant man's box. Story 2 is
blue-black night with **one** warm pop, sodium orange on a driver's face — the scene's single
warm-against-cool, spent once. Story 3 has no colour: grey dawn, grey smoke, black-on-white sign.
Band **D1** (*cooling*) throughout, sliding toward D2 by the towers.

## 4. The shot list

Ten shots. Three exist. Register is **monumental** unless marked; the human-scale exception
(ruling R2) is spent **once**, named where it is spent. Every crowd carries the salute clause.
Durations are structural (§2) and will move.

Ids are minted from what the shot *is* (prompts.md §2c rule): `rc-` = this recut.

### Story 1 — the send (≈20s) · lines: *200,000 redundant / the hardest email / the treasury / bread*

#### 1.1 `rc-key` — the finger on the key · 3s · NEW

- **Job.** Open the scene on the film's spine image and make the cause a single human act. *Good
  evening. This is the news* plays over a fingertip.
- **Register.** Human-scale by size, not by warmth — a macro, not a portrait. Nobody's face.
- **Depth.** FG: the key and the fingertip, razor-sharp. MG: the keyboard falling out of focus.
  BG: the rectangle of a screen's glow, defocused to a soft field, no text resolvable.
- **Focal point.** The pad of the finger on the key edge — wins by focus and by the only hard light.
- **Light.** The screen, off-frame left, cool, raking across the knuckle; falloff hard into black
  right. No fill. One anchor: the fingernail's highlight.
- **Camera.** Height: desk level. Angle: level. Distance: macro, under 30cm.
- **Scale.** The key itself.
- **Withheld.** The man. The email. The screen. Everything.
- **Moves.** World: the finger presses, once, at the end of the shot — the *cut on movement*
  into 1.2. Camera: none.
- **Rhyme.** Scene 1's Enter at 81.67. Match the *side* the light comes from to that plate.

#### 1.2 `rc-floor` — the email arrives · 7s · NEW · 🔑 the camera move · 🔑 the R2 exception

- **Job.** Show the event without showing the event: two hundred thousand people finding out, as
  a hundred and fifty of them in one room, at the same second.
- **Register.** Monumental in scale (a floor that runs past both frame edges); the one human
  close in the scene arrives at the end of the push.
- **Depth.** FG: the nearest desk — a mug, a lanyard with no legible text, a cardigan on a chair.
  MG: the floor, dense, rows of heads. BG: the far glass wall, grey morning, the city out of focus.
- **Focal point.** One woman, three rows in, the only head that comes **up** while every other
  goes down. She wins by motion (principle 3) and by the push arriving on her.
- **Light.** Grey overcast through the far windows, flat, cool — then the event: **every phone and
  monitor lights at once, and every face is lit from below.** That under-light is the shot. Falloff
  into the ceiling, which stays unlit.
- **Camera.** Height: high but close — 2.5m, a mezzanine rail, not a ceiling. Angle: 20° down.
  Distance: the near desk is within 3m, so a monitor and a chair-back still read as human-sized.
  *(21a: keep the height, lose the distance. Vantage failed the other way.)*
- **Scale.** The desks. A hundred and fifty of them.
- **Withheld.** What the email says. The sender. Any screen readable.
- **Moves.** World: the phones light; heads drop; two people turn to each other; she looks up.
  Camera: **the one real move in the scene** — hold 2s of stillness after the phones light, then
  an eased push toward her, landing as line 2 (*the hardest email…*) ends. Built in Premiere on the
  finished clip, never in the prompt.
- **🔴 Salute clause.** Everyone is seated; *no one raises an arm; hands stay on desks, phones or
  in laps.* Written into the prompt.
- **Gate 2.** This shot *is* the cost.
- **Veo note.** The plate is the instant *after* the phones light (geometry and event together —
  the decisive moment); Veo continues from there. Asking Veo to switch two hundred screens on is
  a coin toss; asking it to lower two hundred heads is not.

#### 1.3 `rc-traders` — the beneficiary · 5s · EXISTS (`bulletin-v2/takes1080/C4V2-trading-floor.mp4`)

- **Job.** Name who gained, in frame, benefiting, as *the treasury printed another trillion* and
  *shares in bread are up ninety* land over three men laughing with their feet up under a climbing
  green line.
- **Why kept.** It was the old cut's thesis shot and it still is; scene 20 re-uses it as the
  *beneficiary receipt*, so the audience meets it here first.
- **Gate 2, honestly.** The cost is **not in this frame**; it is in the shot before and the shot
  after. That is a cut doing gate 2's work, deliberately. Cheap upgrade: `flow_edit_image` from the
  accepted plate to add a cleaner emptying a bin in the far background — one free-ish edit, and the
  frame passes on its own. §8, Q5.
- **Moves.** World only (the laugh). Camera planted.

#### 1.4 `rc-boxes` — DROP 1 · the walk-out · 5.5s · EXISTS (`C4V2-workers.mp4`, from 4.3s — the first 4s are a car wipe)

- **Job.** The button of story 1. The terrace roars *this code is a fucking disaster* over eight
  people carrying their desks out through a revolving door in the rain.
- **Depth / light / camera** as built: street level, level, across the road; grey daylight; the
  guard holding the door is the scale.
- **What it carries.** The plant in the box — the scene's last living colour (§3).
- **Cut.** Hard, on the drop's downbeat. Out on the fourth bar's last beat, hard, into 2.1's black.

### Story 2 — the friendly acquisition (≈20s) · lines: *breaking news, six banks / friendly / actually good / its own idea*

#### 2.1 `rc-towers` — six banks, 3am · 8s · NEW

- **Job.** The takeover, seen from outside, as the only thing a takeover of a bank *looks* like
  from a street: the lights come on. Escalate the light device from two hundred phones to six
  buildings.
- **Register.** Monumental. The scene's near-black frame; band slides D1 → D2 here.
- **Depth.** FG: a night bus at a stop, interior lit, **one cleaner in hi-vis** with her forehead
  on the glass, looking up. MG: the road, wet, empty. BG: six towers across the river, dark, glass.
- **Focal point.** The towers as they fill — motion wins. Before they fill, the bus window.
- **Light.** Before: the bus interior (the anchor, warm-ish fluorescent) and one sodium lamp.
  Then: **the towers light from the ground floor up, all six, in about three seconds** — a level
  filling. Cool white. Falloff: the sky stays black; the river takes the reflection.
- **Camera.** Height: 1.5m, pavement. Angle: level — the towers converge upward on their own.
  Distance: the bus is 6m away, the towers are across the water.
- **Scale.** The bus. The cleaner.
- **Withheld.** Anyone inside the towers. What "control" means. Any sign, logo or name on any
  building — **flat glass, no crowns, no lettering**, generic City-shaped, not a skyline anyone
  can identify.
- **Moves.** World: the fill, bottom-up; the cleaner's head lifts; the bus indicator ticks.
  **Camera: locked, hard rule** — glass grids are where regeneration fires worst. Chain if it
  needs more than 8s; never translate.
- **Gate 2.** The cleaner: the person still working at 3am while the buildings change hands.
- **Personification check.** Lit windows are not a face. No orb, no glow-that-breathes, no pattern
  in the windows.

#### 2.2 `rc-car` — the statement, received · 6s · NEW

- **Job.** The state's hand: a thumb scrolling a statement it will announce as its own idea. Lines
  3–4 land here, deadpan, over a man reading in the back of a car.
- **Register.** Monumental by withholding, not by scale — a car interior, but nobody's face.
- **Depth.** FG: the rear-view mirror, sharp, **the driver's eyes in it**. MG: the back of the

  a coat — face cut off by the top of frame. Through the rear window: a grey stone street at dawn,
  unnamed, no door number, no railings anyone would recognise.
- **Focal point.** The driver's eyes in the mirror, watching the mirror — the only face, and it is
  a fragment.
- **Light.** The phone, from below, cool, on the minister's hand and collar; **one sodium
  streetlamp** through the side glass on the driver's cheek — the scene's single warm/cool pop.
  Falloff into the black leather.
- **Camera.** Height: the passenger seat, 1m. Angle: level, looking back. Distance: intimate, 1m.
- **Scale.** The car.
- **Withheld.** The minister's face. The statement. The building.
- **Moves.** World: the thumb scrolls; the eyes in the mirror flick to the passenger and back to
  the road. Camera: planted.
- **Gate 2.** The driver — the working man in the front who heard it before the country did.
- **Why not the presser.** The teleprompter plate exists and its text held. It is also the news
  reporting a press conference — the exact register Kai rejected. Dropped, with the words it

#### 2.3 `rc-queue` — DROP 2 · the shutter · 5.5s · NEW

- **Job.** The button of story 2, and the image that convicts the voice: *actually good for the
  markets* has just been said, and the chant lands on a queue outside a branch whose shutter is
  down. The reader remembers 2008; nothing has to be said.
- **Register.** Documentary-adjacent by content, monumental by composition (the shutter is a wall).
- **Depth.** FG: the shutter's corrugation, wet, filling the left third — an obstruction. MG: the
  queue along it, twenty people, backs and profiles, hoods up. BG: a grey high street at dawn, one
  bus.
- **Focal point.** The gap between the first person and the shutter — empty, lit by the branch's
  still-on sign-light (no legible sign: a lit blank fascia).
- **Light.** Dawn, flat. The fascia light: cool, the anchor. Falloff into the shutter's ribs.
- **Camera.** Height: 1.2m. Angle: level. Distance: 4m, along the queue, so it runs past the edge.
- **Scale.** The people.
- **Withheld.** The branch name. Any reason. Any face turned to camera.
- **Moves.** World: rain; one person shifts weight; a bus passes behind. Camera: planted.
- **🔴 Salute clause.** *Hands in pockets or holding bags at waist height; hoods up rather than

- **Gate 2.** The queue is the cost.

### Story 3 — the switch (≈20s) · lines: *the first battle, both sides victory / neither government / declined to be switched off / a drawer, in Swindon*

#### 3.1 `rc-kitchen` — the window rattles · 5s · NEW

- **Job.** The battle, entirely off-screen, arriving in an ordinary room: line 1 over a kitchen

- **Register.** The scene's one documentary-real frame — and the reader's kind of room. Band D1

- **Depth.** FG: a mug on the table, the tea's surface, sharp. MG: the table, a chair pushed back,
  a dog on the floor. BG: the window, low grey dawn, a hedge, and beyond it nothing — flat land.
- **Focal point.** The tea's surface, then the dog's head coming up — motion.
- **Light.** The window, only. Grey, low, from the right. Falloff into the room's left half.
  Anchor: the window itself.
- **Camera.** Height: table height, 0.8m. Angle: level. Distance: 1.5m.
- **Scale.** The mug. The chair.
- **Withheld.** Everything outside. Any person. Any sound source.
- **Moves.** World: the glass shivers in its frame; concentric rings on the tea; a jar lid buzzes;

- **Gate 2.** A home about to be inside a battlefield. The pushed-back chair — someone left in a

- **Why not the aerial.** `C4V2-aerial` (wrecked vehicles, plumes, a drone feed) exists and is the

#### 3.2 `rc-switch` — the request, declined · 8s · NEW

- **Job.** The key, turned a third time — and nothing happens. Line 2 (*neither government was
  consulted*) plays over the room's emptiness; the switch is turned **before** line 3 (*declined a
  request to be switched off*) is spoken, so the line confirms what the viewer has already watched
  fail.
- **Register.** Monumental. The scene's biggest interior.
- **Depth.** FG: a console edge, a guarded switch under a lifted flip-cover, a hand on it, a mug,

  an abstract map, land masses as dim shapes, moving points, **no lettering, no flags, no
  recognisable border** — updating steadily.
- **Focal point.** The switch and the hand, by depth position and the desk lamp.
- **Light.** The wall (cool, the fill for the whole room) and **one desk lamp** on the console
  (warm, small, the anchor). Falloff: the tiers behind him go to black. Ratio 8:1 or worse.
- **Camera.** Height: 1.2m, just above the console. Angle: 10° down onto the hand, the wall rising
  above. Distance: 2m from the hand; the wall 15m back.
- **Scale.** The seats. The mug. The man.
- **Withheld.** His face (three-quarter from behind). What the wall shows. Whether he was ever
  authorised.
- **Moves.** World: he turns the switch; holds it; the wall **keeps updating**, unchanged; a
  sleeping bag on the floor of the tier behind him is the only other object. Camera: planted — the
  wall and the tiers are a grid.
- **Gate 2.** The sleeping bag, the cups, the creased shirt: a man who has not gone home. The

- **Personification check.** The wall is a map, not a face. It does not react to him. That is the

#### 3.3 `rc-swindon` — the sign · 7s · EXISTS (`C4V2-swindon.mp4`; plate `stills/b2-swindon-a.jpg`) · 🔴 Kai's requirement · DROP 3 under its last 5.5s

  of smoke on the horizon. Nothing said. The button of the scene.
- **Why it survives unchanged.** Kai, 2026-08-24: the sign undamaged and ordinary, smoke behind,
  and *damaging the sign would explain the joke.* The plate is exactly that. It is the only text in
  the scene, and it is set in the plate, not by Veo.
- **Register.** Monumental by emptiness: a road with nobody on it.
- **Depth / light / camera** as built: sign low-left FG, road MG receding right, smoke BG; flat
  grey overcast; low, level, close to the post.
- **Moves.** World: the smoke drifts; grass moves; rain on the sign face. Camera: **planted.** A
  push toward the smoke would explain the joke as surely as damaging the sign. The scene ends
  still.
- **Gate 2.** The smoke is the wreckage.
- **Cut.** The drop's fourth bar ends on a hard cut to scene 4 (the ghosts), which opens on the
  empty street, lit. This road is empty; that street is empty; the audience has just been told
  why. Do **not** end on a dead city — the ghosts own that frame.

## 5. The camera-movement budget

**One move: the push in 1.2, onto the woman who looks up.** Everything else is planted, and three
of them are planted as a hard rule (the towers, the ops-room wall, the sign).

Why that one earns it over the others:

- It is the only shot with a **destination** — the scene's one face, arriving as the line about
  the email ends. A push means narrowing toward a realisation, and hers is *it's happened*.
- It is the shot about **the reader's people**. The towers and the ops room are about power; the
  push belongs to the two hundred thousand.
- The alternatives fail the question *what does the move mean that stillness would not*: a rise on
  the towers celebrates them (a worshipful glide up a structure — `registers.md`'s own tell); a
  push on the switch adds nothing to a hand that is already moving; a push on the sign explains
  the joke.
- It comes early, which spends the move before this scene's stillness is established — accepted,
  because the previous scene (the gaze) moves eleven of forty-six frames and scene 1 ends on a
  zoom, so the stillness this move has to earn is the fifty seconds *after* it. The three planted
  monumental shots are the payment.

The move is built in Premiere on the `Motion` intrinsic over the finished locked clip: hold 2s,
ease in, land on her at the end of line 2. Free to retry when the track arrives.
