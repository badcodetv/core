Written 21 Oct 2026, the autonomous run ([`../second-half-ending-goal.md`](../second-half-ending-goal.md)).
There was no interview: this brief stands in for it, written in story-craft Build mode and shot-craft
Design mode. Every line is `by: claude`. Every call Kai would make is in

**Where it sits.** It comes straight after the vote, which Kai writes himself. All hundred agreed to go.
Scene B ended in the doorway on *"He can talk. From out there. You stay on the step."* / *"Fair."*
This scene ends on the AI's threshold. Scene D follows.

**Runtime target:** 60–90 s (planned ~75 s: 64 s of picture plus the gaps around the lines).

## What it argues

> **Trust you can check turns into trust you give, and giving it costs everything.**

The hundred walk out of the place that kept them alive towards the thing that killed everyone else.
They know they are not coming back, and they go anyway.

- **The turn.** It opens with an act, not a line: the old lady comes out to Maddie on the step, stops
  beside her, straightens her torn bunting, and sends her first (*"Go on, then. You lead."*). Nobody
  argues for trust; a hand gives it. It ends with the machine asking them into its house: *"Come in.
  Mind the step."* The step from scene B is spent once, at the end, as the clean inversion: at the
  bunker the humans set the house rules; at the building the machine is the host.
- **Depends on Kai's vote scene** (between B and C), which keeps Maddie on the step throughout. C01 must
  be checked against how that scene ends once it exists. **Kai to rule.**
- **No, and…** They reach the door, and the cost is still unsaid. Scene D says it.
- **The visible cost.** The scene costs the hundred themselves. They are everyone left, ragged, carrying
  what they own, with an old man on a stick and a trolley of blankets. The kill bots' track marks are
  still gouged in the grass outside the bunker. Derek says it in five words: *"We're not coming back,
  love."* The monumental reveal of the building (C06) carries its cost in the foreground: the old man
  on his stick being helped down to sit on the crest, exhausted, and the trolley of blankets tipped in
  the mud. The building is never seen on its own.
- **Who benefits, named.** The AI benefits, because he gets to go back. Scene B already said it: *"He wants
  something from you. He's not sorry for free."* So does everyone now, the people the crossing is for.
  Scene D names the original beneficiary, who kept the gains. This scene doesn't, because it is the
  walk, not the argument.
- **The moral, never stated.** Nobody says "sacrifice", "trust" or "humanity". Sandra asks whether the
  seat is down.

## Look

This is a step between the bunker and the clean room. It is overcast daylight on the downs, muted
greens and greys, with a fine grain. It carries scene B's exterior look (S6-c) rather than full 1980s
16mm, so the cut from B is seamless. The amber seam is the only warm colour in the scene. Light source:
an overcast sky, and the doorway in C07.

## Shot design (shot-craft)

Each shot steps the argument; `scene.json` → `frames[].purpose` says how.

- **Scale varies.** The opening is low and close on the lady (C01). Then wide on the crowd (C02), the one
  travelling shot (C03), two MCUs (C04–C05), the big reveal (C06) and low at the threshold (C07).
- **The camera is locked everywhere except C03.** C03 is the scene's one moving shot (scarcity budget). It
  copies beat 0's continuous chain, which is the `continuous-shot` skill. C01 and C06 have their pushes
  done in post.
- **Talking shots** (`lip-sync-dub`): C01 (the lady), C04 (Sandra) and C05 (Derek, two phrases, with
  Sandra off screen in his silence).
