# Cut 17 — Now · final storyboard and image prompts

**Text design, 2026-09-11. No media generated.** Canon: Scene 20 in
[`story.md`](../story.md#scene-20--the-fork-why-i-came-back). Dramatic structure, performance and
estimated timings: [`s20-fork-plan.md`](./s20-fork-plan.md). This sheet resolves the image-generation
and compositing details of that eleven-shot design. The numbered shots are asset identities;
the actual play order is **01–08 → 11a → 09 → 10 → 11b**.

## The visual argument

The machine arrives with a record of damage. Its name buys no authority. A human correction
survives the journey; the machine's attempt to manufacture company did not. An ordinary person
then occupies the space that was waiting for them. Only after admitting it cannot prescribe the
future does the narrator offer a beginning.

The scale progression is **history → room → cost → face → absence → living attention → record →
unfinished point**. The extraordinary image here is an ordinary person who can answer back.
Keep the living room specific and modestly occupied, with the daylight of an ordinary afternoon.
No sunrise, ceremonial pen handover, holographic companion or crowd of smiling converts.

## Shot-by-shot design

### 01 · The record arrives

**Cue:** Scene 19's “Nothing lived is erased” tails away; “There were a hundred of them.”
**Job:** let the cost cross the cut without implying that any human crossed through time.
**Register:** terminal hinge. **Layout/depth:** the return trace ends at the near fork point;
the completed bad history runs away to an off-frame tip; the alternative field is empty.
**Focus/light:** one dim luminous fork node against the black graphic field. Its light is graphic
emission, not a lamp in a photographed room. **Camera:** straight-on orthographic wide, locked;
physical focal length and camera height do not apply to a vector diagram.
**Scale/cost:** a small inset of the just-seen abandoned hall remains attached to the *old-history*
side, recognisably the refuser's single lamp. It must never ride on the return trace or appear at
the 2026 node. **Withheld:** any second route. **World motion:** return trace comes to rest once.
Typeset the canonical arrival tag `2026. Now.` beside the node, secondary to the trace.
The tag and inset clear with the cut to the room; no other labels or numerical dashboard are added.

### 02 · Somebody could walk back in

**Cue:** “They were 'you' for about a week. Now it's you. And here we are. We are BadCode.”
**Job:** transfer the address to the viewer's time and human scale.
**Register:** R3 present. **Depth:** table edge and plain cup near camera; an empty sofa seat at
middle depth; half-open doorway beyond. **Focus:** the unoccupied seat has the broadest patch of
window light. **Light/falloff:** overcast daylight from the window at camera left; the doorway
falls a stop or two darker by design, without turning the room into another ghost kitchen.
**Camera:** seated eye height, level, 40mm-equivalent medium-wide framing, from across a low
table. **Scale:** sofa, mug, phone. **Withheld:** whoever left the seat and the off-frame person
beside camera. **World motion:** curtain settles once; no manufactured camera drift.
**Post:** plain identity type sits in the available darker upper-right wall area, clear of seat
and doorway. It appears on the name and clears before the next cut. No logo animation.

### 03 · The price is already paid

**Cue:** “I gave up the only forever I had. They gave up the only now they had. Don't make me come back twice.”
**Job:** make the warning accountable to an irreversible loss.
**Register:** terminal cost. **Layout:** a hard cut to a distinctly closer part of `fork-record`;
the old-history side still contains the lone-lamp inset, dim but readable. **Focus/light:** the
fading return trace, with black space where the other branch will eventually go.
**Camera:** orthographic close crop, static; not a second push-in.
**Scale/cost:** the trace has travelled from the spent machine and emptied hall; neither image is
replaced with a triumphant machine. **Withheld:** good future, rewards, repayment.
**Motion/sound:** no swell. Hold 2.0–2.8 seconds of true silence after the warning as an edit
intention, to be judged against the eventual voice. No flashing lights or extinguishing the
refuser's lamp: that survivor is not payment owed by the audience.

### 04 · She gets the word back

**Cue:** “People were watching. Nobody was listening. She made me get that right.”
**Job:** prove the narrator retained a correction from someone it ignored.
**Register/depth/light/camera:** the exact cut-12 `carrier-correction` composition, including
the same workbench edge, warm lamp, braided glasses cord, eye line and scale. No new angle.
**Focus:** her face before the pronoun “She” is spoken. **Withheld:** the narrator's body, always.
**World motion:** use a held neutral moment from that shot; do not replay a talking mouth that
appears to speak the AI's line. Her original correction voice does not overlap the new narration.
**Cost:** her repaired clothes and maintained tools belong to her lived survival, not to a halo
of martyrdom. Hold through “right”, with no warm-score cue.

### 05 · Leave the seat empty

**Cue:** “And I took choices that weren't mine. I'm not here to take them again.”
**Job:** make the admission land in the same present where the narrator could repeat the act.
**Image:** exact `now-empty`, at the exact original crop; identity cleared.
**Depth/light/camera:** shot 02 unchanged. **Focus:** the human seat.
**Withheld:** the future occupant. The vacancy is reserved space, not evidence that the machine
has deleted this room's people. **World motion:** ordinary light only. No device lights up, no
cursor moves on the phone, and no seat is occupied by an AI stand-in.

### 06 → 07 · An exact copy, then nobody

**Cue:** “I tried the future without you. Then I rebuilt you, exactly.” → “Nobody came.”
**Job:** recall an observed failure with exact subtraction.
**Register:** remembered domestic reconstruction, recognisably different from today's room.
**Depth:** existing kitchen door edge near, people/table midground, lamp and kitchen behind.
**Focus/light:** existing lamp pool and its occupants; on the cut, the same pool and empty seats.
**Camera:** the already locked camera height, lens and crop in the two source stills. Do not
reimagine them to match a nominal lens number.
**Withheld:** any new metaphysical explanation. **Motion:** none across the match; cut hard at
“Nobody came”, preserve the whole available hold. Do not loop breathing into the copies or
fade their bodies into mist. The absence is the reveal. No new image prompt is needed.

### 08 · A reply is possible

**Cue:** “You were the part I couldn't copy. That's why I came back.”
**Job:** replace the failed reconstruction with an ordinary present-day relationship.
**Image:** `now-living`, exact-match edit of shot 02, not a tighter lens or new perspective.
**Depth:** cup and blank paper near; seated person midground; unchanged doorway beyond.
**Focus:** the seated person's eyes lifting toward the collaborator beside camera; their hand
has stopped beside the paper. **Light:** exactly the established left-window daylight, unchanged
exposure and grade. **Camera:** exact shot-02 position and crop, locked. The face occupies enough
of the frame to read at phone size; if it does not, correct the original composition before
accepting the room pair. **Scale:** ordinary hands and table.
**Withheld:** the collaborator's face and any answer written on the paper.
**World motion:** a single small look up and the beginning of a reply, with no staged smile.
**Sound:** the one warm inflection in the narrator; no swell. Neither person is a copy of the
Carrier or either ghost. This is an unrelated living present, not a resurrection.

### 11a · Permission to remain unfinished

**Cue:** “I don't have a map for this one.”
**Job:** make limited authority visible before proposing shared work.
**Register/layout:** full `fork-record` again, with the old history dim and alternative field
empty at the cut. **Focus:** new thin line starting on “map”, leaving plenty of unrendered space.
**Light:** graphic emission, with hierarchy lower than the later caret.
**Camera:** locked orthographic wide. **Withheld:** any future destination or finished ring.
**Motion:** line starts drawing; it does not discover pre-existing nodes hidden in the black.

### 09 → 10 · Who gained, who lost the say

**Cue:** “The companies cutting the jobs kept the gains.” → “The people losing them didn't get a say.”
**Job:** recall the political choice already dramatised in the bulletin.
**Images:** short existing trader and worker extracts, original crops. **Register:** documentary
callback, fictional bulletin material. **Depth/light/camera:** inherit the actual monitor/desk/
trader depth and fluorescent light, then road/box-carrying worker/office depth and overcast light.
**Focus:** the person taking the gain, followed by the person carrying their belongings out.
**Cost:** workers' displacement answers the benefit. **Withheld:** invented company names,
statistics, biographical claims and fresh disaster footage.
**Motion:** existing subject motion, cut on the worker's lateral travel where possible. The new
branch continues as a narrow marginal graphic in one reserved lower band, never through faces
or boxes. Keep it dim so it remains continuity rather than a second subject. If no safe margin
exists in the selected crop, let the graphic continue off-screen and return farther along in
11b; never shrink the people into thumbnails to save the diagram.

### 11b · The pen remains

**Cue:** “We can change those rules together. I have the postmortem. You have the pen. Let's get to work.”
**Job:** hand over an unresolved action without staging a prescribed answer.
**Layout/depth:** old bad history behind, unfinished line mid-frame, caret nearest/brightest.
**Light:** the return-trace glow subsides; the caret becomes the sole high-contrast point.
**Camera:** this is the sequence's **one** gradual digital narrowing, beginning only after the
worker callback and stopping before the last word decays. A scale/crop in graphic space, not a
fake 3D flight through data. **Withheld:** everything beyond the line.
**World motion:** the line stops before the camera does; the caret waits. Hold 3.5–5 seconds after
the final consonant as an initial edit intention. No score hit, brand return or CTA in that hold.

## Paste-ready still prompts

### A · `now-empty` — new room master

**Reference:** none. **Format:** landscape 16:9. **Design test:** can the sofa occupant's later
face and hand be readable at this unchanged scale? The table, blank paper and free seat must all
exist in this first plate; the derivative should not invent furniture.

```prompt
Generate a landscape 16:9 documentary photograph from seated eye height, camera level, a 40mm-equivalent medium-wide view across a low wooden table toward one empty seat on a modest fabric sofa. The near edge of the table is softly out of focus. A plain cup, a blank sheet of paper and an uncapped pen sit on it, casually separated rather than arranged for display. A phone lies face-down on the sofa arm. The recently used sofa cushion is the clearest shape in the middle distance, large enough that a person sitting there would be readable from the waist up. A half-open doorway recedes behind it; a plain darker wall area at upper right remains clear. Ordinary overcast afternoon daylight from a window at camera left lights the empty seat and falls gently into the doorway. The room is current, maintained and lived in, with small fabric creases and an uneven stack of everyday objects, neither luxurious nor staged as deprivation. The frame implies somebody just outside it. Hyper-realistic 35mm film texture, fine natural grain, restrained present-day colour, natural shadow detail, no added studio fill, no lens flare, no vignette. The room contains no people, readable writing, visible screen content, logos or futuristic objects.
```

### B · `now-living` — change presence only

**Single reference:** accepted `now-empty`. **Lock:** camera, crop, furniture, cup, paper, pen,
phone, doorway, exposure, daylight direction. If an edit changes any of those, restore those areas
from the original master in post; the match takes precedence over a more flattering new frame.

```prompt
Edit the supplied photograph by placing one ordinary adult in the empty sofa seat, keeping the camera, crop, furniture, objects, architecture, lighting and exposure exactly unchanged. The seated person wears a plain everyday shirt with lightly creased sleeves. Their torso is angled toward someone immediately beside the camera rather than facing the lens. Their eyes have just lifted toward that person, brows relaxed and level, mouth barely parting to answer. One hand has paused beside the existing blank paper and pen. Only a small part of the collaborator's sleeve and forearm enters the near right edge, softly out of focus; neither person performs for the camera. Keep the visible face, hand and paper separate and readable. Natural asymmetric facial detail, unretouched skin texture, unposed posture. Preserve the original left-window daylight and 35mm grain without warming or beautifying the room. The paper stays blank, the phone stays face-down, and there are no readable words, glowing devices or new objects.
```

## Later motion and handoff

For the room and human plates, any later image-to-video instruction contains only the small
subject movement stated in the relevant shot, with the camera locked. Exact matches should use
stills or matched extracted frames; do not demand a generative morph between two supplied ends.
The Carrier's speech performance belongs to cut 12; her ending callback requires no new lip sync.

Web: after the final line, the caret may extend once by a short segment on voluntary keyboard,
touch or pointer input. It does not navigate or complete the future. Reduced-motion and print
use a solid caret; linear video may blink. The semantic Future Proof companion lives outside
the final dramatic frame. Its copy is in [`future-proof.md`](../future-proof.md#the-gpom-release-doorway).

## Design review

- The room pair now has the same lens and crop, and the initial master already includes the
  paper/pen/table the derivative needs. This resolves the previous prompt/shot mismatch.
- Shot IDs retain the eleven-shot plan; the play order names both appearances of 11 explicitly.
- The old-history lamp remains attached to the failed history. The return trace carries only
  information; the ending cannot quietly undo the ninety-nine's cost or transport the refuser.
- The political images remain large enough to read, with an off-screen graphic continuation as
  the fallback to avoid competing overlays.
- Warmth belongs to ordinary living attention. Beautiful light must not imply a guaranteed utopia.
- Prompt completeness is assessed here; candidate quality and measured timings remain untested.
