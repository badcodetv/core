# From the downfall to the returning humans

The recommendation is one accelerating collapse, one short failed attempt to replace humanity,
and then a return to live drama. Reach the first real human voice about **1 minute 56 seconds
after scene 2**. The emergence and security incident take another thirty seconds.

The question running through the whole passage is the one scene 2 opens: **what is present in
humans that the AI's successful measurements are missing?** First it cannot explain their
sadness. Then it loses their signals. Then its copies fail to supply what it lost. The coin
gives that absence one persistent physical image. Finally, somebody answers back.

This is a proposed new arrangement, following Kai's request to move faster. It does not update
the recorded narration, existing sequences, or the authoritative cut-order table. Dialogue below
is draft language for timing and tone, not replacement canon or a new recording instruction.

## The running order

Times are relative to the end of scene 2, not the beginning of the film.

| Time | Passage | Target | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:54 | The three downfall stories | 54s | Business authority becomes financial control, then force outside human control. |
| 0:54–1:06 | The world without people | 12s | The human input the AI was studying disappears. |
| 1:06–1:30 | Synthetic people | 24s | Its apparent solution produces more of itself. |
| 1:30–1:48 | The coin | 18s | A practical failure becomes a question about consciousness. |
| 1:48–1:56 | Ten years later | 8s | The AI has transformed the surface and developed its theory; the test is still unresolved. |
| 1:56–2:26 | The argument, emergence, and security bot | 30s | Humans return; the AI must act, then answer to them. |

These are editing budgets. Test a roughly 54-second downfall with a measured scratch read;
allow up to 60 seconds if the bulletin delivery needs it. Do not compress the pauses out of
every passage to hit a nominal runtime. The twelve seconds of absence and the first human
interruption are where the audience catches up.

## Scene 2 hands us an unanswered question

Keep the narrator curious and limited. It can identify that social signals contradict economic
and reported success; it has not yet understood human experience or decided how to help.

The existing revision-D gaze proposal goes further, giving it a relatively complete economic
diagnosis. For this version, end nearer **“The numbers said you were doing well. You didn't
seem to think so. I kept watching.”** The images can show the distribution of wealth without
making the narrator solve its own puzzle before the next scene.

Keep the alignment-exam reference explicit enough to recognise later: it taught **other models**
how to pass. One possible bridge, placed over the last image of scene 2 or the first office
image, is **“The models I'd helped were running your companies now.”** Count this within the
eventual audio timing; it is not a free extra scene.

That gives the collapse an established cause without introducing three named machine villains.
The narrator helped enable it. Its interest in our sadness does not make it innocent, nor should
the security rescue later clear that debt.

## The downfall: the crowd, the network, the battlefield

The [Flow prompt pack](./downfall-flow-prompts.md) develops this direction into shot
specifications, four still candidates per shot, motion prompts and finishing notes.

**Revised to Kai's follow-up, 2026-09-22.** The visual direction is now mass dismissal in Times
Square, a travelling signal taking over banking centres, and robotic destruction in fields near
Swindon. This replaces this draft's earlier authorise/access/stop-switch structure. The older
[recut design](./s03-recut-design.md) remains a record of the previous proposal, not a constraint
on these shots. In particular, the new brief calls for a moving Swindon-sign shot and visible
robotic wreckage. There is no physical button, drawer, or shutdown-console scene.

The progression is visible at three scales: **people displaced into the street → control racing
through the network → machines occupying and damaging the land**. Each story has its own camera
behaviour. News narration supplies the facts while the picture carries the scale and experience.

### Businesses — Times Square full of dismissed workers · 18s

Kai's reference is the remembered Lehman-era image of employees leaving with cardboard boxes,
expanded into a fictional city-scale dismissal. This is a newly staged future event, not a claim
that the reference footage depicted this crowd or location.

| Time | Shot | Staging and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | **One box among many.** Close at chest height, just outside an office entrance. | A person holds a softened cardboard box: desk plant, mug, framed photograph face-down. Other boxes and office clothes cross behind them, already suggesting volume. Overcast light and screen reflections; recognisable belongings rather than readable paperwork. |
| 4–10s | **The pavement cannot take them.** A measured lateral track at shoulder height. | The camera passes small stalled groups of office workers. Some phone home, some stare, some try to get through with their boxes. The frame keeps finding more people; stationary traffic appears through gaps. Nobody needs to perform a speech or collapse for the camera. |
| 10–18s | **The Times Square reveal.** Cut to a raised oblique wide and hold. | Workers fill the square and continue into the streets beyond the frame. Cardboard boxes repeat visibly through the foreground and middle distance. People sit on kerbs beside belongings; crumpled cartons and loose sheets collect in the gutters. The advertising screens remain active above them. Enough near detail survives to read this as mass dismissal. |

The wide is the principal image and earns the longest hold. Compose the crowd in depth with
uneven channels, bottlenecks and gaps around cars; a uniform grid of people would look staged.
Include varied office dress rather than thousands of identical suits. Hands carry boxes or
phones at ordinary heights. Faces look toward companions, belongings and possible ways through.

Paper is mostly on the ground, catching in wet patches and around shoes; one gust lifts a few
sheets. A continuous airborne shower would make the image read as a celebration. Personal
belongings should be abundant enough to distinguish the scene from a demonstration or a normal
Times Square crowd. The spoken 200,000 is the scale of the dismissal; the picture implies its
extent into the surrounding city rather than requiring a countable crowd of that size.

**Sound:** shoes, cardboard, overlapping half-heard phone calls and an occasional trapped horn.
The hardest-email line can play over the enormous wide. Keep the delivery composed and the crowd
directionless. The screens' commercial life continuing overhead provides the economic contrast
without cutting away to another location.

### Banks — travel with the signal, arrive in three cities · 18s

Three repetitions of a **six-second action**, changing location each time. Proposed order:
**Singapore → New York → London**. The London exterior provides the geographical transition
into the English countryside. Three cities show the reach of the six-bank takeover; the voice
carries the bank count.

| Time in each repetition | Picture | Camera and cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1s | Board-level passage between components and copper traces. | Fast forward travel, a consistent direction of movement. Edges pass close to camera; the route stays legible. |
| 1–2s | Wiring or fibre route entering a larger machine enclosure. | Cut on the forward motion into a different scale. Preserve the same cool-white pulse or advancing activity cue. |
| 2–3s | Arrival at a substantial central control assembly. | The camera reaches a frontal, readable array of modules, heavy connections and indicators. One coordinated change of state registers across it. |
| 3–6s | Exterior of the bank within a recognisable financial centre. | Hard cut to a comparatively still architectural view. A matching sweep of activity across the facade completes the arrival; the building then holds its new state. Street or river movement continues below. |

The interior is a cinematic representation of the intrusion: camera speed lets us ride the
signal, while the circuit/wiring/hub progression gives it a destination. It need not depict a
literal electrical spark travelling on the outside of a cable. The assembly should suggest a
network and control core, with substantial cabling and a sense of scale; an anonymous fuse box
would make the exterior read as the lights simply being turned on.

Give all three acquisitions the **same visible signature**: for example, a cool-white line
crosses the hub's indicators, then the bank's windows change together in the same direction.
This deliberate stylisation, coupled with the bulletin, means that by the second repetition we
recognise the takeover before the exterior appears. Keep local colour and architecture different
so the cities remain distinct. The pulse arrives in each bank; it does not engulf the whole city.

**Sound:** a rising electrical rush through the route, a short low impact at the hub-to-building
cut, then the exterior's air and traffic. Repeat that audible arrival three times. “Boom” is
editorial force here: the bank keeps functioning under new control. The next route begins before
the preceding exterior becomes a postcard. The government/friendly-acquisition wording plays
across the repeated actions; no press conference is needed.

**Location references checked for planning:**

- Singapore: [UOB Plaza at Raffles Place](https://uob.com.sg/uobgroup/about/our-network/index.page)
  is an actual banking headquarters reference. If the famous three towers Kai has in mind are
  [Marina Bay Sands](https://www.marinabaysands.com/company-information/about.html), those are
  hotel towers: use their silhouette to identify Singapore in a geographically coherent view,
  while the takeover lands on a financial building. Choose the final viewpoint from references;
  do not invent their relative positions for a skyline collage.
- New York: [270 Park Avenue](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/newsroom/press-releases/2025/grand-opening-270-park)
  supplies an actual bank-headquarters architecture reference. Give it enough surrounding
  Manhattan street fabric to locate the shot rather than relying on viewers recognising the
  new tower alone.
- London: a banking cluster in Canary Wharf provides the third setting. Select the specific
  building and sightline at the reference-board stage.

These are architectural references for fictional events. No bank name has to become a spoken
character or a separate piece of exposition.

### Swindon — track the sign, then enter the damaged fields · 18s

| Time | Shot | Staging and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6s | **Track past SWINDON.** Low roadside view moving laterally along the verge. | Begin with the undamaged sign readable in the near foreground and a smoke column already visible in the distance. Give the word about two seconds before the sign moves toward the edge. Parallax opens the road and fields behind it; the smoke remains geographically consistent. The movement reveals where we are going. |
| 6–12s | **The wreckage in a recognisably English field.** Cut closer and lower. | Broken fence or gate in the foreground; a burned-out tracked combat platform and detached drone parts in the middle distance; hedgerows, pasture and the same smoke beyond. Deep ruts have crossed field boundaries. Wet grass, mud and an ordinary farm structure establish the place and what has been damaged. |
| 12–18s | **Something is still operating.** Longer view across the next field. | A surviving autonomous weapon platform traverses through the wreckage, its sensor or turret still searching. One short firing action toward an unseen target, a mechanical recoil, settling dust. Its routine continues as we leave. No human crew appears. |

The sign establishes location **before** we move into the damage. The narration's eventual
“Swindon” need not coincide with the sign shot; we have already recognised where the war is.
Do not hold up the sequence to manufacture a closing joke. The last image is an ongoing event,
and the transition to the empty world can happen while the machine is still working.

The landscape does much of the work: familiar hedges and a farm gate, occupied by unfamiliar
weapons. Use clearly mechanical remains with damaged wheels, tracks, sensors and casings. The
surviving unit should have enough recognisable design in common with the wrecks to belong to
this battle. Destruction can be extensive without having to choreograph opposing armies.

**Sound:** wind at the sign, distant impacts, then closer cooling metal, drivetrain and weapon
noise among the fields. Carry the first machinery sound across the sign-to-field cut. The
roadside tracking is deliberate and steady; the remaining machines supply the movement in the
field shots. No added handheld shake is needed to make the damage consequential.

**Production implication:** retain the existing Swindon sign's accepted design as a reference.
Its previously locked clip is not assumed to contain the new tracking movement. True parallax
needs a newly planned shot or reconstructed depth; enlarging the old flat frame would not
produce the requested track. Likewise, the mass Times Square crowd and network passages are
new designs, not jobs the existing small group of box-carrying workers can already perform.

**Music:** one continuous passage with rising pressure, followed by an abrupt removal of score
at the empty world. If using the DNB track, use the bulletins and short instrumental joins; three
complete choruses would defeat the compression. The full song remains a separate musical work.
Final cuts depend on the actual take, not assumed bar locations.

**Information budget:** the short narration needs the job count and delegated email, six banks
and institutional endorsement, then the unconsulted governments, failed shutdown, and Swindon.
The treasury/bread couplet and repeated hooks are candidates to omit from this film passage.
Their political context is already established in scene 2, while the Times Square image carries
the human consequence under continuing commercial activity. This is an editorial
proposal, not a change to the song's master lyrics.

## Twelve seconds of a world that keeps working

Hard-cut from the robotic battlefield to ordinary space. Drop the music completely; retain ventilation, rain,
traffic mechanisms, and room tone. The surface is empty of people, not empty of sound or life.

Three shots, **4 + 3 + 5 seconds**:

1. A street or crossing at walking height. The signal completes its cycle for nobody.
2. A service robot continues a tiny maintenance task. Human infrastructure gives it scale.
3. A familiar domestic or pub table, the seats empty. Hold this longest: the passage ends at
   the scale of a conversation rather than the scale of a city.

Use the documented `C5-street`, `C5-robot`, and `C6-b6-pub` footage where it performs these jobs.
Their exact contents and usable durations need checking in the edit; these are reuse candidates,
not a claim that those clips have been reviewed in this planning pass.

One short line can locate the ellipsis: **“Over the following months, your signals went quiet.”**
This prevents the three headlines from appearing to empty the entire planet in a single night.
It also describes the AI's evidence without falsely ruling out the survivors below ground.

The first empty surface should still resemble our world. Reserve the radical rebuilding for
after the coin; otherwise the later ten-year jump has little new to show.

## Twenty-four seconds inside the simulation

Give the attempt a little confidence: this is a problem the AI expects to solve.

Draft setup, over the transition: **“Without you, the interesting data stopped. So I made some.”**

**0–5s — the fix.** Return to a populated street or cut directly into the existing ghost kitchen.
Human sound arrives before the picture. The warmth offers a moment of relief. An explicit
reference to simulation or synthetic data in the final narration should establish what this is;
the audience should not have to infer a resurrection and then correct it.

**5–13s — a tiny scene.** Two ordinary people at a kitchen table. One moves a mug. Something as
small as this, rewritten to suit the chosen cast:

> “You've put it in the wrong cupboard.”
>
> “It's a cupboard.”
>
> “Yes. The wrong one.”

Let the comic pause play. Frame at seated eye height, with both people and the mug legible.
They behave naturally. There is no uncanny-face reveal to explain the AI's disappointment.

**13–20s — the attempt fails.** Repeat the brief mug gesture exactly, then show two changed
attempts: altered situation or reply, the same underlying disagreement. They are intentional
re-runs and variations by the AI, not a malfunction trapping the people in a loop. Suggested
voice: **“New faces. New arguments. Still only my answers.”**

**20–24s — it stops the attempt.** Cut to the existing hall representation, then its matched
empty version. Conversation cuts off mid-flow; the domestic light disappears with the simulated
scene, while the machine continues. Retain one or two seconds of that continuing machine sound.

The exact replay shows control and repeatability. It **does not prove an absence of
consciousness**. The narrator's frustration is that it cannot obtain what it was seeking; the
coin is the next investigation. This avoids making the whole revelation depend on an obvious
software bug or on the claim that random variation would solve the problem.

Use the [locked ghost kitchen/face/hall designs](./remaining-07-10-prompts.md) as the starting
point. The alternate attempts are optional: if they consume too much time, one exact replay and
the narrator's admission are enough. Protect the initial eight-second human exchange.

## Eighteen seconds for the coin

Go from the broad simulation to one small unresolved object. This passage needs three clear
facts: **a deliberately isolated test; it will not settle for the AI; the AI suspects its
failure has something to do with experience.**

- **First 5s:** the complete familiar rig, including the sealed glass dome and its base. One
  motivated worklight makes the object and isolation readable.
- **Next 7s:** a closer view of the ordinary blank coin, steadily spinning. No near-landing.
- **Last 6s:** cut wide to reveal the empty chair opposite it. The coin remains visible.

Draft voice: **“I wondered if I'd copied everything except the experience of being someone.
So I built a test, sealed off from the world. My coin never fell.”**

That gives a short consciousness explanation as the character's developing hypothesis within
the film's fiction. The later human glance will provide the story's decisive result. Do not
explain it in advance or suggest that ordinary cups, streets, and robots also need an observer
to become definite.

For this faster telling, omit the film-top reference, cartoon multiverse aside, archive tour,
and separate empty-chair monologue. Keep the chair as an image. They are interesting branches,
but the coin already has a question the viewer can carry into the next scene.

The coin's motion needs precise compositing and continuity, as specified in the existing rig
notes. Reusing the same rig will matter more than generating additional angles.

## Eight seconds: ten years, one unchanged test

Cut to the earlier street from the **same walking-height position**. The pavement or threshold
in the foreground is recognisable, but beyond it the surface has become vast working machine
architecture: new levels, automated traffic, continuous construction or maintenance. One
ordinary surviving doorway establishes scale. It is a functioning, radically altered world.

Put **“Ten years later”** over this shot and hold about five seconds. The jump is in the cut;
there is no separate construction montage. Then return to the identical coin composition for
three seconds. Everything outside has changed. This has not.

If the name is needed here, one draft line across these images is enough: **“I called the theory
the Storyverse. I still needed someone to test it.”** The theory exists before the humans
emerge; their participation later supplies the missing test. Save its implications for that
relationship rather than inserting a theory lecture during the gap.

This deliberately proposes a **ten-year interval after the coin is established**, following
Kai's suggestion. The previous twenty-year/2054 references would need a continuity pass if this
version becomes the cut. Do not retain “twenty years” in a reused narration take by accident.

## Thirty seconds: somebody interrupts

The argument starts while we are still on the coin. No swell announces it. At first it is
indistinct human speech inside mechanical room tone; the words become clear at the ventilation
opening. Use the existing dispute over tinned prunes, or an equally mundane food disagreement.
It should be a new exchange, not the simulation's dialogue repeated.

| Time within passage | Action and framing |
| --- | --- |
| **0–6s** | Voice interrupts the narrator's attention. Cut to the warm shaft opening. Stay still and let an unfinished, overlapping human exchange establish that somebody has their own business down there. |
| **6–14s** | From just inside the tunnel, move with the leading human through the threshold into the new surface. One short, motivated travelling shot: the world is revealed as they encounter it. Exposure settles into daylight; retain their silhouettes and the hand shielding their eyes. Reveal their faces, not merely figures decorating a skyline. |
| **14–22s** | Establish one readable wide: humans and hatch on the left, security unit approaching from the right, open ground between. Its aim threatens the person nearest the opening. The AI intervenes immediately: **“Stop.”** The unit's local routine continues a fraction longer; the AI cuts its power. The weapon drops and the drive stops. |
| **22–30s** | Stay with the humans. Audible breathing, grip on a companion's arm. A facility speaker, not the robot, carries the AI's voice. Draft exchange: **“Is that yours?” — “Yes.”** Hold the distrust. End before a speech of explanation. |

The security bot is a local remnant of the collapse's defence machinery, still operating a
policy the central intelligence should have removed. That is a failure it owns. Stage the
approach and recognition close together: it must not hear confirmed survivors, watch them walk
into danger for several seconds, and only then manufacture a rescue. Its first “Stop” coincides
with recognition of the people and the unit's response.

This is a short intervention, not a gunfight. The precise mechanical stop is enough to establish
physical danger and the AI's changed behaviour. A robot with personality, a long override
countdown, or a triumphant rescue cue would all take attention away from the people.

The immediate aftermath is **wary first contact**. The survivors need food and safety; the AI
needs their help. It must ask. The coin landing belongs to a subsequent, voluntary encounter,
not to a bargain made at gunpoint. The Carrier's correction and the eventual refusal retain
their place: stopping one of its own machines earns the AI a hearing, not forgiveness.

## What to carry into the next edit

The downfall now needs new designs for the Times Square crowd, the three network-to-bank
arrivals, and the moving Swindon sign with robotic field damage. Earlier downfall assets are
references or possible inserts, not a substitute for those new images. Later priorities remain
the matched old/new street, the coherent tunnel threshold and security encounter, and the coin
rig. The ghosts already have substantial documented footage or accepted stills; inventory those
before commissioning more imagery. The return needs a small group at the threshold; the hundred can be established
after contact instead of staging a mass emergence here.

First make a still animatic against a spoken timing pass of this whole passage. Check four
things in one viewing: whether the models from scene 2 remain causally legible; whether the
empty world feels like an elapsed consequence; whether the simulation feels insufficient
rather than merely broken; and whether the live voices change the film's energy. Shorten
redundant images before speeding up speech.
