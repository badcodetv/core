# Swindon — the sign leads into the fields

[Pack index, shot specifications and exact finishing work](./downfall-flow-prompts.md).

**Play order:** W1 6s → W2 6s → W3 6s. The sign establishes the location early, then the camera
enters the damage. The last machine continues its routine when we leave. There is no physical
button or shutdown-room insert.

**Generate W2's anchor candidates first.** W1 must match its chosen weather, field boundaries,
smoke direction and farm-building character. W3 is a one-reference edit of the selected W2
plate, using the surviving platform already present there. Do not use an unselected W2 image
as W3's reference or rely on an asset name typed into the prompt without attaching the image.

For W1–W2 the declared variation is light and colour: A = grey overcast; B = full natural colour
under the same broad sky; C = low afternoon sun; D = late dusk with a readable horizon. For W3
the variable is framing of the surviving machine; B also tests fuller colour. Match the final
field sequence before animation. The four blocks at each shot are alternatives, not a montage.

## W1 — tracking past the sign

The sign's face is blank in the generated plate, with room for the exact word **SWINDON** in
finishing. It is undamaged, ordinary and front-on. It must be fully visible at the start, safely
inside the picture, with smoke already readable beyond it. A short rightward truck sends the
near sign towards the left edge and reveals more of the road and fields.

### W1 · image-to-video

Attach the selected W1 plate as the start image. This needs genuine parallax. The sign plane
starts parallel to the camera sensor; the simple sideways translation also makes later
lettering substantially easier than an orbit or an oblique pass.

```prompt

The near sign slides left faster than the distant hedge, opening more of the road and fields on the right. The sign stays rigid and front-facing as the distant smoke drifts in its existing direction. Keep the travel short and preserve the sign's plain face.

**Finishing:** make the word readable for roughly the opening two seconds, then let the shot
continue. That duration is an editorial choice, not an exact-timestamp instruction for Flow.
Add **SWINDON** to the sign's actual plane, matching its motion and perspective. Keep the word
present while its part of the sign remains in view. A lettered review mock-up belongs beside
the clean animation plate. The existing locked Swindon plate is a possible design reference,
but its old motion is not assumed to satisfy this new track; no existing media was inspected
or reused by this writing pass.

**Sound:** roadside wind and distant machinery. Carry the machinery across the cut to W2.

The still already has the weapon aimed. One firing action is enough; do not combine driving,

```prompt

**Edit/sound:** use the single clean recoil and the machine remaining ready afterward. Keep the
weapon report mechanical and brief; no musical victory cue. Leave while its activity still
feels ongoing. The preceding narration may say the battle has ended while this shot contradicts
that composure.

## Field continuity and finishing

- W2 is the actual location master. The W1 sign view looks toward that field; its smoke and
  light must agree. The sign itself is intact, while damage becomes visible beyond it.
- W3 uses W2's surviving platform, not a repaired version of its foreground wreck. If the
  selected W2 rendered both machines as destroyed, correct that still before deriving W3.
- Variant B's richer colour is a treatment test. If chosen, bring W1–W2 to the same palette;
  preserve their common weather rather than grading daylight into dusk.
- Check the scale against fence posts, grass, ruts and farm buildings. The machine should feel
  capable of crossing a field, not like a toy or a skyscraper-sized robot.
- Keep the ordinary landscape visible. Filling the whole frame with fire or opaque smoke
  would remove the local specificity this scene depends on.
