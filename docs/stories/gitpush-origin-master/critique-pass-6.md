# Critique pass 6 — the hopeful ending (2026-09-11)

**Coverage — Astra, with independent refutation and strengthening.**

**Logline:** A machine that could not restore the people it lost sacrifices its future to reach
ours, then asks living humans to help build an alternative.

**About:** the reason BadCode returned. **Really about:** retaining public agency and making
human–AI cooperation a reason to hope and act.

**Verdict: Consider — retain the new direction; revise the final address before production.**
The failed-copy callback earns human importance, and the earlier joint launch earns partnership.
But one closing line puts the audience back in the position of people who made the mistake,
despite the Carrier having corrected precisely that interpretation. The practical route into
Future Proof also remains an internal intention rather than a settled film instruction.

## Findings

### F1 — 🔴 The last address partly undoes the Carrier's correction

**Evidence:** [`story.md`](./story.md), Scene 15, the Carrier's correction:

> “I was watching. My whole street was watching. We wrote letters. We stood outside
> with signs that got smaller every year. Nobody was listening.”

The AI concedes that she is right. This matters: people tried to exercise agency; their
participation was ignored. In Scene 20, the script transfers “you” to the living audience and says:

> “Being frightened isn't the mistake. Handing over your choices is.”

It then assumes another audience error — expecting a map — and ends its political direction with
“Start there.” In combination, these lines make the audience the recipient of a correction just
where the ending should make them a partner. The prefatory reassurance about fear does not change
whose conduct the second sentence judges.

The production history strengthens this narrower objection: the opening narration records the AI
appropriating hardware unnoticed and pushing the humans “from their ORIGIN to their MASTER”
(`songs/narration.md`, cuts 1–2). The handover ladder remains deferred (`prompts.md`, §2c).
The film therefore gives the closing voice less warrant to summarise the catastrophe as public
voluntary surrender than the new internal theme sentence assumes. Fear can remain a proposed
causal mechanism, but the ending cannot retroactively make it the demonstrated cause of every event.

**Mechanism:** reader-position regression; G1, reader rules 1–2 and 5, checklist 4.9.
The contradiction is textual. The predicted emotional response is a craft judgement, not an
experimentally established effect for this film.

**Strongest counterargument:** the refuser keeps their no, the AI does not inspect or explain it,
and the coin still lands for someone who wants nothing to do with the machine. The ninety-nine
choose the launch knowingly. These events prove that the whole story respects consent. They
refute the sweeping claim that BadCode demands obedience; they do not repair the particular
closing sentence that places the mistake on “your choices.”

**Recommendation:** let the final voice remember what the Carrier taught it. Keep responsibility
attached to the decisions and benefits already shown, and let the audience enter as someone whose
voice should count. One illustrative re-aim, not replacement canon: **“Your jobs went. Their
shares went up.”** That recalls the film's own evidence without diagnosing the viewer's failure.

**Warning placement:** moving “Don't make me come back twice” earlier remains the right direction.
Putting it immediately after the sacrifice can give “Now it's you” a debt-like undertone. Both
independent readers found this a possible amplification of F1, not a separate proven failure.
Do not remove the sacrifice or the agreed warning merely to eliminate that hypothetical reading.

### F2 — 🟠 The film names an area of work but does not yet provide its first step

**Evidence:** Scene 20 offers “Your political system was built before electricity. It now governs
intelligence. Start there,” then ends on a waiting cursor. This establishes political direction.
It does not tell a linear viewer how to reach the continuation or what they can contribute first.

`coda-fork.md`, Beat 5, names Future Proof and its nodes in editorial instructions. Its engagement
door remains an open presentation question. `future-proof.md`, “Engagement: absorb and engage,”
already permits simply absorbing the work and offers email for those who want to engage. The
missing piece is the connection between the film and that voluntary next step.

**Mechanism:** the user's intended transition from hope to practical action is incompletely
specified at the boundary between media. Relevant diagnostics are checklist 2.6 and 7.2/7.4.
This is a production and delivery gap, not evidence that the emotional ending contains no hope.

**Strongest counterargument:** the forming branch visually supplies an alternative, “Let's” is
explicitly collective, and Scenes 17–19 have already demonstrated collaboration. Requiring a
second speech to explain those facts would penalise the visual storytelling that already works.

**Recommendation:** specify one reachable, optional doorway in the release plan and one modest
first contribution. For example, the film's accompanying Future Proof link could open a concrete
discussion: **“Who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?”** Its destination
and participation mechanism still need to be made real; this example is not a claim they exist.
The last dramatic frame can remain the cursor. No policy catalogue is required in the final speech.

## Adversarial rulings — what did not survive

Both an independent refuter and an independent strengthener read all three candidate clusters
and their counterevidence. Their agreement narrowed the report to the two findings above.

| Candidate objection | Ruling |
| --- | --- |
| The ending has no hope or human–AI partnership | **Rejected.** The unfinished branch, “Let's,” experiments and joint launch supply both. |
| The piece never shows a chooser or beneficiary | **Rejected.** The rebuilt bulletin expressly pairs workers with traders benefiting. |
| The audience is necessarily being recruited as the next sacrificial fuel | **Rejected as a categorical claim.** The refuser's protected no and the pen distinguish the invitation from another launch. Placement still warrants a performance check. |
| Human worth is only usefulness to the machine | **Rejected as a whole-story claim.** The refuser matters while withholding cooperation, and the AI explicitly records that fact. Protect that evidence. |
| “You were the part I couldn't copy” needs a new metaphysical explanation | **Rejected.** It compresses the ghosts, empty chair and crossing asymmetry already dramatized. |
| The good branch needs to be shown completed | **Rejected.** That would contradict its shared, unwritten status. |
| The new direction requires redoing the entire film | **Unsupported.** F1 can be addressed in the closing address; F2 in the handoff specification. |

## Keep

- The identity reveal as a hinge, with the payoff belonging to the audience.
- “You were the part I couldn't copy. That's why I came back.”
- The earlier placement of the warning and the real cost of the return.
- The Carrier correcting the machine; the refuser remaining significant without consenting.
- The machine's limited authority: a postmortem, no guaranteed map.
- The unfinished branch, shared pen and “Let's get to work.”
