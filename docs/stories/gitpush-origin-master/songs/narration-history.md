## The dramatic cut — Claude's pass, 2026-09-19

Kai asked for *"the most dramatic version you can think of"*, with **less backing music** and the orchestra used
sparingly. Four craft changes, all from the Suno toolkit — **your words are not rewritten, only re-timed**:

1. **Silence is designed, not implied.** The Style box now says the orchestra is *silent most of the time* and names
   its arrivals as **events** (one violin stab, one low piano note, one late swell). Suno has no bar counter, so an
   event has to be described as a change, not a position — and "swelling all the way through" is what has been
   filling every gap with strings.
2. **The poetry pool is banned.** `spoken word` is a **genre** on Suno — slam, dub poetry — and its default delivery is
   metrical and declamatory. It is now out of the Style box and explicitly excluded, with the whole pool named. This is
   the single biggest lever on "he is reading" versus "he is talking".
3. **The layout carries the timing.** Suno reads a line break as a phrase reset and a blank line as a musical event, so
   the opening is broken into fragments — *Two lights on a board. / In a box. / In the dark.* — and the close falls one
   phrase per line. Same words, slower, with the pauses written in.
4. **Four cues, not eleven.** Dense per-line bracket cues chop the read into micro-sections that each re-decide their
   phrasing, which is the r6/r7 word-mangling cause and the likeliest reason a `w10` take keeps coming back 20s short.
   What survives: one top cue, `[one violin stab]` on the death sentence, `[Build]`, one swell before *to their master*,
   and the outro drum.
