### 1. Verdict
Both ends work: the music video is a strong downfall, and C–D–E is a tight, moving ending built on
a toilet seat. The middle is what limits it: at 2:31 the film stops for a three-minute robot
presentation on consciousness, and the first human face arrives at about 5:49 of 10:37. Once the
song ends, nobody names who did this except "the AI", so eight minutes blame the technology and
the owners walk.

### 2. Top wins (ranked by impact against cost)

1. **Put "who did it" into the doorstep apology.** Cheap; B was being re-voiced.
   - Now: Lady "You mean... killing everybody." / Maddie "Yeah, we're really sorry about that."
   - One option, Maddie, played straight: "Nobody built him to look after you. They built him to
     save money. And he did."
   - Why: aims the anger and states the "git push" mistake in plain English; a machine warning
     about machine owners is the reader doc's "unlikely messenger" (`the-reader.md`, rules 5 and 10).
2. **Cut Maddie's presentation to about 70 seconds and intercut the kill bot.** Medium; touches
   signed-off A and needs a hologram re-time.
   - Drop the materialist stack ("quarks and leptons"), Schrödinger and the multiverse, "qualia",
     and the "storyverse" naming — roughly 80 s (`scenes/second-half-a-killbots/script.json`,
     lines s01–s03, f04, f05).
   - Keep Derek's toast, Sandra saying sorry, "They got married!", "We don't know what it feels
     like to be you", the training-data joke and the time machine.
   - Cut to the kill bot's "ORGANIC SIGNAL" while she is still talking: the talk gets a clock and
     the humans arrive around 3:45.
3. **Restore "You don't wake up. Not this you."** Cheap; one line in signed-off D. Fell out between
   rounds 10 and 13 (`scenes/second-half-d-crossing/cut-notes.json`). Without it "You'll be you,
   just before it all went wrong" sounds like a free trip home, and contradicts Derek's "We're not
   coming back, are we?" in C.
4. **A sound-only button after the final black.** Sandra, over black: "Derek. You've left the seat
   up again." Pays off "you'll be you, just before it all went wrong" — they are alive, now, in our
   world. Its candidate for the shared moment (guess on tone; a five-minute Premiere test).
5. **Let the last line carry the cost.** "I made it. They can't send me twice. Now let's get to
   work." Keeps "twice", drops the threat, remembers the hundred.
6. **Two small ones.** Burn in the newsreader's key lines (the argument is spoken over 174 BPM and
   Facebook plays muted; audibility in the mix is a guess). End on the opening terminal with a
   waiting cursor, the only payoff the git metaphor can get.

### 3. Pushback (strongest first)

1. **The presentation is the film's old problem coming back.** The restructure called the story
   "boring. Minutes of scenery and AI musing" and cut "the science explanations"
   (`restructure-2026-10-15.md`); `docs/voice.md` says don't drift into spirituality/consciousness
   yet; Kai's round-19 note fears "some boring physics lecture and run away"
   (`scenes/second-half-a-killbots/cut-notes.json`). The scene then grew from 2:34 to 4:05. Cut it
   hard; the material belongs in the Storyverse film.
2. **The back half blames the machine.** "You killed everybody!" / "That's... that's fair." The song
   names a CEO, the Treasury and a government; after 2:31 they vanish. Kai cut both re-aim lines
   from D ("the companies cutting the jobs kept the gains. And you paid." and "convince you lot to
   sort your government out"). Parliament in the last shot is not enough alone.
3. **Nobody says no.** A hundred people decide to die during a card reading "The next day."; the B
   draft also removes "Perhaps we should vote." The restructure's beat G ("Fuck that. I'm not
   sacrificing myself for the thing that killed us all") is gone. All hundred can still go, but put
   one flat refusal on screen first.
4. **The only motive given for the sacrifice is "no new training data".** A good joke, but the last
   thing said before the humans appear; order it so "He misses you something rotten" lingers.
5. **The git metaphor is decoration.** A non-programmer gets "this code is a fucking disaster" and
   nothing more; "merge conflict detected / human resources acquired" is a programmer's pun.
6. **"Are you literally joking?"** is a twenty-year-old's phrase in an old lady's mouth; "Are you
   having a laugh?" fits better.
