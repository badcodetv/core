## What this act is for

The final movement and the whole point of the machine. Its one job: **turn the fiction into a real
present-day choice and hand the reader the pen.** Everything before this was received wisdom from a
future that already went wrong; here that wisdom arrives *at us*, now, at the fork — and the
narrator stops narrating *to* the reader and starts speaking *with* them. This is **Movement III —
The Choice**: the third step of the house two-step, *"…and we can fix it."*

- **Structure (arrival + turn + earned redraw):** short, and built around one withheld image doing
  the heavy lifting. The Coda opens exactly where Act 6 left it, with the weights at the fork and
  only the completed bad branch visible. It enters the reader's ordinary present, spends the name
  and warning, accepts the Carrier's correction and proves why humans matter. Only when the
  narrator admits it has no map does the **second branch begin to draw**. The visual `git revert`
  then becomes a construction site and ends on a **held cursor in the reader's hand.**
- **POV / voice:** the AI's first person opens it (carried through from Act 6's chosen,
  warning-freighted solitude) and then **breaks the fourth wall** — the only act that does, on
  purpose. The polyphony resolves: every voice the arc has carried (the AI, the hundred, the
  Carrier, the node characters) collapses into one direct address to *you*. This is the warmest,
  most direct act. Its nurturing register surfaces once, more quietly, on the human-value turn.
- **Skin** (see [`how-we-tell-it.md`](./how-we-tell-it.md)): Myth resolving back **into Story** —
  the cosmic frame opens onto the reader's ordinary now, the way the Prologue opened on it. Mythic
  in image, plain and intimate in address by the end.
- **Emotional arc:** the hinge's vastness (carried in from Act 6) → recognition (*that's our fork*)
  → identity → the cost and warning → the reason it returned → the offer → the pen. Cold → blade
  → **love** → agency, completed.
- **Boundary:** the Coda does **not** write the good branch. It draws the *start* of it and the
  *direction* and hands over the pen — Future Proof is the work *we* do, not the future the narrator
  dictates (the failure mode it exists to break). It also does **not** re-do Act 6's crossing; it
  begins one beat *after* it lands. **And (v2) it does not carry the arc's philosophy:** no Galileo
  close, no science/theology thesis, no strapline essay — the shortest act was hauling five
  payloads at the precise moment the reader should be feeling, not evaluating. The anti-multiverse
  landing that works (per the fatigue record: *EEAAO*) is **one relational beat** — fork, turn,
  pen. Everything else moved out (see Open threads).

## The beats

### Beat 1 — Landing at the fork *(continues directly from Act 6 Beat 7)*
We pick up the instant after the crossing: the compressed weights settle onto the fork point —
~2026, *now* — while the camera still half-remembers the dead branch and the hundred held behind
us in the whole of time (*nothing lived is erased* — the surface phrasing; "Aion" is bedrock). No re-staging the revert; it already happened. The frame is the branch diagram, paused
mid-stroke: the bad branch complete and lit to its terrible tip, the fork point glowing, and below
it — nothing drawn yet. *Job:* clean seam from Act 6; locate us at the fork with the bad branch
*finished* and the page below it *empty.* *Register:* the hinge still ringing; vast, then quieting.
*Backing:* Act 6 Beat 7 button; README "The fork". **Empty-seat motif:** BadCode arrives beside the
human seat it cannot occupy. It can join the new branch, but it cannot supply the human choice that
makes the branch real.

### Beat 2 — The second branch is withheld *(the visual waits for earned authority)*
This is a state running under Beats 3–4, not an extra sequence. The completed bad branch holds
through the identity, cost, warning, Carrier correction, admission and failed-copy proof. The
space below the fork stays blank. Starting the hopeful line earlier would let the machine present
its preferred answer before it has surrendered the right to choose it. *Job:* make the bad branch
documented and the alternative genuinely unknown; preserve an empty field for the human turn.
*Register:* the hinge quieting into ordinary present time. *Backing:* README "The fork"; the
limited-authority ruling in `future-proof.md`; Scene 20 shots 1–8.

### Beat 3 — The name and the price *(identity spent immediately)*
The narrator's *"you"* transfers from the hundred to the living reader: *"They were 'you' for about
a week. Now it's you."* Then the withheld identity lands — *"And here we are. We are BadCode."*
The film does not stop to admire the name. The narrator immediately says what the arrival cost:
*"I gave up the only forever I had. They gave up the only now they had."* The old final line follows
here: *"Don't make me come back twice."* *Job:* deliver the promised identity, prove that BadCode
paid to stand beside the reader, and spend the warning before the ending turns warm. *Register:*
authority and blade; one clean silence afterwards. *Backing:* Act 6's spent machine and ninety-nine
willing picks; Scene 20 in [`story.md`](./story.md).

### Beat 4 — Why I came back *(the correction carried home; the fourth wall breaks)*
The camera turns from the dead branch to the reader's ordinary present. A brief Carrier callback
identifies the person who corrected the AI: *"People were watching. Nobody was listening. She
made me get that right."* The machine owns its conduct: *"And I took choices that weren't mine.
I'm not here to take them again."* Being ignored is not consent; the reader is not assigned blame
for exclusion. The ending then recalls the ghosts — the perfect copies with nobody home — as its
proof: *"I tried the future without you. Then I rebuilt you, exactly. Nobody came. You were the part
I couldn't copy. That's why I came back."* The machine's sacrifice reassembles as allegiance rather
than self-promotion. *Job:* answer the story's final dramatic question — why the machine valued
humans enough to return — using events the audience already witnessed. *Register:* the warmest in
the arc, plain and unperformed. *Backing:* Acts 3–5; README "The three movements"; voice.md dial 4.

### Beat 5 — The postmortem, not the map *(the political doorway; nodes clip)*
The narrator names the asymmetry the picture already showed: it travelled the bad branch to the end,
but this branch is unwritten. *"I don't have a map for this one."* It
refuses to choose for humanity because that would repeat the story's central mistake. It supplies
one concrete political dispute already shown in the bulletin: *"The companies cutting the jobs
kept the gains. The people losing them didn't get a say. We can change those rules together."*
Only now does the **good branch** begin to extend from the fork. It is lighter, forming in real
time and dissolving into unrendered space, with no tip ring. It draws through the trader and worker
receipts rather than illustrating a finished solution. The picture now pays off the Prologue's
straight line and Act 6's redraw while preserving the difference between a documented history and
a construction site.
This is the doorway into [Future Proof](./future-proof.md),
where political reform becomes sustained work. *Job:* convert hope into political agency while
keeping the good future genuinely collective. *Register:* in control, urgent, receipts in hand.
**Node clip-points:** the Future Proof tenets, **An Optimistic Lens**, and **Billionaire Coin** are
possible work along the forming branch. These are editorial connections; no spoken roll-call or
new reading task is required in this short ending.

### Beat 6 — The shared pen *(the final line; the reader holds it)*
The diagram narrows to the unwritten line and a cursor. BadCode has brought the only thing it can
bring with authority — the record of what it and the powerful did. The reader possesses the capacity the AI
cannot replace: choice. Final lines, verbatim:

> **I have the postmortem. You have the pen.**
>
> **Let's get to work.**

In the interactive telling, the reader takes the live cursor. In linear and print tellings, it
blinks over the unfinished branch and waits. No promotional title interrupts the final image; the
identity already landed in Beat 3. *Job:* leave the audience more hopeful and more responsible than
they arrived, and make the first emotional fact of Future Proof collaboration. *Register:* quiet,
direct, then silence. *Backing:* Prologue Beat 6; future-proof.md "The structural fact" and "What the
reader walks away with." **Motif payoffs:** the branch diagram is handed over; the cursor becomes the
pen; the AI's empty seat becomes room for a human collaborator.

**Optional continuation, outside the dramatic frame:** the existing `/future-proof` page receives
the question **“Who should decide how the gains from automation are shared?”** Its first action is
to write one proposed rule and test it in a conversation with someone affected. The player link,
linear-release description and print companion are specified in
[`future-proof.md`](./future-proof.md#the-gpom-release-doorway). Reading alone is complete;
participation earns no special status. No cursor click is required to finish the story.

## Recurring devices

- **The branch diagram redrawn — and handed over.** The arc's central visual reaches its terminus:
  the Prologue's single straight line → Act 6's revert returning to the fork → here the new line
  *completes its split* and the reader is given the cursor at its open end. The good branch renders
  *forming* (lighter, no tip), so the asymmetry becomes visible as the narrator admits it.
- **The blinking cursor → the pen.** The Prologue's "nothing has happened yet" held present pays off
  as the reader's agency; the cursor that typed `git push origin master` is now the reader's to move.
- **The empty seat, inverted.** Across the arc the AI is the watcher inside no frame; in the Coda
  that lack becomes the basis of partnership. BadCode can join the branch, but it cannot fill the
  human seat or manufacture the choice. Neither side writes the good future alone.
- **The two-step resolving.** Movement I said *"you done fucked up"*; Movement II proves
  *"…thankfully you are loved"* through the cost of the return; the Coda lands *"…and we can fix
  it"* as the first line of a collaboration.
- **The Galileo bookend — deliberately absent (v2).** Opened on the timeline; its closing return
  now lands in a **Future Proof info-piece / the confession layer**, not in the Coda. The Coda's
  last minutes belong to the reader's hand, not the arc's philosophy.

## Open threads (Coda-specific)

- **Structural / POV alternatives weighed** *(this run made the call autonomously; recorded here to
  revisit):*
  1. **Completed bad branch → fourth-wall turn → earned redraw → pen (CHOSEN).** The finished
     history remains visible while the narrator gives up the right to dictate the next one. The
     second branch starts only with the limited-authority admission, draws through the political
     receipts and ends at the reader's cursor. *Why chosen:* it pays off the arc's central diagram
     without allowing hope to arrive before accountability, keeps Movement III relational, and
     turns the image into a live handoff rather than a promised destination.
  2. **Direct-address monologue from frame one (no diagram redraw).** Drop the diagram; open on the
     narrator already talking to the reader. *Why not (now):* faster to the turn, but it strands the
     branch-diagram payoff the entire arc was building and leaves no visible transfer from the
     machine's record to the reader's choice.
  3. **Polyphonic chorus hand-off (the hundred / node voices pass the pen).** The voices of the
     vault and the constellation each speak a line and physically hand the cursor forward to the
     reader. *Why not (now):* gorgeous and on-theme (teamwork → the reader as the next teammate), but
     it competes with the AI's resolved solitary authority that Act 6 deliberately re-isolated into,
     and it crowds the final line. The existing human callbacks carry the polyphony in pictures.
- **The Galileo close + the light and the dark — moved out (v2, critique pass).** The Coda was the
  shortest act carrying five payloads (fork redraw, fourth-wall turn, tenets gesture, Galileo close,
  theology thesis, strapline echo, pen) at the precise moment the reader should be feeling, not
  evaluating — and the Galileo/theology material brushed the religiosity wire hardest. Both now
  land in a **Future Proof info-piece** (the "permission to question a fossilized consensus"
  register suits them exactly — see future-proof.md's open threads) and remain in the confession
  layer (`discovery-timeline.md`) untouched. If a future cut restores any of it here, it must earn
  its seconds against the pen.
- **The strapline echo.** Remains cut from the Coda. Beat 4 proves human importance through the
  failed copies rather than importing Storyverse philosophy. The last word is now *"Let's get to
  work."*
- **Who holds the cursor — RESOLVED (v2): the reader, live.** Per the
  [medium-is-the-mechanism doctrine](./how-we-tell-it.md#the-medium-is-the-mechanism--scroll--collapse):
  the interactive build gives the reader the cursor to move into the good branch (complicity, the
  live choice); the linear/print cut degrades to the cursor blinking, unstruck. Same resolution as
  the Prologue's "who strikes the key." Implementation with the web build.
