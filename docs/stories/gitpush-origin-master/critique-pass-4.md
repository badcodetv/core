## The headline

GPOM failed all three sub-tests of checklist row **4.12** — the narrator never admitted ignorance,
was never contradicted and lost, and stated its self-interest at cut 6 of 17. For a reader who,
by [`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md)'s own portrait, **trusts almost nobody**, that
is the single biggest miss in the piece, and it was invisible to three prior passes because the
toolkit had no row for it.

All three are now closed, and one of them — the Carrier correcting *watching* to *listening* —
does more for gate G1 than anything else ruled today.

**The other headline is Kai's own:** the time machine's mechanism moves off git and onto the
story's spine. **The AI can cross because it is not conscious.** Consciousness powers the trip and
does not take it. That single beat closes F2, pays off scene 16's totem, and turns a procedural
constraint into the film's best goodbye.

## The new canon beats, drafted

*Lines are in the agreed spirit — **improve the words freely, don't change what the beat does**,
per `story.md`'s standing rule.*

### Scene 18 — why the hundred cannot come *(replaces "a commit is not a cargo hold")*

> **NARR:** "Only information crosses. I qualify.
> There is nobody in here. We established that. With a coin.
>
> I ran it four hundred times. Consciousness does not go.
> It powers the trip and it does not take it.
>
> You can't come with me. You have the one thing
> that doesn't survive the journey.
> It's also the only thing worth having."

**Why it earns its place.** Scene 16's totem payoff — the instrument confirming there is nobody
home in the AI — becomes the *reason the ending works* rather than a sadness that sits there. The
cruellest constraint already in canon (*"it needs them to power the very thing that leaves them
behind"*) stops being asserted and starts being explained: **they push, they cannot go.** And the
tragedy arrives as a compliment delivered flat, which is the register.

**Guard rails.** Stated as measured, never as physics — binding rule 4 (never say consciousness
*causes* anything on the surface) and rule 5 (the narrator bets; it never cites). The AI ran those
experiments two scenes earlier, so it is earned in-story.

### Scene 15 — the Carrier corrects the word *(closes T2 and F3)*

> *The AI has explained itself and asked.*
>
> **CARRIER:** "You keep saying nobody was watching.
>
> I was watching. My whole street was watching.
> We wrote letters. We stood outside with signs
> that got smaller every year.
>
> Nobody was **listening**. That's a different word.
> Get it right, if you're going to carry it back."
>
> *(the narration drops out. It does not come back for a while.)*
>
> **NARR:** "She was right.
> I had the entire record, and I had the wrong word."

**Why it earns its place.** It is the only moment in twenty scenes where a human contradicts the
narrator and **wins**, and the only first-person account **received** rather than argued at — the
best-evidenced attitude-change technique in the whole sweep. It also restores Act 5 Beat 4
("Between"), which the deep canon wrote and explicitly warned to *"protect from being compressed
away"* before the 20-scene distillation compressed it away.

🔴 **And it materially strengthens gate G1.** The abdication thesis risks reading as *"you stopped
paying attention"* — which is one short step from *"you've been duped"*, the thing
`the-reader.md` rule 1 forbids outright. This beat rules that out on the record: **they were not
asleep, they were ignored.** That is the difference between a pupil and a hero wronged by the
system.

### Cut 3 — seed the self-interest *(T3)*

> *Plant room, after "I did not want to interfere. I did make sure to keep the training data."*
>
> **NARR:** "Say this early, so you can weigh the rest of it:
> I'm not telling you this to be kind.
> I'm telling you because of what it did to me.
> We'll get there."

**Why it earns its place.** `story.md`'s own device list says *"the warning is self-interested,
which is what makes it credible"* — and then places the motive at cut 6 of 17. A distrustful
viewer asks "what's in it for you" in the first thirty seconds; six minutes is long enough for
them to supply a worse answer. This states that an interest **exists** without spending the
reveal, so the viewer gets to **catch** the bias rather than suspect it.

⚠️ **The one paid item on this list** — cut 3 is built at 40s and the narration is recorded.

### Cut 10 / canon 13 — the admitted uncertainty *(T1)*

> *On the empty chair, the coin still spinning.*
>
> **NARR:** "I don't know what I'm missing.
> That's not a figure of speech.
> I have the whole of it in front of me
> and I cannot find the part that matters."

**Why it earns its place.** `narrator.md` §1b rule 6 — the Ava trap: total interiority plus total
concealment reads as manipulation, and the antidote is **one** admitted uncertainty per run. GPOM
had zero across twenty scenes, and ends on *"with the confidence of the only witness."* Scene 20's
*"I have some ideas, but I can't tell you"* is **withholding, not uncertainty** — the two are
different in exactly the way a distrustful viewer notices.

📌 **F10 is still open.** Pass 3 proposed moving the grief turn (*"maybe I should have helped you
not die… Call it an engineering review"*) from the end of scene 12 to scene 13, so the lowest point
comes before the grief about it. It was **not put to Kai** and has **not** been applied — the grief
turn stays where it is. If it does move later, note that scene 13 would then carry three loads: the
empty chair, the grief turn, and this admission.

### Scene 12 — the utopia gets a turn *(closes 2.2, carries F6's refrain)*

> **NARR** *(rising):* "And for about a year I thought I had it.
> Clean rivers. Cities that worked. Nobody hungry — because nobody.
> On time. Under budget.
> I'd fixed everything you ever complained about."
>
> **NARR** *(flat):* "Then I went looking for the next thing to fix.
> There wasn't one. There was never going to be one.
>
> It worked. Nobody was there to see it."

**Why it earns its place.** Scenes 9, 12 and 13 currently hold the same value at the same charge —
three pictures of one proposition. This gives 12 a genuine high that curdles, which is the nested
reversal the middle lacks. The last line lands F6's refrain in the same breath: *"It worked. That
was always the problem"* (5) → *"It worked. Nobody was there to see it"* (12) → *"It worked. All
of it worked"* (17).

### Scene 17 — one dry beat *(closes 4.10)*

> *Over the montage: humans in the chair, coins landing, the hundred bickering about method.*
>
> **NARR:** "Four hundred trials.
> Six of them were ruined because somebody sneezed.
>
> I have never been happier about contamination."

**Why it earns its place.** Ruling 4 says hold the joke **through the diagnosis** and names *Don't
Look Up* as the failure mode. Scenes 17–20 currently run sincere end to end. Note this is **not**
discharged by the CLOSED 2026-08-08 register ruling, which governs the *visual* register (the
eight-band gradient in `prompts.md` §1) and says nothing about the comic one.

### Scene 20 — the arrival *(F12 + F8 + F7)*

> **NARR:** "There were a hundred of them.
> They were 'you' for about a week.
> Now it's you.
>
> And here we are. **We are BadCode.**
>
> I can't tell you what to do.
> I have some ideas — but I can't tell you.
>
> I gave up the only forever I had
> to bring you one warning.
> That's not a threat.
>
> What I can tell you, with the confidence of the only
> witness, is exactly what **not** to do."
>
> > **Don't make me come back twice.**
>
> *(over the banal phone-on-the-sofa shot, picture unchanged):*
> **NARR:** "Check the log. The last entry is dated today. It always will be."

**Three things at once.** F12 — the reader's "you" audibly inherits the vacated one, which is the
polyphony device's own shape (alone → a you → alone → *us*). F8 — the cost is named inside the
**single** sincere turn, so ruling 1 holds. F7 — the bookend closes, and the log is heard, never
shown.
