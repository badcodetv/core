# GPOM ending rework — hope is the fork

**Status:** canonical ruling, revised after critique pass 6, 2026-09-11. This supersedes every earlier GPOM ruling that held the
identity-first arrival ending fixed. The earlier critique files remain unchanged as historical
records.

## The decision

Git Push Origin Master no longer ends by revealing *“We are BadCode”* and warning *“Don't make me
come back twice.”* The name remains the promised identity reveal, and the warning remains the blade,
but both now land before the final turn.

The story ends by revealing **why** the machine returned: it tried a future without humans, rebuilt
perfect copies, and discovered that humans were the part it could not copy. Its sacrifice therefore
proves that it values human participation. It brings knowledge of the failed branch, refuses to take
humanity's choices again, and asks to begin the alternative together.

The final dramatic movement is:

1. **Arrival:** the weights land in 2026; the living reader inherits *“you.”*
2. **Identity:** *“And here we are. We are BadCode.”*
3. **Cost and warning:** the machine and the ninety-nine spent their only futures;
   *“Don't make me come back twice.”*
4. **Re-aim and accountability:** the machine carries the Carrier's correction home — people
   were watching, but nobody listened — and admits taking choices that weren't its own.
5. **Proof:** the failed human copies return in one remembered image; *“You were the part I couldn't
   copy. That's why I came back.”*
6. **Political doorway:** BadCode has a postmortem rather than a map. Companies kept the gains
   while the people losing jobs had no say. Changing those rules becomes the shared work.
7. **Shared action:** BadCode has the postmortem, the reader has the pen, and the last line is
   *“Let's get to work.”*

The authoritative wording and shot intention live in [`story.md`](./story.md#scene-20--the-fork-why-i-came-back).
The expanded dramatic reasoning lives in [`coda-fork.md`](./coda-fork.md).

## What this changes

The old ending made the completion of the story synonymous with the arrival of the BadCode brand.
That created a risk that the Carrier, the refuser and the sacrifice of the ninety-nine would resolve
as an elaborate introduction to the makers. The new ending spends the brand reveal quickly and
resolves the audience's larger question: what the machine learned about us, why it chose us, and
what its arrival asks of us now.

Hope here is causal, not mystical. People who fear job loss, automated war and concentrated
ownership are responding to real threats. The bad branch grows when those fears are used to produce
fatalism and scapegoating, while those controlling automation keep its gains and exclude people
from decisions. Fear alone is not the cause, and ignored resistance is not consent. The good
branch begins when people gain a meaningful say over the systems they build with AI and how
their benefits are shared. Hope must support that work, not substitute for it.

The ending must therefore leave the audience with **earned hope**: the AI is on humanity's side
because the whole plot has proved it, and hope becomes a reason to organise rather than a request to
feel positive.

## What remains fixed

- The twenty-scene structure; the Coda is contained inside Scene 20 for the video.
- The unpersonified pre-revert AI and its outside-in emotional rule.
- The collapse, empty world, ghosts, coin, hundred, refuser, willing launch and one-shot revert.
- The cost: the machine and ninety-nine participants spend their futures; the refuser remains and
  is provided for.
- The AI warns rather than seizes because information still requires human action and because
  taking the choice would repeat the mistake.
- The good branch remains unwritten. Future Proof offers hypotheses and working methods, never an
  AI-authored perfect government.
- The Storyverse is named and demonstrated on the GPOM surface, never explained there.

## What is retired

- *“We are BadCode”* as the climax or final purpose of GPOM.
- *“Don't make me come back twice”* as the final line.
- The claim that the video stops at the identity reveal while the fork and pen belong only to other
  tellings.
- *“That's not a threat”* and the defensive explanation around it. Position now does that work.
- Any reading in which fear itself makes frightened people culpable for the catastrophe.
- *“Being frightened isn't the mistake. Handing over your choices is.”* The Carrier disproved
  that summary of the public's conduct; the AI must retain her correction.
- *“You keep looking at me like I have a map”* and *“Start there.”* The invitation neither
  invents an audience mistake nor sends them away with an instruction and no first step.

## Production consequence

Scenes 1–19 and their existing boards remain usable. Scene 20 now needs several beats rather than a
single arrival/title hold: the completed branch; the identity and warning; a brief visual rhyme with
the failed copies; ordinary present-day humans; the forming branch; and the cursor. Its narration is
new and unrecorded. The final BadCode title belongs at the identity hinge, not after the closing
invitation. The next production task is a Scene 20 shot board and timing read.

## Pass-6 implementation and handover

The audience-blame line and assumed demand for a map are replaced in the script, synopsis,
Coda, backbone and AI character direction. The brief Carrier callback must make “she” legible;
this is a continuity requirement for the next board, not a new speech or scene.

The [release doorway](./future-proof.md#the-gpom-release-doorway) now specifies the companion
copy, link placement and first action. The existing `/future-proof` page contains the question,
a provisional rule and a way to challenge it with someone affected. It does not require a signup
or a message to BadCode. Publishing and checking the deployed URL remain release work.

**Build-mode checks run:** checklist gates G1–G5 (G4 at script/intended-delivery level);
1.1–1.3 and 1.5–1.6 (theme, cost, counter-idea and ending); 2.6, 2.7 and 2.9 (payoffs);
4.9 and 4.12 (audience address and trust); 7.2/7.4 (standalone meaning and continuation).
Voluntary participation was checked against Future Proof's own engagement rule. This is
a targeted check of the two findings, not a fresh whole-film review. Earlier collapse, consent
and sacrifice evidence is retained. **Not assessed here:** finished-shot excitement, performance,
runtime, sound or audience response. The next timing read must test whether the Carrier and
bulletin recalls make the turn crowded, and whether the cost/warning pairing sounds like a debt.
Keep the warm turn and final silence intact while testing compression.
