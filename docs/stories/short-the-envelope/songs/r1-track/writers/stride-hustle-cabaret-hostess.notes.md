# stride-hustle-cabaret-hostess: what shaped it

- **The piano.** Harlem stride: left hand strikes a bass note on 1 and 3 and a chord on 2 and 4 (the oom-pah), right hand improvises fast syncopated figures; often very fast. That is the shell-game motion: the left hand never stops, the right hand darts. Named by genre and era only ("1930s Harlem stride piano, original composition"), no player or tune named, so nothing is borrowed from The Sting. Sources: https://jazzfuel.com/complete-guide-to-stride-piano/ , https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stride_jazz , https://www.kennedy-center.org/education/resources-for-educators/classroom-resources/media-and-interactives/artists/waller-fats/
- **Ragtime excluded on purpose, as briefed**, even though stride shares its oom-pah: the risk is that the exclude pulls the left hand away from the pattern too. If takes come back with a walking or swing bass instead of oom-pah, drop "ragtime" from the exclude first (unverified, a guess).
- **Spoken, not sung.** Community guides agree Suno tends to sing a line it is asked to speak; the levers are a [Spoken Word] section tag, "no singing" in the style box, and a short, uncrowded line. A [Break] before it is used as the vocal-out stop the brief asks for; tag behaviour in v6 is not a fixed grammar, so treat the stop as a hope. Sources: https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/suno-commands , https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-4-5-metatag-tutorial , https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-v6-vocal-prompts-2026/
- **Style box kept short**, genre and instrument first, delivery last (guides say early terms weigh most): https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-prompts-guide-2026/
- **Settings.** v6, 15 s (fixed). vocalGender female for the hostess. styleInfluence 65 to keep the stride from drifting into swing band; weirdness 40 and variety normal leave room for an odd wrong chord. Max Mode off (15 s piece, no long-form vocal consistency to protect).
- **Timing risk.** The line is ~20 words, about 6 s spoken unhurried; with 15 s total, the piano gets ~9 s, close to the brief's 10 s. If the voice gets cut off, the piano is running long.
