# radio-serial-organ-stings — what shaped it

- Old-time radio organists often worked with no sheet music, improvising chords from script notes like "danger", "sting", "ominous"; the organ was used to "punctuate, bridge, underscore" and change scenes. Effects split into action sounds and mood sounds, with turntables for big sounds and hand foley for small ones. So the style box asks for one held stab chord per scene change, cued like a studio organist, with hand foley (envelopes, bell, crate, curtain) in the gaps. Source: https://otrr.org/FILES/Articles/Jack_French_Articles/Old_Time_Radio_Sound_Effects.htm
- A sting described as an organist holding one long note under a line: https://ruyasonic.com/mus_score_1.htm (from search results). Theatre-organ stops (tibia, vox humana, tremulant) are named to get the Wurlitzer colour, not a church organ: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_organ
- Making Suno speak, not sing: speech-first words at the front of the style box, a voice-forward bed, "no chorus / no melodic lead" style exclusions, a [Spoken word] cue, short punctuated lines with no rhyme or repetition. Stage directions that get read aloud should move to the style box, so the bracket cues are kept to two or three words each. Source: https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-spoken-narration-workflow
- Suno is a music model, so expect some music to creep in and roll a few takes (same guide, plus https://lilys.ai/en/notes/suno-ai-20260130/suno-ai-sound-mode-pro, seen in search results only).
- Settings: v6, Duration 15 s as fixed; male voice; style influence 75 to hold the radio-play frame; weirdness 40 and variety normal to leave some room without breaking the frame. Piano is excluded on purpose so nothing drifts towards a rag.
