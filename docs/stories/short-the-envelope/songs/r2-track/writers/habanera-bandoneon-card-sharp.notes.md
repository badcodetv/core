# habanera-bandoneon-card-sharp: what shaped it

- **Habanera vs 1930s tango.** By the 1930s the orquestas típicas (D'Arienzo, Típica Victor) had dropped the dotted habanera accompaniment for straight marcato eighths; the habanera survived in the milonga and in pre-1920 tango. So the prompt asks for "tango-habanera" with the "dotted habanera bass" named outright, so v6 gets the limp rather than the four-square marcato. The 1930s comes from the room ("smoke-room parlour"), not the genre tag. Source: https://elvictrolerocastizo.blogspot.com/2017/07/tango-or-milonga.html
- **Instrument roles.** The bandoneon is the genre's idiomatic voice and the piano gives the left-hand bass under it, so the line goes to the bandoneon and the habanera to the piano, as a duet rather than a full orquesta (a full band would crowd 15 s). Source: https://fiveable.me/music-of-latin-america/unit-5/tango/study-guide/r3hawRz1CirwVJEF
- **Instrumental.** Guides say "no vocals" in the style box does not guarantee silence, so the style box ends with it, the exclude box names vocals and spoken word, and lyrics stay empty (the Instrumental switch should be on as well). Exclude is kept to three items because short lists work better, and "ragtime" is there because of the story's no-Joplin rule. Sources: https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/negative-prompting-suno-v5-guide, https://howtopromptsuno.com/style-lyrics/how-do-i-make-instrumental
- **Timing.** The switch stab is placed at 7 s (THE SWITCH card), followed by silence, and the last 4 s are kept sparse (one held note and a curtain swish) so there is room for the line, which is added outside Suno. "Unresolved" sets up the payoff. Duration is set to 15 s. The settings are v6 and normal variety, with weirdness 45 so the invented melody wanders a little without losing the period.
- **Unverified:** whether v6 places the stab at an exact second. Expect it to land roughly, not exactly, and trim in the edit.
