# Clean-room engine research: masked reporter, night protest, reaction cam

Researched 2026-11-09 from the open web only (no repo files read). Tags: **[official]** = Google docs or Google-relayed; **[tested]** = someone reports running it; **[opinion]** = guide/vendor advice, untested. Most of what exists on Omni Flash is third-party; Google's own prompt guide was only partly reachable. Anything marked *inference* is mine and unverified.

## 1. One reference image → consistent character in Nano Banana 2

- **Describe what changes, not who the character is.** Runware's NB2 guide puts it as "Describe what changes, not who. Let the reference carry the subject." Re-describing the face wastes words that should go on the scene, pose and light. [opinion, vendor doc] https://runware.ai/docs/models/google-nano-banana-2/guides/character-consistency.md (undated, 2026)
- **Too much description makes it less consistent.** A detailed description repeated in every prompt can lower consistency, because the model tries to match both the text and the image. [opinion] krea / framia NB2 guides, via search 2026-11-09
- **Name the details that must survive.** Use a short "keep … identical" list: for this job, the hood colour, the logo, the gloves and the mic. Naming details helps them survive busy scenes. [opinion] Runware, as above. *Inference:* the logo is the weak point. On-screen text and logos degrade in both engines (see §2), so name it as an object to keep and don't spell its text.
- **The engine guesses the angles it hasn't seen.** One front reference left the model to invent the back of a garment. Add a reference for every angle you will show. [tested, vendor example] Runware, as above. In a crowd shot the reporter will be seen side-on and from behind, so a side or back reference helps if one exists.
- **Clean references transfer best.** A sharp, unobstructed reference transfers identity better than a busy or low-resolution one. [opinion] Runware.
- **The reference copies its own background and pose.** No source documents a fix for NB2 specifically. The advice that recurs: give each image a named role ("the person from the reference", identity only), state the new pose and setting positively, and avoid negative phrasing ("do not add a background"), which tends to backfire. [opinion] Syntx guide https://docs.syntx.ai/en/🎨-Design-with-AI/🍌-Nano-Banana-Pro ; Leonardo NB guide https://www.leonardo.ai/news/nano-banana-prompt-guide . *Inference:* a cut-out or plain-background reference copies less of its original setting.
- **Several characters in one project.** Zilliz says NB2 holds "reasonable" consistency for one or two primary characters [opinion]. For long sequences, re-establish the reference at the start of each chapter [opinion]. Atlas Cloud scores Omni 3/5 on character consistency across 4+ shots and expects drift after shot 4 [tested, third-party], via https://geminiomniprompts.pages.dev/guide (updated 2026-05-29). I found nothing reliable on a "one image per project" limit in Flow.

## 2. Omni Flash image-to-video prompting (2026)

**What Google says** (Gemini API Omni docs, https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni.md.txt, read 2026-11-09):
- Give "scene description, camera movement, lighting and mood". For image-to-video, use high-resolution stills and **specific motion descriptions**; "make it move" is weak. [official]
- **The model may make several shots by default.** Ask for "a single unbroken / continuous shot" or "no scene cuts". [official] OpenArt confirms that cuts are the default (2026-11-06). [opinion] https://openart.ai/blog/gemini-omni-flash-prompt-guide/
- There is no negative-prompt field, so exclusions go inline as "Do not …". [official]
- Audio is generated by default. Describe the audio you want. [official]
- Time events with "After 3 seconds…" or ranges like `[0-5s]`. [official]
- 16:9 is the default; set 9:16 for portrait. Lengths run 3–15 s (default 10); first/last-frame interpolation runs 3–10 s. English prompts only are evaluated. [official]
- Uploading or editing images of "certain recognizable people is not supported". Safety filters run on the prompt and on the generated video, and vary by region. [official]
- Google's five tips (posted 2026-05-26, relayed by https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/3513/mastering-gemini-omni-flash-video-prompting-guide): lean on world knowledge instead of long description; use camera terms ("one continuous shot", "push in", "locked off"); edit iteratively; give each uploaded image a role ("uploads without a defined role produce the most disappointing results"). [official, relayed]

**Length.** One guide says keep it under ~50 words, because longer prompts dilute focus (it cites Seaart's analysis) [opinion] https://geminiomniprompts.pages.dev/guide . Veo-era guides say 3–5 sentences, about 100 words [opinion] https://ltx.studio/blog/veo-prompt-guide . Google's own line is "you don't have to be as prescriptive" [official, relayed by Runware]. Over-detailed prompts produce flat scenes and unintended changes [official for edits; opinion for generation]. *Practical read:* a shot brief of about 50–90 words. The still carries the look; the words carry motion, camera and sound.

**Handheld and phone camera.** Name the camera behaviour explicitly. Left unsaid, Veo-family models fall back to static or slightly drifting cameras [opinion] https://ltx.studio/blog/veo-prompt-guide . Wordings that come up: "handheld, micro-jitter and organic movement", "the small drift a real arm makes", "handheld tracking shot", "subtle natural movement, documentary feel" [opinion]. Name the cause and shape of the shake (running, footsteps, being bumped) rather than just "shaky" [opinion] https://hackernoon.com/stop-writing-camera-moves-into-your-ai-video-prompts-film-them-instead . Use motion verbs, not camera brands; Omni doesn't parse brand names [opinion].

**Crowds.**
- Evidence is thin and mixed. RuntimeWire's head-to-head praised Omni 1.1's overhead street crowd: "pedestrians moving independently and stable geometry" [tested, small set; article body not retrievable]. AI Tools SME's breakdance test found the crowd "moves too much in sync" [tested].
- invideo reports complex multi-object physics at about 50/50 across 30+ tests. It also says "Omni will not generate real contact-based actions or anything resembling violence": force applied to a person may be blocked or softened into people stopping short or passing through each other [tested, vendor] https://invideo.io/faq/why-does-gemini-omni-flash-get-physics-prompts-wrong-in/ (2026-08-10).
- Complex motion (dancing, gymnastics) artifacts more than walking or talking [tested, digit.in via the field guide].
- *Inference for crowd-surfing:* write it as a celebratory or carnival lift, "hands raising and passing him overhead, the man rides on raised palms", with the crowd's mood stated (jeering, laughing, chanting). Avoid words for force on a person. Let the start frame already show him aloft, so the clip only has to continue the motion, not invent the lift. Expect several takes.

**Morphing, frozen extras, the mask.**
- Background extras get the least generation effort: one-legged walks, impossible cycles, people melting into each other [opinion] https://www.overseeros.com/blog/why-ai-videos-look-fake .
- Hands and held objects drift; frame to hide hands where possible [tested, PixVerse hands-on via the field guide]. This bears directly on the gloved hand on the stick mic.
- Clips with a start frame hold identity better than text-only clips, but expect drift over the clip [opinion].
- *Inference:* fewer simultaneous actions per clip gives fewer morphs. One clear action for the reporter, one for the crowd.
- Masks: the only finding is that Omni's own Avatar feature rejects masked references [official, via the field guide]. That doesn't apply to image-to-video, but it suggests faces are not tracked as faces here. *Inference:* "the mask stays fixed and unchanged" as a keep-identical line is cheap insurance.

**Audio, ambience only.** State it positively ("realistic street sound: chanting crowd, whistles, drums, traffic, no music") plus "no dialogue / no speech". Google's own example prompt is "No music, just realistic real world sound" [official, deepmind.google/models/gemini-omni]. Unprompted audio is the model's choice, which can mean music [opinion, OpenArt]. A chanting crowd is speech-like. *Inference:* "indistinct chanting" or "drums and whistles" is less likely to produce intelligible words than "shouting slogans".

**What to avoid:** stacking several camera moves; asking for text or signage (gibberish, flicker); vague style words; many changes in one prompt; prompts in Spanish (unevaluated) [official/opinion as above].

## 3. Making it read as real phone or mockumentary footage

- The 12 AI tells (Opus, 2026-04-17) https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-slop-aesthetic-12-tells : morphing hands, background that shifts between cuts, **flat lighting that ignores the setting**, over-saturation, **motion that is too smooth with no micro-jitter**, mismatched reflections, gibberish text, **extras with unnatural walks**, repeated textures, breathless voices. [opinion]
- Background people that stay **too crisp** for the lens read as AI, as does uniform blur [opinion] overseeros as above. Cloned faces repeated in a crowd and blank stares are another tell [opinion, forum].
- Phone cues worth naming: handheld micro-jitter and footstep bob, **rolling-shutter wobble** on fast pans, **autofocus hunting or breathing**, sensor noise in the shadows, imperfect framing, a wide lens with mild distortion. Shake should be subtle; heavy shake reads as fake. Avoid cinematic grading, film grain and slow motion. [opinion] carat.im / magichour realistic-video guides; https://magichour.ai/blog/realistic-ai-video-prompting
- Night-specific *inference:* sodium-orange streetlight mixed with cold phone-torch white, blown highlights on phone lights, exposure pumping as a light swings past, and crushed noisy blacks. These are mixed, motivated sources, the opposite of the "flat lighting" tell.
- **Vertical:** generate natively at 9:16; the start still must also be 9:16, and only 16:9 and 9:16 are supported [official API / opinion Runware]. *Inference:* a mockumentary often shows vertical phone clips inside a 16:9 frame (pillarbox, blurred sides, or a phone-screen UI). That is an edit decision, and it lets each clip be "bad" phone footage while the film stays 16:9.
- Compression, a smartphone timestamp overlay and a recording-UI dot are best added in post. In-model text degrades. [inference from the text-rendering findings]

## 4. Policy risks

- Google's Veo filter categories include **Violence**, **Celebrity** ("photorealistic representation of a prominent person") and Child. If fewer videos come back than you asked for, some were blocked. [official] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/responsible-ai-and-usage-guidelines
- **Flow's "prominent people" false positives.** A Google forum thread (opened 2026-06-30, replies to 2026-11-07) reports Flow blocking original fictional characters, users' own avatars and Characters only a day old, with no workaround and no Google reply. Omni sometimes worked in the Gemini app when Flow refused. [tested, user reports] https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/critical-bug-google-flow-prominent-people-safety-filter-broken-systematically-blocking-all-fictional-character-generations/173121
- Policy false positives on harmless prompts were reported from 2026-05-19. The advice is to rephrase slightly or retry later, not to keep rewriting. Failed requests stopped counting against quota from 2026-05-28. [tested / official-relayed] field guide.
- Violence and contact: force on a person gets blocked or softened (invideo, above) [tested, vendor].
- *Safe-phrasing inferences* (unverified):
  - "Protest" plus "police" or "riot" is riskier than "a huge night street demonstration, crowd chanting".
  - Never name a real politician or party.
  - Keep the suited man generic: an anonymous official, no likeness.
  - Describe the crowd-surf as being lifted and carried by a jeering crowd, not attacked, dragged or thrown.
  - Leave out weapons, fire, blood and police.
  - The prompt and the output frames are both screened, so a still that already looks violent can fail even with a mild prompt.
- UK note: the API docs limit some *uploaded-video* editing and minor-related uploads in the UK/EEA. Image-to-video from your own still is not listed as restricted [official]; Flow-specific behaviour is unverified.

## 5. The reaction-cam shot

- Lighting matters more than the camera. Typical streamer looks are a **ring light or key light right behind the webcam**, giving flat, shadowless, catchlight-ring eyes, often with an **RGB LED strip** or monitor glow behind. [opinion] https://www.howtogeek.com/865651/dont-upgrade-your-webcam-upgrade-your-lighting-instead/ ; streamscheme / budgetloadout roundups
- Cheap-webcam artifacts: soft, compressed image ("webcam style, compressed and slightly soft" is a phrase that works in Omni [opinion, field guide]), white-balance shifts, flicker, noisy shadows, a slightly high wide-angle with the face close and a bit off-centre, cluttered background. [opinion]
- *Inference:* realism comes from the small physical things: leaning toward the mic, glancing off to a second monitor, the headset cable, the monitor's colour flicker on the face, a cheap chair creaking in the audio. Lock the camera (a webcam doesn't move) and let only the subject move. Locked shots also morph less than moving ones.
- Talking to camera is speech. If the voice is dubbed in post, prompt mouth movement without words, or "no audible speech, room tone only". *Inference:* Omni can still generate words if you mention talking, so expect to mute.
