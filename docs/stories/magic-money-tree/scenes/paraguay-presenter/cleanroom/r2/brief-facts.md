# Facts about the job (not style advice)

- **What it is for:** 10–15 second satirical meme videos about a bank collapse in Paraguay (Nov 2026). The presenter is BadCode's recurring masked host. Jack picks one design from this round; it becomes the reference for every later shot, animated by a video model.
- **"The money for something guy":** the host of a cheap comedy panel show: a dishevelled comic, crumpled black suit jacket, white shirt, a glass of red wine, slightly guilty "who, me?" energy. Comic, not menacing. Words 2 above: the deadpan roving street reporter with a big mic, everyone a fool, absurd. Those are two of many possible presenters, not the only ones.
- **"Make them look like presenters":** in round 1 most images read as a masked man posing, not as someone hosting a TV programme. Each image this round must read at a glance as a TV presenter doing his job.
- **"Every design very different":** in round 1 too many shared the same suit-and-wine look. This round every design is a different kind of presenter, in a different place, era, wardrobe, hood colour and material, light and camera.
- **Real people's names never go in a prompt** (no real presenters, channels or shows named). Describe the register in plain words.

# The mask (fixed: it is the brand logo). An image of it is attached to every prompt.

The attached reference (`mask-sheet.jpg`) is a strip of eight cropped heads, all wearing the same logo layout in different colours and materials (six knitted hoods: purple, green, red, navy, mustard, grey; one green-and-red tweed; one silver duct tape). What is the same in all eight, and must be the same in every new image:
- The hood covers the whole head. No eye, nose or mouth openings: one unbroken surface.
- A **thin** cream-white ring (a narrow band, roughly a finger wide, stitched or piped) traces the **outer edge of the face area**, like an oval picture frame running from brow to chin and cheek to cheek. It is big: almost the whole face is inside it. It is not a fat ring in the middle of the face.
- One **small, flat, solid red triangle** (felt or tape, rounded corners) sits **inside** the ring, **well clear of the ring**, where the wearer's LEFT eye would be. In the picture that is upper RIGHT of the face. The triangle points sideways or up. It is never on the ring, never on the edge, never centred.
- The inside of the ring is otherwise plain: just the hood's own material.
- Round 1 failures to beat: the ring came out fat and small in the middle of the face; the triangle sat ON the ring's edge; one take showed eye holes with eyes; one showed a bare hand holding a glass.

The prompt should tell the model to take only the mask's ring-and-triangle layout from the attached image and nothing else (not its colours, clothes, framing or background).

# No skin, ever

Gloves on both hands (full-finger), cuffs over the wrists, the hood's neck tucked into a collar or roll-neck. Nothing human shows, so it could be a person or a machine underneath, but no robot features (no metal, lights, visor). Gloves: plain, or a small ring-and-triangle patch on the back (Jack mentioned "gloves with the badcode logo on"), the writer's choice per design.

# Other facts

- Photoreal, not illustration. 16:9. Head and gloved hands clearly visible; head not tiny in frame (a video model animates it later).
- Screens, signs, graphics and papers: blank, plain colour, or illegible. No invented words.
- Other people in frame: invented ordinary members of the public or crew, never famous faces.
- The engine: Google Flow, Nano Banana 2, one prompt plus the one attached reference image. Prompts end with "Thanks." (a house habit, harmless).
