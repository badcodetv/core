## 0. The ten verdicts

1. **The war chapter is green.** Capra's *Why We Fight* — *The Battle of Britain* (1943), *Divide
   and Conquer* (1943, Dunkirk), *Prelude to War* (1942), *The Nazis Strike* (1943) — plus the
   actual **28 June 1919 Versailles signing** film all sit on trusted CC0/PD prefixes on archive.org.
   The two hardest-sounding asks (Versailles, the Nazi rallies) are solved.
2. **The British-made films are a minefield.** UK Crown-copyright *films* follow CDPA **s.13B**
   (70 years from the death of the last principal director / writer / composer), **not** the
   50-years-from-publication rule everyone assumes — so *Listen to Britain* may be in copyright to
   **2032** and *London Can Take It!* to **2057**. *Your Very Good Health* (1948, the NHS cartoon)
   and *New Town* (1948) are explicitly **"UK Crown Copyright"** on archive.org and CC-BY-NC at
   Wellcome. **The NHS launch and the council-housing beats are stills, not film.**
3. **Keynes's words are public domain** (UK from 1 Jan 2017, CDPA s.12 verified). **No public-domain
   recording of his voice or film of him speaking was found.** The best cleared face is the Crown
   photograph of the loan signing, 6 Dec 1945 (Commons `File:Keynes_1945.jpg`). The *How to Pay for
   the War* line is verbatim: *"A suitable proportion of each man's earnings must take the form of
   deferred pay."* And **"BBC, 2 April 1942" is The Listener's print date** — the broadcast date is
   unconfirmed.
4. **The ledger's spine number is unsettled at the source:** the OBR's own site says **249%** ("at
   the end of WWII… an all-time high") on one page and **"around 270%"** ("shortly after") on
   another. Say *around 250%* and cite the 300-years page. The two post-war sentences we lean on
   are **verified verbatim** (27 consecutive years / 206 points; debt +137% vs GDP +1,200%).
5. **The £65bn of September 2022 was a ceiling.** The Bank bought **~£19.3bn** before closing the
   operation on 14 Oct 2022. Say *stood up a £65bn facility in five days; used nineteen.*
6. **The May clip stands on CDPA s.30(1) fair dealing** (verified verbatim; no non-commercial gate
   in the text), with **s.178 sufficient acknowledgement** — caption it *"BBC Question Time, 2 June
   2017"*. Expect a BBC Studios Content ID claim regardless; dispute it. **gov.uk speech pages clear
   the words and one still — never the video.** Commons video for a monetised film is
   **unresolved** (parliamentlive.tv's own terms bar "financial gain").
7. **The phrase boomeranged.** Hansard (2013–25) shows "magic money tree" used overwhelmingly by
   backbenchers taunting governments — then by **Starmer as PM** (13 Nov 2024: *"The magic money
   tree is back"*) and **Reeves as Chancellor** (26 Mar 2025). Badenoch, Oct 2025: *"…all these men
   are shaking the same magic money tree."* Cross-party is proven; **the verb is *shake* in 1851,
   2017 and 2025.**
8. **Every pure-archive feature in the survey stayed small** (*Spirit of '45* $349k UK box office;
   *Finding the Money* <50 audience ratings). **Chapter the film; build the vertical door first.**
   Steal *The Big Short*'s cutaway, *Inside Job*'s play-the-quote-back, Oliver's *"and now, this"*;
   avoid Curtis's uncaptioned juxtaposition — four named critics call it the Kuleshov lie.
9. **The thesis survives the hostile economist only in its precise form** — and gets stronger:
   Keynes's pamphlet was *anti*-inflationary demand suppression; Bevan resigned in 1951 making a
   **real-capacity argument** against the cuts; markets discipline the *composition and credibility*
   of a package, not spending as such; **never say "MMT"** (the 0-of-38 IGM survey rejected a
   strawman — Andolfatto). **Name two decision-makers, not one: the Bank and the Treasury.**
10. **Never attack the 1940 myth — extend it three years.** Claassen & Devine (BJPS 2025): backlash
    to criticism of the nostalgic story is asymmetrically stronger on the right. The Beveridge
    Report's own numbers (95% awareness; 600k+ copies; queues at the Stationery Office) and **Hansard
    2 Feb 1943 — the War Office withdrew the soldiers' own pamphlet on it** — prove the war
    generation read the NHS as the war's reward. **And the ending has no picture:** no publishable
    photograph of the Bank's mulberries exists; the Bank's own words do (verified).

**The Crown-copyright correction (verified-live, legislation.gov.uk).** CDPA 1988 **s.13B** governs
films — 70 years from the end of the year of death of the last to die of the principal director,
screenplay author, dialogue author, and composer — and "contains no special provisions for works
created by the Crown". **s.163** (125 years from creation / 50 from commercial publication) is
expressly limited to "a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work" — films are carved out.
Schedule 1 para 41 keeps pre-1989 published Crown works on the 1956 Act term, but the 1995
harmonisation (S.I. 1995/3297) now controls. Applied (death dates secondary, Wikipedia): Jennings
d.1950, McAllister d.1962, Watt d.1987 → *Fires Were Started* possibly PD from end-2020;
*Listen to Britain* to end-2032; *London Can Take It!* to end-2057. ⚠️ "Principal director" is a term
of art; a media lawyer signs this off, not us. **This supersedes footage-sources.md's
"pre-June-1957 Crown material generally expired" line for films.**

## 2. Keynes — status, face, voice, words

- **Copyright:** UK life+70 (CDPA s.12, verified) → his published works PD from **1 Jan 2017**.
  US: *Economic Consequences of the Peace* PD (Gutenberg #15776); *Tract* PD from 2019 (Cornell
  95-year rule). CUP's *Collected Writings* carries an editorial copyright (RES 1980/2013 —
  secondary) over Moggridge's apparatus, **not over Keynes's sentences** — quote him, not the editors.
- **The 1942 talk:** *"How Much Does Finance Matter?"* — printed in *The Listener*, **2 April
  1942** (CW XXVII pp.264–70). **No source gives a distinct broadcast date; BBC Genome was
  unreachable.** ⚠️ Canon (`economics-of-money-creation.md` §1, `keynes.md`) says "BBC, 2 April
  1942" — treat as the print date until Genome is read.
- **Voice/film:** none public-domain confirmed. Leads: the CC0 1944 United News reel (Keynes
  unconfirmed); a commercial newsreel with his voice on gold (NHPR 2019 segment); a 23 May 1939 BBC
  talk *"Will Re-armament Cure Unemployment?"* (location/rights unknown). IWM: zero items. **Plan
  the interview device on text over stills — never a cloned voice.**
- **Face:** `File:Keynes_1945.jpg` (PD, Crown, TNA) is the clean one — and it *is* the loan beat.
  ~18 more Commons images unvetted per file. NPG's Bassano negatives: the NPG asserts
  "unauthorised reproduction prohibited" on an image Commons tags PD-anon-70; *THJ v Sheridan*
  [2023] EWCA Civ 1354 (secondary, three converging legal-press reports) holds a faithful
  reproduction of a PD 2D work earns no new copyright — but the Commons file also carries
  Not-PD-US-URAA, so don't use it for a US-facing upload anyway.

## 4. Modern clips and fair dealing (verified against legislation.gov.uk and gov.uk)

- **CDPA s.30(1)** (verbatim): *"Fair dealing with a work for the purpose of criticism or review,
  of that or another work or of a performance of a work, does not infringe any copyright in the
  work provided that it is accompanied by a sufficient acknowledgement… and provided that the work
  has been made available to the public."* **No non-commercial gate.** **s.30(1ZA)** (quotation,
  2014) overlaps. **s.30A** (parody) — contract terms cannot override it; that override is *not*
  written into s.30(1). **s.178**: sufficient acknowledgement = title/description + author → the
  caption *"BBC Question Time, 2 June 2017"*. **IPO fairness factors** (gov.uk, verified): market
  substitution; amount taken and whether it was necessary.
- **Content ID:** YouTube's own pages confirm fair dealing is a valid dispute ground; the video
  goes back up on dispute; the claimant has 30 days; escalation = takedown + strike. EFF: <1% of
  claims are disputed; >60% of disputes resolve for the uploader. **Budget the claim.**
- **gov.uk clears words, not pictures.** Both speech pages checked (Sunak 20 Mar 2020; Cameron 7
  Mar 2013) are **text + one still** under OGL v3 — no video. OGL's "Information" definition is
  silent on video. Pool ownership of the 2020 briefings: unknown. No.10's YouTube per-video
  licence: unchecked.
- **Parliament:** OPL v3 permits commercial use of *information*; **parliamentlive.tv's downloading
  terms** reportedly bar use "for advertising, promotion, commercial sponsorship, or any other form
  of publicity for commercial purposes or financial gain" ⚠️(snippet; page 403). **Statute (s.30)
  vs contract: unresolved — a media lawyer's call.**

## 8. The reader — what the evidence says

- **Claassen & Devine, *BJPS* 2025** (abstract verified): imperial nostalgia predicts party
  evaluation and vote intention "with effects comparable to those of immigration attitudes"; and

  **contradicting the story has a measured cost; never attack the 1940 myth.**

  sense that things have changed for the worse and drives action to "protect the national ingroup
  and limit the presence of immigrant outgroups." → **the lever we pull seeks an immigration outlet
  if unaimed** — every nostalgia beat lands on a named economic decision-maker in the same breath.
