## Points the director needs to know
1. The Covid inquiry headline (14 July 2026) carries a trap. The BBC body says Baroness Hallett described the VIP lane as a misguided policy but found "no evidence of cronyism or corruption" by ministers or officials when awarding the final contracts. The headline itself is safe (waste, not corruption). Do not pair it with a voiceover saying corruption.
2. The January 2022 VIP-lane ruling found the lane UNLAWFUL because of unequal treatment, and per BBC the judge said both offers were likely to have got contracts anyway. It concerned PestFix and Ayanda Capital, not PPE Medpro. It does not say Medpro's contract was unlawful. Do not let narration imply it.
3. The October 2025 judgment was a BREACH OF CONTRACT finding (gowns not shown to be sterile), not a fraud finding. Barrowman's spokesman called it a "whitewash" and Mone said it needed a "quantum leap of faith" (BBC live page). Headlines that say "must pay back £122m" are accurate; anything saying "fraud" or "stole" is not supported by these pages.
4. "Tory peer" in the ITV, LBC and BBC-Dec-2023 headlines is now stale. The Guardian Dec 2025 headline says "ex-Tory peer". Mone announced she has "no wish" to remain a Conservative peer (Guardian, Oct 2025). She is still a member of the Lords per those reports. Avoid narration that repeats "Tory peer" as current.
5. £122m versus £148m: the BBC uses £148m from Nov 2025 on (judgment plus interest and costs). Pick one and say which.
6. The June 2026 liquidators' case is a live claim, not a finding. Barrowman and Mone "have been approached for comment" (BBC). Show with attribution on screen ("liquidators allege / are suing") and say nothing beyond it.
7. Where they said it herself: the 17 Dec 2023 BBC headline is the cleanest first-person admission. The 10 Dec 2023 BBC piece is her statement of regret.
8. Photos: 15 of 18 captured pages show Mone or Barrowman in the top of the page. The director does not want her photo, so use the `--headline-only.png` files. I visually checked those for BBC, ITV, Law Gazette, Boat International and the TheBusinessDesk one. Details: the BBC live page and TheBusinessDesk headline-only crops have no masthead; TheBusinessDesk has a grey box where the photo was (I hid the image with a style change in my own tab before shooting; text untouched). The BBC crops are of the live page with images hidden by the same method in my tab, text and layout untouched. Say so if provenance is asked.

## The 4 to put on screen first, and why
1. BBC, 1 Oct 2025, "Baroness Mone-linked company ordered to pay £122m over PPE contract" (2025-10-01--bbc--high-court-mone-linked-company-breached-contract--headline-only.png). A court finding, the number carries the scene, BBC masthead is the most trusted furniture. (Runner-up with the ITV wording "must pay back £122 million for faulty Covid PPE", which also shows the date in the crop.)
2. BBC, 17 Dec 2023, "Michelle Mone admits she stands to benefit from £60m PPE profit". Her own admission, no interpretation needed, and it is the "money was found" beat in her own words.
3. BBC, 12 Jan 2022, "Covid: Government's PPE 'VIP lane' unlawful, court rules". Court finding on the priority lane itself. Pair with the caveat in point 2 above. Law Gazette version is a fine alternative with a legal-trade masthead and no person in the crop.
4. BBC, 14 Jul 2026, "UK wasted £10bn on PPE that left NHS staff poorly protected, Covid inquiry finds". Official inquiry, widest frame (the whole system, not one woman), newest. Do not caption it as corruption (point 1).
Option for a fifth beat: BBC 19 Dec 2025 "Michelle Mone-linked PPE firm liquidated and unlikely to repay £148m", for "and the money did not come back".

## Would NOT show
- LBC, "Tory peer Michelle Mone launches £80m fire sale of luxury villa, superyacht and private jet amid PPE scandal". The body says her bank accounts were frozen following a two-year National Crime Agency investigation "into allegations of fraud and bribery", and the headline's "fire sale" is LBC's framing; the collage image includes a swimsuit photo. High defamation and taste risk, weak evidence.
- Boat International yacht piece. Accurate, but the headline does not name her (the body says "last known owners"), so on its own it is a picture of a boat with no story.
- Good Law Project. It was the claimant in the 2022 case and the copy is campaigning ("the Tories' unlawful VIP lane"). Use the BBC/ITV court reports instead.
- TheBusinessDesk (25 Jun 2026) and BBC (24 Jun 2026) liquidator-claim headlines, unless attributed on screen. They are unproven claims in live litigation. If used, add the words "liquidators are suing" as an on-screen caption.
- BBC 1 Feb 2022 "Government writes off £8.7bn of pandemic PPE". Real and useful, but it never mentions the priority lane or Mone, so it makes a different scene.
- BBC 5 Nov 2025 "owes £39m in taxes": true (HMRC debt per administrators' filings) but a tax-debt line invites the viewer to think of wrongdoing; not needed.
