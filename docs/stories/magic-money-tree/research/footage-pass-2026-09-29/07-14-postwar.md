# British post-war footage sweep (scenes 07, 09, 10, 10b, 12, 14)

## 1. Verdict
Yes. Free British post-war FILM exists, and route 1 paid off. Ministry and Central Office of Information films are on archive.org and Commons as expired Crown copyright, and the picture is usable (see 2). It is cartoons and actuality, not live footage of 1948 hospitals or council estates. Route 2 gave one clean US-federal VE Day London clip. Routes 3 and 4 gave nothing new for film. Nothing found gives free live 1948 NHS wards, live post-war house-building, or moving 1945 election footage.

## 2. Crown copyright finding (quoted)
- National Archives (nationalarchives.gov.uk/terms-and-conditions/copyright/copyright-and-related-rights/): "Films made before 1 June 1957 were not protected as films but as sequences of photographs. Fiction films made before this date are also protected as dramatic works." Duration is "the same as for the photographs comprising each frame, and in appropriate circumstances is also equivalent to dramatic works of that period." Also: "The standard duration of Crown copyright is for 50 years after the year of publication or for 125 years after creation."
- CDPA 1988 Sch.1 para 7(1) (legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/schedule/1): "No copyright subsists in a film, as such, made before 1st June 1957." Para 7(3) treats photographs within such films like ordinary photographs.
- Commons Template:PD-UKGov covers photographs before 1 June 1957, works published before 1976, and artistic works before 1976. It says nothing about films.
- Reading: the picture of a 1945-56 Crown film is out of copyright (old Crown photographs and drawings, 50 years on). Separately authored elements (commissioned music, scripts, commentary) can survive: E.M. Forster's commentary on A Diary for Timothy runs to 2040. Use picture only, never the soundtrack. The film-level PD-UKGov tag on Commons is a volunteer inference citing a National Archives film page that now 404s (nationalarchives.gov.uk/films/1945to1951/filmpage_cyvgh.htm). Ownership of private-studio work (Halas & Batchelor) rests on archive.org's rights field "UK Crown Copyright", set by IA staff in 2005.
