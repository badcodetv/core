# Research: "There isn't a magic money tree" — Theresa May, Question Time 2017

> Deep-research brief, 2026-08-05 session (story re-founding). Facts triangulated from
> secondary sources; primary BBC transcript unobtainable. ⚠️ flags = verify before print.

## 1. The exchange

**Nurse:** **Victoria Davey**, a nurse of 26 years at the time. Identity corroborated by RCNi (*Nursing Standard*), Gulf News/AP, and wordhistories.net. ⚠️ Employer, specialty, location, pronouns NOT verified — name rests on search-index snippets plus secondary citations.

**Her question, as reported:**
> "As a nurse for 26 years, do the Tories expect our support in light of another 1% pay increase?"

and, following up:
> "My wage slips from 2009 reflect what I'm earning today so how can that be fair, in light of the job that we actually do?"

**A second nurse in the audience** (name not established) interjected:
> "We've had a real terms decrease of 14% since 2010 so don't tell us we're getting an increase."

**May's reply — the canonical wording** (HuffPost UK, Channel 4 FactCheck, wordhistories.net, Positive Money agree):
> "I'm being honest with you, in terms of saying that we will put more money into the NHS, but there isn't a magic money tree that we can shake that suddenly provides for everything that people want."

Variants: Gulf News/AP: *"…that we can shake to get everything we want."* RCNi: *"that **you** can shake"*. She also said: *"I recognise the job that you do, but we've had to take some hard choices across the public sector in relation to pay restraint."*

The clip-length version that entered the culture — **"there is no magic money tree"** — is a paraphrase; she never said exactly those six words in that exchange.

Note: Davey's device is not a statistic — her **2009 payslip matched her 2017 payslip in cash terms**. A nominal-freeze observation, stronger than any percentage.

## 2. Date, programme, venue

- *Question Time* Leaders Special, BBC One, chaired by **David Dimbleby**.
- **Friday 2 June 2017**, 20:30, 90 minutes.
- **Ron Cooke Hub, University of York** (confirmed from the University of York's own news page — the one primary source read in full).
- May and Corbyn appeared **separately, not head-to-head**. Invitation-only audience.
- Context: snap election called 18 April 2017; polling day 8 June — six days after broadcast, the last major set-piece TV appearance of the campaign. Manchester Arena bombing 22 May; London Bridge attack 3 June, the following day.
- May had **skipped** the seven-way BBC Election Debate on 31 May, sending Amber Rudd.

## 3. Reaction and the phrase's afterlife

**Immediate framing.** *The Independent*, 3 June 2017: "Theresa May prompts anger after telling nurse who hasn't had pay rise for eight years: 'There's no magic money tree.'" Widely read as condescending: a PM explaining basic economics to a 26-year NHS veteran.

**The DUP deal — 24 days later.** 26 June 2017: confidence-and-supply agreement with the DUP — **£1bn extra for Northern Ireland over two years** (£910m/2yr + £90m/3yr, per Channel 4 FactCheck) plus flexibilities on £500m already committed.
- **Angela Rayner:** the *"magic money tree well and truly shaken for this agreement."*
- **Ian Blackford:** *"For years, the Tories have been cutting budgets and services, but suddenly they have found a magic money tree to help them stay in power."*
- **Damian Green** (defending): "We can afford this because we have a strong economy."
- **Nick Macpherson** (ex-Treasury Perm Sec): "just a downpayment"; the DUP "will be back for more … again and again."

**2018 sequel.** 17 June 2018: May promises the NHS £20bn/yr by 2023 funded partly by a "Brexit dividend." Sarah Wollaston (Con, Health Committee chair): "tosh." IFS's Paul Johnson: "there is no Brexit dividend." *Spectator* headline: **"The 'Brexit dividend' for the NHS is Theresa May's new Magic Money Tree."**

**COVID afterlife, 2020–21:**
- BoE QE: gilt holdings **£425bn → £875bn between March and June/November 2020** — **£450bn created in roughly three months of announcements** (£200bn Mar, £100bn Jun, £150bn Nov). Programme total **£895bn**. The entire 2009–2017 programme was ~£445–456bn — 2020 roughly doubled a decade of money creation.
- Deficit ~**£355bn** FY to March 2021 (~17% of GDP) vs ~£60bn the year before.
- Total COVID measures: NAO Sept 2021 — **£261bn spent across 374 measures**, lifetime ~£370bn; HMT later tracker **£384.66bn**.
- **March 2021: the 1% pay offer to NHS staff.** RCN: "pitiful", demanded 12.5%; Dame Donna Kinnair: government "dangerously out of touch." Johnson: 1% was "as much" as possible in "tough times." The 2017 clip recirculated heavily.
- Commentary framing (Positive Money, REDD-Monitor): the tree was always there — it just only ever grew in one part of the garden; QE flowed to financial institutions and asset markets, not wages.

## 4. Origin of the phrase

**Not May's line, not new in 2017.**

- **David Cameron, 7 March 2013**, economy speech (GOV.UK says Yorkshire; ⚠️ some accounts say Keynsham near Bristol — unresolved). Verbatim from GOV.UK:
  > "It's as if they think there is some magic money tree, and let me tell you a plain truth: there isn't."
  Immediate rebuttals: ConservativeHome, "David Cameron is wrong – there is a magic money tree, but it only grows in the gardens of the rich" (Mar 2013); Positive Money, "Our Magic Money Tree" (same month).
- **Amber Rudd, 31 May 2017** — two days before May, standing in for her at the BBC Election Debate, used it repeatedly against Corbyn: "…we have to stop thinking — as you do — that there is a magic money tree." **It was the campaign's scripted attack line before May aimed it at a nurse.**
- **Deeper ancestry:** "money tree" in *The London Magazine*, **1749**; "shake the money-tree" in the *Royal Cornwall Gazette*, **14 February 1851** (farmers' dinner at Probus: "they had not been able… to shake the money-tree and bring down a shower of sovereigns"). Ideological ancestor: **Thatcher, October 1983 conference**: "the state has no source of money, other than the money people earn themselves."
- **International echo: Emmanuel Macron, 5 April 2018**, CHU Rouen, to a nurse challenging him over closures: **"Y a pas d'argent magique."** Near-identical scene, ten months later.

## 5. Nurses' real-terms pay, 2010–2017

Policy timeline (Commons Library CBP-8037): Budget 2010 two-year freeze (2011–13, except ≤£21k); from 2013 average awards of **1%**; Summer Budget 2015 extended the 1% cap to 2019/20; cap scrapped in the **Autumn Budget 2017** — roughly three months after the exchange.

| Source | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| RCN | **14%** real-terms cut 2010–2017 | Campaign figure (RPI-type deflator); the number the second nurse quoted |
| Health Foundation | ~**9%** below 2010 by 2017 (−1.2%/yr) | CPI; vs −0.6%/yr whole economy |
| Nuffield Trust | **3%** below 2010/11 (starting salary, by 2021/22) | CPI, starting salary only |
| New Statesman | 9.2% less in real terms in 2017 | |

**The RCN's 14% is the highest credible figure and a campaigning number; ~9% (CPI) is the unimpeachable one.** All agree on direction and mechanism: the seven-year cap.
