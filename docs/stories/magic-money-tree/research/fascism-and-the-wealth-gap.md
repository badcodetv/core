## 1. Keynes, *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919) — ALL VERIFIED

Macmillan, December 1919 (US: Harcourt, Brace and Howe, 1920). Preface signed King's
College, Cambridge, November 1919. Text: Project Gutenberg #15776.

**Resignation (Preface, verbatim):** official Treasury representative at the Paris
Peace Conference to 7 June 1919; deputy for the Chancellor on the Supreme Economic
Council; "He resigned from these positions when it became evident that hope could no
longer be entertained of substantial modification in the draft Terms of Peace."

**THE PROPHECY (Ch. VII, verbatim):**
> "If we aim deliberately at the impoverishment of Central Europe, **vengeance, I dare
> predict, will not limp.** Nothing can then delay for very long that final civil war
> between the forces of Reaction and the despairing convulsions of Revolution, before
> which the horrors of the late German war will fade into nothing, and which will
> destroy, whoever is victor, the civilization and the progress of our generation."

**Second prophecy (Ch. VI, verbatim):**
> "**Men will not always die quietly.** For starvation, which brings to some lethargy
> and a helpless despair, drives other temperaments to the nervous instability of
> hysteria and to a mad despair."

**The currency passage (Ch. VI, verbatim) — written four years BEFORE the hyperinflation:**
> "By a continuing process of inflation, governments can confiscate, secretly and
> unobserved, an important part of the wealth of their citizens… **There is no subtler,
> no surer means of overturning the existing basis of society than to debauch the
> currency.** The process engages all the hidden forces of economic law on the side of
> destruction, and does it in a manner which not one man in a million is able to
> diagnose."

⚠️ Keynes writes "Lenin *is said to have* declared" — the Lenin attribution is
apocryphal. Never put those words in Lenin's mouth; "There is no subtler, no surer
means…" is Keynes in his own voice and safe.

Also verified: "Carthaginian Peace" (Ch. III).

## 2. The Weimar double burn

### (a) Hyperinflation, 1923 — VERIFIED
- Peak, November 1923: **$1 = 4,210,500,000,000 marks** (4.2 trillion). 1914: 4.2.
- Rentenmark introduced **16 November 1923** ("Twelve zeros were cut from prices");
  post-stabilisation $1 = 4.2 Rentenmark — exactly the 1914 rate.
- Savings/debts wiped; 1925 revaluation decrees reinstated some mortgages at 25% of
  face value.
- **Beer Hall Putsch: 8–9 November 1923** — the Nazis' first armed grab happened in
  the same fortnight as the currency peak, days before the Rentenmark. ⚠️ The timing
  is factual; any causal reading is ours — make it by juxtaposition, never as proof.

### (b) The votes came from the deflation — VERIFIED (the load-bearing pair)
- **20 May 1928: NSDAP 2.63%** (810,127 votes, 12 of 491 seats).
- **31 July 1932: NSDAP 37.27%** (13.7m votes, 230 seats, largest party).
- Context: unemployment 8.5% → ~30% (1929–32); **6m+ unemployed 1932**; industrial
  production −42%.

**Five years after the hyperinflation the Nazis were a 2.6% fringe. The catastrophe
came after the deflation, not after the inflation.**

### (c) Galofré-Vilà, Meissner, McKee & Stuckler, "Austerity and the Rise of the Nazi Party" — VERIFIED
*Journal of Economic History* 81(1), 2021, 81–113; NBER WP 24106 (PDF read in full).
- Voting data, ~1,000 districts + 100 cities, four elections 1930–33: **areas more
  affected by austerity (spending cuts and tax rises) had relatively higher Nazi vote
  shares**; robust to IV and border-pair designs.
- Effect size: each 1 SD of austerity ≈ **¼ to ½ SD of Nazi vote share**.
- The austerity: 1930–32 Reich real expenditure −14% (−30% nominal); state −8%
  (−25% nominal); state+Reich ≈ 30% of GDP. Brüning ruled by **emergency decree
  under Article 48**, "the Reichstag eventually consenting without formal debate."
- The swing: **6m votes (Sept 1930) → 14m (July 1932)** after austerity launched.
- Mechanism: where public health spending fell more, mortality rose more, and Nazi
  support rose more.
- **★ Hitler's own pamphlet**, twelve days after Brüning's last decree (*The Great
  Illusion of the Last Emergency Decree*), verbatim: "**Although that was not the
  intention, this emergency decree will help my party to victory, and therefore put
  an end to the illusions of the present system.**"
- Authors' own caveat (reproduce this restraint): austerity is *one* factor — also
  business backing, antisemitism's deep roots, social capital, the 1931 banking
  collapse, radio propaganda.

### Verdict on "Germany burned twice" — SUSTAINABLE, with one edit
Both burns are fact; the econometrics support burn #2. ⚠️ UNVERIFIED: the popular
bridge that *memory of 1923* is why Germany refused to reflate in 1930 — the
documented driver of Brüning's deflation was the gold-standard/reparations
constraint. Assert the two burns as fact; treat the memory link as interpretation or
cut it. **Approved formulation:** *"Germany burned twice — once by printing without
limit, once by refusing to print at all. The first burn took their savings. The
second took their republic."*

## 3. Financial crises → far-right votes — VERIFIED with two corrections

Funke, Schularick & Trebesch, "Going to Extremes: Politics after Financial Crises,
1870–2014," *European Economic Review* 88 (2016), 227–260. 20 advanced economies,
800+ elections, 140 years. Abstract verbatim: "**On average, far-right parties
increase their vote share by 30% after a financial crisis.**" And: "we do not observe
similar political dynamics in normal recessions or after severe macroeconomic shocks
that are not financial in nature."

- ⚠️ **CORRECTION 1: the 30% is RELATIVE** — a party on 10% goes to ~13%, not 40%.
  Write "by about a third of what they had."
- ⚠️ **CORRECTION 2: the "following 5 years" window is UNVERIFIED** (not in the
  abstract; full text unobtainable this session). Omit the window or verify first.
- The sharpest verified point: **it isn't hardship as such — it's hardship after a
  finance blow-up, where there is a visible culprit who was bailed out.**
- ⚠️ Critiques of the paper not researched.

## 4. The postwar settlement as anti-fascism design — MIXED (the mix matters)

**STRONGEST — FDR, State of the Union, 11 January 1944 (verbatim, verified):**
> "We have come to a clear realization of the fact that true individual freedom
> cannot exist without economic security and independence. 'Necessitous men are not
> free men.' **People who are hungry and out of a job are the stuff of which
> dictatorships are made.**"
Followed by the "Second Bill of Rights" (job, food, home, medical care, education…).
**This is the quote for the story** — the explicit wartime statement that economic
insecurity manufactures dictatorship.

**ILO Declaration of Philadelphia, 10 May 1944 (verbatim, verified):** "Labour is not
a commodity"; "**Poverty anywhere constitutes a danger to prosperity everywhere.**"

**Atlantic Charter, 14 August 1941:** points 5–6 — global social welfare; "a world
free of want and fear."

**UK material (verified but war-aims-framed, not explicitly anti-fascism):**
- Beveridge Report, Cmd. 6404, Nov 1942: the five giants ("Want… Disease, Ignorance,
  Squalor and Idleness"); "**A revolutionary moment in the world's history is a time
  for revolutions, not for patching.**" 95% public awareness within a fortnight.
  Nazi propaganda attacked the Report — quoted in the Commons debate (nice detail).
- Beveridge, *Full Employment in a Free Society* (1944): full employment = ≤3%
  unemployment; maintaining demand "must be undertaken by the State."
- ⚠️ **NEGATIVE FINDING:** Hansard for the Beveridge debate (16 Feb 1943) and the
  Employment White Paper debate (21 Jun 1944) contains **no MP explicitly saying
  welfare/full employment prevents fascism**. The British framing was war aims
  ("cheated" if not implemented; the Atlantic Charter pledge). **Do not invent a
  British quote making the link — use FDR and the ILO for the explicit version.**
- ⚠️ The 1944 White Paper's famous opening commitment wording: UNVERIFIED — check
  Cmd. 6527 before quoting.

## 5. The modern rhyme — guardrails confirmed

- **UK CPI peaked 11.1% in October 2022** (ONS series D7G7/MM23, primary source).
  Weimar at peak was ~29,500% *per month*. Not the same phenomenon — the UK parallel
  is to the **austerity burn, not the hyperinflation** (also the more honest and
  more interesting parallel). US peak 9.1% (Jun 2022); eurozone 10.7%; Germany 11.7%.
- **Far-right results (verified):** AfD **20.8%, second place** (Feb 2025 federal,
  doubled from 2021); RN **33.21% first round 2024** (142 seats); Reform UK 14.3%/5
  seats (2024), **first on ~30% projected national share in May 2025 locals**, 8 MPs
  by Jan 2026, polling low-to-mid 20s in Aug 2026 — ⚠️ **a tight three-way race:
  "leading in some polls" is defensible; "surged ahead" is not.**
- **Fetzer, "Did Austerity Cause Brexit?"** — *AER* 109(11), 2019: significant
  association between exposure to post-2010 welfare reforms and UKIP/Leave support;
  the Remain-counterfactual is **his inference — attribute it, don't state as fact.**
- ⚠️ **INEQUALITY — where the story could most easily get it wrong:**
  - **US: the claim holds.** Top-1% income share back at Roaring-Twenties levels
    (~19–20.7% vs the 10.9% trough); top 1% own ~half the stock market, top 10% own
    87% of it, bottom half 1.1% (Fed 2025).
  - **UK: the claim does NOT hold on income.** ONS FYE 2024: Gini 32.9% (falling);
    top-1% income share 6.6% — *lowest since 2002* on that survey measure. **Never
    say UK income inequality is at a century high.** The honest UK framing: **the
    1980s dismantled the postwar compression and it was never restored — "we already
    slid, and then we stopped noticing."** The stronger UK card is **wealth**: top
    10% own **48.6% of all wealth** (ONS WAS 2018–20).
  - ⚠️ Tax-record-based UK top-1% income estimates (~13–15%) are higher than the
    survey figure but UNVERIFIED this session — check WID/HMRC before printing.

## 6. Veteran voices + archive footage

### Harry Leslie Smith — VERIFIED, and near-unique
Born 25 Feb 1923, Barnsley; died 28 Nov 2018. **RAF 1941–48**, occupation duty in
Hamburg. Books incl. **Harry's Last Stand** (2014) and **Don't Let My Past Be Your
Future** (2017). Addressed the **Labour conference, Sept 2014**, on the NHS —
verified line: before the NHS, preventable diseases "snuffed out life like a cold
breath on a warm candle flame."
⚠️ "I don't want my past to be your future" is verified as the **2017 book title**,
not as a verbatim 2014 spoken line — verify against video before quoting as speech.
Findable footage (YouTube IDs listed in the agent transcript; conference footage
rights sit with the Labour Party — their upload is the place to seek permission).

### The honest gap — build on one voice, not a chorus
⚠️ **No documented body of WWII veterans saying "this is not what we fought for"
about modern Britain was found. Smith is the outstanding, possibly near-unique,
on-record example.** "The last of them said this" is true and moving; "they all say
this" is not established. **Harry Billinge** (D-Day sapper, MBE, d. 2022) is a
remember-the-dead voice — "All the heroes are dead" — **not** a political voice;
do not recruit him into an argument he never made. Best untapped source: the IWM
Sound Archive (thousands of veteran oral histories).
