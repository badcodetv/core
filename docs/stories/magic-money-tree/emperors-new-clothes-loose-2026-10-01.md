## The whole thing in one breath

An emperor runs out of money, finds a tree that prints it, promises to pay it back, spends the lot,
and is told the answer is to borrow more. He does that forever. Biggie Smalls tells him the one rule
he broke. The clock hits 40 trillion.

### 1. "There is no magic money tree" — *Proposal*

- **Happens:** somebody important says the line, flat, to someone who asked for something small.
- **We see:** a podium or a throne. Possibly the real clip, possibly the emperor's own herald.
- **The laugh:** the narrator, from the future: there is. I have seen it. Let me show you.
- **To decide:** real politician clip, or keep the whole film inside the fairy tale? The song
  already opens and closes on this line.

### 2. The emperor is skint — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the emperor has run out of money.
- **We see:** an empty treasury, a very grand man, a very small pile.
- **The laugh:** how he ran out. The sillier and more recognisable the better.
- **To decide:** who is the emperor? A fairy-tale king, a modern head of state in a crown, or a
  central banker? One man or a type?

### 3. The tree and its one condition — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** he finds the magic money tree. It will print him money, **on condition he pays it
  back.**
- **We see:** the tree. He shakes it. Money falls.
- **The laugh:** the tree is the only sensible character in the film and it knows exactly who it is
  dealing with.
- **To decide:** does the tree talk? What does it sound like? Is it on his side?

### 4. He spends the lot — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** he spends all of it. Again.
- **We see:** a spending montage.
- **The laugh:** what it goes on.
- **To decide:** **what does he spend it on, and who ends up holding it?** This is the scene where
  the film says who benefits. If the money just vanishes, the story has no villain.

### 5. The bill, and the tree's advice — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the debt falls due. He cannot pay. The tree tells him he can simply borrow more to
  pay off the last lot.
- **We see:** the emperor's face as he realises this is allowed.
- **The laugh:** this is the punchline of the whole film. It should land as the best idea he has
  ever heard.
- **To decide:** is the tree being helpful, sarcastic, or tired?

### 6. The loop — *Proposal*

- **Happens:** shake, spend, owe, shake. Faster each time.
- **We see:** the same three shots, getting quicker and bigger. A tally somewhere in frame, climbing.
- **The laugh:** repetition and speed. No explanation.
- **To decide:** how many times round before it stops being funny? Does anything change each lap,
  or is the joke that nothing does?

### 7. Who is standing under the tree — *Proposal*

- **Happens:** someone ordinary asks for something small and is told there is no money. Behind them,
  the emperor is shaking the tree.
- **We see:** both in one frame.
- **The laugh:** nobody in the scene notices.
- **To decide:** is this scene in the film at all? It is the bridge to the 28 September point ("we
  can't afford it" is a choice) and it is where a nurse, or Bob, could come back. Without it the
  ending can read as "debt is the problem" and nothing else. With it, the film gets longer and
  closer to the cut you both disliked.

### 8. Biggie — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** Biggie Smalls states the rule: don't get high off your own supply.
- **We see:** to decide.
- **The laugh:** the one piece of sound financial advice in the film comes from a crack-dealing
  manual.
- **To decide:** the real recording (a clearance question), the line on a card, or the line spoken
  by the narrator or the tree? The song's flow reference is already this track.

### 9. The clock — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the US national debt clock ticks over to 40 trillion dollars.
- **We see:** the clock, real footage, held long enough to watch a digit turn.
- **The laugh:** none. This is the consequence shot, played straight.
- **To decide:** end here, hard cut to black? And does the emperor's tally from scene 6 turn out to
  have been this clock all along?

### 10. Sign-off — *Proposal*

- **Happens:** one last line from the narrator, or nothing.
- **To decide:** whether it exists. Scene 9 may be the better ending.

1. **What is it?** A narrated short, a music video for the bar song, or a comic? How long?
2. **Where does the song go?** One option that joins two of Kai's three ideas: the whole story is a
   bar song — a medieval tavern, a bard, the punters joining in on the chorus. Then the "narration"
   is the song and scenes 2 to 6 are its verses.
3. **Who benefits?** Scene 4. [`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md) asks for a named
   beneficiary in every piece.
4. **Does anything survive from the 28 September film?** The nurse, Keynes, shake / starve / plant,
   the sixteen cleared archive files.
5. **What does it look like?** Storybook fable, archive footage, or the BadCode near-black register?
