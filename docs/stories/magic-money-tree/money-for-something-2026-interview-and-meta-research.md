## Part A2. The meta angle: an AI host on a show made with AI

The rules that decide this (all from `using-ai.md`):
- §8: the narrator is a **defendant**, not a prophet. Self-awareness lands as confession, said flat.
- §7: **the confession and the reason never share a sentence.** A joke that admits the cost is fine. A joke that admits it *and excuses it* is the thing that fails.
- §7 never-say list: "can't afford", "can't draw", "democratises", "inevitable", "it's capitalism" as a permission slip, "at least we're honest", "AI learns like a human", "AI artists".
- §6 bright lines: no real person's face or voice; no photoreal fake evidence; never target a named artist.
- §8 guard rail: contempt never at the objectors and never at the reader.

## Part B. Format research for an interview segment

### B1. Philomena Cunk (Diane Morgan, written by Charlie Brooker)

**Where it came from**
- Began as a talking head on *Charlie Brooker's Weekly Wipe* (BBC Two, from 2013), beside Barry Shitpeas (Al Campbell). Then "Moments of Wonder" segments, then *Cunk on Shakespeare*, *Cunk on Britain* (2018), *Cunk on Earth* (2022), *Cunk on Life* (2024). ✅ [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Series/PhilomenaCunk) · [Joel Morris](https://joelmorris.substack.com/p/al-campbell-the-barry-shitpeas-story-897) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunk_on_Britain)
- Brooker: she began summing up the news; then "why don't we send her out into the real world" to interview experts. ✅ [WPR](https://www.wpr.org/culture/charlie-brooker-cunk-on-life-black-mirror)

**The experts are real, and they react for real**
- Academics know it is comedy but still react with real shock and exasperation; one early expert got so irritated filming stopped while he calmed down. ✅ [AOL/LA Times](https://www.aol.com/news/far-philomena-cunk-laugh-breaks-110030899.html) · [MetaFilter](https://fanfare.metafilter.com/25256/Cunk-on-Life)
- Morgan: "the straighter and more serious they are, the more it tickles me." The comedy needs the expert to defend their subject sincerely; Brooker says they "feel a bit defensive of their subject". 🟡
- Experts often reply "What do you mean?", so Morgan has counter-answers ready. Interviews are long; the cut keeps seconds. ✅ [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/tv/news/cunk-on-life-diane-morgan-charlie-booker-1236487805/)
- Expert laughter is mostly cut out (IMDb trivia, 🔴). The finished show keeps the expert straight.
- Brooker's writers found sex jokes don't suit Cunk: the character is innocent, not crude. ✅ Variety. (This matters: Jack's host is vulgar. The interviewer in our segment can be crude; the Cunk mechanism does not need it.)

**The question types** (examples described, not quoted at length)
1. **Category error**: treat two things of different kinds as the same kind. Asking whether a rock is made of stone or a stone of rock. ✅ (widely quoted)
2. **Malapropism that stays in**: she mishears the term and builds the question on the mishearing ("the Soviet Onion"). ✅ TV Tropes
3. **Literal reading**: take a metaphor or a name at face value (asking whether China has a roof). ✅ TV Tropes
4. **Wrong century / anachronism**: modern product applied to the ancient past: whether a Mesopotamian clay tablet was the first audiobook; the expert (Irving Finkel, British Museum) just says "no". ✅
5. **False equivalence / consumer framing**: rank incomparables as if shopping: which holy book is the best one. ✅ (review)
6. **Pop-culture yardstick**: measure a great figure against a celebrity or a TV show. 🔴 (from memory; consistent with the show)
7. **Confident wrong premise stated as fact**, then a question built on it, so the expert must first undo the premise. 🔴 (from memory)
8. **The accidental profound question**: dumb on the surface, but it exposes something true; reviewers single these out. ✅ [Daily Star review](https://www.thedailystar.net/shout/news/journey-through-the-centre-cunk-review-cunk-earth-3252296)

**Editing rhythm** (my reading, 🔴 not sourced to the production)
- Exchange: question (3–6 s) → the expert's silent stare or slow blink (1–2 s, the real laugh) → a short, careful answer cut off early, or "no" → Cunk's flat follow-up or straight to the next topic.
- Most exchanges run 10–25 s. Many are just the question plus the stare.
- The reaction shot is the punchline. Cunk never laughs, never signals the joke.
- Locked medium shots, a two-camera interview (single and reverse); no camera movement in the joke.

**What this means for us**
- The Cunk engine is: *innocent wrong question* + *sincere, defensive expert*. The comedy is the gap.
- **Swap the roles to fit the reader rules:** our host, the superintelligence, asks the Cunk questions; the people answering are the powerful (a fictional minister, a fictional fund manager, a fictional tech founder). His "stupid" questions are type 8: they expose the trick. ("So the money for the hospital doesn't exist, but the money for the bank did. Where was it kept?")
- The powerful man answering sincerely and defensively is where the laugh is, and it lands on the powerful.

### B2. The Diary of a CEO and the podcast-bro format

**The set and the tropes**
- Dark studio, two chairs facing, large microphones, warm low light, black background; multi-camera close-ups. 🔴 (from memory; the look is well known)

### B3. Legal and ethical: parodying a real UK presenter

Not legal advice. 🟡 throughout; I am not a lawyer.
- **Copyright**: s.30A CDPA 1988 (since 1 Oct 2014) allows fair dealing for caricature, parody or pastiche. It covers copyright only, not defamation, passing off or privacy, and only where the use is fair. A format (two chairs, cards) is not protected by copyright anyway. [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/the-development-of-the-parody-exception-to-copyright-infringement) · [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/parody-law-in-the-uk-key-rules-and-exceptions/)
- **Passing off / false endorsement**: the UK has no general image right, but using a famous person so the public thinks they endorse you is actionable: *Irvine v Talksport* (2002, £25,000) and *Fenty v Arcadia* (Rihanna v Topshop, CA 2015). A comedy show that looks like his show and uses his face could be read as false endorsement, especially next to the existing Bartlett deepfake scams. [Simkins](https://www.simkins.com/news/rihanna-v-topshop-t-shirt-held-to-be-false-endorsement) · [SCL](https://www.scl.org/2869-celebrity-images-lessons-from-rihanna-s-t-shirt/)
- **Defamation**: Defamation Act 2013 needs "serious harm"; honest opinion and truth are defences. Obvious satire is read in context, but putting *invented* words in a real person's mouth (e.g. a fake Bartlett endorsing quack cures) is the risky case. Repeating the *documented* criticism (BBC finding, ASA ruling) as fact is safe if accurate. 🔴 (from general knowledge)
- **Platform rules**: YouTube requires disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content and acts on impersonation of real people. 🔴 (from memory; check the current policy before upload)
- **Our own rule beats the law**: `using-ai.md` §6 forbids any real person's face or voice without consent. So a generated Bartlett is out regardless of s.30A.
- **The safe route, in order:**
  1. ✅ A **fictional host** in a format that is plainly the genre (dark studio, cards, "the closing tradition"), with a different name and face. A pastiche of the genre, not of the man. The genre is shared by dozens of shows.
  2. ✅ The **documented facts** about the real show said by our host on our set, with the source logged (the ASA ruling, the BBC finding).
  3. 🟡 A name that nods to him ("Diary of a Billionaire") is fine; a name or look that could be mistaken for his show is not.
  4. ❌ His name on the fake host, his face, his voice, his logo, his thumbnails.

### B4. The weather-correspondent cut-away

**Precedents**
- *The Day Today* (1994): Chris Morris's correspondents (Collaterlie Sisters on business, Peter O'Hanraha-hanrahan on economics) — the gag is the format played perfectly straight with nonsense inside it, and the anchor's contempt for his own reporter. ✅ [Chortle](https://chortle.co.uk/features/2019/01/19/42103/because_fact_into_doubt_won%e2%80%99t_go) · [BCG](https://comedy.co.uk/tv/the_day_today)
- *Brass Eye* (1997, 2001) reused the news-correspondent grammar to go after prime-time news itself. ✅ (same sources)
- *The Mash Report* (BBC Two to 2021) and *Late Night Mash* (Dave, to 2023): correspondent segments in a late-night desk format; Rachel Parris began as a correspondent. ✅ [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/nish-kumars-the-mash-report-has-been-axed-by-the-bbc-2899425) · [Chortle](https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2023/03/10/52682/dave_axes_late_night_mash)
- **Real blooper, the template**: Weather Channel's Mike Seidel bracing against Hurricane Florence (Sept 2018) while two men stroll past behind him; 13.6m views. The joke is the gap between the reporter's drama and what the background shows. ✅ [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/weather-channel-responds-to-claims-reporter-was-faking-coverage-of-hurricane-florence/) · [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2018/09/weather-channel-mike-seidel-hurricane-florence-exaggerates-1202465386)

**What makes a 5-second cut-away land** (my reading, 🔴)
- One fact, one picture, back to the studio. No set-up, because the audience knows the grammar of a live cross.
- The correspondent is sincere; the background contradicts him, or he reports something nobody needed (still raining).
- The anchor's flat "Thank you" or no reply at all is half the joke. Repetition makes it a callback: same correspondent, same spot, worse each time.
- **Fit for us**: locked camera, one speaker, under 10 s — exactly what Flow does well. Put the correspondent somewhere with no crowd: a flooded empty car park, an empty pier at night, outside a closed Treasury door.
- Aim: a correspondent outside the Bank of England reporting that the money is still there, it's still raining, back to you. Not a correspondent in a deprived high street.
