## What actually happened (sourced, as of 7 Nov 2026; still moving)

- **Ueno Bank**: a young, fast-grown bank (Grupo Vázquez, founder Miguel Vázquez) that signed up
  ~2.9–3m customers, about 60% of Paraguay's adults, with supermarket rebates; >1m were people's
  first ever bank account. Sponsored the national football team.
- **Mon 2 – Tue 3 Nov**: the central bank found its solvency below half the legal minimum: loans to
  the owners' own companies, poorly documented, and expenses booked as assets. Seized before dawn.
  App, cards, transfers, branches: all dead. Ride-hailing app shut (3,000 drivers); the national
  team's players can't reach their money.
- **Public money inside it**: ~US$800–850m of state deposits, including ~US$355–380m of the IPS
  (pensions and health) fund, placed beyond legal limits per lawmakers. 32,000 retirees were paid
  through it.
- **The links**: President **Santiago Peña** was a shareholder in Ueno's holding company from 2019
  until ~March/April 2025. He made **Carlos Carvallo**, who chaired Ueno's predecessor, governor
  of the central bank, the regulator of Ueno. Carvallo "recused" himself.
- **Thu 5 Nov**: Peña announced his resignation. Congress **refused to accept it** and impeached him
  (lower house 78–0). Payouts began, capped at first at Gs 10m (~US$1,667).
- **Fri 6 Nov**: Senate removed him 44–0. VP Pedro Alliana sworn in. Peña charged (breach of trust;
  France 24 also lists influence peddling) and put under **house arrest**; after protesters threw
  stones and eggs at the armoured cars outside his house he was moved to a military regiment
  (La Nueva, Examedia). Six bank executives jailed, Vázquez among them.
- **Protests**: thousands; cards burned outside Ueno HQ; an effigy of Peña burned; "Santi Peña to
  Tacumbú!" (the prison where the banker already is); "resignation is not enough"; tear gas and
  water cannon at Congress. Former candidate Payo Cubas tried to join a march and was booed,
  shoved and **chased out** ("oportunista").
- **Deposit insurance**: up to ~US$36–38k, which covers ~97% of customers in full, paid in stages,
  only into an account at another bank, and only if you owe Ueno nothing.

## Meme, not news-with-a-joke (research 2026-11-07)

What the web says (heuristics, mostly marketing blogs; the academic bits are marked):
- **Familiar beats specific.** Memes spread when they feel like an inside joke a huge group is already
  in on (brainblogger summary of meme studies). Paraguay is nobody's inside joke; "the politician
  who turns up at the protest" is everybody's.
- **Character, close-up, one emotion** predicted virality in a study of 100 image memes (arXiv
  2101.06535; small, /pol/ sample, so weak).
- **Templates that get remixed live longer** (study of image-with-text memes, in the same summary).
  So: labels and formats people can reuse, not a one-off gag.
- **Muted and fast:** text readable in the first ~1–1.5 s; a big share watches with the sound off;
  one line, sans-serif, away from the app buttons; loop back to the first frame.
- **Satire gets more likes than news clips** but less argument (study of US satire shows' posts).

The test: **delete every proper noun. If it still lands, it's a meme. If it needs the news, it's a
news story with a joke.** The news goes in the post caption, one line, as the receipt.
