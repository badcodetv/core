## The show in one breath

A drunk game-show host from the future reads this week's news as a quiz he already knows the answers
to. Every story is a question about money, and the answer is always one of three: **shake, starve or
plant.** The contestants' chairs are empty.

**What makes it BadCode and not another panel show:**

- **He has seen how it ends.** A normal host guesses what happens next. Ours knows, and is bored of
  it. That gives a free segment no other show can do: *next week's news*, read with total certainty.
- **Every story gets a price tag.** No story is "politics". Each one is "there was money for this,
  and there was no money for that", shown as two numbers.
- **Every story names who got paid.** The snark is for the mess; the name of who benefits is said
  plainly, once. That is the reader rule that matters most
  ([`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md), rules 5, 6 and 9).
- **The tree is the scoreboard.** Shaken, left to die, or watered, round by round.

## The spine of an episode: the pair

The strongest thing this week is not one story. It is **two letters in the same week**: one saying
there is no money for nurses, one saying there is money for missiles. The history show spent ten
minutes explaining that idea. The news hands it over free, with dates.

So each episode is built on **one pair**: a "no" and a "yes" from the same seven days. Everything
else is quick-fire around it.

## Two sizes

- **The short (45 to 60 seconds):** one headline, one game. Weekly. Beat 3 alone is a short. So is beat 5.
- **The episode (3 to 4 minutes):** one pair plus quick-fire. Monthly, or for a big date like the Budget.

## Guardrails

- **Real people are Kai's call.** The history show hid behind 1916. This one names living people
  every week: Burnham, Healey, Farage, Bezos, Musk, the King. It needs a ruling before anything is made.
- **Real footage is still not ruled.** The BBC question from 1 October is open. Everything above
  works with headlines as graphics, quote cards and stills, so the pilot does not have to wait for it.
- **The triple lock (story 8) can lose us the reader.** Many of them are pensioners or close to one.
  Never joke at people who want their pension kept. If it is used, the joke is on the choice of who pays.
- **Reform (stories 12 and 13).** Joke about the money and the donors, never about the voters. Never
  "you've been had".
- **Hit all sides.** Both men agreed on 6 October to go at left and right equally. This week that is
  easy: Labour refuses the nurses, the Tories buy a dome, Reform has the donors, the Greens lost.
- **Every fact gets a second look on the day.** A topical show that gets a number wrong is finished.
- **AI stories always name the man, not the machine** (stories 10, 11, 15).
- **No studio laughter, and pounds not dollars**, as ruled for the set on 4 October.
