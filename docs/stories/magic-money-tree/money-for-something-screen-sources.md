## How to read this

- 89 files are on disk. All 89 came from Wikimedia Commons. Every licence below was read from the Commons API today (`extmetadata`), per file. None was assumed.
- **Green** means public domain, CC0, CC BY or OGL, read today. CC BY and OGL need the credit shown. Put it in the video.
- **Amber** means the licence tag is there but the chain behind it is weak. A human should look before it ships. The reason is in the last column.
- Anything CC BY-SA, NC or ND was skipped. That is most UK street photography on Commons (Geograph is CC BY-SA).
- Pictures wider than 4000px were saved as Commons' 3840px rendering. The full-size original is at the source URL.
- Videos were cut to 12 to 15 seconds and re-encoded to H.264 mp4. The three missile clips have no sound.
- No receipts were written to `docs/footage/`. This file is the only record. The task said to touch no other file.

## Three warnings that apply to the whole set

1. **US military pictures.** The copyright is public domain. But the DVIDS notice says its material may not be used in a way that could imply endorsement of "any political party or candidate". `docs/video-fx/footage-sources.md` rates DVIDS amber for that reason. These files came through Commons with a public domain tag, so they are green on copyright. The clause is a judgement call for Kai. Hardware shots carry little risk.
2. **Real people.** A clean licence does not clear what the cut implies about a named person. That is defamation by juxtaposition and it is Kai's call, shot by shot. It bites hardest on beats 6 and 7 (Musk, Epstein, Andrew, the King). Two files carry a Commons personality rights flag: the Farage portrait and the Charles and Trump state dinner photo.
3. **Trade marks.** Google, Shell, Amazon, Palantir and the bank logos are trade marks. Showing them in commentary is normal. Do not use them as if they were our branding.
