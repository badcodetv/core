**How to read the "From" column:** **Meeting** means you or Kai said it at 12:18. **Proposal** means
I added it to join the beats together — cut or change freely.

**Rules from the meeting:** very short (Kai: three minutes, not fifteen). Heavy on real footage.
Sarcastic narrator. Never "now let me tell you how it all works". The comedy is the narrator and
the soldier.

## The whole thing in one breath

Theresa May says there is no magic money tree. A sarcastic narrator from the future goes and asks a
man who was there. Germany shook it, Germany starved it, and he got a war. Then his country planted
it and he got a house. Then he gets old and watches us shake it, starve it and shake it again, and
he has nothing left to say.

### 1. "There is no magic money tree" — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the Theresa May clip, cold. The narrator cuts in: "Oh, sweet summer child. Let me
  show you how history actually works."
- **We see:** the real clip.
- **The laugh:** the clip is a joke at the top, not the argument.
- **To decide:** the clip is BBC footage. See the BBC question in the direction file.

### 2. Meet the man who was there — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** "Let's actually chat to someone who was there, shall we?" A chair, a man, an
  interviewer we never see.
- **We see:** the interview set-up we come back to all film. One chair, one frame.
- **The laugh:** the narrator is all-knowing and smug; the soldier is not impressed by it.
- **To decide:** who is he? Name, regiment, town, how he talks. Is he Bob? Is he alive, a ghost, or
  something the narrator has pulled out of time? What does the narrator sound like?

### 3. 1923, shake: wheelbarrows, and an empty chair — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** Germany prints. "Oh god, here we go, wheelbarrows again." Cut to the chair. It is
  empty. Narrator: he's not born yet.
- **We see:** the wheelbarrow footage, then the empty chair.
- **The laugh:** the empty chair. Or a two-year-old in it.
- **To decide:** empty chair or toddler? This is the first verb card, so how the word SHAKE appears
  is settled here for all three.

### 4. The 1930s, starve: the flashes start — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** they stop shaking and starve it instead. People get desperate.
- **We see:** Jack's idea: flashes of the war that is coming, cutting in, getting longer each time.
- **The laugh:** not much. This is where it turns.
- **To decide:** is the soldier in the chair as a boy? How horror does it go? And how do we say
  "this led there" in one line without claiming one caused the other outright?

### 5. The war — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the flashes become the thing itself. Dunkirk.
- **We see:** the best archive footage in the film. Then the soldier, young, in the chair.
- **The laugh:** none. Kai: play it real, "do a Ray Winstone on it".
- **To decide:** what does he say about it? This is the one interview where he talks properly.

### 6. "How did we pay for all that?" — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** silence. The narrator asks the question nobody can answer.
- **We see:** Jack's idea: after the silence, flashes of printing money start, building the same way
  the war flashes did.
- **The laugh:** the narrator daring the audience to know.
- **To decide:** how long the silence holds.

### 7. 1948, plant: the house and the NHS — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** in more debt than ever, the country borrows again and builds. Keynes: we can afford
  what we can create.
- **We see:** council houses going up, the NHS opening. The soldier: "I didn't have a penny and all
  of a sudden I had a house."
- **The laugh:** the warmth. This is the Britain the audience is nostalgic for, and he lived in it.
- **To decide:** how much Keynes — a name and a line, or a face? This is the only scene allowed to
  be sincere, so how long does it get?

### 8. 2008, shake: head in hands, part one — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the crash. They print, and it goes to the banks.
- **We see:** the news footage. Then the chair. He is very old. His head is in his hands.
- **The laugh:** the narrator asks if he's all right. He isn't.
- **To decide:** how much do we say about where the money went? Kai's warning: the second you
  explain it, it is a documentary again. Is the Tarquin reference here?

### 9. The 2010s, starve: head in hands, part two — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** Osborne and Cameron. Austerity.
- **We see:** the clips. The chair. Same pose.
- **The laugh:** the narrator has stopped bothering to explain.
- **To decide:** does the nurse and her payslip from the old storyboard survive, even as one shot?

### 10. 2020, shake: head in hands, part three — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** Covid. They shake it again. "Won't you lot make your minds up."
- **We see:** Sunak, Eat Out to Help Out. The chair.
- **The laugh:** by now the scenes are seconds long. Slapstick speed.
- **To decide:** Eat Out to Help Out or the Covid priority lane? The old storyboard swapped one for
  the other on 28 September.

### 11. The empty chair — *Meeting*

- **Happens:** the narrator: what happens next? Not spelling it out.
- **We see:** Jack's ending: the chair, empty again.
- **The laugh:** Kai floated a Curb Your Enthusiasm sting.
- **To decide:** empty chair, the sting, or both? Does Biggie go here? Does anyone say "plant it",
  or is that left for the audience to say?

## Questions that sit above every scene

1. **Who is the soldier, and how is he here?** Scene 2. Everything else hangs on it.
2. **How is he made?** A Flow character interviewed across five ages, against real archive footage.
   The standing rule is no dialogue in Flow video, with the voice recorded separately.
3. **How long?** Three minutes means about fifteen seconds a scene.
4. **Where does the song go?** Kai is finishing it. Under the modern half, at the end, or separate?
5. **Biggie.** Wanted, not placed. "The soldier listens to Biggie" is the best lead so far.
6. **The BBC clips.** Scenes 1 and 8 to 10 lean on them.
