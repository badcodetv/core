# Magic Money Tree — the game show

**Status: an idea, captured 6 October 2026. Nothing here is ruled.** It builds on
[Money For Something](./money-for-something.md) and does not replace it, the 1 October direction
([`direction-2026-10-01.md`](./direction-2026-10-01.md)) or the documentary storyboard.

## The idea, as told

- **A game show called Magic Money Tree.**
- **It asks about current events** of the week, or of the year.
- **Each contestant answers through one lens: starve, shake or plant.**
- **It is a comedy.**

## How it differs from Money For Something

| | Money For Something | Magic Money Tree, the game show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | History: the First World War panel | The news: this week, or this year |
| Panel | Two soldiers and a politician, who were there | Contestants who each *are* one of the three verbs |
| The joke | The rules change depending on who is asking for money | Whatever happens, each contestant gives the same answer |
| Shelf life | Made once | Repeatable: a new episode every week or every year |
| Name | Working title | Uses the story's own name |

## The three lenses

From the documentary storyboard ([`storyboard.md`](./storyboard.md), scenes 5 to 13): a country
can do three things with its money.

- **Shake it.** Make money and hand it out without making anything with it.
- **Starve it.** "We can't afford it."
- **Plant it.** Put it into work that can actually be done.

## First sketch (mine, written to be argued with)

None of this was asked for. It is here so there is something to push against.

- **One contestant per verb, and they never change their answer.** The host reads out a real
  headline. The Shaker says "print it and give it to the banks." The Starver says "we can't afford
  it." The Planter says "build something." The comedy is that two of them have won every round
  for fifteen years.
- **The host from Money For Something could carry over** (the dishevelled comic, Flow Character
  "The Host"), with the same narrator split. Not decided.
- **A round is one headline.** A real clip or front page, the question "shake, starve or plant?",
  three answers, then what the government actually did. The reveal is the scoreboard.
- **The tree is the scoreboard.** Money For Something already has a tree in a bucket on set. It
  gets shaken, left to die, or watered, round by round.
- **A weekly version is a short** (one headline, under a minute). **A yearly version is an
  episode** (the year in ten headlines).

## What it needs before it is real

- **A ruling on who the contestants are.** Three archetypes, three real job titles (a Chancellor,
  a central banker, a builder), or three members of the public. The reader rule says the person
  the reader recognises as themselves is never the butt of the joke
  ([`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md), rules 1 and 2), so the Planter is the
  obvious seat for them.
- **A ruling on real footage.** A topical show leans on news clips. The BBC question from
  1 October is still open.
- **A way to keep it true.** Every round states what really happened, so every round needs a
  checked source on the day. No episode has been researched.
- **A check on the name.** "The Magic Money Tree" as a show title has not been searched for.

## Story or claim?

A claim so far: "every government picks one of three things, and it is usually the wrong two."
It becomes a story when a contestant has something to lose. Not solved here.
