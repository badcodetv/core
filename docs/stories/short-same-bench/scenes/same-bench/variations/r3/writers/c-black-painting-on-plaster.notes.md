# c-black-painting-on-plaster: notes (redo)

What shaped it:

- **Oil straight onto plaster.** The early-1820s murals painted onto the walls of a house outside Madrid were oil on dry plaster, later pulled off the walls onto canvas, so cracking, flaking and loss are part of how they look now. That gave the crazed, blistered wall surface and the bare chalky ground showing through.
  https://eclecticlight.co/2021/09/22/francisco-goya-black-paintings-1/ ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witches%27_Sabbath_(The_Great_He-Goat)
- **A huge empty field with one figure crushed to an edge.** The "half-submerged dog" mural is a tall, narrow panel (131 x 79 cm, close to our 9:16): a small head at one edge and a vast undefined field of colour, read as isolation and dread. Photos taken before the transfer show figures that were lost later, which gave the "grey ghosts where earlier figures were painted out" and the idea that he outlived the rest of the wall.
  https://fundaciongoyaenaragon.es/eng/obra/perro-semihundido/671 ·
  https://artincontext.org/the-dog-by-francisco-goya
- **Palette.** Earth tones, blacks and a lead white. One source says the paintings may first have held more colour, so I kept the brief's restricted ochre / umber / grey / black plus one white (the shirt bib) as a deliberate choice. The palette sources are weak (secondary sites), so treat this as unverified.
- **Horror without monsters.** The dread comes from scale (he presses out of the wall), sunken eyes with a wet glint, a too-wide grin, and the darkness eating the bench. Nothing is taken from any game.

Redo fixes: removed every phrase the sameness gate flagged ("man in a", "rich man in", "of the frame", "anywhere in the", "black frock coat and a tall"). He is now a "pot-bellied plutocrat" in a "stovepipe hat and a long dark tailcoat". Edges are named without "of the frame", and "anywhere" is gone.
