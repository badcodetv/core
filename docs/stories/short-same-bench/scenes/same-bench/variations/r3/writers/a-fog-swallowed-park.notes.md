# a-fog-swallowed-park — notes

What shaped it:

- **Fog as erasure, not decoration.** Survival-horror writing on fog keeps returning to one idea: limited visibility frightens because the hidden supplies possibilities, and big empty areas make the eye search. So the prompt grades the park from hard line to faint to nothing within a few strides, and gives the open fog the top third (which doubles as caption space).
  - https://www.itechguides.com/what-makes-ps1-style-survival-horror-games-scary/
  - https://www.theouterhaven.net/how-visual-design-shapes-psychological-horror-in-games/
- **Fog alone doesn't carry the scare** (Thomas Grip, quoted by CGMagazine): it needs something wrong inside it. Here that is scale and line weight: the rich man is the only dense, fully-drawn black mass, too big for the bench, his hat dissolving upward into the fog, while the soldier is drawn in thin broken line, already being erased. The menace is that he is the one thing the fog cannot touch.
  - https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/creating-dread-design-decisions-behind-horror-games/
- **Ink-wash phrasing for Nano Banana:** restrained monochrome plus one tone (here the yellow of the paper), wet ink spreading into paper, controlled crosshatching against sparse shading; fog written as an atmosphere with a stated density and light quality (flat, sourceless, shadowless) rather than as a vague mood word.
  - https://www.curify-ai.com/blog/10-prompting-tips-nano-banana
  - https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-image/prompt-guide/
  - https://automatio.ai/prompts/nano-banana/sumi-e-ink-splash-dynamic-eastern-art-prompt
- Kept the comedy: a thin satisfied smile and a bored wrist-flick brush-off, not a monster. Our own garden, bench and composition (high wide, bench centred, the pair at opposite ends); no reference panel recreated, no game or artist named. Model and aspect fixed by the story (Nano Banana 2, 9:16). Ends with "Thanks." per house convention.
