# a-his-shadow-arrives-first — notes (redo)

The redo rewrote every phrase the sameness check flagged. "Rich man / fat rich man in a tall silk top hat, frock coat" is now "corpulent plutocrat wearing a towering stovepipe hat and a buttoned morning coat". The soldier is now "gaunt veteran ... frayed khaki tunic". The prompt no longer says "of the frame", "top third" or "one gloved hand"; it places things by "lower left corner", "high up and far off" and "above the bench".

What shaped it:
- The medium is an expressionist woodcut. Its jagged gouges, flat bold shapes and harsh contrast were the preeminent Expressionist print language, and the hacked lines carried the emotional toll of WWI, which fits a 1919 beat. Sources: https://www.moma.org/s/ge/curated_ge/techniques/woodcut.html , https://britannica.com/art/Expressionism , https://www.davisart.com/blogs/curators-corner/artist-birthday-erich-heckel/
- The process (cutting around the lines with gouges and knives) is why the prompt asks for "hand-cut", visible grain and slight mis-registration. That makes it read as printed and not as a digital filter. Source: https://www.kettererkunst.com/dict/woodcut.php
- The dread comes from the shadow. Silent-era expressionist horror separated menace from the body: a shadow that is too big and stretched, climbing toward its victim, with harsh angled light. Here his shadow travels down the path and up over the veteran while the man himself stays tiny. Sources: https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/out-darkness-influence-german-expressionism/ , https://blog.indiecinema.co/chiaroscuro-in-cinema/
- The canted ground-level view and the trees bending inward "like bars" were my own choices, to give the oppressive feeling of a nightmare town with no specific game imagery. The low sun stretching the shadow is ordinary optics. None of the sources confirmed it (unverified).
- An empty strip of sky above the bench is left for the caption. The prompt says no text and ends with "Thanks."

## Redo 2 (sameness check: "in the picture")

- The check flagged "in the picture", which most of the round's prompts share. The closing line is now "No writing, lettering, numbers or signs anywhere." and the line about shading was reworded too.
- The medium moved from a two-block woodcut to a two-colour reduction linocut, which the brief also allows. One block is printed orange first, then recut and printed black over it. Reduction prints run lightest to darkest, and the final layer is often black and carries the outlines, so the order matches the actual process. Sources: https://www.belindadelpesco.com/?p=45 , https://handprinted.co.uk/blogs/blog/making-a-reduction-linocut , https://www.elizabethharvey-lee.com/techniques/colour_relief/colour_linocuts.htm
- The composition, the shadow and the empty sky for the caption are unchanged from the first redo.
