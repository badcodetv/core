What shaped it:
- The two-pens trick is a collage idea: a vintage printed cut-out pasted against modern drawn line. Collage practice of layering an aged printed cutout over hand or digital line work, with a worn/transfer look, is described across contemporary mixed-media collage listings (e.g. https://www.artfinder.com/product/310, https://www.creativeboom.com/work/intriguing-collages-that-bring-together-discovered-finds-with-a-charm-of-yesteryear/). So the man gets a "foxed, cream-coloured scrap of paper with a ragged scissor edge" to sell the pasted-on look.
- Engraving/etching-style hatching keeps its handmade feel and varied line weight lets a modern subject be set against traditional means (https://illustrationx.com/styles/wood-engraving-and-etching) — hence heavy hatched and cross-hatched strokes for him only, and uniform even line plus washes, explicitly "no hatching", for her and the street, so the two hands stay distinct.
- The fat-capitalist type in 1900s-1920s pen-and-ink cartoons (stout, top hat, cigar, watch chain; e.g. https://grapefruitmoongallery.com/9771, https://newpol.org/?p=1516) informed his costume; described generically, no artist named, no specific panel recreated.
