# b-over-the-nurse-shoulder (r2, redo) — what shaped it

- Rewrote the caption-space sentence to drop the flagged shared run ("empty so words can be laid over it"); it now reads "open, unwashed morning sky for a caption to sit on later". Rest reworded throughout.
- Period media: museum and library catalogue entries for 1906-1917 newspaper cartoons record pen and ink over graphite underdrawing, often with ink wash, on illustration board — so: dip pen, India ink, faint pencil under the ink, thin wash, bristol board. Also noted originals were drawn large and photo-reduced for print, which suits confident tapering contours and cross-hatching over fine detail.
  - https://www.themorgan.org/node/38251 (Edmund J. Sullivan, 1917, pen and black ink over graphite)
  - https://www.princeton.edu/~graphicarts/2010/01/wilsons_triple_wall_of_privile.html (1913, pen, ink wash, gouache)
  - https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/running-for-office/reproducing.html (originals photographed and reduced for newspaper print)
- Over-the-shoulder framing: a "dirty over" keeps the near shoulder soft in the foreground while the far subject holds focus; guides warn the blurred shoulder must not become a distracting blob. Ink cannot blur, so the nurse is drawn in "loose, broken, unfinished strokes, like a near object out of focus" and kept to the lowest part of the frame.
  - https://www.adorama.com/alc/over-the-shoulder-shot
  - https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/over-the-shoulder-shot
  - https://www.studiobinder.com/camera-shots/focus/shallow-focus-shot/
- Rim light rendered the pen-and-ink way: a line of untouched white paper around the hat and shoulders against hatched shade, rather than a painted glow.
- Not a copy of the 1919 panel: close dirty-over framing, dawn, nurse, glass towers, gloved palm-up gesture; no artist or title named. Settings fixed by the story: Nano Banana 2, 9:16.
