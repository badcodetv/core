# Same Bench r1: research notes (ideas writer)

- Historians group editorial-cartoon drawing into three schools: the crosshatch school (fine pen lines built into tone), the "ashcan" school (loose grease crayon) and the built-in-texture school (tone from textured paper). This gives three different 1910s ink looks, none of them a copy of one cartoon. Sources: https://bergetoons.blogspot.com/2019/06/gladly-crosshatched-bear.html (summarised in search), https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090103101440/http:/www.loc.gov/exhibits/cartoonamerica/cartoon-political.html
- In the 1910s, newspaper cartoonists without halftone suggested tone with strongly textured (laid) paper and drawing over it, so grey came from paper grain, not wash. Source: https://comicstriphistory.com/?p=26137 (via search summary)
- Grease or lithographic crayon with ink washes was the radical-magazine look of 1910-17: heavy black crayon, soft broken edges. Its magazine was printed as vivid two-colour covers plus black crayon drawings, so one spot colour on black ink is period-true. Sources: https://www.laborarts.org/exhibits/themasses/history.cfm.html, https://www.annexgalleries.com/artists/biography/2012, https://d.lib.msu.edu/masses
- Period media records: pen and ink over a graphite underdrawing, often with gouache white for correction. Source: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090112095022/http:/www.loc.gov/exhibits/treasures/trm047.html
- The plutocrat vocabulary of the era: a suit patterned with little dollar signs, moneybags, a burly oversized "Trust" figure towering over small workers. Usable as our own rich man's costume without copying any one panel (and without depicting a real politician). Sources: https://davenporthorses.org/2011/04/homer-davenport-cartoonist/, https://www.lambiek.net/artists/d/davenport_homer.htm
- Nano Banana prompting: describe subject, composition and style as separate blocks, be explicit about framing; useful texture words for this look are "fine-line cross-hatching", "aged paper grain", "ink bleed", "monochrome black ink on off-white"; say "no text, no lettering" explicitly. Sources: https://blog.google/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/, https://fal.ai/learn/tools/nano-banana-pro-prompting-guide, https://youmind.com/prompts/vintage-noir-ink-illustration-24223
