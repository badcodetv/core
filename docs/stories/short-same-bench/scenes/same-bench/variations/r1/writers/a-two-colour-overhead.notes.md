# a-two-colour-overhead — notes (redo)

Redo after the sameness gate flagged the round-shared phrase "for a caption". The empty top band is now described as bare paper with nothing drawn, with no mention of what it is for; the rest of the wording was reworked so it is not a copy of the first draft.

What shaped it:
- Two-ink relief printing on period magazine work: Punch in 1910 printed spot colour over a black key (Courtauld record), and newspapers until the 1920s often ran pages in only two or three inks, or one (Comic Strip History). Hence: black key plate plus one flat red plate, a hair out of register, no outline on the red. https://gallerycollections.courtauld.ac.uk/object-g-1990-wl-7829 , https://comicstriphistory.com/?p=24623
- 1914 two-colour satirical weekly plates (Simplicissimus listing), for the flat spot colour on cream stock: https://www.periodpaper.com/products/1914-color-print-simplicissimus-satire-political-cartoon-olaf-gulbransson-art-240624-lam2-053
- Overhead lamp-post viewpoint, diagonal bench/shadow composition and the red used only on the dollar-sign waistcoat come from the experiment brief; our own characters and composition, not a recreation of the 1919 panel.
