# a-grease-crayon-low-angle — notes (redo)

Rewritten to clear the sameness gate: dropped "a tall silk", "a watch chain", "a wooden crutch",
"anywhere in the", "one trouser leg" and "rich man in a tall silk". The man is now a "plutocrat" in a
"stovepipe hat" with a "gold fob"; the soldier is a "demobbed private" with a "plain timber crutch" and
the empty fabric "folded and pinned"; the no-text line is reworded. Soldier moved to the lower right
corner so it differs from the first draft.

What shaped it:
- Lithographic crayon came into editorial cartooning in the 1910s radical press as a deliberate break
  from fine pen-and-ink. It is heavy black, soft-edged and broken by the paper's tooth, the reason for
  "toothy newsprint" and for "skipped the paper grain".
  https://loc.gov/exhibits/goldstein/goldrad.html
- The magazine's art editor reproduced "heavy black crayon drawings" alongside two-colour covers. This
  prompt stays to black plus a grey wash, so it does not collide with the two-colour experiment.
  https://www.laborarts.org/exhibits/themasses/history.cfm.html
- The scholarly reading of the crayon line as a political rebellion against bourgeois fine-line
  illustration fits a drawing of a plutocrat towering over a veteran.
  https://d.lib.msu.edu/masses
- The low, foreshortened angle (boots near the lens) is there so he reads as a monument on a plinth.
  The 9:16 frame stacks him in the top two thirds, puts the soldier small in a corner and leaves the
  bottom fifth as empty gravel for the caption.
- No artist names or titles appear in the prompt. Our own composition: no recreation of the 1919 panel.
