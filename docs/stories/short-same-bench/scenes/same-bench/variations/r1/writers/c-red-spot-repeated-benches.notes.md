# c-red-spot-repeated-benches — notes (redo)

Rewritten to clear the sameness gate: dropped "in the picture", "of the frame", "one trouser leg", "only red thing", and the "kept clear for a caption / no text, no lettering" tail; same content in new words.

What shaped it:
- Period two-ink printing: papers commonly printed one side of the sheet in full colour and the other in just one or two inks, often a solid red, into the 1920s; a 1918 strip ran in black, blue and red. That justifies "black plus one flat spot of red" as a newspaper-native device rather than a modern graphic trick. Sources: https://legionofandy.com/2015/06/19/ben-day-dots-part-3/ , https://comicstriphistory.com/?p=6940
- Chicago Tribune's front-page colour cartoons used colour emotively and symbolically with a lean aesthetic, which is the job the red waistcoat does here (one colour = one meaning: money). Source: https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/39172/FULL_TEXT/view
- Composition: one-point perspective down a path with the red waistcoats as a repeated accent leading the eye to the vanishing point; the nearest bench (nurse, today) is largest, eras recede backward to the 1919 soldier, so time reads as distance.
- Settings fixed by the story: Nano Banana 2, 9:16.
