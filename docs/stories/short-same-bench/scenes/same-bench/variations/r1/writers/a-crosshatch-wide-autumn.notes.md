# a-crosshatch-wide-autumn — notes

What shaped the prompt:

- **Hatching as the only tone.** The dense school of 1910s newspaper cartooning built light and shade from meticulous crosshatching, with fine inking of fabric folds; a looser line was the more common alternative. So the prompt asks for tone from hatching only, no grey wash, and puts the density in the waistcoat strain and under the bench. Source: https://bergetoons.blogspot.com/2019/06/gladly-crosshatched-bear.html
- **Line weight from pen pressure.** A pen line thickens with pressure, which is what separates nib work from an even digital stroke; the prompt asks for swelling and tapering lines and slight ink spread so it reads as hand-drawn. Source: https://graphicarts.princeton.edu/2019/12/31/the-chalk-plate-process
- **Media of the period:** pen and ink over pencil, newsprint stock. Source: https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/cartoonamerica/cartoon-checklist.html
- **Nano Banana phrasing.** Google's guide: name the aesthetic plainly, then the framing (wide, eye-level) and the canvas shape (tall vertical). Community guides suggest naming the technique (crosshatching) and stating exclusions (no colour, no grey wash). Small text renders badly, so the prompt bans any lettering explicitly. Sources: https://blog.google/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/ , https://skills.cat/skills/minilozio/nano-banana-prompting-skill , https://www.curify-ai.com/blog/10-prompting-tips-nano-banana
- **Not a copy.** Our own staging: a wrought-iron slatted bench, a plane tree and fallen leaves, the rich man shoving the soldier to the last third, a gloved flick away rather than a pointed speech pose, a checked waistcoat and watch chain, the soldier holding his cap and looking up with patience. No artist name or title anywhere.
- **Caption room:** the top quarter is asked for as plain pale sky with a faint hatched edge, so it reads as part of the drawing and not as a blank band.
- Ends with "Thanks." per the house habit for Flow prompts.
