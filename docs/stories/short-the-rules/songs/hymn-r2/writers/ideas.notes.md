# Hymn round 2: research notes

- **Grime's core sound**: about 140 BPM (138 to 142), snare on 2 and 4 so it feels fast; a raw, unfiltered square-wave bass in short stabs; sparse, syncopated drums with the dry off-beat "eski click"; one cold repeating synth stab or string-preset riff, because the vocalist carries the melody; something changes every 8 bars and the beat drops almost to nothing at least once. Template: Wiley "Eskimo" (2002) and "Ice Rink" (2003); first instrumental often cited as Musical Mob "Pulse X" (2002). https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-grime-music
- **Grime vs its nearest wrong answers**: leave space and negative space for the vocal; the usual misses are lush pads and arpeggios filling the gaps, garage swing, US hip-hop loop logic, dubstep drop architecture, drill drum grammar and EDM build/drop. 140 BPM alone does not make it grime (dubstep shares the tempo). https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/grime-ai-music-genre-control-guide
- **Drill is a separate grammar** (half-time feel, snare on 3, sliding 808s), descended from grime and garage, so it can be asked for as a different idea rather than a variant. https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-grime-music ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_drill
- **Precedent for kids singing something dark and blasé**: Pink Floyd "Another Brick in the Wall (Part 2)": 23 Islington Green School pupils, recorded in about 40 minutes, flat London-accented unison and overdubbed to sound bigger. That is the closest famous sound to "school children singing it in a blasé way" over a hard beat. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Brick_in_the_Wall
- **Nursery rhymes turned dark**: Orbital's "Ringa Ringa (The Old Pandemic Folk Song)" and alt-J's "Choice Kingdom" both use a children's rhyme as the vehicle for something sinister. The contrast lands best when the children's part stays innocent and the menace comes from what is under it. https://www.eatsdrinksandleaves.com/orbital-ringa-ringa-the-old-pandemic-folk-song/ ; https://readdork.com/track/alt-j-choice-kingdom
- **Repetitive nursery-rhyme phrasing over a grime beat** shows up in UK meme songs (low-quality source, unverified). https://ava.ead.domalberto.edu.br/i-wanna-kill-mum-meme-song
- **Suno and children's voices**: guides disagree. One warns that "children's choir" or "kids singing" can give unsettling synthetic child voices; others list "children's choir / kid chorus" as a usable tag ("bright open vowels, light reverb"). Group energy in a prompt often adds choirs you didn't ask for, and Exclude does not reliably block them. https://idea-to-music.pages.dev/articles/kids-songs-suno ; https://sunohk.pages.dev/suno-tutorial/suno-timbre-vocal-prompt-guide/ ; https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/stop-suno-adding-crowd-vocals-choirs-backing-voices
- **Suno v6 field roles**: Style holds the global sound (genre, rhythm, instruments, broad vocal identity, mood; earlier words weigh more). Performance cues belong in the Lyrics as bracketed tags. Delivery words in Style only work sometimes on v6; shaping the lyrics is more reliable. https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/where-to-put-your-suno-prompt-guide ; https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-v6-vocal-prompts-2026/
- **UK school mass singing** (Young Voices and similar) is unison children's voices in a big hall, sometimes with pop or beatbox acts, but no grime precedent turned up, so the combination is open ground. https://youngvoices.co.uk
