# dnb-r3 ideas: research notes

- Hardstep was Grooverider's 1994 word for what he was playing; Renegade's "Terrorist" (Ray Keith, 1994) is its anthem and "probably the biggest amen track of them all". Its Reese came from Kevin Saunderson's "Just Want Another Chance". Underneath it sat a second "patrolling digi-dub bass". Overusing the raw Amen is part of what made hardstep heavy. Sources: https://ukf.com/read/20-essential-jungle-tunes-everyone-should-know-about/, https://drumtrip.co.uk/?p=796, https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Ragga_jungle
- Bass in jungle by year (Drumtrip): 1992 had sparse sub bass. 1993 had "big sub-bass hits". 1994 brought the Reese, plus the first *reversed sub* bassline (Dred Bass). 1995 tech-step paired "huge subs" with the Reese. 1996 brought "massive (deliberately) distorted bass" (Dillinja, "Threshold"). Goldie's "Terminator" was the first multi-pitched, timestretched drum loop. So a heavy mid-90s Amen bass with no Reese means a sub boom, a reversed sub or a distorted sub. https://drumtrip.co.uk/?p=796
- Mixmag on mid-90s jungle: "creeping and predatory" bassline under "a heavy-handed jungle assault" (Dillinja, "The Angels Fell"). "Booming bass" and "cymbals smashing" (Lemon D). "Hard, menacing smacks" of drums (Nasty Habits). "Aggressive and industrial, with barely any respite" (Pulse & Reece, "Kudos"). https://mixmag.net/feature/the-12-best-mid-90s-jungle-tracks
- Dillinja and Lemon D built the Valve sound system because clubs could not reproduce D&B's sub-bass. Bass weight was the whole point ("Deadly Deep Subs"). https://linenoise.substack.com/p/dillinja-is-a-jungle-king
- Council estate link: jungle went out "from improvised studios in empty flats, via aerials on tower block rooftops". Kool FM broadcast from Hackney council flats. Weekend Rush broadcast from the Nightingale Estate, six 21-storey blocks. The tower block became "the locus of an alternative cultural infrastructure", and a news report said pirates had "turned the tops of tower blocks into fortresses". https://eurozine.com/mutualism-massive-and-the-city-to-come, https://vice.com/en/article/kool-fm-jungle-pirate-london-945
- Samplers and breaks behind 90s jungle (Akai-era sampling, timestretched breaks, a "cyborg" feel). https://reverb.com/news/the-samplers-behind-90s-jungle-and-drum-and-bass, https://splice.com/blog/amen-break/
- Suno prompting: the first words of a prompt carry the most weight, so put the genre first. Keep to 6 to 10 focused tags. Write "instrumental" in the style box. Words like "jungle-ish" are too vague to steer anything. Jungle prompts do best when they ask for chopped breaks, sub space and rough edits. https://medium.com/@aitooldiscovery/suno-reddit-tips-and-prompts-what-r-sunoai-users-actually-do-65b59d51bcad, https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/continue-your-journey-suno-ai-prompts-guide-j-m-for-music-creation-mastery, https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-dnb-prompts/
- Suno sliders: Weirdness runs from Safe to Chaos (default 50) and controls exploration, not quality. A "creative detour" sits at Weirdness 60 to 75. High Style plus high Weirdness gives "unusual choices still receiving a strong style signal". The risk is confused arrangements if the brief is overloaded, so keep high-weirdness prompts short. Keep Variety at 0 so the tags stay intact, and don't stack v6-wild on top. https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/how-to-use-suno-s-advanced-sliders-weirdness-style-audio-influence, https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-v6-variety-style-influence-weirdness/
