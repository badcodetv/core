# ganja-dusty-jump-up — notes (redo)

Redo after the sameness gate flagged "the very first" / "very first downbeat" (shared with most of the round). The opening instruction is now "no intro: full drums and bass from beat one".

What shaped it:
- Ganja Records (DJ Hype, DJ Zinc, Pascal) foregrounded jungle's hip-hop DNA: "hyper-percussive", dusty breaks chopped into inventive patterns, dub "space & bass mixdowns", repurposed funk samples, "UN-electronic everything sounds" with "barely a synth or drum machine in earshot", "bruk-out euphoria". Phrases borrowed into the box: barely a drum machine in earshot, un-electronic, bruk-out bounce, dub-style space. https://trenchtrenchtrench.com/features/tiger-style-how-ganja-records-foregrounded-jungles-hip-hop-dna
- DJ Zinc's "Super Sharp Shooter" (Ganja, 1995) is a hip-hop/jungle fusion and one of the first jump-up anthems; jump-up came out of hardstep in 1995 and is defined by its bassline. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Zinc , https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Jungle_music
- 90s jungle producers sampled the Amen and other funk/jazz breaks off records on hardware samplers. https://reverb.com/news/the-samplers-behind-90s-jungle-and-drum-and-bass
