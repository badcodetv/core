# basement-worms-eye — notes

- **Angle and lens.** A worm's-eye view needs an ultra-wide lens (14–24mm) because it makes the subject look taller, plus something close to the lens to lead the eye. So the camera sits on the floor at 14mm, with grit and a spilled box in the foreground (https://shotkit.com/worms-eye-view/, https://www.adorama.com/alc/?p=102128).
- **Deep focus.** Worm's-eye guides say to use f/8–f/16 so everything from the foreground to the subject is sharp, which is why the prompt names f/11 and keeps it all in focus "so the stack seems to go on forever" (https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tutorials/worms-eye-view-photography-tips-for-unique-flower-and-nature-photography, https://www.thephotographyinstitute.com/us/en/blog-worms-eye-view-photography).
- **Prompt craft.** Google's Nano Banana guidance says to name the camera, angle, lens and light the way a cinematographer would, using terms like "low angle". The prompt states each of those plainly (https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/, https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana).
- **Choices of my own.**
  - The words THE RULES are on a printed label stuck to the side of the stack, not on a sheet seen edge-on, because text drawn edge-on in a generated image tends to come out unreadable.
  - Each mask colour is named, and each member wears a different modern garment (the hoodies, puffer and hi-vis from the brief), so the gang is clearly today's Britain.
  - The mask is described only in the locked preamble.
- **Model.** I picked "Nano Banana 2" rather than Pro so this round's settings are not all the same.
