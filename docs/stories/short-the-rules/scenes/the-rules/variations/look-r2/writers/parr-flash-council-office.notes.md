# parr-flash-council-office — notes

What shaped the prompt:

- **Flash in daylight, wide lens, saturated colour.** The photographer's own account: a wide-angle lens used with flash in daylight (from his 1982 Wallasey/New Brighton work on), slow colour-negative film plus flash giving "very high colour saturation", no Photoshop boost; the ring flash gives a garish, portable-studio edge in daylight. So the prompt asks for one hard flash fired from the camera into a midday room, flat frontal light with thin hugging shadows, glossy hot spots, background a touch darker than the subjects, and colour "as on cheap colour negative film" (no artist named, per the brief).

  - https://www.canon.co.uk/pro/stories/martin-parr-style-vision/ (search snippet; page 403'd on fetch)
  - https://petapixel.com/2020/03/14/martin-parr-a-leading-documentary-photographer-of-post-war-britain
  - https://aboutphotography.blog/photographer/martin-parr
- **Deadpan of the everyday.** His subject is ordinary British leisure and consumer clutter treated as serious; hence the room is built from small, specific sad objects (number dispenser, sun-bleached leaflets, bolted plastic chairs, rubber plant, sanitiser station) and the gang do not pose, they just wait.
- **Prompt craft (Nano Banana).** Third-party guides: concrete visual description over abstractions; state lens, distance, depth of field and a named lighting set-up; keep to one style direction. Deep focus (everything sharp) chosen so the A4 sign and the masks both read.
