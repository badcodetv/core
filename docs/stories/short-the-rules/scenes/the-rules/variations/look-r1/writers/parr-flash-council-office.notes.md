# parr-flash-council-office: notes (redo)

The rewrite drops the phrase the check flagged ("with no openings"). The mask face is now "smooth solid knit from brow to chin, featureless cloth where eyes, nose and mouth would be". I also reworded the rest instead of reusing the earlier draft.

What shaped it:
- **Flash on colour negative gives the saturation.** Parr said that shooting colour negative with flash gives very saturated colours. He used slow film (Agfa Ultra, Fuji 100) with a ring flash, and the flash fired in daylight gave a garish, slightly surreal edge with the shadows killed. Hence "one hard flash fired from the camera into daylight" and "slow colour-negative film from a high-street minilab". https://www.canon.co.uk/pro/stories/martin-parr-style-vision/ and https://www.photocrowd.com/blog/171-how-shoot-martin-parr/
- **The colour comes from the camera, not the edit.** He let the colour stay natural and relied on the flash, so the prompt describes the light and the film, not a grade. https://petapixel.com/2020/03/14/martin-parr-a-leading-documentary-photographer-of-post-war-britain
- **Ordinary British places and deadpan.** His subject is ordinary places, so the prompt fills the frame with specific, cheap council-office props (yucca, perspex leaflet rack, take-a-number pole) and ends on "deadpan, unstaged, quietly absurd". https://aboutphotography.blog/blog/martin-parr-one-of-the-most-significant-documentary-photographers-of-post-war-britain

Choices: Nano Banana Pro rather than 2, so the round doesn't all share one setting and because Pro handles the dense prop list and the A4 sign text better. The aspect is 9:16, as the story fixes. The triangle's placement is stated three ways (wearer's left, viewer's right, two o'clock, "never centred") because earlier rounds centred it. Gloves on "both hands of every figure" is there because one earlier round showed a bare hand.
