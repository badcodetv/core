# crewdson-terrace-blue-hour: notes (redo)

Rewritten from scratch to drop the phrase flagged as shared with other prompts ("upper right as we look at"). The triangle placement is now described from the wearer's side first ("high on the wearer's own left side, which from the camera reads as the two o'clock position, clearly off-centre"), so the off-centre fix from Bank Robbery stays in without the shared wording.

What shaped it:
- Twilight staging: dusk, street lamps fitted with special lights for an eerie glow, lights in windows and cars, and the streetlamp as the pivotal source. Hence the sodium cones, each window a different warm shade, and one bedroom lamp. https://petapixel.com/2016/05/18/interview-gregory-crewdson/ , https://artdaily.com/news/127545/images/close.gif , https://marketplace-web-git-main.preview.avantarte.com/insights/articles/gregory-crewdson-25-years-of-twilight
- "Light within light" (lamps, signals and window glow set against the dusk) led to the cold sky over the warm street, split at the roofline. https://www.artforum.com/events/gregory-crewdson-3-199613/
- Off-camera rigging (lights on cranes and out of frame) is where the single hard floodlight raking the stack from low and off to the left comes from. https://petapixel.com/2016/05/18/interview-gregory-crewdson/
- Nano Banana text guidance: keep the copy short and exact, name the lettering style and where it goes, and use the Pro model when the text has to be legible. That led to Nano Banana Pro (the previous draft was Nano Banana 2, so the round's settings vary too), "bold black block letters reading THE RULES", and "no other words". https://blog.google/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/ , https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/nano-banana-prompting-guide
- Photoreal levers: tripod, lens, camera height and deep focus, written as plain camera language with no film titles or artist names.

Brand mask rules are kept: no openings, a white ring, the red triangle off-centre over the wearer's left eye, collars, gloves on both hands, no skin, no robot features. The streetwear is modern British, with no 1920s cues.
