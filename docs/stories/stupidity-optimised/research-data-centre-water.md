# Data centres and water: what the film may say

Web research by a subagent, 2026-11-02, for Jack's note: "make it more clear that the meeting is about ai data centers
and how they are anti ai, not anti water, the whole water argument makes no sense, people just dont know how they work."
Grades: P primary data, J reputable journalism, A advocacy/industry, U unverified. Secondary summaries only: no source was
re-read in this session.

## The line in the film (088, narrator, my draft)

"Ted's leaflet says the data centre will drink the town dry. Rubbish. England's pipes leak more water in a day than every
data centre in England uses in a year. Nobody in that room knows how one works. They're not against the water. They're
against the AI."

- **The comparison rests on two sources:** England's data centres use about 1.88 billion litres of mains water a year
  (WRc for MOSL, 26 Feb 2026, P, an estimate). England's leaks are 2,617 million litres a day in 2024–25 (Environment
  Agency, "Water resources 2024 to 2025", 18 Nov 2025, P). A year of data-centre mains water is about 17 hours of leaks.
  This counts mains water in England only.
- **"Nobody in that room knows how one works"** is a joke about the room. It has some support: 89% of UK adults say they
  know little or nothing about data centres (SEC Newgate via benews.co.uk, A).
- **"They're against the AI"** is the characters' motive, not a claim about all opposition. The polls disagree:
  - Milltown Partners (May 2026, via Axios, J) found AI and Big Tech attitudes predicted opposition better than living
    near a site.
  - UMass (Sept 2026) found most opponents' reasons were local.

## Safe to say

- How cooling works: closed-loop systems recirculate their water and need only top-ups. Dry-cooled sites use almost no
  water. Evaporative sites lose water to the air, up to about 2.5 L/kWh (arXiv 2603.02705, A).
- 51% of the commercial English sites surveyed use no water for cooling, 44% are hybrid and 5% fully water-cooled. 64%
  use under 10,000 m³ a year (techUK with the Environment Agency, 18 Aug 2025, A, self-reported, about 73 sites).
- Data centres take about 0.2% of the non-household water market. The top six sites use 65% of that (WRc/MOSL, P).
- In the US, the water argument moved voters less than the electricity-bill argument (Heatmap, Sept 2025, J).
- Support drops locally: 69% back data centres nationally, 56% in their own area (YouGov for Cavendish, Mar 2026, J).

## Do not say

- "Data centres use no water." Evaporative and hybrid sites do, and the UK figures are self-reported.
- "Water worries are made up." These are real:
  - peak-day demand on hot days (WRc)
  - water-stressed Oxfordshire, Cambridgeshire and East Anglia
  - Arizona monthly peaks above 9 L/kWh
  - indirect water from generating the electricity (U)
- Any single figure for one big site. Estimates differ 15–50 times.
- That the Abingdon reservoir is "for" the Culham AI zone. That is local opinion only.
- "3 billion litres a day of leaks" as the current figure. It is an older England and Wales average.

The film's site is fictional. The model in shot 62 shows blue water pipes, which the narrator now contradicts on purpose.
