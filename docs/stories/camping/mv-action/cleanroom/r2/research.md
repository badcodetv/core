# Clean-room research r2: Bob bleak, Tarquin kinetic (web only, 2026-11-06)

## Answer
1. Bob's bleakness comes from the conditions, not from a sad face: being moved on, hosed, kept awake by noise, nowhere legal to lie down. Show the effort of getting through a cold night (wet cardboard, the bag held close, eyes open at 4am, waiting). Keep the dignity with eye level, a plain composition and stillness. Avoid tears, begging poses and pity lighting.
2. Name the expression in positive physical terms: "jaw set, mouth closed and flat, eyes heavy and red-rimmed, looking past camera". Google's guidance is to describe what you want, not to write "no smile".
3. Tarquin needs a peak-of-action frame, the instant before or during impact. Use a low wide lens, a Dutch tilt, something in the near foreground, debris or banknotes in the air, and a body caught off-balance mid-movement. A pose he is holding still will not do it.
4. Rich-villain action vocabulary: a supercar drifting or launching, a helicopter skid-step, a speedboat wake, a cash pile or a money-rain, smashing a screen on a trading floor, a shotgun at a shoot, polo mid-swing. Use each in a way that shows contempt or recklessness.
5. For image-to-video, the still must contain the motion already. The video prompt should name only what changes (action, camera move, pace), not describe the image. A blurred still gets animated as blur, so put the blur in the prompt or keep it to the background.
6. Gaps: I found no official Google guidance on stopping Nano Banana smiling, and no tested study of fast action from a mid-action start frame in Veo or Omni. Those points are marked [inference].

## 1. What rough sleeping in the UK actually looks like
- Defensive architecture: spikes, curved or segregated benches, gated doorways, "making sitting or lying down impossible". 6 in 10 of the 458 people surveyed saw more of it, and 35% "could not find anywhere to sleep or rest" [reported, Crisis survey] https://www.crisis.org.uk/about-us/latest-news/new-research-from-crisis-uncovers-dehumanising-effects-of-defensive-architecture
- Being kept awake: 1 in 5 hit by deliberate noise (loud music, recorded birdsong). Some were "moved along repeatedly in the middle of the night". 21% were "wetted down", their sleeping spot hosed while they were still in it. 63% saw more wardens and security guards [reported, same]. Visual cues: wet cardboard, a security guard's torch beam, a hi-vis jacket at the edge of frame, a sprinkler or hose puddle under the tent.
- Tents cleared: about 10 tents removed in Camden (Nov 2024) and the spot filled with bike racks and planters [reported] https://www.bigissue.com/news/housing/hostile-architecture-rough-sleepers-tents-destroyed-camden/ and https://www.camdennewjournal.co.uk/article/hostile-environment-claim-as-council-moves-bike-racks-and-planters-into-space-used-by-rough-sleepers. Visual cue: new planters or bollards placed exactly where a tent used to be.
- Mortality and scale: average age of death of people sleeping rough in London is 45 [reported, GLA] https://www.london.gov.uk/talk-london/topics/housing/rough-sleeping-london/background. Nearly 13,000 people seen sleeping rough in London in 2025/26 [reported] https://www.trustforlondon.org.uk/data/rough-sleeping-london/
- There is no average day; the environment changes constantly [reported, Connection at St Martin's] https://www.connection-at-stmartins.org.uk/news-item/a-day-in-the-life-homelessness/. Day centres register people through StreetLink [reported] https://uk.news.yahoo.com/rough-sleeper-learned-spent-week-100318469.html
- Implied visual details (not in the sources I could read, so [inference]): layers worn at once, a hood up indoors, hands wrapped round a paper cup for warmth more than for the drink, the bag strap looped round a wrist or ankle while asleep, a sleeping bag gone dark with damp, sitting upright because lying down gets you moved on, condensation inside the tent, a supermarket's floodlit car park that is never dark.

How respectful photographers show bleakness with dignity
- Bring out resilience rather than making people "look miserable and like victims"; shooting without consent "makes you part of the problem" (Marc Davenant, *Outsiders*, B&W portraits) [reported] https://www.creativeboom.com/work/the-outsiders-by-marc-davenant/
- Plain backgrounds remove preconceptions (portraits of London Big Issue vendors) [reported] https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/homeless-by-bryan-adams-features-portraits-of-londons-the-big-issue-vendors/?p=78456
- A photobook about homelessness that contains no pictures of rough sleepers, made to avoid the voyeur's view [reported] https://www.inkl.com/news/the-photobook-about-homelessness-without-a-single-rough-sleeper
- "Poverty porn" is imagery that exploits hardship for sympathy and shows subjects as helpless; examine your motive for each frame and show strengths too [reported, NGO ethics guides] https://storytelling.comnetwork.org/explore/102/nonprofit-photography-ethics-and-approaches and https://plantwithpurpose.org/ethical-photography-upholding-the-dignity-of-our-partners/
- How that translates to a frame [inference]: camera at his eye level or lower, never looking down on him. He is doing something (guarding, waiting, mending, warming his hands) rather than displaying suffering. He holds a steady gaze or looks inward. Cold, flat light (sodium orange, supermarket white, grey dawn) and no warm golden glow. Give him room in the frame and avoid a pitying close-up crop.

## 2. Kinetic action frames
- Wide lens close to the action makes movement feel "faster and more visceral". A low camera puts pavement or debris right at the lens with the subject beyond it, which gives depth and scale [reported, practitioner guides] https://outdoors.nordling.de/learn/dynamic-motion-in-wide-shots/ and https://pixflow.net/blog/low-angle-shots-explained/
- Dutch tilt adds "frantic energy", and a wide lens exaggerates the tilt. If faces warp too much it distracts [reported] https://www.skillshare.com/en/blog/the-dutch-angle-a-cinematic-shot-for-dramatic-impact/ and https://lensviewing.com/what-is-a-dutch-camera-angle/
- A low angle combined with a push-in "implies greater potential for action" [reported] https://www.praguefilminstitute.cz/?p=30427
- Anatomy of a peak-action frame [inference, from the above]: (a) the body off-balance, at the extreme of the move or the frame before impact; (b) a clear direction of motion, diagonal across the frame; (c) secondary motion already in the air: notes, glass, spray, gravel, tie and coat-tails flying; (d) foreground occlusion: a wheel, a rotor or a shoulder sweeping past the lens; (e) a low wide lens with a Dutch tilt; (f) a sharp subject against a streaked background.
- Rich-villain set pieces: a cash pile used as a stage (the money-burning scene in *The Dark Knight*) [reported] https://nofilmschool.com/2020/07/joker-film-theory; money raining over a crowd (*Now You See Me*) [reported] https://giving.fimrc.org/royal-record/now-you-see-me-the-iconic-vegas-scene-1764801205; helicopter landings, tenders and yacht decks as wealth signifiers [reported, Robb Report on *Succession*] https://www.pressreader.com/usa/robb-report-usa/20240507/282205130957351; supercar chases paired with yachts and offshore accounts [reported] https://bookmarks.reviews/reviewer/guy-bergstrom/
- Tarquin action ideas with character in them [inference]: a supercar handbrake-turning through a puddle that sprays a bus queue; leaping from a helicopter skid onto a yacht deck, champagne in hand; a speedboat jumping its own wake on the Thames with a phone at his ear; kicking a trading-floor chair across the room while screens flash red and he celebrates; throwing a fistful of notes from a convertible like confetti; a full-gallop polo swing with the mallet shattering a divot; a shotgun recoil with a clay bursting, shot from the bird's side; vaulting a turnstile at a City station without paying; sliding down a cash pile; sprinting across a skybridge with a briefcase, tie whipping behind him.

## 3. Prompting Nano Banana 2
(a) Unsmiling and weathered
- Google: "Describe what you want, not what you don't." Their example is "an empty, deserted street with no signs of traffic" rather than "no cars" [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/multimodal/gemini-image-generation-best-practices. The Veo guide says the same: describe the exclusion as a positive scene [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- Google's own refinement example, "Change the character's expression to be more serious", shows that expression is steerable by plain words [vendor, same Vertex page]. With a reference attached, "describe the change in emotion or pose while maintaining the identity" [vendor, Google AI dev guide] https://dev.to/googleai/nano-banana-pro-prompting-guide-strategies-1h9n
- Emotion words drive energy: "mid-celebration" and "laughing expression" push the model toward dynamic, smiling images [reported] https://www.digit.in/how-to/general/nano-banana-2-tips-how-to-make-5-different-types-of-images.html. So cut warm or cheerful words anywhere in the prompt ("friendly", "content", "warm glow", "cosy", "peaceful") [inference].
- Generators showed men older and more neutral or angry, and women younger and smiling (study of 3 models; smiles scored by a face API) [tested, arXiv 2403.02726] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.02726. No study found showing a universal default smile.
- Wording that should work [inference, untested here]: "mouth closed and level, lips pressed thin, jaw tight against the cold, brow lowered, eyes heavy-lidded and red-rimmed from no sleep, gaze fixed somewhere past the camera, skin windburnt and grey, stubble, expression of someone enduring". Put it first in the subject line, and remove anything that summons a smile (talking to someone, being given something, eye contact with a kind stranger).
- "No smile" style negatives are called counterproductive on Gemini by one community reference [reported, unofficial] https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/claude-code-skills@0.5.0/data/skills/image-generation/reference/gemini-image.md. Google only says to prefer positives.

(b) Action that isn't static
- Use photographic terms, e.g. "low-angle perspective" and "wide-angle shot" [vendor, Vertex]. Name the pose and its direction in the frame [vendor, dev.to Google guide].
- For complex scenes, "split your request into steps" [vendor, Vertex]. For a frame that has to animate: one action, one direction [inference].
- "Mid-action" or "mid-movement" wording builds in motion potential [reported, single vendor blog] https://blog.picassoia.com/turn-ai-images-into-short-videos-with-veo-3
- Verbs in the participle or at the instant ("mid-leap, both feet off the deck", "the instant the glass shatters") rather than "standing with" or "posing" [inference].

## 4. Image-to-video start frames for fast motion
- Veo formula: [Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style]. Camera terms: dolly, tracking, crane, aerial, POV; "whip pan" for "rapid movement"; handheld for chaos; pacing words "fast-paced action" and "slow-motion" [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1 and https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide
- Timestamp prompting ([00:00-00:02] ...) splits actions inside one clip [vendor, same]. First and last frame gives a defined endpoint for a move [vendor].
- Treat the image as fixed and write only what changes. Describing the image back tends to give "subtle jittering instead of real movement" [reported] https://www.imaginary.app/blog/veo-31-image-to-video-prompts-that-actually-animate-not-just-wiggle-a-beginner-g. Name the action, the camera move and the pace [reported] https://blog.segmind.com/veo-3-image-to-video-guide/
- A blurred source gets animated, blur included ("it'll just animate the blur") [reported, Kling tutorial] https://crepal.ai/blog/?p=7651. So keep Tarquin's face and hands sharp and put the blur only in the background or as streaks [inference].
- Pair a mid-action keyframe with restrained camera movement [reported] https://videopromptlab.vercel.app/image-to-video-prompt-generator. Sharper stills reduce face and hand warping [reported] https://martini.art/en/blog/how-to-turn-image-into-video
- Avoid [inference]: a symmetrical neutral stance (it animates as a wiggle); several competing actions; a camera move against the subject's motion; describing the money or debris as "frozen"; prompts that make a crowd the subject.
