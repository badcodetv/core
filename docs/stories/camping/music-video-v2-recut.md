## 🎬 Shot design: making it artful and not preachy (2026-09-29)

Jack pasted the bullets above back on 2026-09-29 and asked for cinematic, artful and non-preachy ways to show them,
with no AI slop. This section is the `shot-craft` design pass. Nothing here is generated yet.

It draws on four sources:
- the repo toolkit (`docs/cinematography/`)
- three web sweeps run the same day: the verified defences, dignity and in-world text, and a Nano Banana delta
  (now `docs/google-flow/nano-banana-2.md` §fourteenth pass)

### The five ideas that hold it together

1. **One image system, held for the whole film.** The verified political videos that land without lecturing
   (Ghost Town, Common People, This Is America) each commit to a single visual device and never explain it.
   `[verified examples; the lesson is opinion]`
   - **Ours is the sitting height of a person on the pavement.** Every shot of the street is taken from where
     Bob's eyes are, never from standing height and never looking down on anyone.
   - Tarquin's world is the only place the camera stands up.
2. **The gap sits inside one frame, not across a cut.** Rich and poor share the picture: a warm window behind a cold
   pavement, a lit shop behind a man with a card. It is argued without a line of text. `[This Is America, verified]`
3. **Nobody states the moral.** The eleven lines are the only words, and each is written by an object in its own
   physics: dot-matrix LEDs, till roll, frosted glass film, a finger in condensation. The words feel found, not
   added. `[craft opinion, from Sherlock, Panic Room and Stranger Than Fiction]`
4. **Gritty, never pitiful, never fun** (Jack's ruling, plus the dignity research):
   - Eye level.
   - Lit the same way as Tarquin: no saintly spotlight kept for the poor man.
   - No lingering on dirt, wounds or sleeping bodies.
   - At least one beat where the person looks back at the lens on their own terms, closed-mouthed.
   - Show them doing ordinary things: counting coins, wringing out a sock, keeping a bag dry.
   - Show the cause (a stud, a price, a barrier), not only the symptom.
   `[Homeless Hub, Street Roots, Museum of Homelessness, verified]`
5. **Tarquin's fall is never a comeuppance joke.** The bridge lands as "same side all along", not "serves you
   right". `[research opinion, and it matches the canon]`

### Intro: "The Catalogue" (after s1's studs)

**Job:** turn a line of spikes into a worldwide habit before a single person appears, without a word.
**Register:** the Bechers' *indifferent typology* (`registers.md` §1). It's the register the toolkit says "we have
never used". The menace comes from subtracting drama.
- 🔑 **Every plate is identical in grammar:**
  - frontal and level
  - 35mm lens, the same distance of about four metres
  - the same dusk after rain
  - the camera **30 cm off the ground, the eye-line of someone lying down**
  The only thing that changes is the object and the city. The repetition is the argument: this is not one bad
  landlord, it's a design language.
- **Visible cost (gate 2) in every plate, small:** a flattened cardboard sheet, a lone sock, a plastic bag
  snagged on a point. Spend the biggest one last: a **dry pale rectangle the size of a body on the otherwise wet
  ground**, where someone slept until the rain came.
- **The world is alive, not sterile** (a named slop tell): one passer-by per plate, cut off by the frame or
  motion-blurred, walking past and **never looking**. It plants "you keep on walking" before the lyric.
- **Motion:** each plate is an Omni clip with the camera locked. Only the rain, the drips or a passing car move,
  so no camera budget is spent. The sprinkler plate is the one that *acts*.
- **Cut:** one plate per bar or two, even durations (a catalogue is allowed to be regular), no text.
- **Order** (verified: place, year, what it is):

### Verse 1

### rc02: Guangzhou, the pyramids under the flyover · round 1 · written 2026-09-29 (result not yet logged)

**Spec (shot-craft):**
- **Job:** the second plate of the catalogue. It turns London's one ledge into a field of hundreds, so the viewer
  learns the scale of the habit.
- **Register:** indifferent typology: frontal, level, 35mm at about 4 m. The camera sits 30 cm off the ground,
  where a sleeper's head would be. Continuity with s1: Portra 800.
- **Depth:** FG the nearest pyramids, large, at the lens · MG the field receding into the dark under the deck · BG
  the lit road beyond, one scooter passing, motion-blurred.
- **Light:** one white LED streetlamp beyond the flyover's edge rakes low across the field, and each pyramid throws
  a long shadow toward the lens. The lamp head and the lit tips are the bright anchor. Under the deck it falls to
  near-black with detail.
- **Visible cost:** a flattened, sodden cardboard sheet lying at the field's near edge, and a plastic bag snagged on
  one point.
- **Rain (§32):** drips fall from the deck's edge against the dark underside. No falling streaks against the bright
  road.
- **Withheld:** no person under the deck. The scooter rider never looks.

Settings: Nano Banana Pro · 16:9 · x2 · 2K
Attach: nothing (no Character, no reference)
**Paste into: Flow prompt box**

```
SCENE:

A plain documentary photograph from a typology series recording the objects cities put where homeless people used to sleep. Kodak Portra 800, a single exposure on a tripod at dusk just after rain, under a concrete road flyover in Guangzhou, China. Fine grain in the shadows, muted colour, flat and matter-of-fact, unposed and ordinary.

Camera and framing: A 35mm lens at f/8, the camera set low on the ground with the lens only thirty centimetres above the concrete, the height of a sleeping person's head, facing straight into the space under the flyover, level and frontal, about four metres from the nearest objects. Everything from near to far is acceptably sharp. The underside of the flyover deck forms a heavy dark band across the top third of the frame, held up by square concrete pillars on the left and right.

Subject: The whole ground under the flyover is covered by a field of hundreds of small cement pyramids, each about knee-high with a blunt point, cast in rough grey cement and set in tight staggered rows with barely a hand's width between them, so nobody could lie down anywhere. They run from the bottom edge of the frame back into the dark under the deck. The cement is stained with grime and water runs, a few tips are chipped, and litter and dead leaves are caught between the rows.

Foreground: At the near edge of the field, across the bottom left of the frame, lies a flattened sheet of brown cardboard, sodden and darkened by the rain, half caught on the first row of points. A thin white plastic bag is snagged on one pyramid near it.

Background: Beyond the far side of the flyover, the city street is still lit: low shopfronts with lit signboards far out of focus and unreadable, and one man on a small electric scooter crossing from left to right, motion-blurred, head down in a dark rain jacket, not looking toward the camera.

Light: One tall white LED streetlamp stands just beyond the flyover's edge on the right and is the only strong light. It rakes in low across the field, catching the tops of the pyramids and throwing their long shadows toward the camera, and the lamp head itself burns out to white. Under the deck the light falls away into near-darkness, but the cement keeps its texture. The wet concrete between the rows holds each light as a short vertical smear, with a mirror image only in one shallow puddle.

The rain: It has just stopped. Water drips from the edge of the deck in a line of falling drops, visible only against the dark underside of the flyover. The ground is wet, not flooded.

Details: Real surface texture, rough cement, grime and fine natural grain, ordinary and unstyled.

Constraints: The pyramids are cement, dull and grey, never shiny. No signs, shopfronts, vehicles or walls anywhere in the frame carry readable lettering.

Compose for a 16:9 frame.

Thanks.
```

## 🫁 Breathing room: a music video with some lyrics (proposal, 2026-09-30)

**Jack, 2026-09-30:** *"a bit too much with the lyrics on screen, there needs to be more filler in between them, so
it is not just a lyric music video, rather a music video with some lyrics."* This is a proposal from the
`shot-craft` re-cut pass (`motion-and-cutting.md` §4) and `story-craft` (`forms.md` §5). Nothing here is ruled or
built yet.

### Diagnosis: two layers, not one
1. **Too much text.** "Lyrics on every card" (2026-09-29) took the cut from 11 text moments to **39 text renders**
   (`recut-signs.json`). Estimated from the timeline table above (not measured on the live timeline, because the
   bridge was down): **about 80% of the film after the intro has words on screen.** A device spent everywhere
   lands nowhere (story-craft principle 26).
2. **Too many cards.** Even with blank cards, nearly every shot is a person facing the lens holding a sign. That
   shot *is* lyric-video grammar, so the eye waits for the next word. Fixing (1) alone won't fix it.

### What the research says (web sweep 2026-09-30; V = source read, S = search summary only)
- **Every precedent that works treats text as a device or a section, not the wallpaper.** Dylan's cards carry
  selected words only, with a human world going on behind them (V). INXS "Mediate" gives the cards the second
  half only, and the last card reads "Sax Solo", handing the instrumental back to picture (S). Prince's
  "Sign o' the Times" is all text, and it is remembered as an early *lyric video* (S), the thing we're avoiding.
- **The protest videos we keep citing have no text at all.** Ghost Town is empty streets at dawn (S);
  Unfinished Sympathy is a walk past an indifferent street (S); Jorja Smith's "Blue Lights" is ordinary men doing
  ordinary things (S).
- **Text costs the picture.** In an eye-tracking study, subtitles cut the time spent looking at the image, 92% of
  viewers felt they lost visual information, and they preferred reading to looking (V,
  [PMC10723748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10723748/)). So every card is a moment the picture goes
  largely unseen, and a card should never straddle a cut.
- **Timing:** BBC subtitle practice is about 0.33 s per word and at least 1.5 s between captions (V, via
  [Clevercast](https://www.clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines/)). A 60/40 main-to-B-roll split is a blog
  rule of thumb, not a finding (S).
- **Without poverty porn:** eye level, natural light, the camera reacting, ordinary tasks, and landscapes that
  *carry* the feeling (Nomadland's DP, V; I, Daniel Blake, V). This matches rulings 4 and 5 already.

### The proposal: four rules
1. **Back to the eleven.** Return every `*-lyric` render to its raw clip (same in/out, `changeMediaFilePath`).
   That takes the film to about 12 text moments (line 4's two halves land once each), roughly 35–40 s of 240 s. The call wanted this too ("only on chosen
   lines, briefly").
2. **Never two text shots in a row.** At least two picture-only shots (about 5–8 s) between any two lyrics, so
   each one arrives after a breath and pops. Text holds inside one shot: 0.33 s a word plus about 1 s.
3. **Cards to lens become the chorus motif, not the verse wallpaper.** Blank-card match cuts stay in the three
   choruses (three times makes a motif). Verses belong to the world. Wai (line 4) and Bob's own card (line 5)
   are the exceptions, because the card is the point.
4. **Whole sections with no words:** the intro, the instrumental (88.9–96.68), chorus 2 and the final chorus.
   Chorus 1 keeps one text beat for the hook.
