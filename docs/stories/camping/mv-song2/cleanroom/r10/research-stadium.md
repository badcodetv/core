## 1. How real shows stage the blackout and reveal

- Stadium designers use a full blackout broken by one beam or one spotlight as the large "macro" statement. **C** (a vendor blog that sells LED wristbands): https://xylobands.com/blog/layers-of-light-arena-stadium-tour-design , https://www.xylobands.com/blog/macro-micro-stadium-tour-lighting
- Football stadium LED floodlights on DMX can black out instantly and run chases and music-synced shows. They are used before player introductions. **B/C**: Signify blog https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/blog/cities/cometh-the-match-cometh-the-light-show ; La Bombonera has 248 Thorn 5700K floods on DMX with pre-programmed dynamic scenes **B**: https://iaks.sport/news/new-floodlights-la-bombonera-and-opel-arena
- UEFA's rules for these shows: a pre-match show must finish before the players appear, and match lighting must be full by the time they leave the tunnel. So "floodlights as a light show" is a real, regulated thing at football grounds. **A**: UEFA Guide for Stadium Entertainment Lighting 2023, https://www.sporteimpianti.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/UEFA-Guide-for-Stadium-Entertainment-Lighting-2023-edition.pdf
- Realism point: old metal-halide floods take minutes to restrike (one source says up to 15, another up to 45). Only LED floods can slam on one bank at a time. **C** (LED vendors): https://www.accessfixtures.com/avoid-restrike-time-led-sports-lighters/ , https://ohsonline.com/Articles/2015/09/14/Safety-and-Reliability-in-High-Mast-Light-Tower-and-Area-Lighting.aspx . *Inference*: write "LED floodlight banks", not generic "stadium lights", if the snap should look believable.
- Scale comes from a reveal move: Coldplay's Dodger Stadium film used a cable camera rising over the roof to reveal the stage and crowd, then a helicopter pull-back into the city. **B** (summary of a British Cinematographer interview; the page returned 403, so I worked from the search snippet): https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/paul-dugdale-and-brett-turnbull-on-capturing-coldplays-music-of-the-spheres-live-at-river-plate-concert-film/
- U2 at Sphere opens by appearing to split the screen into a widening band of light that reveals the band. **B** (American Cinematographer, first-hand): https://theasc.com/articles/u2uv-achtung-baby-sphere
- One stadium designer keeps the IMAG screens off at the start so the audience's eyes adjust to the stage first. **B**: https://www.wallpaper.com/art/music/lighting-designers-tobias-rylander-and-michael-straun-interview
- In a Super Bowl halftime show, the stage transforms in the first seconds, and in the wides moving beams and colour fields carry the sense of scale. **C**: https://www.documentarytube.com/blog/what-makes-a-super-bowl-halftime-show-feel-like-a-spectacle-stage-lighting-and-sound-explained
- Daft Punk Alive 2007, the closest precedent for a hidden performer in a lit structure: a pyramid of LEDs and screens, the robots firing light cues from inside, opening on "Robot Rock / Oh Yeah". I could **not** verify a blackout-then-reveal moment. Only spam pages claim one. **B**: https://www.spin.com/?p=13227 , https://varsity.co.uk/music/21292 , https://ca.news.yahoo.com/daft-punk-talks-kanye-west-000436207.html
- Haze makes beams visible as shafts, and backlit performers in haze read as silhouettes. **B** (concert photographers): https://www.photographytalk.com/photography-articles/437-photography-tipthe-excitement-and-challenges-of-shooting-live-music-concerts-part-2 , https://photographylife.com/concert-photography-tips
- *Inference*, with no source found: "huge rather than generic" comes from (a) a size cue such as a tiny lone figure against a vast dark bowl, (b) depth layers of haze, beams and crowd receding, and (c) the crowd reacting to the light rather than just being lit.

## 2. Cutting light hits to the beat

- Murch's rule is to cut just *before* the blink, and he can find the frame ahead of it. This is the principle behind cutting fractionally ahead of an event. **B** (Murch interviews): https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-bcpc/ , https://www.webofstories.com/play/56385
- One editor reports that a cut one frame late lets the sound hit register first, and a cut one frame early gives a jolt that suits action. **C**: https://www.eoshd.com/comments/topic/46696-editing/
- Workflow: lay the audio first and place cuts on the waveform and on markers. **B**: https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/
- Removing one frame from performance footage was not noticed. **C**: https://creativecow.net/?p=2437971
- A sequence of bright flash frames can trigger seizures, so check brightness as well as cut rate. **C**: https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/clip-duration-below-a-second/m-p/10470981 . *Inference*: run any strobe passage through a PSE checker before upload.
- I found no source giving shots per beat or a density curve toward a drop. *Inference* for this track: hold black or near-black through the 2.8 s riser, put the screen flash as the first image, put the first bank on the 2.8 s hit, add a bank per beat (0.682 s), and land the full crowd reveal on the bass at about 4 s.

## 3. Lighting vocabulary and real looks

- Football floods are about 5700K cool daylight-white, TV-grade (CRI 86, TLCI above 80) and flicker-free for super slow-motion. **B**: https://iaks.sport/news/new-floodlights-la-bombonera-and-opel-arena . Broadcast stadiums run at roughly 600–1000+ lux. **C**: https://www.chz-lighting.com/blog/what-are-the-led-stadium-lighting-standards.html
- The terms that turn up in practice are DMX, blackout, chase, beam, spotlight, haze, LED floodlight, and pre-match light show. **B/C** (sources above). *Inference*: cool white floods plus coloured stage beams is the real mix, two colour temperatures in one frame.
- Spots aimed into the lens make the camera underexpose the subject, and photographers accept clipped highlights and shadows to keep the face. **B**: https://shutterbug.com/node/587093 , https://www.uniquephoto.com/community/qa/can-i-recover-more-facial-detail-from-a-raw-concert-photo-shot-in-dim-stage-lighting-and-how-can-i-improve-this-next-time . I found nothing citable on flare or veiling glare.

## 4. Nano Banana 2 and Veo / Flow prompting

- **Text (stills)**: put the words in quotes, name the font style and where it sits, and work out the text before you make the image. **A** (Google): https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana . Keep it to 3–5 words. **C**: https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide . Give the text a physical surface. **C**: https://www.roborhythms.com/nano-banana-2-prompts-for-content-creators/ . One guide rates Pro above NB2 for text. **C**: https://www.leonardo.ai/news/nano-banana-prompt-guide
- **Text (video)**: Veo often garbles text, and upscaling just makes the gibberish sharper. Common fixes are a flat, front-on, glare-free surface held long enough to read, or putting the word in afterwards in post. **C**: https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/text-in-ai-video-is-still-tricky-2026-9-prompt-patterns-that-make-labels-signs-a , https://arsturn.com/blog/how-to-get-clean-text-in-veo-3-a-guide-to-fixing-ai-gibberish . Veo also adds unwanted garbled subtitles. **B** (MIT Tech Review): https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/amp/ . *Inference*: the safest route is a CRT screen showing a solid colour in the clip, with the word composited onto it in Premiere.
- **Darkness**: Google says to describe the light that *is* there (for example "dark stage, lit by a single spotlight") and to phrase exclusions positively ("empty street", not "no cars"). **A**: Veo 3.1 guide https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1 and the NB guide above. Too much stated light washes a night scene out, so name one weak motivated source. **C**: https://veo3prompts.pages.dev/prompt/KER5NaViZS . Google's guides say nothing about lights changing mid-clip. *Inference*: a light switching on inside a clip is unreliable. Cut between a dark clip and a lit clip instead.
- **Crowds**: busy crowds are a known weak spot in Veo image-to-video, with identity swaps and edge artifacts. **C**: https://geekchamp.com/?p=479117 . Faces start repeating as headcount rises and in identical clothes, so give people varied roles and outfits. **C**: https://wikidocs.net/blog/@tasol_blog/5798/ . A long lens or shallow focus turns the background into soft blobs instead of faces to get wrong. **C**: Reddit snapshot https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1pwugwh/snapshots/2026-02-21T04%3A40%3A34.400181Z . Don't give a number for crowd size. **C**: https://ltx.io/blog/crowd-scene-generation . Users report faces morphing in Flow even with references. **C**: https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/veo-flow-generation-issues-lost-credits-consistency-problems-and-excessive-failed-generations/147374
- **AI gloss**: quality words like "8K", "masterpiece" and "ultra-realistic" push the output towards CGI. Symmetrical, centred, eye-contact framing reads as staged. Name a real stock or camera rather than writing "film grain". Flat front light makes skin look plastic, and NB2 runs slightly smooth. **C**: https://www.hedra.com/blog/make-ai-images-look-like-real-photos-prompting , https://oakgen.ai/blog/fix-plastic-skin-ai-portraits . Google suggests naming a camera such as GoPro, Fujifilm or a disposable. **A**: the NB guide.

## 5. A matched dark/lit pair

- Nano Banana edits: state exactly what stays the same, and change one thing per pass. **A**: the Google NB guide. Wording like "only relight the scene" works better than "make it moodier". If it drifts, step back one pass. **C**: https://artlist.io/blog/how-to-edit-images-with-nano-banana/ . Day-to-night edits have been tested on NB2, but nobody has published how well they held. **C**: https://wiro.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-6-before-after-image-edits/
- Up to 14 reference images, using the pattern reference + relationship + new scenario. **A**: the Google NB guide.
- Veo first/last frame makes a transition between two stills, and you describe the transition in the prompt. **A**: the Veo 3.1 guide. Dark-to-lit is a documented showcase. Match the camera and composition across the pair, because big jumps in lighting or perspective confuse the interpolation. **C**: https://www.jaiportal.com/model/google-veo-3-1-first-last-frame , https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/veo-31-firstlast-frame-transitions-a-creator-workflow-for-smooth-beforeafter-rev
- *Inference*: make the **lit** still first, because it is the hard one with the crowd, then derive the dark version from it by editing only the light. Darkening hides crowd faults, and brightening invents new ones. For a hard cut, animate each still with a locked camera so the two plates line up on the beat.
