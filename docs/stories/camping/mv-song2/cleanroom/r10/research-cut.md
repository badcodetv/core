# Research: cutting a three-strand hip-hop bridge (r10, sealed web research, 2026-11-09)

Grid: 88 BPM. 1 beat = 0.682 s (about 16 frames at 24 fps). 1 bar = 2.73 s. A bridge of about 40 s is about 14–15 bars.
Tiers: **A** = first-hand editor/director or official/standard; **B** = credible practitioner or academic; **C** = forum, vendor blog or student work. **Inference** = mine, not from a source.

## 1. Intercutting performance and narrative

- **Performance first, narrative fills the gaps.** Chris Rodriguez (Anthem Films): "I always tackle the performance first". That pass leaves 10–20 gaps where the performance was weak, and the narrative goes there. Pacing is a separate final pass, checked against the treatment. **A** — https://imvdb.com/blog/2013/03/editor-profile-chris-rodriguez
- **Two cuts from one shoot.** Ben Guzman delivered a narrative cut and a performance-only cut, so the strands need to stand up on their own. **A** — https://imvdb.com/blog/2013/02/editor-profile-ben-guzman
- **The direct precedent for this bridge** is Eminem ft. Rihanna, *Love the Way You Lie* (dir. Joseph Kahn, 2010). A couple's fight (Megan Fox, Dominic Monaghan, no lip-sync) is intercut with the two singers performing on their own. **A/B** (credits) — https://imvdb.com/video/eminem/love-the-way-you-lie , https://www.promonews.tv/videos/2010/08/09/eminem%E2%80%99s-love-way-you-lie-feat-rihanna-joseph-kahn/5935
- **The opposite approach** is Kendrick Lamar and Taylour Paige, *We Cry Together* (dir. Schreier / Free / Lamar, 2022). It is a back-and-forth argument rap shot in one take with live vocals, and it ends in reconciliation. It shows the argument can carry itself without cuts. **A/B** — https://www.papermag.com/kendrick-lamar-we-cry-together
- **Who speaks is who we see** (inference, consistent with the sources above). In the performance strand, cut to rapper B when B's line starts. In the drama strand, show the *listener's* reaction on the other rapper's line: the argument lands on the person being hit by it. Lip-sync shots must be on the right mouth, while drama shots can belong to either person.
- **Breaking continuity is a convention of the form.** This covers 180° breaks and jumps in time and space, and editing that follows the musical *phrase* as well as the beat. Carol Vernallis, *Experiencing Music Video* (2004) and "The Kindest Cut" (summary slides). **B** — https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/carol-vernalliss-music-video-theory/73507931

## 2. Shot lengths and where the cut sits

- **What the measurements show.** In a large set of official music videos, about **two-thirds** of clips have shot durations synced at **beat level** and about **one-fifth** at **bar level**. Editors often **anticipate** musical events. Genre matters: reggaeton is cut faster than country. Prétet, Richard & Peeters 2021. **B** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00970
- **Cutting on the beat is expected, and it goes stale.** Ian McLaughlin (CHIEF.) says knowing when beat-cutting has become predictable is part of the job. **A** (interview, page blocked to fetch; quoted from the search snippet) — https://lbbonline.com/news/more-than-just-cutting-to-the-beat-the-nuance-of-editing-music-videos
- **Vinnie Hobbs**, editor of Kendrick's *Alright*, was told that "cutting to the beat is not hard". He moved to spacing cuts "like a musician spacing notes" and uses footage speed to shape rhythm. **A/B** (reported talk) — https://nofilmschool.com/from-music-videos-to-feature-films-editing-lessons-that-translate , https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2020/08/28/why-music-video-pros-choose-adobe-premiere-pro
- **Max Windows:** "good editing is judged on the frequency of the edit, but this does not always constitute a quality cut." **A** — https://shots.net/news/view/cut-the-tempo-how-to-edit-for-music-video
- **Picture slightly before the beat.** Editors commonly say to cut 2–4 frames early so the hit reads on the new shot. **C** (blogs and vendors, no data). The perception science backs the *direction*. Under ITU-R BT.1359, viewers detect sound arriving *before* picture at about 45 ms but tolerate sound arriving *after* picture up to about 125 ms. So picture 1–3 frames early (about 40–125 ms) is safe, and picture late is not. **A** (standard; I did not open the primary PDF, so the sign convention is unverified) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization , https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-S.pdf
- **Downbeat or snare** (practitioner lore, **C**). The kick or downbeat is for strand changes and structural cuts. Snares (beats 2 and 4) are for punches inside a strand. Vendor guides say "kicks for primary cuts, snares for transitions". https://beatsyncpro.ai/blog/boom-bap-music-video-editing-guide-2026.html
- **Emotion outranks rhythm.** Walter Murch's Rule of Six ranks emotion (51%), story (23%) and rhythm (10%). When a cut can't do everything, drop rhythm before emotion. **A** (Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye*) — https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch

## 3. Literal lyric cutaways

- **The risk is real.** "Literal music video" is a parody genre (Dustin McLean's *Take On Me*, 2008) built on images that illustrate each line too closely. Stereogum's split: literal = "images line up pretty closely with the lyrics", interpretive = separate. **B/C** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_music_video
- **Rules for timing the inserts** (inference from the above, not sourced):
  - Land the insert **on the stressed syllable of the key noun**, or 1–3 frames before it, never after. A late insert reads as a caption.
  - Hold it **half a beat to one beat** (about 0.34–0.68 s, 8–16 frames). Long enough to read, too short to become a scene.
  - **Two to four inserts in the whole bridge**, not one per line. A literal image on every line is the parody.
  - Let the image **be in the scene's world, or move**. A still stock-style illustration is the cheesy version, and a character-specific image is not. Prefer an image that is *slightly off* the word (Vernallis's "partial" relation) over a dictionary picture.
  - Cluster the inserts in the **argument** section, where fast cutting hides them. Remove them from the regret and toast sections.

## 4. Cut rhythm by mood

- **Argument:** cut on the beat, every 1–2 beats (0.7–1.4 s), alternating rapper A / rapper B / drama. Inserts live here. (Inference, consistent with the genre-pace finding in §2.)
- **Regret:** cuts every 1–2 **bars** (2.7–5.5 s). Mostly drama strand, singles, and the listener's face. Fewer strand changes.
- **Reconciliation / toast:** one **long hold** of 1–2 bars on the two-shot. The clink is the cut point: cut *on* the clink or 1–2 frames before it, ideally to a close-up of the glasses. "Silence/pause lets the next hit land": **C** — https://suno.bi/blog/silence-and-pause-dramatic-mv-method-en
- **Contrast does the work.** Vernallis lists "changes in pace, including extreme changes" as a core device. **B**. A slow section only feels slow if the section before it was fast.

## 5. The drum stop, the unison shout, the drop

- Practitioner options (**C**, no editor interview found that addresses this directly):
  - **Hold:** no cut across the stop. One frame, both rappers in a two-shot shouting. The absence of cuts matches the absence of drums.
  - **Black or solid frame:** 0.5–1 s before the chorus "as a charge-up", **once per song** at most.
  - **Smash cut** to the widest, loudest chorus image on the chorus downbeat, picture 1–2 frames early (§2).
- **Inference for this bridge:** both strands converge in the unison shout. Performance and drama can merge into one two-shot or a single split image, because the lyric has reunited them. Then smash to the chorus. If there is a black frame, put it in the gap after the shout, not instead of it.

## 6. A skeleton to test (inference, about 14.5 bars)

| Section | Bars | Cut every | Strands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argument | ~5 | 1–2 beats | perf A/B on the speaker, drama reaction, 2–3 inserts |
| Regret | ~4 | 1–2 bars | drama-led, one performance face per line |
| Toast | ~2 | 1 hold, cut on the clink | drama two-shot, glass close-up |
| Unison over the drum stop | ~1–2 | none | both together, one frame |
| Drop | downbeat | smash, 1–2 frames early | chorus |
