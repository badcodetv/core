## 2. Cutting light hits to the beat

- Murch's rule is to cut just *before* the blink, and he can find the frame ahead of it. This is the principle behind cutting fractionally ahead of an event. **B** (Murch interviews): https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-bcpc/ , https://www.webofstories.com/play/56385
- One editor reports that a cut one frame late lets the sound hit register first, and a cut one frame early gives a jolt that suits action. **C**: https://www.eoshd.com/comments/topic/46696-editing/
- Workflow: lay the audio first and place cuts on the waveform and on markers. **B**: https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/
- Removing one frame from performance footage was not noticed. **C**: https://creativecow.net/?p=2437971
- A sequence of bright flash frames can trigger seizures, so check brightness as well as cut rate. **C**: https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/clip-duration-below-a-second/m-p/10470981 . *Inference*: run any strobe passage through a PSE checker before upload.
- I found no source giving shots per beat or a density curve toward a drop. *Inference* for this track: hold black or near-black through the 2.8 s riser, put the screen flash as the first image, put the first bank on the 2.8 s hit, add a bank per beat (0.682 s), and land the full crowd reveal on the bass at about 4 s.

- Nano Banana edits: state exactly what stays the same, and change one thing per pass. **A**: the Google NB guide. Wording like "only relight the scene" works better than "make it moodier". If it drifts, step back one pass. **C**: https://artlist.io/blog/how-to-edit-images-with-nano-banana/ . # Research: face-off bridge (A) and unmasked argument (B)

## A1. How rap videos and battles stage two rappers trading lines

- **Handheld as a dance partner, not a documentary look.** Rodrigo Prieto operated the 8 Mile battles handheld himself. He calls it "a dance between the rappers and myself with a camera", following the rhythm rather than faking documentary. The crew didn't know the bars in advance, so the camera reacted to them. The crowd was backlit with cool-white Kino Flos and the performers were lit by a harsh spotlight. **A** (ASC interview) https://theasc.com/articles/shooting-8-mile-prieto
  - Time's review says it was two-camera handheld coverage throughout, and Grantland says the cameras worked from inside the crowd. **B** https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,1003634-2,00.html · https://grantland.com/features/oral-history-8-mile-rap-battles-eminem/?print=1
- **Gaze switching is what keeps a face-off tense.** A frame-by-frame study of a Clips vs Rex battle found the following:
  - The winner, Clips, moved his gaze between his opponent and the crowd, landed finger-points and "gun-hand" snaps on stressed syllables, and let silences build.
  - Rex looked at the spectators about 93% of the time, which defused the face-off. His broad, metronomic gestures produced fewer peaks.
  - Crowd responses more than doubled on Clips's turns.
  - **B** (peer-reviewed, Global Hip Hop Studies) https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/from-bars-to-bodies-multimodal-power-plays-in-trex-vs-charlie-clips/
  - *Synthesis:* rapping AT someone means eyes on the opponent, with the point landing on the stressed word. Turning out to camera is a release you use on purpose, such as the unison moment.
- **Close quarters and the "you" point.**
  - Battle accounts describe rappers working "an inch away from your face", stare-downs (Murda Mook vs Loaded Lux), and a pointed hand meaning "YOU" to tie the bar to the man opposite. **C/B** https://www.complex.com/music/a/c-vernon-coleman/what-happens-when-battle-rapping-goes-wrong · https://www.thedailybeast.com/americas-poets-battle-rap-gets-real
  - Gestures are used as weapons: to back up your own bar or to react to the opponent's. **C** https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-win-a-rap-battle-an-experts-guide/
- **Named examples to watch:**
  - **ArrDee & Aitch, "War"** (dir. Gabriella Kingsley, 2022). The two "battle it out" on a Peacehaven beach. **B** https://promonews.tv/videos/2022/02/10/arrdee-aitch-war-gabriella-kingsley/74903 (page did not load; details are from the search snippet only)
  - **Lil Wayne & Eminem, "Drop the World"** (dir. Chris Robinson). The visuals switch when Eminem's verse comes in. **B** https://hiphop-n-more.com/2010/02/chris-robinson-speaks-on-drop-the-world-video
  - **Bodied** (Joseph Kahn, 2017). Kahn walks through the opening battle shot by shot on video, but there is no transcript on the page. **B** https://www.slashfilm.com/bodied-opening-scene-breakdown
  - **Hamilton, "Cabinet Battle"**. Two men take turns at anachronistic mics, and the staging is reportedly influenced by 8 Mile. **C** https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Cabinet_Battle
  - **Epic Rap Battles of History**. Shot on green screen, and at least some performers recorded separately. They face each other only in the edit. **C** https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/EpicRapBattlesOfHistory
  - *Synthesis:* ERB is evidence that a face-off can be built out of two single-subject shots with matched eyelines, which is the cheap route for AI.

## A2. Body language: arguing, then softening into regret

- **Dominance reads as expansion.** Expansive, open posture signals power and constricted posture signals submission. **B** https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/blog/cutting-edge-leadership/201211/the-nonverbal-power-cues-men-and-women · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7870468/table/T1
  - Practitioner lists of hostility cues include chin jut, chest puff, finger jabbing and space invasion. These aren't tested as causal. **C** https://bodylanguageproject.com/nonverbal-dictionary/category/hostility/page/3 · https://www.scienceofpeople.com/aggressive-body-language/
- **Distance and height carry the relationship.** Height signals dominance and side-by-side signals equals. In Shōgun's hot-spring scene the two start in separate frames, then one moves into the other's frame and they end level in a shared shot, so the reconciliation happens without any dialogue. **B** https://nofilmschool.com/blocking-not-boring
- **Regret, shame and guilt:**
  - The stereotyped shame display is a slumped posture, head tilted down, gaze averted and speech inhibited. A downcast, gaze-averted picture drew more empathy than any other expressed emotion. **B** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11078964 · https://doaj.org/article/cf35f851b3324890a57f773f5cade685
  - Gaze aversion in guilt specifically is "mixed" in the studies. No study looks at posture in guilt; embarrassment and shame suggest a "collapsed, diminished" body. **B** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60980-0.pdf
  - Visible nonverbal remorse makes an apology land better than words alone. **B** (same PMC source)
- *Synthesis for the arc.* This matches 8 Mile and the battle study. It is untested.
  - **Argue:** close distance, square shoulders, points on the stressed beat, eyes locked.
  - **Regret:** a step back, head down, gaze off, hands lowered, the prop hanging.
  - **Unity:** level heights, the two drift into one frame, then both turn to camera together.

## A4. AI: two people in one frame, props, contact

- **Official Google:**
  - Veo 3.1 "Ingredients to Video" takes character and setting reference images. Google's dialogue workflow builds a two-character scene as separate shots (a medium on A, then a shot "focusing on" B), each reusing all the references.
  - "Two-shot" is a listed composition term. First/last frame and timestamped shot segments are supported.
  - Clips are 4, 6 or 8 s. Google calls the features "experimental".
  - **A** https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- **Nano Banana 2 multi-reference:**
  - Accepts up to 14 references. Refer to each person by array position ("the first image") plus a short description once there are three or more references.
  - The prompt has to state the relationship and the contact, because the references can't convey it. Clean, plain-background references composite more predictably.
  - For complex frames, get two elements right, feed the result back in, then add the next.
  - No documented failure data for multiple people. **A/B** (Runware model docs) https://runware.ai/docs/models/google-nano-banana-2/guides/multi-image-composition
  - Label the characters A and B, give left/right placement, and say who touches what and whose face stays visible. Two people in one frame is reported achievable; four is not reliably. **C** https://ask.atlascloud.ai/14-image-reference-budget-five-nano-banana-characters · https://framia.converge.ai/page/en-US/blog/nano-banana-2-character-consistency
- **Failure modes:**
  - At touch points the pixels of the two bodies or objects blend, giving merged limbs, swapped textures, props passing through hands and identity swaps. Video models have no skeleton or collision.
  - Training data is thin on hand-on-hand and prop-in-hand interaction.
  - Error rates rise with the number of subjects even without contact. One creative director: "Multi-character consistency (ropes, props, bodies in contact) breaks models faster than anything else."
  - Reported cost: about 3 generations per usable contact shot, and over 40% of final shots composited from 2+ takes.
  - **C** (vendor, sells a competing pipeline) https://invideo.io/faq/why-do-ai-video-models-struggle-with-physical-contact/
- **Fixes reported:**
  - Lock the contact pose as a still first, from a sketch of who holds what. Animate from that locked frame.
  - If it still fails, break the moment into single-subject shots and cut between them. **C** (same source)
  - Generate characters empty-handed and props as separate references, because "hand-object contact points are where multi-angle sheets break". **C** https://invideo.io/faq/whats-the-best-way-to-handle-props-or-handheld-objects/
  - Name every point where the fingers touch the object; unspecified contact is where merging starts. **C** https://higgsfield.ai/blog/ai-video-hands-faces
  - A preservation instruction is a request, not a constraint, so reject and regenerate from a clean reference. **C** https://magichour.ai/blog/how-to-fix-ai-video-faces-and-hands
  - Re-upload both references every time both characters are in the shot. Lock the reference tightly and keep the prompt mechanical (pose, angle). Added complexity spawns unrequested extra characters. **C** https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rkd925/snapshots/2026-03-04T15%3A51%3A58.715653Z · https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/ai/20251207-veo3-character-consistency-guide/
  - Veo can re-render an uploaded character into someone else entirely, and can invent objects during camera moves. **C** https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/veo-3-1-consistency-help-preventing-hallucinations-in-professional-dental-workflow-gemini-2-5-veo-pipeline/111898
- **Eyeline:** nothing found that specifically addresses mutual eyeline in Veo or Nano Banana. *Synthesis:* separate singles with matched screen direction (ERB, Google's own dialogue workflow) sidestep the problem.

## B. Unmasked argument, intercut

- **Coverage:**
  - Shoot the whole exchange as a wider two-shot plus both over-the-shoulders.
  - In an OTS the speaker takes about two-thirds of the frame and the listener's shoulder about a third.
  - A slow push-in while holding the OTS raises tension. A two-shot shows the strain between them.
  - Handheld OTS in tight quarters adds unpredictability.
