> 🖐 **Camping's imagery is Jack's, made by hand in Flow** ([`README.md`](./README.md)). This file says
> what the look *is*, so a lost frame can be rebuilt and a new one matches. It is not a licence to

*(This file was `style.md` until 2026-10-28. It used to defer to a "global BadCode house style" in the

## In one paragraph

A **hyper-realistic documentary press photograph on 35mm film**: fine natural grain, muted
cool-neutral colour, flat overcast or naturalistic motivated light only, no lens flares, a calm
observational eye that has not been noticed. Ordinary British places, class-coded and recognisable
(the City, a Waitrose car park, a country lane, a therapist's room). Nothing is glamorous, nothing is
pitied, nothing is staged. **The environment carries the argument**, and **warmth is rationed**: it
appears in three places only (below). The ruin of scenes 10–12 goes darker — deep black shadows, one
fire as the key — but it is the same camera and the same film stock.

## Rules for this story

- **Never name a repo character in a prompt** — cast the Flow Character and say *"the man in the
  reference image"*. When a Character is cast, don't describe his face unless you are pushing it in a
  stated direction (4b; [`characters/tarquin.md`](./characters/tarquin.md)).
- **Name the failure in `Constraints:`** when a round goes wrong (4b: *"He is not sad, not tired…"*).
- **Advert words commission an advert.** "Immaculate, glossy, mirror-wet, perfect bokeh" made 5a
  round 1 a car advert. The fix was the world: dirt, people, street clutter (5a).
- **Anchor on the plate, keep the prompt short.** Once a set exists, the reference carries the place,
  light and look; the prompt says only what changes (6a, 6b, 6c).
- **Anything named in a keep-list gets drawn.** Describe a bay as empty; never "remove the car" (6a, 6c).
- **One key light.** Scene 10 worked when one close drum fire became the key and blacks were real
  (10a/10b round 2).
- **Rain on the lens** is free realism on wet exteriors only, never inside the tent (ruled 2026-08-26).
- **No readable lettering** except what the story needs: the Waitrose fascia (ruled readable
  2026-08-26), the year numerals on the foreshore (`1y`, `4y`), the X8's `BAD C0DE` plate and badge
  (Jack, 2026-09-17).
- **Describe landmarks, don't name them** (1a: the NatWest Tower described as three chevron shafts;
  modern towers excluded as shapes, not names).
- **Never put "homeless" or any destitution word in a Flow field** — prompt or Character Name
  (`@homeless-Tarquin` renamed `@Future-Tarquin`, 2026-08-24; [`characters/world-cast.md`](./characters/world-cast.md)).
- **Faces do the emotional work in stillness** — the 5d smile-fade is a held face, not an action.
- **Video:** Omni Flash (ruled 2026-08-25); a changing face on a moving shot went to Veo 3.1 Fast (5d).
  Lightning, flashes and lettering are layered in Premiere/ffmpeg, never asked of Flow (10a/10b).

### Where warmth is allowed (and only here)

Warmth is an argument in this story, never decoration:

1. The **wank tank's gold headlights** through the night city (Scene 5) — opulence and menace.
2. The **ceremony fire** (Scene 9) — contained, slightly sickly.
3. The **fire in the ruins** (Scenes 10–12) — the one *earned* human warmth, the heat the whole story
   withheld, shared between the two men at the end, and the engine of the closing image: the newspaper
   burns, the camera pans up, **the rising embers become the bad code.**

Everywhere else: muted, cool, unforgiving.

### Motifs to hold

- **The matched year pair:** `1y` (2008) and `4y` (2026) on the Thames foreshore, same framing, one
  number changing ([`year-device.md`](./year-device.md); the skyline pair 1a/4a is the earlier version).
- **The tent** is one object in every appearance ([`characters/tent.md`](./characters/tent.md)).
- **The POV rhyme:** 6c (Bob's view out of the tent) and Tarquin's wake-up in scene 10 share the exact
  door-flap framing and low horizon. 6c was derived from the scene-8 frame; derive scene 10 from 6c.
- **The blink** (Scene 9 → 10) is the jump device: eyes close on the trip, open in the tent.
- **Recognisable across time:** young, Shard and ruined Tarquin share bone structure; same for young Bob
  and car-park Bob.

## Places

- **The City, 2008** (Scene 1) — the old NatWest Tower skyline, then a 2008 trading floor: CRT-adjacent
  screens, shirtsleeves, fluorescent glare (the Superia green-bias preamble).
- **The City, 2026** (Scene 4) — the same skyline framing now led by the Shard: cold glass, flat
  fluorescent and grey daylight, self-satisfied, no warmth. 4b: a near-empty corner office, *"space he
  can afford rather than space he is lost in."*
- **The Thames foreshore** — where the year is shown (`1y` 2008 / `4y` 2026), numerals in the weed.
- **The Waitrose car park** (Scenes 6, 8; ruined in 10–12) — wet tarmac, painted bays, a glazed
  barrel-roofed trolley shelter, the tent pitched **in a bay**, the **Waitrose fascia in green livery,
  readable** (ruled 2026-08-26; the scene-10 `W-AI-trose` gag needs it). Before: ordinary, overcast.
  After: frost, tents, wreckage, one burning fire, near-black; a lightning flash reveals the sign, then
  two green letters `AI` stay lit (design ruled 2026-08-28). After-state Easter eggs: the W-AI-trose
  sign; the clamped dead wank tank.
- **The consulting room** (Scene 7) — tasteful, warm-neutral, money-quiet; the comedy plays against its
  calm. The therapist is a woman (ruled 2026-09-09, so she and Tarquin don't read as one man twice).
  7b is dead and 7a probably dead; [7c](./prompts.md#7c--the-two-shot--still---not-yet-shot), a
  side-on two-shot, carries the scene. Re-shoot any reverse pair *from 7c*.
- **The country lane** (Scenes 2–3) — wet single-track English lane, hedgerows, grey sky; the crash
  is shown by objects afterwards (3b the indicator still ticking), never by the impact.
- **The Welsh retreat clearing** (Scene 9) — executive dome tents and a yurt by a river, fog, a small
  ceremony fire; damp, late-70s film-negative texture on exteriors.

## Never

- Lens flares, glow, bloom, "photorealistic" as a bare word (it gets you plastic).
- Advert vocabulary: immaculate, glossy, valeted, perfect bokeh, mirror-wet.
- Warmth outside the three places above.
- The tent made pitiable or picturesque — *"simply there, in the corner, where nobody has to look at it"* (6a).
- The crash shown, or who died.
- A face asked to carry a smile it shouldn't (9-mug round 1: *"he should not be smiling"*).
- Readable lettering beyond the three allowed items.
- Wikimedia ShareAlike images as Flow references.
- Generating new Camping imagery unasked — the frames are Jack's.

  ([`characters/world-cast.md`](./characters/world-cast.md)), with dignity rules. Ask before prompting

  holds the brief and the shot design; the rules every mv2 frame keeps:
  - **Camera at sitting height** — the height of a person on the pavement (the intro catalogue: lens about
    30 cm off the ground).
  - **Gritty, never pitiful, never fun** (Jack, 2026-09-29): homeless people are never shown having a good
    time (no boombox); gritty the way Bob is — cold, wet, tired, worn down — with dignity. No comeuppance joke.
  - **Adults only** (Jack, 2026-09-28: *"Please do that again with no kids"*); crowds stated as adults.
  - **No "homeless" in any Flow field**; the five world-cast Characters are cast by name
    ([`characters/world-cast.md`](./characters/world-cast.md)), never described.

    tracked on in post with `scripts/camping-mv2/signtext.py`. Fonts: **Permanent Marker** for cardboard,
    **Cinzel** (engraved or gold serif) for Tarquin's plaques, boards, gate and label, **DotGothic16** (amber LED
    dots) for the gantry. Caption-style text only for cards with no sign (the stat card, the BADCODE end card).
  - **Final text ruling** (Jack, 2026-09-30, supersedes "fill every blank card"): only his 11 chosen lines
