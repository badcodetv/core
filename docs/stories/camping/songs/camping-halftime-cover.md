## 🔴 The c1 finding: we wrote the cheese ourselves

Jack, 2026-08-27, on the c1 takes: **"too early 2000s cheesy."** He is right, and the cause was
not Suno — it was our own vocabulary. The c1 style box asked, in these words, for:

> `nu-metal energy, aggressive rapped verses with a melodic sung hook` … `chiming rhythm guitar
> and a melodic lead guitar trading lines, driving and energetic` … `guitars roaring back in`

That is a description of early-2000s rap-rock, and the punk lane's `fast strummed power chords`
+ `gang-shout energy` is pop-punk of the same era. **Two of our own earlier decisions fed it:**

1. **The h12 pivot** swapped `never rapped` for a rapped rap-rock delivery. Kai's reason was
   sound (the half-time spoken vocal was drifting off the beat) but the cure imported the disease.
   **c2's answer: ranted spoken and LOCKED to the beat** — neither rapped nor drifting.
2. **h12 lifted the sung-vocal bans** (`sung chorus`, `melodic vocal`, `vocal hooks`,
   `clean sung melody`) to allow the sung-hook half of that sound. **All restored in c2.**

🔴 **And the attached source is itself the cheesy take** — `Camping HT AJ rap-rock W45 (h12)`. So
Audio Influence now pulls *toward* the problem by construction. That is why c2 sweeps it low
(10/25 rather than c1's 15/25/35) and why, if the cheese survives at AI 10, the honest next step
is a new Create-mode take rather than another cover.

**The register we are aiming at** is a real and documented British lineage — minimal abrasive
beats under a ranted working-class spoken vocal, descending from The Fall, John Cooper Clarke,
Ian Dury, The Streets and Linton Kwesi Johnson. It is also exactly the cultural register
[`the-reader.md`](../../../marketing/the-reader.md) targets. 🔴 **It goes in as description, never
as a band name** — Suno flagged `Linkin Park` live on 2026-08-26, so named artists are off the
table for this sheet (same rule as a Flow policy block: describe the sound, never name the act).

The technical distinction the lanes below are built on: post-punk guitar is trebly, angular,
staccato single-note lines with natural amp distortion; pop-punk is bright, clean, mid-focused
power chords engineered to stay radio-friendly. **Radio-friendly is the cheese.**

## 6. How this is driven

```bash
npx tsx scripts/suno/cover-genre.mts plan             # resolved boxes, offline
npx tsx scripts/suno/cover-genre.mts check            # live cover form + My Taste, spend nothing
npx tsx scripts/suno/cover-genre.mts taste-backup     # REQUIRED before run
npx tsx scripts/suno/cover-genre.mts run [ids...]     # 10 credits and 2 takes per id
npx tsx scripts/suno/cover-genre.mts taste-restore    # put the house profile back — DO THIS AFTER
```

Reuses every guard from `cover-ab.mts` and `halftime.mts`: refuses to spend a credit if the cover
audio is not attached, if the words on the page have drifted from `camping.md` §4, or if My Taste
has no backup on disk; re-checks the attachment survived after every Create.

🔴 **The tab matters now.** Kai works in his own Suno tab in the same browser. `suno.mts` claims
one tab with a `sessionStorage` marker and drives only that one — `npx tsx scripts/suno/suno.mts
tabs` flags it, `open-tab` claims a fresh one. **Do the Remix ▸ Cover attach in the marked tab.**
CDP page order is *not* creation order and reshuffles under you, so never target a tab by index.
