## 🔑 The number that decides the edit

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Narration** (`narration.mp3`, now on A2) | **150.1s** |
| **Picture** (34 clips, untrimmed) | **272.0s** |
| **Overhang** | **–121.9s — the picture is 81% longer than the words** |

**So this is not a trim, it is a cut of nearly half.** Average clip has to fall from **8.0s to
about 4.6s**. That is not a mutilation — it is the normal ratio for generated footage. One
documented production reported **only ~5 seconds of each 15-second generated clip reaching the
timeline**, and our own 8s takes are no different: the usable part of a Flow clip is the middle.

⚠️ **Caveat, stated up front: `narration.mp3` is dated 3 August** — before the entire 24–30 August
production run. **It is a proxy for length, not the final track.** If it is the old cut, the target
moves; the *shape* of the recommendation does not.

## 🔴 The one thing that is not an editing problem

**`4a` is not on the timeline** — see [`assembly.md`](./assembly.md). No amount of cutting fixes
a missing shot, and this one carries the eighteen-year jump. **Look in the old `clips/` folder
first.** Everything below assumes a ~3s `4a` slots between clips 6 and 7.

## The seven edits, worst offender first

### 1. 🔴 Scene 9 is 54 seconds doing one beat's work — cut it to 20

**Seven clips (21–27) for what canon calls three beats.** Five of them make the same argument —
*he has gone to an expensive retreat* — and the corpus is blunt that two shots doing one job means
one is padding and is **diluting** the other. This is the single biggest win available and it
costs no footage anyone has documented.

| Clip | What it is | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21 | Forest drive, aerial | **Keep, 3s.** Cheap transit; it moves us. |
| 22 | Yurt interior, the circle | **Keep, 5s** — the canon location |
| 24 | Mug POV, dome by the river | 🔪 **Cut.** Says what 22 said, in the dropped location |
| 25 | The POV goes kaleidoscopic | **Keep, 5s** — the only clip that does a job nothing else does |
| 26 | Man entering the dome | 🔪 **Cut.** Pure connective tissue |
| 23 | Eye macro | **Keep, 3s** |
| 27 | Dome interior, river through the glass | **Keep, 4s** — serves `9c`, the water and the eyes closing |

**54s → 20s. Two clips out, five retimed.** ⚠️ **A knock-on worth saying out loud:** three of the
keepers are the **river-bank dome**, which the shot list records as *dropped in favour of the
yurt*. The cut above still mixes both locations. **Either the yurt or the dome is the retreat** —
that is a canon call, not an edit, and it is currently being made by accident.

### 2. 🔴 Every clip is exactly 8 seconds, and that is a metronome

Thirty-four identical durations is a pulse the audience feels before it notices anything in the
frame. **Varying duration is free, needs no new footage, and is the biggest single improvement
available on existing material.** The rule outside is the same one `motion-and-cutting.md` gives:
**hold the dwell shots, cut the punctuation short, and let one length be conspicuous.**

**Proposed cut list.** Durations, not trim points — the usable second is generally the middle, so
trim from both ends rather than the tail.

### 3. ⚠️ Swap clips 2 and 3 back to canon order

The timeline runs `1a → 1c → 1b`; canon is `1a → 1b → 1c`. **One `premiere_move_clip` call.**
Argue for the current order if you like it — landing on the man and pulling out to the floor is a
real move — but then amend `shot-list.md`, because right now the two disagree silently.

### 4. 🆕 Put the `3a` black beat in — it costs nothing and canon asks for it

Canon is explicit: *"**3a The crash — never shown.** Cut to black mid-laugh straight out of 2b;
the impact happens in the black. **No narration at all.**"* **There is no black on the timeline.**
Right now `2b`'s laugh cuts straight to the dashboard, so the film's most withheld moment is
carried by a hard cut instead of by the withholding.

**~1.5s of black between clips 5 and 6.** A held beat of black is on the corpus's list of upgrades
that need no footage at all.

### 5. ⚠️ `12a` is two clips of the same two men at the same drum — use one

Clip 31 (hands over the fire) and clip 32 (facing each other) are the same setup, the same light
and the same distance. **That is exactly the gate-1 failure that got `12b` struck** — *"basically
the same as the last one, they are just talking."* Striking `12b` and then playing `12a` twice
puts the problem straight back in.

🔑 **Take 31, not 32.** Canon's beat is *the apology happens the man way, nothing said straight* —
**31 has them both looking into the fire; 32 has them looking at each other**, which says it
straight and spends the thing the scene is meant to withhold.

### 6. 🔴 Day-for-night `12a`, and the odds are better than they look

The ending runs **daylight → pitch dark → pitch dark** on one fire. `12a` is also brighter than
scene 10, so the light runs backwards into the final scene.

**Two facts make the grade worth trying before a re-shoot:**

1. **Overcast is the easiest possible source.** The whole difficulty in day-for-night is strong
   directional sun and blue sky; `12a` has neither — flat grey, no shadows, no sun.
2. **There is a practical light in frame.** A believable night grade needs a motivated source, and
   `12a` has a burning drum dead centre.

**Lumetri, on both `12a` clips, via `premiere_apply_effect` + `premiere_set_param`:** exposure
down hard, highlights down further than shadows, temperature cool, saturation off the ambient —
and a vignette (amount ≈ −0.8, midpoint 0, feather 100) to fall the edges off the way night does.
🔴 **Watch the blacks** — [`delivery.md`](../../video-fx/delivery.md) exists because `camping.mp4`
already shipped once with everything under 16 crushed. Grade to dark, not to zero.

⚠️ **What the grade cannot do:** the faces are lit flatly from the sky, not from below by the
fire. Darkening will not move a light source. **If it reads wrong at the cut, that is the answer
to whether `12a` gets re-shot** — and the written prompt is ready to run.

### 7. 🆓 Free upgrades once the above is in

- **L-cuts across every scene change** — run the narration over the last half-second of the
  outgoing shot. Costs nothing, and it is the standard fix for a cut that feels like a slideshow.
- **A bookend.** `12d` ends on sky; `1a` opens on skyline. **That button already closes** — worth
  protecting rather than inventing.
- **Markers at every scene head** so the narration pass has something to hang on.

## What to do in what order

1. 🔎 **Find `4a`** — old `clips/` folder. Nothing else is blocked by it, but the cut is wrong without it.
2. 🔪 **Cut scene 9** — clips 24 and 26 out. Biggest win, one call.
3. ⏱ **Apply the duration table** — 32 trims.
4. ↔️ **Swap 2 and 3**, insert the black, drop one `12a`.
5. 🎨 **Grade `12a`** and look at it against `12c`.
6. 🗣 **Then lay narration** and fine-trim to it — never the other way round.

**The last one is the actual method, and it is worth stating as a rule:** lay the audio first and
let the picture answer to it. It is the standard documentary approach (the "radio edit"), and with
34 shots of near-identical length it is the only thing that will impose a rhythm the footage does
not have on its own.
