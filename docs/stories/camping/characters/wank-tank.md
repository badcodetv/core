# The Wank Tank

Tarquin's car — a blacked-out BMW X8, the oversized luxury SUV whose
disparaging nickname *is* the point. The rolling argument for his self-regard:
it drives him home from the Shard past the homeless (Scene 5), and he parks it
inches from Bob's tent without a second thought (Scene 8). Treat it as a
character: **the same vehicle in every appearance.**

## Visual (house style: hyper-realistic documentary photography)

- A very large, glossy black BMW SUV badged X8 — slab sides, huge blacked-out
  kidney grille, dark wheels, privacy glass. Immaculate, recently valeted, beading
  water. It dwarfs everything near it; in the car park it straddles two bays beside
  the sagging tent — the size contrast is the class map.
- **⚠️ Name it in the prompt: "a blacked-out BMW X8"** *(ruled 2026-08-26)*. Camping's
  prompts otherwise carry a blanket *no badges, brand names or readable lettering* rule —
  **the car is the exception.** Anonymising it into "a large black luxury SUV" strips the
  thing that makes the joke land; the nickname only works if the audience recognises the
  specific vehicle. Keep the no-lettering rule for everything else in frame, number plates
  included.
- **⚠️ Interiors too — any time Tarquin is in a car, it is the X8 and the prompt says so**
  *(extended 2026-08-26)*. Give the cabin its identifying furniture: the wide curved digital
  display sweeping from the driver across the dash, a thick flat-bottomed steering wheel with
  the **round BMW badge visible at the centre of the hub**, the faceted crystal-effect gear
  selector and rotary controller on the console, thin illuminated trim, black leather. The
  wheel badge is the one piece of branding that *must* be legible — it is all that identifies
  the car from inside. Applies to 5c/5d, and to scene 8.

## ⚠️ Naming it is not enough — there is no production BMW X8 *(2026-08-26)*

The first 5a attempt named the car *"a blacked-out BMW X8"* and **came back not looking like
one**. The reason is simple: **BMW never built an X8.** The range runs X1–X7 plus the XM; the
X8 was rumoured for years and never made. So the name has no referent for the engine to draw
on, and it falls back on a generic big black SUV.

**The nickname stays — the prompt describes the shape.** Name it *and* spell out the design
signature of BMW's current big SUVs, which is what actually makes it read:

- an **enormous vertical stacked double-kidney grille** filling the whole upright nose
- **split headlights** — thin horizontal LED daytime-running strips set high at the top of the
  front wings, with the main headlamp units as separate blocks below them
- a **tall, flat, upright front end**, a long slab flank, a high beltline and a shallow
  greenhouse that makes the body look enormous under it
- the round BMW roundel on the bonnet and tailgate

**And frame it so the front is visible.** A car driving away shows only its tailgate, which is
the least identifiable angle there is. Stage the departure as a **swing out past the camera**
so the front three-quarter comes round into frame — which also points the gold headlights at
the lens and makes the scene's one allowance of warmth do far more work.
- **Warmth as argument:** the headlights and DRLs bleed gold through the night
  city on the Scene 5 drive home — the only heat in an otherwise cool-neutral
  frame. Opulence and a faint menace.
- **Ruined variant (Scenes 10–12, Easter egg):** the same car five years dead in
  the same spot — filthy, tyres flat, clamped. The punchline to "I'm going to
  get in my car and go home."
- Shot observationally: from a high angle over wet tarmac, or low past the tent,
  not as a glamour car advert. The gloss should read as obscene next to the
  tent, not aspirational.
