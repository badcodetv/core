## 2. Hip-hop gesture vocabulary (what the hands actually do)

Named gestures, with the concrete motion:
- **One-finger chop:** a single finger chops down once or repeatedly to land one key point (Snoop Dogg, "Still D.R.E."). [reported] https://www.thestranger.com/music/the-language-of-hiphop-hands-16346921/
- **Not-having-it hands:** both hands wave side to side, palms down. Rappers give it "more pronounced rhythmic articulation" than everyday refusal (Mos Def, "Respiration"). [reported] same URL
- **World hands:** hands go up and out wide for "the whole of it" (Ishmael Butler). **Face hands:** hands cover the face, come away, then a slow side-to-side head turn (MCA, "So What'cha Want"). **Back-up-off-me hands:** need room (Fabolous, "Breathe"). [reported] same URL
- **Hands toward the lens** read as welcoming. **A point at the lens** "commands your attention" (50 Cent). **Double point** is "very rare" and "dangerous". **The deuces:** angle matters; Kendrick's is fully horizontal, which reads as attitude. **Holding up the chain.** [reported] https://djbooth.net/features/rapper-poses
- **Best and worst (DJ Booth):** best are Craig Mack's rigid open hands with circular chops and jabs toward the lens, Birdman's slow palm rub, Lil B's "chef stir", Busta Rhymes' fingers "snapping in and out of the frame" on a peephole or fisheye lens. Worst are Token pointing along with his own words, KRS-One's hands always open and palm-down, Jaden Smith repeating one move, Mystikal's "awkwardly timed pantomiming", and Kendrick's hands "constantly bent and bouncing with each word". [reported] https://djbooth.net/features/2018-08-16-best-worst-hand-gestures-in-rap-history/
  - [inference] The worst list is the cheese list: pantomiming the lyric, one gesture repeated, bouncing on every word.
- **Accents on stressed syllables:** the stronger battle rapper "accents stressed syllables with finger-points, 'gun-hand' snaps and beat taps", lets silences swell, and toggles his gaze between opponent and crowd. The weaker one had "near-metronomic delivery" with **broad sweeping gestures**. [reported, abstract only] https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/from-bars-to-bodies-multimodal-power-plays-in-trex-vs-charlie-clips/
- **Timing, from a movement coach:** a young rapper's knee bounce started on the beat but "always looked like it was behind the beat". The audience reads the peak of the movement, so the peak must land on the beat. [reported] https://www.hypebot.com/does-your-band-need-a-movement-coach/
- **Stillness versus detonation:** a profile of Ro$ama praises knowing when to stay still and when to explode (seen only as a search-result summary, not read in full). [reported] https://www.hypebot.com/ro-ama-is-using-the-road-as-his-classroom.md
- **Not every rapper stares into the lens:** in "Not Like Us", Kendrick "doesn't look into the camera that much", which keeps a laid-back demeanour. In Vince Staples' "Señorita", the close-ups press up against the lens when he raps to camera. [reported] https://www.theroot.com/a-breakdown-of-symbolism-in-kendrick-lamar-s-not-like-u-1851603994 ; https://www.oca.ac.uk/?p=55239
- **Wide lens, close in:** Hype Williams shot most of his 1990s videos on a fisheye with cinematographer John Perez. One writer says the lens gives "cartoonish pop"; another says it keeps the subject intimate while "centering the artist". [reported] https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Hype_Williams ; https://thecolrgirl.substack.com/p/the-colors-of-hype-wlliams
- **The UK freestyle format** (Handycam lineage, grime DVDs such as *Practice Hours* and *Risky Roadz*, SBTV Warm Up, No Miming): one take, a cameraman walking backwards "sticking lenses in the faces" of rappers, "no hiding". [reported] https://uk.news.yahoo.com/touch-mic-shell-british-freestyle-153409910.html
- **A motion-capture study** of rapper speech and body coordination (Kodama, CogSci pilot). I only saw a search summary and could not read the PDF, so I cannot state its findings. https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3cr0g2fs

## 3. How Omni reads prompts, and the Flow settings that matter

- **Less prescriptive than Veo:** "With Veo, you need to share precise instructions... with Gemini Omni, you don't have to be as prescriptive... tell Omni what you want to create." Omni also uses cultural knowledge ("You don't need to over-explain"). [vendor] https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-omni/prompt-guide/
  - [inference] Naming a real format ("UK freestyle filmed on a Handycam") may do more on Omni than listing gestures. Nobody has tested this for performers.
- **It cuts by default:** "by default Omni Flash will try to create a video with a few different shots". Ask for "In a single continuous shot" or "No scene cuts". [vendor] https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni . Google's own examples end with "One continuous shot, no jump cuts." [vendor] https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-omni/prompt-guide/
- **Timing:** natural language works ("After 3 seconds, ...") and so do timecodes ("[0-5s] ..."). [vendor] https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni
- **Negatives:** there is no separate negative field; write "Do not do X" in the prompt. "No dialogue", "No embellishments" and "No extra sound effects" work as simple exclusions. [vendor] same URL
- **Audio:** an audio track is generated by default. Describe it ("Sound design: ... No dialogue."). [vendor] same URL. One tester found removed music came back in the second half of the clip. [tested] https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/google-gemini-omni-testing-results
- **Camera vocabulary Google lists:** "oner", "static / locked off / fixed", "push in / punch in / dolly zoom", "natural smartphone zoom", "film camera", "webcam style"; also "handheld shot" in the API examples. [vendor] https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-omni/prompt-guide/ ; https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni
- **Granular speed beats "fast" or "slow":** Google says Omni understands "fast" and "slow" but more granular wording gives better results. [vendor, via search summary of the Flow X article; the article itself returned 402] https://x.com/FlowbyGoogle/article/2105398907702595802
- **Image-to-video:** use "specific motion descriptions", not "make it move". [vendor] https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni. Describe only the added motion, because the frame already sets the composition. [reported] https://pixeldojo.ai/guides/google-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide. "Describe what changes, not what is there." [reported] https://www.dreamdrive.ai/learn/google-labs/flow-frames-to-video-guide/. The prompt should "complement, not contradict" and should reference the frames. [vendor] https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353334
- **One camera move per clip:** stacking motion types in a short clip "usually produces compromised, jittery motion". [reported] https://pixeldojo.ai/guides/google-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide. Consistency drifts on "fast camera moves". [reported] https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide-2026
- **Style:** natural language, "drop the 2023-era keyword spam". [reported] https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide-2026
- **Flow Frames settings:**
  - Omni Flash offers 4, 6, 8 or 10 s, plus a 360p draft at half the credits. [reported] https://www.dreamdrive.ai/learn/google-labs/flow-frames-to-video-guide/
  - Google says 360p is up to 60% faster and a third of the cost of 720p. [vendor] https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/
  - Through the Flow API, start/end frames are the only input in that mode: no reference images, characters, audio or video alongside them. [reported] https://github.com/useapi/google-flow-api/tree/main/omni-flash
- **Motion reference (API, Omni 1.1):** a video reference of up to 3 s can drive body motion; Google's example has characters perform dances from reference clips. [vendor] https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/. Whether Flow Frames accepts this is not confirmed; see Open questions.
- **Omni 1.2 Flash:** SunoMV says Google shipped it on 5 November 2026 (3 to 15 s, fewer jump cuts, closer reference-following). useapi lists a model "omni-flash-1.2". I found no Google page confirming it. [reported, unverified] https://suno.bi/en/blog/gemini-omni-1-2-flash-music-video ; https://github.com/useapi/google-flow-api/tree/main/omni-flash

## 4. Techniques against the "AI performance" look

- **Describe the physics of the motion, not only the action.** Without stride, weight or contact detail, the model "falls back on generic animation. The kind that moves but doesn't feel like anyone's actually walking." [reported; claims 60 Kling 3.0 tests, outputs not compared] https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/guides/mastering-kling-3.0-10-advanced-ai-video-prompts-for-realistic-human-motion
  - For a rapper [inference]: weight sits in the heels, knees soft, a small bounce from the knees, shoulders roll forward on the accent, the chin dips.
- **Anchor the hands.** Hands are "much more stable when they're anchored to an object" (same source) [reported]. [inference] Give one hand a job: holding a mic, gripping the jacket's drawstring, resting on the chest. Leave only one hand free to gesture.
- **Map the body to the drums.** "feet hit on the kick, shoulders pop on the snare" (P8) [reported]. Cuts "land on the snare" is the Freebeat claim. [reported] https://www.onemoreshot.ai/blog/ai-music-videos-for-hip-hop. Big moves go on "action points", where the vocals, tempo or instruments change. [reported] https://blog.audionetwork.com/the-edit/music/how-to-create-a-hip-hop-music-video/
  - [inference] A BPM figure is untested with a silent clip in Flow; Omni has no beat to lock to. Give counts in seconds instead ("a sharp accent at about 2 s, 4 s and 6 s").
- **Handheld with a reason.** "The small drift a real arm makes"; a locked frame "reads as a tripod, and a tripod reads as an ad" (P9). [reported] P3 asks for "like a friend recording a video with an iPhone (including camera shake, etc.)" [reported]. Google lists "natural smartphone zoom" and "webcam style". [vendor]
- **Lens and gear.** Google's own guidance for Veo 3.1 lists lens terms ("wide-angle lens", "shallow depth of field"). [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1. Fisheye is the hip-hop lineage (§2). Named focal lengths appear in tested Kling prompts. [reported]
  - [inference] Omni's world knowledge should parse "fisheye on a camcorder" or "Sony VX1000"-style references, but no performer test exists.
- **Real era or format:** "Use Gemini Omni's knowledge of history, science, and culture" [vendor]. "1990s hip hop music video aesthetic", "documentary approach" and "minimalist performance video" are suggested as keywords for Veo, untested. [reported] https://beatstorapon.com/blog/the-new-frontier-rap-hip-hop-video-creation-with-google-veo-3/
- **Documentary wrapper:** the "documentary session template" asks for handheld with natural movement and "avoid slow-motion unless specifying a deliberate impact moment". [reported] https://promptslove.com/blog/google-omni-prompting-guide/
- **Restraint words that appear in the better prompts:** "controlled hand movement", "understated performance", "keep the camera disciplined" (P5), "dominant, stoic" (P2). [reported]
- **Stage expressions in steps** to avoid morphing. [reported] Atlas Kling guide, URL above.
- **Start frame:**
  - Fix hands and faces in the still, because "motion exaggerates flaws". Keep the same aspect ratio as the clip. [reported] https://www.dreamdrive.ai/learn/google-labs/flow-frames-to-video-guide/
  - Use high-resolution images. [vendor] https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni
  - [inference] Start mid-gesture (a hand already up, weight already on one leg), not from a neutral catalogue pose. A neutral pose invites the model to "begin a performance", which is where presenter energy comes from. Untested.

## 5. Known failure modes for performers, and the fixes found

- **Omni human motion trails rivals:** complex human motion (dance, sports) was behind Seedance 2.0 and Kling 3.0 at launch. [reported, secondary aggregation] https://vantaige.io/ai-tool/gemini-omni
- **Footwork fails and crowds move in unison:** in a breakdancing-nun test, the nun "struggles with the footwork", the crowd moves "too much in unison", and it "looks rehearsed rather than spontaneous"; generations were "a bit bumpy". [tested] https://www.aitoolssme.com/review/gemini-omni
- **The first take is cliché:** a "90s ravers" test came out "so cheesy I genuinely refused to upload it", like a Halloween costume shop. It improved with refinement. [tested] same URL. [inference] A named era can pull out costume-shop cliché unless it is anchored by a specific start frame.
- **Abrupt cuts mid-clip:** a jarring cut in the second half of a clip. [tested] https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/google-gemini-omni-testing-results. Fix: "single continuous shot / no scene cuts". [vendor]
- **Music returns after being removed.** [tested] same URL. Fix: none tested. [inference] Restate "no music, no vocals" plainly in the prompt and strip the audio in the edit anyway.
- **An unprompted invented line:** "Keep mouths closed unless you want the model to invent a line." [reported] https://suno.bi/en/blog/gemini-omni-1-2-flash-music-video. [inference] With a full-face mask, the risk is the mask fabric moving like a mouth. Say "the mask stays still over the mouth, no talking, no singing".
- **Floaty or slow motion:** add "no slow motion" as an explicit negative (Veo 3). [reported] https://alejandrodinsmore.substack.com/p/deep-dive-veo-3-i-spent-400-testing. Without a speed word, the model picks its own default; put one tempo word next to the action verb and avoid contradicting speed words. [reported] https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/veo-31-shot-first-prompting-a-10-minute-method-to-fix-stiff-motion-random-moves-
- **Gliding and sliding feet:**
  - Describe heel strike and weight transfer; "visible impact" helps; use a short negative list ("sliding feet, moonwalk, floating, gliding, no foot contact"). [reported] Atlas Kling guide, URL above.
  - Shorten the action; "both feet planted". [reported] https://www.pippit.ai/blog/how-to-prompt-walking-without-sliding
  - Fewer steps before changing anything else. [reported] https://memeburn.com/why-ai-videos-look-fake-in-2026-and-how-to-fix-them/
  - [inference] For a to-camera rapper, plant the feet and keep the bounce in the knees. Do not ask for walking.
- **Long negative lists stiffen motion:** "Extremely long negative prompts can actually make the animation look stiff." [reported] Atlas Kling guide.
- **Too many actions confuse Veo:** use one well-defined action per clip. [reported] https://veotutorials.substack.com/p/7-ways-to-get-better-results-with. Unspecified action means it "will make something up or have a character doing nothing". [reported] same source family.
- **Retry variance is large:** "Sometimes I can try a prompt and it provides a terrible result and then I try it again and it's perfect." [reported] https://alejandrodinsmore.substack.com/p/deep-dive-veo-3-i-spent-400-testing. Draft at 360p first. [vendor]
- **Over-acting fix (Kling, Veo):** soften verbs ("subtle", "micro"); fix the positive prompt rather than piling on negatives. [reported] https://artlist.io/blog/negative-prompts-ai-video/ (via search summary)

## 6. Words that reliably make it cheesy (avoid)

- "dancing emotionally" and "TikTok viral hook, trending, high energy" (anti-examples); "cinematic camera", "epic", "8K". [reported] https://suno.bi/en/blog/gemini-omni-1-2-flash-music-video
- "flawless" pushes toward retouched skin. [reported] https://memeburn.com/why-ai-videos-look-fake-in-2026-and-how-to-fix-them/
- "expressive gestures", "energetic", "dynamic camera movement", "professional hip-hop music video aesthetic". These generic phrases fill the rap-duo templates (P11), which show no outputs. [reported as present; inference that they cause cheese]
- "slow-motion ... chest hit and hand flick", "heroic", "swagger", "spits rapid bars" (P1). [inference]
- Lyric-pantomime cues such as "points at...", "acts out", "mimes" (DJ Booth's "worst" list), and "broad sweeping gestures" (the losing battle rapper). [reported] (§2 URLs)
- "Talks to the camera", "explains", "addresses the viewer", "says": these invite speech and a presenter register. [inference]; the vendor docs confirm speech is generated when it is asked for.
- Uniformity words such as "continuously", "throughout", "the whole time" applied to the gesture. [inference] They are likely to give one looped move; compare Jaden Smith's single repeated move on the DJ Booth worst list.

The masked rapper from the start frame delivers the verse to the lens with an understated, controlled performance: a steady head nod in time, one hand holding [a mic / the strap of his bag], the other making small, precise moves close to his body — a chop, a turn of the wrist, an open palm toward the lens on the last bar.
