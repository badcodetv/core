=== DESIGN a1 ===
NAME: Boro Indigo Mends
A man sits on the cold pavement of the Thames embankment in London at night, his back to the stone river wall, a small green tent beside him and the lit dome of St Paul's behind across the water. His whole head and face are covered by a closed hood pieced from faded indigo cotton and hemp patches in a dozen blues, patched over and over, held by rows of white running stitches in straight geometric lines, frayed edges lifting. Where the face would be is unbroken patched cloth, with no eye holes, no mouth and no openings. A thick white cotton ring sewn with dense white cross-stitches circles the face, and high inside it, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, sits a small cut patch of faded red cotton in a triangle. He wears a big ex-army field jacket over a navy hoodie, jumpers at the cuffs, mismatched full wool gloves, a matted grey scarf tucked round the neck, a rolled sleeping bag at his side. Mid-verse, one gloved hand pressed flat on his chest, the other palm open toward the lens, head tilted forward. Waist-up, eye level, lit by a sodium streetlamp, high-ISO grain.
WHY: A mask mended for decades carries poverty and care at once, so his dignity is in the stitches.

=== DESIGN a2 ===
NAME: Foil Blanket Wrap
Under a concrete motorway flyover in west London at night, a man raps straight into a wide lens, close and waist-up, graffiti on the pillars and small tents behind, a fire burning in an oil drum beside him. His whole head and face are wrapped tight in a crinkled silver-and-gold emergency foil blanket, fastened with strips of matte black gaffer tape; the face is one unbroken crumpled surface with no eye holes, no mouth and no openings. A wide band of white cloth tape is wound in a ring round the face, and high inside it, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, is a small triangle cut from red electrical tape, its edges slightly lifting. He wears a rain-dark parka over a grey hoodie, black work gloves covering every finger, the foil tucked into a wound brown scarf. He jabs a pointed gloved finger at the lens, foreshortened, as the fire's orange light flares in hard, broken highlights across the creases. Handheld, low angle, crushed blacks, high-ISO noise.
WHY: The cheapest armour against a night outside, and firelight on crinkled foil looks like no other frame.

=== DESIGN a3 ===
NAME: Sleeping Bag Hood
On a flat tar rooftop in London at dusk, a man stands waist-up facing the camera, chimney pots and old brick in front of a hazy skyline with the Shard and the dome of St Paul's in the distance. His whole head and face are inside a balaclava sewn from a faded orange ripstop sleeping-bag shell, quilted in puffy horizontal baffles stuffed with fibre, scuffed grey at the high points, closed at the neck with a black cord-lock toggle. The face is smooth quilted nylon with no eye holes, no mouth and no openings. A white nylon webbing strap is stitched in a ring over the baffles round the face, and high inside it, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, is a small red nylon repair patch shaped as a triangle with rounded corners. He wears a navy rain shell over a quilted gilet and fleece, padded black ski gloves, the drawcord neck tucked into his collar, a rucksack with a bedroll on bungee cord. Both gloved hands chop low, palms down on the beat, shoulders rolled into the lens. Cold blue dusk light, 35mm, fine grain.
WHY: He wears his bed on his head, a puffy orange shape no one could mistake for a plain knit.

=== DESIGN a4 ===
NAME: Carrier Bag Crochet
In a narrow brick alley in east London at night, close and waist-up, a man raps into the lens between big wheelie bins, paste-ups and graffiti on the walls, steam drifting from a vent behind him. His whole head and face are covered by a dense, crinkly crochet hood made from plastic carrier bags cut into strips, mostly white and pale blue with flecks of orange and green, each stitch catching small glints of light, with no legible text anywhere. The face is unbroken crochet with no eye holes, no mouth and no openings. Three rounds of solid white bag strips stand proud of the surface in a ring round the face, and high inside the ring, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, is a small tight triangle crocheted from red bag strips. He wears a dark green donkey jacket over two jumpers, thin rubber work gloves over knitted gloves covering every finger, a frayed scarf tucked into the collar, carrier bags of belongings at his feet. Hands roll over each other in front of his chest, head nodding on the beat. Lit by one bare bulb over a back door, high-ISO grain.
WHY: Something made by hand from what others threw away reads as resourcefulness, not pity.
