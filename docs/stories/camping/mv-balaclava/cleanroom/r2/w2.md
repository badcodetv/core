=== DESIGN a5 ===
NAME: Hi-Vis Workman
A night-time film still shot on location under a concrete motorway flyover in west London, waist-up, 28mm lens at eye level. A man raps straight into the lens in a full-head balaclava sewn from fluorescent yellow hi-vis polyester mesh, grimed grey in the creases and stitched up the back with black thread. His face is one smooth, unbroken stretch of mesh with no eye holes, no mouth hole and no openings at all. A band of silver retroreflective tape circles the whole face, flaring white where a passing headlight catches it. High inside that ring, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, over his left eye, sits a small solid red reflective triangle. He wears a worn orange-and-yellow hi-vis work jacket over a black hoodie, a cement-stained scarf tucked into the collar, and heavy grey rigger gloves on both hands, no skin showing. Mid-verse, he counts points off on gloved fingers raised to the lens, chin pushed forward. Behind him, graffitied pillars, small tents and an oil-drum fire throwing orange light. High-ISO grain, deep blacks.
WHY: The man who built the city sleeps under it, and the light of the traffic he paved for makes his ring burn.

=== DESIGN a6 ===
NAME: Hessian Sack Hood
A night-time film still on the Victoria Embankment in London, medium close-up from a low camera. A man sits on the pavement against the stone river wall, a small green tent beside him and the lit dome of St Paul's Cathedral soft behind. His whole head is covered by a hood cut from a coarse brown jute sack: open weave, loose fibres sticking out, faded stencil smudges with no legible text, sewn together with thick twine and tied at the neck. The face is closed, unbroken sacking with no eye holes, no mouth opening, no gaps. A coil of white cotton rope is stitched in a ring round the face, and high inside it, on the right side of the picture at about two o'clock, over his left eye, is a small piece of red canvas sewn on with twine. He wears a brown waxed coat gone pale at the seams over a cream fisherman's jumper, a sack-coloured scarf, and leather work gloves on both hands, no skin showing. Mid-verse, both gloved palms open wide at his chest in a "me?" shrug, head tilted. Sodium streetlight from one side, fine grain.
WHY: Earthy and almost medieval, a man reduced to sacking who still sits upright and holds the camera's eye.

=== DESIGN a7 ===
NAME: Duct Tape Armour
A night-time film still in a narrow brick alley in Shoreditch, London, close and waist-up, 35mm lens at eye level. A man raps into the lens wearing a full-head hood built from grey duct tape laid strip over strip like overlapping scales, wrinkled and frayed, newer mends taped over older ones, the cloth weave showing through the tape's matte silver-grey shine. The face is a sealed, unbroken surface of tape with no eye holes, no mouth hole and no openings. A circle of white tape laid in short overlapping pieces rings the face, and high inside it, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, over his left eye, is a small triangle cut from red tape. He wears a black bomber jacket patched at the elbows with the same grey tape over a grey hoodie, the hood running down into a black scarf, and black gloves taped at the wrists on both hands, no skin anywhere. Mid-verse, he flicks the back of one gloved hand away from the lens in dismissal, shoulder turned. Graffiti, big wheelie bins, steam from a wall vent lit by one caged bulb. Grainy, deep shadow.
WHY: Holding everything together with tape is the most honest picture of getting by.

=== DESIGN a8 ===
NAME: Bus Seat Moquette
A dusk film still on a flat London rooftop of brick and chimney pots, the Shard and the dome of St Paul's soft in the haze behind, waist-up, low camera on a 35mm lens. A man stands rapping into the lens in a balaclava sewn from salvaged bus-seat moquette: a dense plush in a bold, large geometric pattern of teal, mustard and dark blue, its nap rubbed bald in patches, stitched up at the seams. The face is one unbroken piece of upholstery with no eye holes, no mouth hole, no openings of any kind. A band of plain white plush moquette, piped at both edges, rings the face, and high inside it, on the right-hand side of the picture at about two o'clock, over his left eye, sits a small solid red wool triangle. He wears a charcoal overcoat two sizes too big over a mustard cardigan, the moquette neck tucked into a teal scarf, and brown leather gloves cracked at the knuckles on both hands, no skin showing. Mid-verse, head tipped back, one gloved fist low and clenched, the other hand raised flat as if stopping traffic. Cold blue-grey dusk light, fine grain.
WHY: The night bus is where many rough sleepers sleep, so the city's own seat cloth becomes his face.
