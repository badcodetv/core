=== DESIGN r1 ===
NAME: The Darned Charcoal
A man stands waist-up in a narrow East London brick alley at night, facing the camera, caught mid-verse: one gloved hand pressed flat on his chest, the other chopping palm-down toward the lens. His whole head is covered by a hand-knitted charcoal wool balaclava, chunky and uneven, darned in mustard yarn, with no eye holes and no mouth hole, just unbroken knit pulled taut over the jaw. A thick cream ring is knitted round the face, and on the right-hand side of the picture, at two o'clock inside the ring, sits a small felt red triangle stitched on by hand. He wears an olive ex-army field jacket two sizes too big over a grey hoodie, three jumpers showing at the cuffs, mismatched full wool gloves, no skin visible. One sodium lamp above, steam from a vent, big bins, abstract graffiti shapes. Shot on a 35mm lens, high-ISO grain, crushed blacks.
WHY: He is the man who mends what he has, heavy and grounded, his dignity in every darn.

=== DESIGN r2 ===
NAME: The Donkey-Jacket Tradesman
Under a concrete motorway flyover in west London at night, a broad man stands close to a low wide-angle camera, leaning in and jabbing one finger straight at the lens, his other fist low at his hip. His head is fully enclosed in a stone-grey cable-knit wool balaclava, the knit continuous over eyes, nose and mouth with no openings of any kind. A bright white ring is stitched boldly round the face, and high on the right-hand side of the image, at two o'clock within the ring as the camera sees it, sits a small red triangle of thick wool. He wears a navy donkey jacket with a worn leather shoulder yoke, collar up over the balaclava's neck, a brown cord shirt beneath, heavy leather work gloves, cuffs over the wrists, no skin. Firelight from an oil drum beside him paints his side orange; graffiti colour on the pillars, small tents behind in shadow. Grainy colour film look, 28mm lens.
WHY: A skilled working man the economy threw out, still squaring up like he is owed an answer.

=== DESIGN r3 ===
NAME: The Foil-Wrapped Sentinel
A lean man sits upright on flattened cardboard on the Thames embankment pavement at night, close to the camera at eye level, his back against the river wall and a small green tent beside him. Mid-verse, he holds both gloved palms open at his chest, head tilted sharply. His head is wrapped in a faded cornflower-blue knitted balaclava, pilled and machine-knit, entirely closed, smooth knit where the face would be with no eye or mouth holes. A white ring circles the face; small and high on the picture's right side, at two o'clock inside the ring, sits a neat red triangle. An unzipped sleeping bag hangs round his shoulders like a cloak, a folded foil blanket glinting at the collar, a mustard puffer jacket patched with grey tape beneath, two layers of full gloves, scarf tucked high. A cold streetlamp lights him from above; the dome of St Paul's glows soft behind. 50mm lens, light grain.
WHY: He keeps watch over his little patch of the city with a still, proud patience, wrapped in everything he owns.
