# Action Bronson: how his body moves (observed 2026-11-07)

Watched (frame sheets at 1 and 4 fps, private reference only):
- NPR Tiny Desk Concert (CUN8pdgA0m8), 2:00-2:30: the clearest rapping-to-lens footage.
- Freestyle on Funkmaster Flex, Hot 97 (UYfEpSgbfNE), 0:40-1:10: seated then standing at a mic.
- "Live From an Old Folks Home" (QS-c9757LY0), 0:40-1:10: handheld mic, walking among people.
- Official videos "Strictly 4 My Jeeps" (0:30-1:00), "Baby Blue" (0:30-1:00), "SubZero" (0:20-0:50).
  In his own music videos he mostly does NOT rap to the lens: he is busy doing something (lifting, walking a dog,
  laughing in a cab, surfing a flood). The performance is a big man enjoying himself, not posing.

Stance and weight
- Big, settled, heavy. Feet planted roughly shoulder width; weight sits back on the heels and hips, belly forward.
- He rocks and sways from the hips rather than bouncing at the knees. Leans back to laugh, leans in to make a point.
- Never jumps, never hops, never stands to attention.

Framing / camera
- Tiny Desk and radio: medium close, chest up, slightly below eye level, him a little off centre, head tipped.
- Videos: handheld, wide-ish lens, often low and close (the camera next to the dog, the cab fisheye).

Hands
- One hand is always occupied: mic glued to the mouth, or an object (food, leash, bar). It barely moves.
- The free hand does all the talking: loose, open, fingers spread, rolling at the wrist beside his face at
  shoulder height, paddling and flicking like a man telling a story over dinner. Small to medium size.
- On a big line: the whole arm goes straight up, fist or one index finger to the sky, held for 2-3 beats, then
  dropped. One big move between lots of small loose ones.
- Hand slapped to the forehead / over the eyes in mock disbelief, then laughing.
- Palm-down "settle down" pats and a palm-up shrug.

Head and shoulders
- Head tips back, eyes half shut, chin up: savouring the line. Nods are loose and late, on the beat but lazy.
- Shoulders roll and shake when he laughs mid-bar. A little side-to-side sway with the beat.

How a gesture starts and stops
- Starts loose and lazy, lands with one sharp accent (a finger jab, a wrist flick), then melts away. Nothing is
  held rigid; the hand drifts back toward the body and immediately starts noodling again.

What he never does
- No crossed arms, no clenched-chest "statement" pose, no symmetric two-hand choreography, no hands-at-temple thinking.
- No menace at the lens: the threat is in the words, the body is amused, comfortable, a bit of a showman.
