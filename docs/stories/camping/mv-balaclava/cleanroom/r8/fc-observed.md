# 50 Cent to camera: what his body does (watched 2026-11-07)

Watched (frame sheets at 1 and 4 fps, private scratch, not kept in the repo):
- "In Da Club" official video (YouTube 5qm8PH4xAss), 0:50-1:20 and 2:00-2:30. The to-camera verses are the gym
  shots at about 1:06-1:09 and 1:17-1:20; the vocal-booth mic shots at about 2:04-2:29.
- "Many Men (Wish Death)" (YouTube 5D3crqpClPY), 1:10-1:40: tight close-ups by a window grille and an alley gate.

Stance and weight
- Almost no stance at all: he stays planted and loose, weight sunk, shoulders dropped. Never bounces on his toes.
- The movement is in the upper body: he leans his chest and head IN towards the lens, then settles back. Slow lean, not a lunge.
- A small, lazy sway side to side; the hips barely move. Knees soft, feet never travel.

Framing and camera
- Very close: chest-up or tighter, lens close to him and a little below his eye line, slightly wide lens so a reaching hand
  looms large. Often off centre, one shoulder nearer the camera (body turned about 20-30 degrees, face back to lens).
- Many Men: head and shoulders filling half the frame, the other half dark background; he looks slightly off and back.

Hands
- One hand does the work, the other hangs or rests low. The working hand comes forward TOWARDS the lens at chest height
  or lower, fingers loose and spread, palm up or sideways, as if handing you the line. Then it drops back.
- Wrist flicks: short, loose flicks from the wrist on the beat, fingers fanning; a point that is lazy, two fingers half bent.
- Signature: both hands crossed low in front of the chest/stomach, fingers flicked down and out, held a beat, then released.
- Moves are small to medium in size; the biggest is a forearm reach at the lens. Nothing above the shoulders.

Head and shoulders
- Head tilted to one side, chin dipped, eyes up under the brow: the cool, half-amused look. Small late nods, not a pump.
- Shoulders roll a little with the sway; one shoulder leads.

Start and stop
- Gestures start from the wrist, unhurried, and stop softly; the hand drifts back down rather than snapping.
- In the booth he just leans into the mic, head turning a little, hand by his ear or holding the lyric sheet. Calm.

What he never does
- No jumping, no arms above the head, no windmilling, no fast double-hand chopping, no pacing or stomping.
- No hand to the temple, no clutching the chest. Never stiff or symmetrical: always a tilt, a lean, one side leading.
- Not shouting energy: menace comes from being relaxed and close.
