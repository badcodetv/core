# RZA (Wu-Tang Clan): how he moves to camera (watched 2026-11-07)

Watched (frame sheets at 0.5, 1 and 4 fps):
- Wu-Tang Clan, "Protect Ya Neck" official video (YouTube R0IUR4gkPIE), 3:10-3:40: his own verse ("Exit Prince Rakeem... enter the RZA"), raps straight into the lens. Main source.
- Same video 2:50-3:10 and 3:28-3:36 for his hype moves behind other verses.
- Also skimmed "Wu-Tang Clan Ain't Nuthing ta F' Wit" (0:15-0:55) and Bobby Digital "La Rhumba" (0:20-1:00): little to-camera RZA, crew and story shots, not used. A Sway freestyle clip (uATSs5MFP_A) was skimmed but I could not be sure who is who, so it is not used.

Stance and weight
- Feet planted wide, knees bent, weight forward on the balls of the feet. He does not walk anywhere; the travel is all from the hips up.
- The upper body is almost never vertical: it pitches diagonally towards the lens, one shoulder dropped low, then swings to the other side. A pendulum lurch, side to side, roughly once per bar, with smaller dips on the beat.

Framing and camera
- Low, very close, wide-angle/fisheye, handheld, often dutch-tilted. His face fills a third of the frame and bends with the lens.
- He is off-centre, usually one side of frame, crew packed behind him. Never square-on symmetrical.

Hands
- Big, loose, whole-arm swings from the shoulder, mostly at hip-to-chest height: backhand sweeps out to the side, an open palm flung down/out at the lens, the other arm swinging low across the body.
- Both arms work; neither rests. No mic in hand. Hands rarely go above the head and never to the temple.
- Gestures start from the shoulder throw and stop loose: the arm swings through and falls back, it is never held in a pose.

Head and shoulders
- Head cocked to one side, chin forward, nods hard on the beat; lunges his face right into the lens on punchlines, then rocks back out.
- Shoulders roll and drop with the beat; the jacket swings with them.
- Expression: wide grin or open-mouthed shout, mischievous, not stern.

What he never does
- Stands still, centred, square to camera; crosses arms; holds a "thinking" pose; repeats one tidy gesture; walks towards the camera.

Sources: the videos above; web search on his stage presence found nothing gesture-specific (AARP, Huck, Bustle pieces describe showmanship and a stage "costume", not body movement).
