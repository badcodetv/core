# Camping — what the narration has to do

> **What this is:** the brief for writing the narration script, 2026-08-30. **Not the script** —
> the list of what each pass of narration must carry, what it must not, and the rules that decide
> whether a line survives. Drafts to work from are in
> [`story.md`](./story.md#storyboard--scene-by-scene-video-guide); canon wins over anything here.

## The hard numbers

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Existing `narration.mp3` | **150s** (on A2 of `camping assembly`) |
| Ceiling on delivery | **~155 words/minute**, and an authoritative voice sits *under* it |
| So the whole script is | **≈ 250–320 words** — not 380, because three stretches are wordless |
| Picture | Cut to the words, not the other way round |

⚠️ `narration.mp3` predates the whole production run. Treat 150s as the shape, not the target.

## 🔇 Three places with NO narration — canon, not preference

1. **`3a` the crash.** *"No narration at all. We do not show or say who died."* Sound design only.
2. **`5d` the smile fading.** *"No narration during the drop — the face tells everything."*
3. **The `12d` tilt** ends **exactly on the last word.** Write backwards from that: the final line
   is the last thing in the film, and the embers hold after it.

## The one rule that outranks the others

🔴 **The headline says AI took the jobs. The narration must never let that land as *AI did it*.**

[`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md) rule 6: never raise automation fear without naming
the beneficiary in the same piece — unaimed, it demonstrably feeds nativism. **This film already
names him in scene 4**, buying social housing and leaving it empty. **So scene 11's reveal has to
reach back and touch scene 4**, or the film becomes an anti-AI film by accident, which is the one
thing it is not.

🔑 **The moral is never stated. The beneficiary always is.**

## Scene by scene — what the narration must DO

### Scene 1 · 2008, Tarquin
- Name him, date it, and make the trading floor read as **cause**, not backdrop.
- The load-bearing line already exists: **"He was the reason 2008 hit."** Keep its function.
- ⚠️ Do **not** point at the newspaper in the background. A plant that gets narrated stops being a plant.

### Scene 2 · 2008, Bob
- **Keep the job deliberately unremarkable.** "Nice guy. Middle manager. Normal."
- The narration's job is to make him ordinary enough that the audience stops watching him.

### Scene 3 · the crash — 🔇 **silent**
- Nothing. Not a word. The audience joins the dots when Bob reappears alone.

### Scene 4 · 2026, the Shard
- **Eighteen years.** The skyline is the timestamp; the words confirm it, they don't do it.
- **Name the mechanism plainly:** social housing bought and left empty because *asset inflation
  pays better than rent*. This is the beneficiary being named, and everything later depends on it.
- The sarcasm lands here — *"what a brilliant businessman"* — **paired with the empty flats**, which
  is the undeniable consequence the pairing test demands.

### Scene 5 · the drive
- Tarquin's own line does the contempt (rates up 1%, "loads more mates").
- 🔇 **Then say nothing over the hold.**
- **Then the U-turn — the one spell-it-out moment in the film.** Keep the empathy **cold, not
  guilt**: *his car heater broke last week.* He is not sorry; he has simply been briefly cold.

### Scene 6 · outside Waitrose
- **The three reactions: ignore / politely reject / engage.** *"Have a guess which one's rarest."*
- 🔑 **This is the line aimed at the audience's own behaviour** — the closest the film comes to
  implicating the viewer, and it works because it never says *you*.

### Scene 7 · therapy
- **Play it for comedy and get out.** The punchline is the therapist's, and it must survive intact:
  *the most powerful psychedelic known to man.*
- Narration should stay out of the way — the scene is dialogue-shaped.

### Scene 8 · the standoff
- **One beat each, no prolonging.** The two judgement lines are the whole scene.
- The narration's only job: register that **the crack from scene 5 slaps shut.**

### Scene 9 · the trip
- 🔴 **NO MENTION OF AI. Canon is explicit.** The vision is *wind your neck in* — not a preview.
  The newspaper does the reveal, and spending it here kills scene 11.
- The beat: **the distance between him and that man outside the supermarket is thinner than he
  thought.** Fragility of existence, delivered dry.

### Scene 10 · the wake-up
- **The narrator does the freak-out — and still thinks it's the drugs.** That is the joke and the
  dramatic irony at once: the audience is ahead of the narrator for the only time in the film.
- Keep it short. The picture is doing a lot here.

### Scene 11 · the newspaper
- **"It's not the drugs."** The moment the trip stops being deniable — for Tarquin *and* the narrator.
- **The date does the time; the headline does the AI. One prop, two jobs.**
- 🔑 **Say out loud that it is the last newspaper ever printed.** From the transcript and still
  missing from canon — it closes the hole (why does a paper exist in 2031?) and it is a better
  line than the explanation it replaces.
- **Bob's empathy is man-sized and lands on Tarquin:** *where the fuck have you been? You alright?*
- 🔴 **The five missing years are NEVER explained.** Not by Bob, not by the narrator, not ever.
- ⬅️ **This is where the reveal must reach back to scene 4.** Whoever owns the AI owned the flats.

### Scene 12 · the fire
- The apology happens **the man way — nothing said straight.** The clink is a sound cue, not a picture.
- **The closing line is still open.** *"Well. We were both wrong, weren't we."* is the draft.
- 🔴 **Land the shared-humans note without going soft.** Ruling 4: do not drop the register for
  sincerity in the last third. Re-aim short and straight, then stop.
- **Write the last word first** — the tilt is timed to it.

## Traps — a line that does any of these gets rebuilt

| Trap | Test |
| --- | --- |
| 🔴 **"You've been duped"** | Could an unsympathetic reader summarise the line as *you were fooled*? Rebuild. The trick runs on a third party, never on the audience |
| 🔴 **Unaimed automation fear** | Is a beneficiary named in the same breath? If not, it feeds the wrong cause for free |
| **The lecture** | Is narration doing a scene's job — explaining a mechanism the picture already dramatised? |
| **Sarcasm with no consequence** | Every ironic line must be pinned to something undeniable on screen in the same beat |
| **The Lee/Coe test** | If a viewer who believes the opposite saw *only this beat*, whose contempt does it confirm? If it is the claimant's or the migrant's, rebuild |
| **Snark at the dying** | The contempt is for the mistake, never the people. Bob is never the joke |
| **Personification** | No robot, no face, no body. The camera *is* the narrator's eyes |
| **Cadence monotony** | Every line the same length and dryness. Fix: **one long cosmic sentence among the short ones** |
| **The Chaplin swerve** | Dropping the register for a sincere speech at the end |

## Delivery

- **Baseline `{FLAT}`** — certain, dry, administrative. The narrator knows how this ends.
- **One `{WARM}` crack, spent once.** It is worth more than every joke in the script; do not spend
  it before scene 11.
- 🔑 **One admitted uncertainty across the whole run.** A narrator that is never wrong reads as a
  device to keep you watching. The crack has to cost it something.
- **Read every line aloud with the picture off.** If it is not a complete radio-drama beat on its
  own, it is not finished.
