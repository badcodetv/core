# shortwave-static-signal: notes (round 2, 2026-11-16)

The experiment: a radio as the lead performer; static, tuning whine and interference as the music, a buried fragment of an old melody drifting in and out, the signal worsening as something nears, bursts on each flicker, a final blast and hard cut to dead air.

What shaped it:

- The reference series uses radio static as a proximity warning: noise that rises when a threat is near, and doubles as tension and as part of the score (Destructoid, via search summary: https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=165030; Sputnik Music review counting radio noise, footsteps and dogs as part of the soundtrack: https://sputnikmusic.com/review/73089/Akira-Yamaoka-Silent-Hill/). That is the "something getting nearer, signal worsening" spine of the prompt.
- Reviewers describe the ambient tracks as a faint hum "like a faraway breeze", a "low buzzing", knives being sharpened, breathing, then eruptions into "a thundering wall of industrial noise", with melody only briefly surfacing (Soundtrack Central: https://soundtrackcentral.com/albums/307/silent-hill-original-soundtrack). Hence the mains hum, the metallic shutter scrape, the noise thickening into a wall, and the melody kept to a two-second scrap.
- The composer's stated reason for industrial sound: cold and rusty (2002 interview: https://www.silenthillmemories.net/creators/interviews/2002.07.16_yamaoka_spelmusik_en.htm). Kept the "rust" in the scrape, not in drums (drums are another writer's lane).
- Radio-as-instrument precedent: Conflux Coldwell's *AM*, an album made only from a 1980s worldband receiver, and the Conet Project's long stretches of static (search summaries: https://treblezine.com/conet-project-our-band-couldnt-be-life/, https://cdm.link/music-from-numbers-an-eclectic-free-cc-compilation-of-numbers-station-inspired-tracks/). Shortwave vocabulary taken from there: carrier tones, heterodyne squeals, fading.
- Round 1 reference "Damned" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LGD79pQY1kI): its public page gave only the title, no description.

Choices:
- No game, composer or track named. The "old melody" is a far-off 1950s waltz so Suno has something faint to bury, but "clean melody" and "piano lead" are excluded so it cannot take over.
- Four flicker bursts written to match the picture (lights dip, door, tube browns out, lightning flash), not a fixed grid; "near silence" between them so the hits read.
- Dry, mono, no reverb tail: a portable radio in a small shop, and it keeps the end cut hard. Excluded "riser" and "bass drop" so the trailer ending is a blast of static, not a cinematic whoosh.
- Excluded "radio announcer" and "spoken word": radio prompts invite a DJ voice, and shorts tracks carry no speech.
- v6, variety high, weirdness 75 (noise-first piece needs room), style influence 70, duration 17 (expect ~15).
