# basses-from-below: what shaped it

- The low semitone figure that speeds up as the threat nears is the classic double-bass approach motif (the shark film's E-F pulse, a minor second alternating with its root for instability). - Basses and cellos can carry a rumbling drone and grow just by digging in with more bow pressure, so the prompt asks for "heavy bow pressure" and "bows biting the string" rather than more instruments. Source: https://premiumbeat.com/blog/what-makes-music-sound-scary
- Rare high violin stabs and upward glissandi are the only top end, so the stabs land hard against a dark bottom-heavy bed and can be cut to the scare frames in the edit. Low glissandi are menacing when slow (Indie Tips on A Quiet Place); the high ones here are fast and sudden for contrast. Source: https://indietips.com/?p=61845
- Suno advice: keep Styles positive and concrete and under ~100 words, put unwanted sounds in Exclude rather than "no ..." phrases, and don't overload Exclude or contradict it. So "choir" is left out of the Styles text (the brief's "choir of basses" is described as a low string section) and kept in Exclude.
