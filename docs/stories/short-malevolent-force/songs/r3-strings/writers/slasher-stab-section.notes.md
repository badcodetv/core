# slasher-stab-section: what shaped it

- The 1960 shower-murder cue is built from each string part entering in turn, stacking into a semitone cluster (E, E-flat, F, G-flat), with the lower strings repeating a descending half step; the bowing is hard and the glissandi make a slashing gesture. Hence "semitone cluster chords, violas and cellos rubbing a half step apart", "hard downbows", "slashing upward glissando". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Murder , https://royalholloway.ac.uk/research-and-teaching/departments-and-schools/music/about-us/teacherhubmusic/bernard-herrmanns-film-music-psycho-and-others , https://royalholloway.ac.uk/media/19436/herrmann%20teachinghub.pdf
- The score used muted strings for an unusual, restricted timbre, and fermatas set against glissandi to stretch tension; that became "muted violin section" plus the near-silent held bass and black gaps. Source: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/bernard-hermann-and-the-terrifying-sound-of-psycho/
- No composer or film named (Suno filters names; brief forbids real people): described plainly as a "1960 black-and-white thriller recording", "small dry string orchestra", "mono-feeling" to keep it away from big modern trailer sound.
- Exclude field: guides say list the few elements that matter most and give the removed role a replacement in the style. So drums/braam/epic/trailer/synth/choir are excluded, and the style names exactly what carries the cue (muted violins, low bass pedal). "Major key resolution" added because the brief says Suno tends to resolve the dissonance.
