# Pub Quiz: Housing: script, draft 1 (2026-11-20)

> The argument in plain words, so the point is right before anything is funny. **Every ⬜ is a joke slot for Jack and
> Kai** (`docs/rules/working.md`): it says what the joke is about, never the joke. Lines marked DRAFT are the facts and
> reasoning each person brings; rewrite them freely. Whispered, quick, overlapping; nobody makes a speech.

## The question (decided by Jack: ⬜)

Draft 1 uses: **"How much did councils in England spend on temporary accommodation last year? Nearest wins."**
- Why this one: the answer is a number nobody guesses high enough (£2.9 billion), and the follow-up (where it went) is the
  point. Every person at the table has a different way into it.
- Other questions that fit the same table, for later rounds or other shorts: how many homes have stood empty for six months
  or more (303,185); how many children are in temporary accommodation (176,130, Dec 2025); how many homes are nobody's main
  home (second homes, 267,894). Facts and sources: `badcode.md`.

## Who is at the table (proposed; Jack and Kai rule)

| Who | What they know, from their own life | Their way into the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The plasterer** (a tradesperson) | He's done up rooms the council rents | "I've seen what they pay for" |
| **The banker** | How money moves; he's not the villain | "Renting by the night is the dearest way to buy anything" |
| **Bob** | He's lived in one of those rooms | Quiet; the one who actually knows |
| **The student** ⬜ (a guess at "from the campus": voice-typed, maybe "from the Camping story". Jack to say) | Rent, and what most people think it costs | Guesses low, like the viewer would |

The next table along leans in to listen; the whispering is to stop them. ⬜ what the eavesdroppers do.

## The beats (about 50 s)

### 1. The question (0-6 s)
- **Quizmaster** (BadCode in the balaclava, in a Guy Ritchie-film-style outfit: Jack, 2026-11-20), into the mic: reads the question. "Thirty seconds." The clock starts on the screen.
- The table pulls in close. ⬜ the team name on their answer sheet.

### 2. The huddle (6-38 s): PICKED, take 1D (Jack, 2026-11-20: "1A combined with 1C")
From the dialogue page https://claude.ai/artifact/TQ54rNs2whoBWjLo3Z1thC. Whispered and short; about 32 s.

1. **Student** [writes on the sheet, pen in close-up]: "A hundred million? That's loads."
2. **Plasterer** [crosses it out]: "More. I've done three of them B&B jobs for the council this year. Every room, every night."
3. **Student** [on a beer mat]: "Every night… Say fifty quid a night. I don't know how much things are, I only buy CBD ramen suppositories. Times three-sixty-five. Times a hundred thousand rooms?" (Jack's line, 2026-11-20)
4. **Student** [stares at the beer mat]: "That's billions. That can't be right."
5. **Next table** leans in. **Plasterer**: "Fuck off, get your own answers." They lean back. (Jack's line, 2026-11-20)
6. **Quizmaster** [off]: "Twenty seconds."
7. **Banker** [doesn't look up, like he's explaining it to children]: "Paying by the night is the dearest way to buy anything. It's billions with a B. Keep up, you morons." (Jack's line, 2026-11-20) ⬜ what the banker lets slip about himself (still open).
8. **Banker** [low]: "Bob."
9. **Bob** [takes the pen, no hurry]: "I know literally no one who's been in them." (Jack's line, 2026-11-20)
10. **Bob** writes a number and turns the sheet so only the table sees.
11. **Banker** reads it: a small nod.
12. **Quizmaster**: "Pens down."

- The student's sum is his guess ("say", and he admits he doesn't know prices): fifty × 365 × 100,000 ≈ £1.8 billion, short of the real £2.9 billion, so Bob is still nearer.
- To trim to ~28 s: drop "Twenty seconds." and "Bob."

### 3. The answer (38-46 s): AGREED (Jack, 2026-11-20: "this works")
- **Quizmaster**: "Two point nine billion pounds. One point eight billion of it on B&Bs and nightly lets."
- The table looks at Bob's sheet. ⬜ how close Bob was, and what that does to the table.

### 4. The way out (46-54 s): Jack's picks, 2026-11-20 (two of the suggested exchanges; the order is Claude's)
- **Student**: "So they rent the same rooms every year? Why don't they just own them?"
- **Banker**: "Now he gets it."
- **Plasterer**: "Every year. And at the end nobody owns a single brick."
- **Banker**: "So buy the bricks."
- Logo.

## What this draft is trying to get right
- **The argument is the point, said by people who'd know** (Jack's ask): the trades, the money and the lived side each get one reason.
- **Not preachy** (Kai, 17 Nov): nobody explains to the camera; they argue to win a quiz.
- **The reader** (`docs/marketing/the-reader.md`): Bob is the hero, not a lesson; the anger points at a decision (renting
  by the night) and not at anyone at the table; built to be repeated in a real pub (rule 8).
- **Length:** about 50 s, over the 10-20 s shorts guide and under the 60 s limit. If it must be shorter, the student's
  push-back (huddle 4) and the eavesdroppers go first.
