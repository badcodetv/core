# The Bank Robbery — review of cut 1 (8 October 2026)

**Asked for by Jack, 8 October 2026:** "be as honest as possible, what would you get rid of, what would you
change."

**How it was reviewed.** Picture: one frame a second of the 8 min 30 s render, read by eye (510 frames).
Sound: the whole soundtrack through the listening tool (Gemini 3.5 Flash) in two halves, plus level
measurements of every clip. Nobody has heard it with human ears in this session, so anything about accent or
performance below is **the model's report, not a finding.** Web research on comedy cutting, ducking and AI
footage is summarised at the foot.

**Coverage line.** Eleven rich people rob a bank by putting money in, while the country watches two of them
argue. Really about: the row is the distraction and the theft is legal. **Verdict: the narrator works, the
film around him does not yet.**

## Gates

Passed. The beneficiary is named in words (the Donor, "he's just early"), the crowds are never the joke, and
the immigration line keeps its proportion ("some of that's real").

## Kept (do not fix)

- The narrator's voice and almost all his lines. Best: the opening, "an accountant becoming aroused", "it's
  got BANK written on it", "that's a hobby", "there isn't a crime number for that".
- "Nothing. We put it in." and "Thanks for the… advice."
- The low-angle look: the hearing, the Ex on the steps, the vault through its round door, the model town.
- Denise's finger-and-thumb line, played offhand.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Music:** one heist bed for the whole film and an 80s sting for the cards (Suno).
2. **Re-make six shots:** a readable SOLD sign; the savings-book shot with café Denise and no sign behind her;
   the kebab shop with a blank sign for the logo; Mr Blue on the name card, at the party and in the standoff;
   one pallet walk that matches the dusk flares.
3. **Jack's tags for the name cards.** ("THE ACCOUNTANT — ACCOUNTANT" is, by accident, the only funny one.)
4. **Decide the six cut lines and scene 6** in cut 2.
5. **New lettering.**

## Research used (8 October 2026)

- Practitioner: Roger Nygard (Veep, Curb), "faster is funnier", an "air pass" to remove pauses, never continue
  after the punchline. Gary Dollner (Fleabag): a held pause has to earn its length. Nobody found states a
  fixed number of frames.
- Standard: UK DPP asks for speech at least 4 LU over background; WCAG 1.4.7 asks for 20 dB. YouTube plays
  at about −14 LUFS and turns loud files down, never quiet ones up.
- Measured, one study (Sarfraz, Karim, Kim, IASDR 2025, n=87): image-to-video clips were spotted as AI more
  often than text-to-video. Ours are image-to-video. One study, not replicated.
- Found nothing on joke density for The Mash Report or Frankie Boyle's shows.
