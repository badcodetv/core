## The sentence

- **Theme:** new money goes to the people who own things first, so everyone who rents pays
  double for the same life, and the row keeps all of us looking the other way.
- **Anti-theme (let it breathe first):** we are poorer because of each other. That is Barry and
  Denise's row.
- **The costed objects:** the Fredo jar and Denise's rent. Both planted in the opening, both pay
  off at the end.
- **Hero:** Denise and Barry. **Chooser:** the planners. **Who gains, on screen:** the man who
  picks up the house keys (beat 23).

## The opening — AGREED (Jack, 15 October 2026)

**Jack's brief:** everything quick; one café, no flags; two working-class people with the same
problem, rent: **Barry in a hi-vis jacket, and Denise, who owns the café.** Doris (from the
meeting) is dropped. Lean into the right-wing side of things. Start on a Dunkirk action scene,
push in, pull out: it's a painting on the café wall, with Denise and Barry arguing about rent
under it. **The row:** same problem, different culprit. Barry blames the boats, Denise blames the
landlords and the council; neither is knocked down; the narrator then points at the third
culprit.

**The look of the beach (Jack):** Saving Private Ryan, or Call of Duty: World at War. My notes,
not yet through `shot-craft`: drained colour, hard grain, a choppy narrow-shutter feel, handheld at
the waterline with spray on the lens, men wading out to the little boats, a dive bomber screaming
over, sand thrown up. **No gore** (Flow will block it, and the point is the courage, not the
wounds). Saving Private Ryan is D-Day, not Dunkirk: we borrow the look and keep the event, the
evacuation under fire.
