1. **The flank-mount.** The camera is bolted low on the getaway car's running board and looks forward along its black, rain-wet bodywork, so the car stays locked in the frame for all 8 seconds and never jumps or drifts. Over the shouted "Robbery!", a gloved arm pushes a tommy gun up out of the rear window and fires a short burst at the night sky. Brass shell casings tumble past the lens, and the police car's red lamp bobs far behind. The psychedelic overlay is the car's own paint and chrome. The whole city slides across the curved panels as a warped reflection: unlit neon tubes, shop-window bulbs and the police lamp stretch, pool and swirl into liquid colour, like the city in the man's jacket but moving. **Why it won't look like AI slop:** the car can't teleport because the camera is fixed to it, there's only one camera move, and every bit of psychedelic colour comes from a real reflective surface, not added digital trails.

2. **The puddle.** A strange, David Lynch-style angle. The camera sits still at road level, almost inside a puddle on a cobbled crossroads lit by gas lamps. The getaway car drives along one straight, readable line, its front wheel hits the puddle a few inches from the lens, and the police car follows on the same line a beat behind. As it passes, we glimpse the tommy gun angled up out of the window at the sky. The psychedelic overlay is a film of spilled engine oil on the water. The splash breaks it into rainbow swirls that spread outward, and the upside-down reflection of the car, the gun and the neon sky bends inside it. **Why it won't look like AI slop:** a fixed camera with a clear path in and out means the cars have nowhere to teleport, and the colour comes from a real physical effect (light on oil and water) with real splash physics, which reads as filmed and not generated.

3. **The pursuer's windscreen.** The camera sits in the police car's passenger seat and looks through its rain-streaked windscreen at the getaway car, which stays the same distance ahead the whole time. Its rear lamp glows, and the tommy gun sticks straight up out of its window at the sky. On "Robbery!" the gun's muzzle flashes light up the falling rain. The psychedelic overlay is the windscreen itself. Each slow sweep of the wiper wipes the glass clear for a moment, then fresh rain smears the neon, headlamps and muzzle flashes back into soft, sprayed arcs of saturated colour, the "paint-spray" dream look Jack asked for, re-forming with every sweep. **Why it won't look like AI slop:** the distance between the two cars never changes so the chase can't break, the dreamlike look comes from real glass, water and a mechanical wiper with a steady rhythm, and the dashboard, gauges and the officer's hands in the foreground give the frame period texture instead of the usual empty, glossy AI scene.

All three keep the gun pointed only at the sky, and none has a number plate or lit sign with readable lettering. My research supports the main choices: Google's own docs and guides say to use one camera move and one clear action per clip, and to describe only the motion when you animate a still.
