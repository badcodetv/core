=== SCENE 30 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, January 1928, late afternoon in hard cold. The camera sits at knee height behind a wooden wheelbarrow at the very back of a long bread queue, tilted a few degrees, looking up the line toward a small bakery under the iron girders of the elevated railway; the bakery door sits small and off to the right third, far away. The wheelbarrow is heaped with bundled banknotes, their faces blurred and illegible, a cracked handle wrapped in rag. Ahead, the price grows backwards down the line: a woman in a man's overcoat clutches a canvas sack, a South Asian dock worker holds a fistful of notes, and at the front an old man holds out one coin. Snow is dry, trampled grey, banked against soot-crusted brick. The air is full of diamond dust: tiny ice crystals hanging in the low sun, each one flashing red, green and violet, and around the sun a huge pale halo ring with two bright rainbow sun dogs either side, the whole sky ringed in spectral colour over the black girders. Breath steams from every mouth. Skin chapped and raw, grain in the shadows. The shop window and every sign are frosted over and blank.
WHY: The queue's growing money and the sun-dog halo, a real ice-crystal phenomenon, make the inflation visible in a frozen sky as rich colour with a physical cause.

=== SCENE 31 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, night. The camera is down inside the open trapdoor behind a bakery counter, looking straight up through the square wooden hatch, the frame canted so the hatch sits as a tilted diamond. Ringed around its edge, faces lean over and stare down into the lens: working people young and old, men and women, Black, white and South Asian, some in red scarves, some in blue, shoulder to shoulder now, the shoving forgotten, a flour-dusted counter edge and a hanging oil lamp behind them. The only light comes from below, from the mint's furnace where copper blanks are melting: the flames burn green and blue-violet the way copper burns, so the faces are lit from underneath in shifting emerald and violet, with hot orange at the rims of nostrils and chins. Rising heat bends the air, so the ring of faces ripples and warps like a reflection in disturbed water, one face stretched long, another doubled. Splintered hatch boards, iron hinge rust, flour and coal grit drifting down through the light. Real skin, stubble, chapped lips, wet eyes. No lettering anywhere.
WHY: Copper's real green-violet flame and the heat shimmer rise from the mint itself, so the crowd's discovery is coloured and bent by the thing they have just found.

=== SCENE 32 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, a brick-vaulted basement mint lit by a coal furnace. Intimate and low: the camera sits at the height of a polished silver tray, close, tilted, the lower third of the frame filled by the tray's curved mirror surface. Holding it out with trembling hands is the mayor, a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache, sweat running down his temple, his back pressed against the furnace's riveted iron door whose seams glow orange. On the tray stands a three-tiered iced cake, one tier slumping in the heat. The crowd fills the room beyond him, out of focus, many holding old railway hand lanterns with red, blue and green glass lenses, the beams crossing through coal smoke in coloured shafts. In the tray's convex mirror the crowd and its lanterns curve into a bulging bowl of stretched faces and smeared red, blue and green light, while heat shimmer off the furnace makes the mayor's outline waver. Soot on the bricks, printing ink on the floor. No lettering anywhere.
WHY: The silver tray becomes a funhouse mirror holding the whole crowd, so the mayor's offering literally reflects who now has him cornered, in coloured lantern light with a real source.

=== SCENE 33 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, deep night. The camera looks down from a second-floor tenement window, high and steeply angled, off-centre, through a hanging net-lace curtain close to the lens. Below lies the street under the elevated railway where the car chase ran, now completely empty: dry pavement, a long black getaway sedan abandoned askew across the tram tracks with both doors open, a crushed fedora in the gutter, one red scarf and one blue scarf lying tangled together on the kerb, scattered playing cards. Down the street the bakery's cellar hatch stands open, a faint warm glow rising from it. The fine weave of the lace diffracts every light: each street lamp, the El's signal lamps and the cellar glow bloom into large soft star-crosses and rings of rainbow colour, magenta, gold and green spokes radiating across the dark girders and black sky, the curtain's pattern itself softly visible as a veil. One real light per pool, deep unlit black between them, coal soot on the brick, steam drifting from a grate. Every shopfront and sign is dark and blank.
WHY: Seen through a curtain whose weave turns the lamps into rainbow stars, the emptied chase street is beautiful and silent, and the open cellar tells us where everyone went.
