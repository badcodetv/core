=== SCENE 14 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed: Chicago, 1928, a cold dry night in a small town square of trampled frost and cinder grit, the iron girders of the elevated railway cutting across the top of the frame. We look from low behind the bandstand rail, a peeling green rail and a dented brass music stand in the near foreground, past the mayor's shoulder: a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache, one arm flung up as a tray of flash powder bursts in his hand. The burst is the only light. It throws billowing clouds of chemical smoke, strontium crimson boiling into copper blue-green and sodium yellow, lit from inside so the colour rolls over a hundred upturned faces in flat caps and headscarves, mouths open. Through a torn gap in the coloured smoke, off to the right and far back, a dusty black Packard sedan lurches away from the granite bank steps, rear wheels spitting grit, a tommy gun barrel poking up at the sky from its back window. The frame tilts a few degrees. Everything outside the smoke falls to deep black. All signs and paper stay blank, no lettering.
WHY: The mayor's distraction is literally the light source, coloured pyrotechnic smoke makes the psychedelic layer physical, and the robbery hides in the one gap nobody is looking at.

=== SCENE 15 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed: Chicago, 1928, night, seen from inside a shut bakery, low behind its front-window display. In the near foreground, out of focus, a row of tall glass sweet jars and domed glass cake covers filled with boiled sweets in raspberry red, lime, violet and amber; the street beyond is seen partly through them, bent and swollen into coloured lenses, partly through the clear glass between. Outside on the dry, ash-scuffed pavement, under one gas street lamp, a red-scarf crowd and a blue-scarf crowd shove each other: working people, young and old, men and women, Black, white and South Asian, in worn coats and mismatched caps, team colour only on their scarves and on two hand-stitched cloth banners with no lettering. Fists grab collars, a hat is knocked flying. Walking calmly through the gap between them, unhurried, is a gangster in a chalk-stripe suit and fedora, a pockmarked face and a toothpick, a lumpy canvas sack of money over his shoulder. The lamp is the only light outside; inside, a flour-dusted counter edge and a cold proving tray sit in black shadow. Camera off-centre, framed low and slightly canted. Sweet jars and windows carry no labels or lettering.
WHY: Shooting through the bakery's own jars of coloured sweets turns the street fight into a candy-coloured distortion while the theft walks through the clear glass, unbent.

=== SCENE 16 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed: Chicago, 1928, night, a tight crush of bodies in the street. The camera sits at hip height inside the crowd, tilted, so the two back pockets fill the lower half of the frame in sharp focus: a red-scarf man on the left and a blue-scarf man on the right, both pockets holding the same thin brown pay envelope, creased, blank, sticking out. Above, looming and cropped by the top edge, their two faces press nose to nose, shouting, spit in the air, stubble, a split lip, a broken tooth, sweat on a vein in the temple. Someone in the crowd holds a burning red railway flare on one side and someone else a lantern with blue glass on the other; the camera lens has been smeared at its edges with grease, leaving the centre clear, so the flare and the lantern smear outward into long dragging streaks of crimson and cobalt that wrap around the frame like hot wax, while the envelopes in the middle stay crisp and plain. Shoulders, coat wool and elbows crowd every edge in near darkness. Dry night, the street unseen. The envelopes and anything else carry no writing at all.
WHY: A grease-smeared lens with a clear centre turns the two team colours into a raging smear around the frame while the identical pay envelopes, the actual point, stay sharp in the middle.

=== SCENE 17 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed: Chicago, 1928, a low-ceilinged speakeasy club in a brick basement, sawdust on the floor, spilled gin glinting on a scarred bar. The camera lies on the floor beside the bass drum, looking up through the legs of the band, gangsters in pinstripes with a battered trumpet and a banjo, at three people dancing arm in arm in the middle: the mayor, a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache, between the red leader, a ginger man with mutton-chop whiskers and a red carnation, and the blue leader, a woman with a black bob and a blue carnation, all three laughing. Behind the band, a hand-cranked lantern projector throws oil and dye squeezed between two hot glass plates, so slow bubbling blooms of magenta, emerald and orange crawl across the bricks, the dancers' faces and the low plank ceiling. Through the gaps in those ceiling boards, thin blades of lamplight from the street above flicker with the shadows of stamping, fighting feet, and dust sifts down onto the dancers. The frame is canted. No lettering anywhere.
WHY: The money shot needs the richest layer, so a liquid-light projector bathes the leaders' cosy dance while the fight upstairs only reaches them as shadows and dust through the floorboards.
