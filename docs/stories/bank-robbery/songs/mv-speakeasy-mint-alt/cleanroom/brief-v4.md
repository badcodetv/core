# Omni / Veo image-to-video: psychedelic but filmed (web research, 2026-10-29)

Tiers: **T1** official Google docs · **T2** repeated by several practitioners · **T3** a single anecdote or vendor claim · **Inference** is my own reasoning and is marked as such.

## Bottom line

No source, official or community, tests "psychedelic but realistic" on Omni or Veo. What follows combines three things. First, the official rules for how Omni and Veo read a start-frame prompt. Second, general failure-mode advice from practitioners. Third, the history of in-camera psychedelia, which is the strongest lever we have. The idea: ask for **a real optical device or a real material in the frame**, rather than for "trippy". The rest of this file supports that idea, but it is an inference and it needs testing.

## 1. How the model reads a start-frame prompt

- **Omni chooses how to use an image from your words.** "Depending on your prompt, the model will decide how to use the image." Google says to use high-resolution images and describe the motion specifically: "Vague prompts like 'make it move' produce less compelling results." **T1**. Source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni
- **First frame and reference are separate roles.** In the API, `<FIRST_FRAME>` makes the image the opening frame. `<IMAGE_REF_N>` makes it a reference, and "should not be used as literal initial frames." **T1** (same page). *Inference:* the Flow "Frames" slot probably maps to FIRST_FRAME and "Ingredients" to references. No Flow help page confirms this.
- **Omni defaults to several shots.** Google says the model makes several shots by default. For one take you must write "In a single continuous shot" / "No scene cuts". Google's own example opens "Continuous, unbroken handheld shot of…" **T1** (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni). Google's Flow team repeats this: "By default, Gemini Omni attempts to cut between multiple angles… If you want a continuous 'oner' shot, you must explicitly state it." **T1** (Flow's X article, https://x.com/FlowbyGoogle/article/2105398907702595802. It was paywalled when I fetched it, so the quote comes via a search summary.) **This is the most important line for an 8s clip.**
- **Omni wants description, not micro-instructions.** DeepMind: compared with Veo, which "needs precise instructions", Omni does better when told what you want, and fills gaps from world knowledge. The five dimensions are shot framing and motion, style, lighting, location and action. **T1**. Source: https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-omni/prompt-guide
- **Timing cues work.** Natural language ("After 3 seconds, a woman enters the scene") and timecodes (`[0-3s] A person is walking`, `[3-6s] They stop and turn around`) are both official. **T1** (ai.google.dev). Veo 3.1's guide uses `[00:00-00:02]` blocks to direct several shots in one generation. **T1**. Source: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1. *Caution (inference):* on Veo, timestamp blocks are presented as a way to make several *shots*, so they may invite cuts. For a oner, pair them with "single continuous shot".
- **Audio is generated by default.** Describe the audio you want. Remove what you don't want in plain words, for example "No dialogue" or "No extra sound effects". **T1** (ai.google.dev). Veo's official syntax puts dialogue in quotes, sound effects after `SFX:` and ambience after `Ambient noise:`, in sentences separate from the visuals. **T1** (Veo 3.1 guide; Vertex prompt guide https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide). Omni can also time visuals to sound: "The lights of the apartments start turning on in sync with the music." **T1** (DeepMind guide).
- **Don't re-describe the image; describe what changes.** Practitioners say the image is "static truth" and the text should say what changes. A prompt that contradicts the image can make the model swing between the two, which produces jitter or a "wiggle". **T2**. Sources: https://www.imaginary.app/blog/image-to-video-that-actually-moves-not-just-wiggles-a-9-step-troubleshooting-che and https://replicate.com/blog/veo-3-image (Veo "maintains the artistic integrity of your original image", so you rarely need to restate the look). Google's own example does describe the scene ("Continuous, unbroken handheld shot of a fluffy tabby cat sitting on a sunny windowsill…"), so a short anchor that agrees with the frame is fine. Contradicting the frame is the problem.
- **Length.** No official word count. Google says be "extremely detailed" about characters and environments when generating, and keep edit prompts simple. **T1**. Third-party guides suggest about 20 words (Omni) or 15–30 words (Veo 3). **T3**: https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/3513/mastering-gemini-omni-flash-video-prompting-guide. *Unknown:* whether long prompts hurt Omni on a start frame. Nobody has measured it.
- **Prose or lists.** Every official example is prose, apart from the timecode blocks. **T1** (observation).

**What the models can do (inference; untested by any source):**
- *Likely fine:* a real thing in frame changing, such as light through a glass refracting into colour, coloured gels changing on a practical lamp, a projection of oil and colour sliding over a face or wall, smoke catching a coloured beam, a rack focus through a cut-glass tumbler. All of these are "world knowledge" that Omni says it uses. DeepMind says film look comes from naming a "film camera" or "vintage film camera" style. **T1** for the vocabulary, inference for the result.
- *Risky:* true kaleidoscope symmetry over 8s, slit-scan time-smear, step-printing and smear frames. These are effects of *time* and *optics*, not things in a scene, and video diffusion models are known to be weak on optics. Research benchmarks report missing or wrong mirror reflections and impossible refraction (VideoPhy-2, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06800; PhyEduVideo, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00943; PhyGround shows a reflection morphing through several shapes, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10806). **T2**, but none of these benchmarks tested Veo or Omni.
- *Probably better in post:* step printing, film burn, light leaks, chromatic aberration, strobe, double exposure across shots. These are exact, repeatable and cheap in an editor. *Inference.* Note that Google lists "double exposure" as a *text-rendering* effect Omni can do (Pasqualepillitteri, quoting the @GoogleAI post https://twitter.com/GoogleAI/status/2059381218660270435, T3 via a secondary source), not as an image effect.

**Unknown:** I found no tested Veo or Omni psychedelic prompt. The nearest is a vendor template ("Glass Dreams", a stained-glass fish casting rainbows, https://www.hooked.so/templates/veo3/glass-dreams-a-surreal-dance-in-the-desert) with no evidence about its results.

## 3. Failure modes and fixes

- **Random cuts.** This is the Omni default (**T1**). Fix: "single continuous shot, no scene cuts", stated plainly.
- **Melting, everything moving at once, motion starting then melting.** Practitioners put it down to too many competing actions. Fix: one subject, one main motion, one beat; "a single action followed by a stop"; name the part that moves and say everything else stays still. **T2** (imaginary.app; https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/why-your-ai-video-looks-too-smooth-2026-a-troubleshooting-guide-to-fix-plastic-s gives the same advice: one action beat plus one camera move).
- **Face drift and morphing.** Fix: let the start frame carry identity, don't contradict it, and keep "what must stay" lines short. For first-and-last-frame work, make the two frames similar and describe physical motion, not "smooth transition". **T2** (bigprompthub first/last-frame guide, https://bigprompthub.com/google-flow-start-end-frame-video ; imaginary.app). Changing one word in a character description changes the character (**T3**, https://alejandrodinsmore.substack.com/p/deep-dive-veo-3-i-spent-400-testing).
- **Vague words give generic results.** "Make it move" is weak (**T1**). Without surprising specifics Veo "fills in the blanks" generically (**T3**, Dinsmore). *Inference:* "trippy" and "psychedelic" on their own will summon the generic swirl. Name the device instead.
- **Slow motion.** I found no official statement about a slow-motion default. A third-party guide says slow motion is triggered by words such as "slow motion", "high-speed camera", "high frame rate" and "time stretched" (**T3**, https://www.veo3ai.io/blog/veo-3-slow-motion-video-prompts-2026). Dinsmore used "no slow motion" as a negative, with no evidence it works (**T3**). *Inference:* "real-time", "normal speed", and naming a quick physical action are positive phrasings to try.
- **Camera drift.** Omni's vocabulary: "static", "locked off", "fixed". **T1** (DeepMind). One practitioner says avoid "handheld" if you want clean motion (**T3**, imaginary.app). Google's own oner example *is* handheld, so this is a choice, not a rule.
- **Plastic or over-clean look.** Name a lens, light and film stock ("shot as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy" is Google's own Veo example, **T1**). On Reddit, a Veo 3.1 user reported that negatives for "plastic" did nothing, and grain or handheld words helped a little (**T3**). Several vendors say raw AI video is over-sharp, over-saturated and too clean, and do the fix in post: slight blur, then grain at low opacity (**T2**, vendor sources: https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/why-your-ai-videos-look-fake-(and-how-to-fix-them-step-by-step) ; https://invideo.io/faq/why-does-ai-generated-video-look-plasticky-or-fake-and/).
- **Text appearing.** Omni renders text readily and treats it as a feature (**T1**). Put "no on-screen text, no captions" in the prompt.
- **Should you ban or describe?** The official guidance differs by model. Omni has no negative-prompt parameter, so write "Do not do X" in the prompt itself (**T1**, ai.google.dev). For Veo, Google says describe what you want instead of what to exclude: "a desolate landscape with no buildings or roads" rather than "no man-made structures" (**T1**, Veo 3.1 guide). The Vertex guide warns against "no"/"don't" in the negative field and says to list the items instead (**T1**). *Practical reading:* describe the positive state first, and keep a very short "no cuts, no text, no dialogue" line for the things Omni does by default.

## 4. Keeping motion physical

- **One clear action, as a change from one state to another, with concrete verbs** (lift, pour, turn, not drift or float). **T2** (imaginary.app).
- **Give the camera one job.** Push in, pan, dolly zoom, locked off, or one continuous shot. Verbs matter: "glide" and "rush" give noticeably different results (**T3**, https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/guides/gemini-omni-prompt-guide).
- **Give light a source.** DeepMind's lighting dimension: the source (sun, streetlamp, off-screen) and the quality (crisp, warm, ethereal). **T1**. *Inference:* a colour change will read as real if a source in frame causes it: a gel wheel turning on a lamp, a projector beam crossing, a neon sign flickering.
- **Have a real object do the transforming (inference, built on the history above).** Instead of "the room melts", write "a cut-glass decanter is lifted into the lamp beam and its facets throw sliding prisms of colour across the faces", or "an oil-and-dye projection slides across the wall and over his face", or "the camera pushes in through a whisky glass and the room bends in the curved glass". Everything strange then has a cause in the scene, as it did in the 1960s light shows and Gondry's in-camera kaleidoscopes. Beware: the benchmarks above say refraction and reflection geometry are weak points, so expect some takes to bend wrongly. Re-roll rather than add words.

## 5. Flow and Omni specifics (2025–2026)

- **Durations:** generations run 3 to 10s at 24fps. One FAQ says the Flow UI offers fixed 4, 6, 8 and 10s. **T3** (https://invideo.io/faq/what-video-clip-lengths-does-google-omni-flash-support/). Extension adds up to 10s at a time, to 40s in total (**T1**, ai.google.dev). Omni 1.1 is newer (https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/). I could not confirm which version Flow serves today.
- **Audio** is generated with every clip (**T1**).
- **First and last frames** are supported in Flow and are Google's main tool against drift: they fix composition, palette and subject before the move. **T1/T2** (DeepMind guide; Flow X article via summary; https://androidauthority.com/google-flow-gemini-omni-1-1-flash-video-update-3704288).
- **Conversational editing:** "Keep everything else the same", one change per turn. **T1**. Useful when a take is nearly right ("Speed up the pacing", "Change the camera angle…"), rather than re-rolling.
- **English only** is fully supported (**T1**).
- **Unknown:** Flow's own help pages for Omni (I found none), whether Flow's "Frames" mode passes the image as FIRST_FRAME exactly, and any community test of psychedelic work on Omni. Reddit turned up nothing usable.

## Do this

1. Start with **"Single continuous shot, no scene cuts."** Omni cuts by default.
2. **Don't re-describe the still.** Give one short anchor that agrees with it, then write what *changes*: one action, from start state to end state.
3. **One camera job** (locked off / slow push in / dolly zoom), named in plain camera words. Add "vintage film camera" or a named film stock for the filmed look.
4. **Make the psychedelia physical:** name the object that causes it (cut glass, prism, gel wheel, oil projection, smoke in a beam, a curved bottle) and what its light does. Never just "trippy".
5. **Motivate every colour change** with a source in frame.
6. Use **timing cues** ("after 4 seconds…") for an 8s two-beat clip, but keep it to one shot.
7. **Audio as its own sentences.** State what you want, and add "No dialogue" if needed.
8. **Positive state first, then a short guard line:** "no on-screen text, no captions". Write "real-time speed" instead of hoping it won't go slow.
9. **Leave to post** what models can't do reliably: step printing, film burn, light leaks, chromatic aberration, strobe, grain.
10. If a take is nearly there, **edit it in conversation** ("keep everything else the same") rather than re-prompting. Expect wrong refraction on some takes.
