=== SCENE 2 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a basement bakery kitchen under a Chicago tenement, 1928, four in the morning. The camera sits on the flour-caked brick floor beside the open mouth of a coal-fired brick oven, tilted, looking up past the glowing door. In the left third of the frame stands the baker, a heavy-set Black man in his fifties, shaved head, broken nose, double chin, sweat-stained vest, flour on his forearms, eyes half shut, fists sunk in a slab of dough on a scarred maple bench. Two other bakers knead behind him, soft and half lost in the dark. The oven is the only light: deep orange and coal-red, falling off to black in the corners. Heat pouring from the oven mouth bends the air into a rippling mirage, so the baker's shoulders, the hanging copper pans and the soot-blackened ceiling joists wobble and stretch like a reflection in moving water, and the flour drifting in the air catches the fire as swirling clouds of amber, rose and violet sparks. Burst flour sacks, a coal scuttle, cracked tiles, grease on the brick. Ordinary skin with pores and sweat, film grain. Every surface free of lettering.
WHY: The heat mirage makes the oven itself the psychedelic layer, warping the one tired man who does the real work from the floor where his flour falls.

=== SCENE 3 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: an extreme close shot at the back door of a Chicago bakery at dawn, 1928, dry cinders and spilled flour on the step. The frame is filled by the baker's open palm, broad, cracked, flour in the creases, held low at the bottom right; tipped into it from a torn thin brown pay envelope are three small worn coins and one pale dusty moth, wings half open. Above, out of focus and cut off by the top edge, the foreman's waistcoat and watch chain. The camera is level with the palm, tilted, as if lying on a crate. Low dawn sun comes through a stacked crate of empty green, amber and cobalt soda bottles beside the door, and the bottle glass throws shifting caustic pools of light, liquid rings and veins of emerald, honey and deep blue, swimming across the palm, the coins, the moth's wings and the soot-streaked brick wall behind. Everything else is cold grey dawn and deep shadow. Specific wear: a chipped enamel doorplate, a broken bottle neck, frayed cuff of a flour-dusted shirt, the coins dull and nicked. Grain, real skin, no polish. All paper and metal plain, with no printing or lettering.
WHY: An intimate dawn close where the pay is so small the light through empty bottles is worth more than the coins, the caustics a physical psychedelic layer nobody used before.

=== SCENE 4 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a wide, high, vertiginous view straight down the stairwell of a five-storey Chicago tenement, 1928, grey morning light falling from a grimy skylight above. In the near foreground at upper left, huge and close, a landlord in a moth-eaten fur collar leans on the banister with his gloved palm turned up and out, waiting. On the landing shelf right beside the lens sits a round glass goldfish bowl, and half the frame is seen through it: the curved water bends the square stairwell into a spiralling funnel, the worn banisters coiling inward like a whirlpool, one orange fish smeared into a long copper streak across it. At the very bottom of the well, five floors down and tiny, the baker, a heavy-set Black man with a shaved head in a sweat-stained vest, climbs with a small envelope in his hand. A bare bulb on each landing gives a pool of sour yellow, and a red-glass night lamp on the third floor stains one coil of the stair crimson. Dry, scuffed treads worn to bare wood, peeling paint, a baby pram, a coal bucket, rope-tied laundry. Grain, deep shadow between the landings. Doors and walls bare of numbers or lettering.
WHY: The fishbowl lens turns the stairwell into a drain pulling the baker's pay upward to the waiting palm, a wide high shot to balance the close ones.

=== SCENE 5 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: night, the narrow back stairs behind a Chicago bakery, 1928, a plain dented steel door with a small sliding slot at eye height. The only light comes out of the open slot: inside, behind the door, a revolving mica lampshade painted with coloured fish and stars turns on a lamp, so a band of light spills out through the slot carrying drifting shapes of ruby, sea-green, saffron and violet. That moving band lies across the face of a heavy-set gangster in a chalk-stripe suit and a crushed brown fedora, pockmarked cheeks, a scar through one eyebrow, a toothpick in the corner of his mouth, leaning in close to murmur the password. The coloured shapes slide over his nose, his teeth and the curling smoke from his cigarette, which catches them like a projection screen. In the slot, two wary bloodshot eyes look out. The camera is low on the stairs below him, crooked, his chin and the slot filling the upper right; the rest of the stairwell falls to pure black. Scuffed steel, chipped green paint, a burst coal sack on the bottom step, cigarette ends ground into the boards. Grain, real skin. The door, walls and slot carry no lettering at all.
WHY: The first glimpse of the hidden world leaks out of a slot as turning lantern colour, a lamplight-night close that promises the party below.
