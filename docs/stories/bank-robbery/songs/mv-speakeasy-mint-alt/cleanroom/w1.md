=== STILL PROMPT (Nano Banana 2.1, 16:9) ===
A real film frame from a 1929 gangster picture, shot on 35mm Kodak 5219 with an anamorphic lens. It is a night getaway in the rain. The camera is clamped low on the front of the running board of a black 1928 Packard sedan and looks back along the car's left side toward the rear. It sits a few inches above the running board, with a slight Dutch tilt, like an odd, uneasy David Lynch angle. The long curved black body of the car runs from the near edge of the frame toward the back of the car on the right. The paint is soaked with rain, and there are mud splashes and small dents in the chrome trim. Out of the rear side window, a man's arm in a worn black leather glove and a wet wool overcoat sleeve pushes a Thompson submachine gun with a round drum magazine. Its muzzle points straight up at the night sky and away from everyone. We can't see his face. Far behind on the shiny cobblestone street, a single 1920s police car follows with one round red lamp on its roof, blurred by the shallow focus. Most of the colour comes from reflections. The curved black paint and chrome act like a fun-house mirror for the city going past: brick buildings, shop windows lit by warm bare bulbs, neon tubes bent into abstract loops and zigzags with no letters, and the red police lamp. In the reflection they stretch and swirl into liquid ribbons of magenta, emerald, cyan and amber, like paint marbled on water. The mist thrown up by the wheels lights up in coloured haze, like spray paint in the air. The colours are pushed and dreamlike, but every light comes from a real lamp, bulb, neon tube or reflection in the scene. The mood is film noir with deep black shadows. Real lens imperfections: rain beads on the lens, soft halation glow around the brightest lights, anamorphic oval bokeh, motion blur only on the passing street, a fine film grain, and a hard-sharp car that is completely still in the frame. The shop windows and signs have no readable words, letters or numbers anywhere, the licence plate is covered in mud, and there is no text, no logo, no watermark, no glossy CGI look, and no smooth plastic surfaces.

=== VIDEO PROMPT (Omni Flash, image-to-video, 8 s, sound on) ===
Animate this exact frame as one unbroken, continuous 8-second shot with no cuts. Keep the composition, lighting, colours, car, glove and gun just as they are in the reference image. The camera is bolted to the car's running board, so the black Packard stays fixed in the frame the whole time. It does not move, slide, jump, change shape or teleport. The only camera movement is the slight engine vibration and jolts from the road you get with a camera bolted to a car. The street and city go backward past the car at a steady speed, and the police car's red lamp stays far behind, bobbing gently over the cobblestones and never getting closer.
[0-2s] Rain streaks past. The reflections of neon, shop bulbs and the red lamp flow along the curved black panels and chrome, stretching and twisting into slow liquid swirls of magenta, emerald, cyan and amber as the curve of the body bends them. Coloured spray mist rolls off the wheels.
[2-4s] The gloved arm pushes the Thompson higher out of the rear window, muzzle straight up at the sky, and fires one short burst: bright orange muzzle flashes light up the wet roof and flash across all the reflections at once. Brass shell casings spin out and tumble past the lens in slow arcs, and they catch the neon light.
[4-8s] The gun stays pointed at the sky. Smoke from the barrel is blown back into the rain and lit with colour. The reflections keep warping and pooling like moving oil paint as the car speeds through the city.
Look: real 35mm film footage, with film grain, halation around the lights, rain beads on the lens, and real physics.
Sound: a deep 1920s straight-eight engine roaring, tyres hissing on wet cobblestones, rain drumming on the steel roof, a loud mechanical Thompson burst, brass casings clinking on the running board, and a distant clanging police bell. No dialogue, no voices, no music, no readable text.

=== WHY (two sentences) ===
I followed what the guides say about getting a realistic look: Nano Banana gets a written scene with a real camera, lens, film stock and lens flaws, every colour comes from a real light source in the scene, and the no-text rules are written into the prompt itself. On the Omni side, Google's own docs and the PixelDojo guide say to keep one camera setup, write "single continuous shot, no cuts", say exactly what moves and when using timecodes, list the sounds, and put exclusions in the prompt itself because there's no negative-prompt field, so the car stays locked in place instead of teleporting and the psychedelic colour stays as moving reflections on the paint instead of basic added trails. I made one change from the approved staging: the camera looks back along the car instead of forward, because otherwise the police lamp "far behind" would only show up in reflections. The gun already sticks out the window in the still so Omni doesn't have to make it up.
