# Facts about the video job

- Engine: Google Flow, the "Omni Flash" video model, image-to-video. Each clip starts from ONE picked still (the start frame, shown to you as an image file). There is no end frame. 16:9, 8 seconds, two takes per prompt.
- The clips are cut together into a music video. The clip's own sound will be replaced by the song, so nobody speaks or sings; sound in the prompt is optional and only physical (a press clanking, a match striking).
- The director picked every still you will animate. Keep what is in the still: the place, the people, the light. The video's job is the movement and the psychedelic layer coming alive.
- The director's note on the first video round (before the clean room): "it looks like ai slop, the cars teleport and the pschedelic trails/affects look too basic and simple. the setting looks very much like ai slop." Also his idea from the start: settings that "constantly transition in clever ways", e.g. "zooms into the fire and zooms out to something on fire".

# Your task

Write one image-to-video prompt for each of your scenes, for Omni Flash in Google Flow, 8 seconds.

- Look at each scene's start-frame image (paths below) before writing; describe what MOVES and how the camera moves, not what is already in the picture (a few words to anchor who/what is fine).
- Use the web research in this brief as your guide; you may also search the web yourself. Do not read any file on this computer apart from this brief and the image files listed in your instructions.
- Each clip gets a rich psychedelic layer that grows out of a real thing in that still (the glass, smoke, flame, reflection, frost, whatever is already there) and changes over the 8 seconds; real, filmed, physical; not a generic swirl, not a few smudges. Use a different technique per scene in your batch.
- The hand-off: in the last second or two, move the camera or the layer toward something whose shape, colour or light could match-cut into the NEXT scene's first frame (you are shown it). Say what that is.
- One clear physical action per clip; people keep their faces; vehicles move like real heavy cars (no teleporting). Guns only ever point at the sky or the ceiling, never at a person. No readable words anywhere.
- Plain narrative prose with timing if useful, roughly 80–160 words each. No quality buzzwords.

Write your output to the file named in your instructions, exactly in this format and nothing else:

=== SCENE <n> ===
<the prompt>
HANDOFF: <what the last second lands on, in one line>
WHY: <one sentence>

(repeat for each scene)
