=== SCENE 26 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a cramped bakery counter in Chicago, 1928, late morning, under the elevated railway. The camera sits inside the curved glass bread case, low, looking up through two warped panes and a single crusty loaf on a paper doily. Beyond the glass, a heavy-set Black man in his fifties, shaved head, broken nose, double chin, sweat-stained vest, flour on his forearms, pushes a creased brown pay envelope across the scarred oak counter with two fingers, not letting go yet. Taking it, cut off at the shoulder on the frame's right edge, a gangster's hand in a chalk-stripe cuff, pockmarked jaw and a toothpick just in view. Behind them a chalkboard with the price smeared out by a thumb and chalked again over the ghost, all strokes blurred and illegible. One hard shaft of sun through the street window hits a row of glass sweet jars and a cut-glass cake stand on the shelf, and the glass breaks it into wobbling caustic rainbows, violet, green, amber, crawling over the baker's knuckles and the envelope, and lighting the drifting flour dust in the air like coloured snow. The rest of the shop falls into soft brown shade. The glass carries no lettering.
WHY: The camera sits behind the loaf like a prize, and the colour comes from sun and flour dust, so his whole wage looks like a pretty trick of the light.

=== SCENE 27 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: the Chicago River at night, 1928, under the black iron underside of a raised bascule bridge. The camera sits right at the waterline, the lens half in the river: the top half of the frame is air, the bottom half is underwater, split by a ragged line of surface. Above, a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, long narrow face, hollow cheeks, pencil-thin grey moustache, kneels in a sinking wooden rowing boat with peeling green paint, tipping a galvanised bucket of soaked banknotes over the side, his cuffs drenched. Below the line, the sodden notes sink and their ink bleeds out in slow billowing clouds, violet, bottle green and rust orange, unfurling and marbling through the black water like dye in a tank. The only light is a carbide searchlight from a coal tug off frame left, its beam slicing down into the river and lighting the ink clouds from inside. Floating coal dust and a drowned felt hat drift through it. Above the waterline, deep black, the bridge girders just catching the edge of the beam. The banknotes are blurred, with no readable printing.
WHY: Bailing a boat with money makes the river richer and the boat no lighter, and the bleeding ink makes the psychedelic layer the money itself dissolving.

=== SCENE 28 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9, divided down its centre by a soot-crusted brick chimney flue rising through a Chicago tenement, 1928, the camera pressed close to it and tilted ten degrees. Left of the chimney, the secret mint in the basement: three gangsters in chalk-stripe trousers and rolled shirtsleeves, braces down, faces running with sweat, one with a burn scar on his forearm, one chewing a toothpick, feeding the furnace with a coal shovel; the air above the open firebox ripples with heat haze that bends the furnace glow into wavering bands of orange, rose and violet across their wet skin. Right of the chimney, the attic at the same instant: an old woman with a deeply lined face and a mole on her cheek, a grey wool shawl over her head, her cheek and palm flat against the cold bricks for the warmth passing up inside them, frost on her eyelashes, a candle burnt to a stub on a saucer. Behind her the window is grown over with frost ferns, and the streetlamp outside breaks through them into fine spectral needles of ice blue, green and pale gold. Bare boards, a cracked enamel jug with frozen water. No lettering anywhere.
WHY: The same chimney carries the mint's heat straight past grandma's cheek, and heat haze against frost ferns gives each half its own light.

=== SCENE 29 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a four-storey Chicago bakery building in 1928, sliced open top to bottom like a life-size film set, every floor visible at once, shot from a low angle in the snow-covered street, slightly off-centre and tilted, the elevated railway's iron legs cutting across the lower left corner. Bottom: the hidden mint, an iron printing press and a roaring coal furnace, stacked gold bars, gangsters in chalk-stripe suits and fedoras stripped to shirtsleeves. Above it the bakery with flour sacks and cold ovens; above that a tenement kitchen; at the very top a bare attic, frost on the rafters, an old woman in a grey wool shawl over her head huddled by a candle stub. A projected oil-and-dye light show washes the whole cutaway, real liquid-light swirls thrown from a projector across the street: in the basement the bubbles pool molten gold and blood orange, they thin through amber and sickly green on the middle floors, and reach the attic as faint cold violet and pale blue barely reaching her. Coal smoke leaks from cracked plaster; icicles hang from the top floor's cut edge. Every sign and wall stays blank.
WHY: The wealth gap drawn as height, with projected colour that pools rich at the bottom and runs out before it reaches the top.
