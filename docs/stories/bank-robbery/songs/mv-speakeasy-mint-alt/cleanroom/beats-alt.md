# The film, scene by scene, in plain words (2026-10-29)

The story only: what happens and who is in it. No look, no prompt wording. Lyrics are Kai's, word for word.

**What the film argues:** printing money makes whoever holds it first richer and everyone paid in it poorer, and the fight on the street is what stops anyone looking down. Under a bakery in a 1920s American city, gangsters and the mayor run a secret mint in a speakeasy basement. Each chorus the camera drops one floor deeper. The loaf of bread costs more every time we see it. Grandma's candle burns lower every time we visit her. The film opens on a getaway car chase at night and ends on the same city with the streets empty, because everyone has gone downstairs.

## The recurring people (describe them the same way every time they appear)

- **The mayor:** a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache.
- **The baker:** a heavy-set Black man in his fifties, shaved head, broken nose, double chin, sweat-stained vest, flour on his forearms.
- **Grandma:** an old woman with a deeply lined face and a mole on her cheek, a grey wool shawl over her head.
- **The gangsters:** men in chalk-stripe suits and fedoras, ordinary specific faces (pockmarks, scars, a toothpick), never handsome models. Also the band in the speakeasy, who are gangsters in pinstripes.
- **The two crowds:** working people, young and old, men and women, Black, white and South Asian. One side in red scarves, the other in blue scarves. Team colour only on scarves and banners.
- **The two police officers:** American, peaked caps, long blue coats; one has a walrus moustache, one has ginger sideburns.
