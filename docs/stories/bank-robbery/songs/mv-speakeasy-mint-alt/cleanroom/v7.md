=== SCENE 25 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera. The basement counting room: three gangsters in fedoras thumb through banknotes under the hanging lamp while the rat tugs at the rubber-banded bundle in the foreground. For the first four seconds the rat bites the rubber band and drags the bundle a hand's width across the wood; the men keep counting, fingers riffling, cigarette smoke curling up into the lamp. The goldfish tank behind them is the light source: its green water throws moving caustic ripples across the table, the cash and the men's faces, and the ripples grow stronger and more coloured, splitting into green, gold and violet bands as a fish flicks past the bulb. From four seconds the camera pushes slowly in past the men toward the tank, until the room is seen bent and doubled through the curved, wet aquarium glass. Sound: riffling paper, the tank's air pump, the rat's claws. No dialogue, no on-screen text, no captions.
HANDOFF: the frame ends looking through curved, rippling glass with warm blurred shapes behind it, matching the curved display-case glass that opens scene 26.
WHY: the caustics come from a real lit fish tank already in the still, and the push through its glass gives a physical lens-warp that rhymes with the bakery's display case.

=== SCENE 26 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera, seen through the bakery's curved glass display case. The baker slowly slides his whole pay envelope across the floury counter into the gangster's waiting hand; the gangster's fingers close on it and draw it out of frame. Morning sun from the window hits the cut-glass cake dome on the left, and its facets throw a spectrum onto the scene: small rainbow bars slide across the baker's forearm, the flour dust in the air and the counter, multiplying into a fan of prisms as the sun strengthens, red to violet sweeping slowly with the light. The flour hanging in the sunbeam glitters. The chalkboard stays a dim smear in the background. In the last two seconds the camera tilts down past the loaf to the dark shadow under the counter, where one diagonal shaft of dusty window light cuts the black, its edges split into colour. Sound: paper on wood, a street tram outside. No dialogue, no readable writing, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: a single diagonal beam of light slanting down through darkness with coloured edges, matching the lamp beam slanting into the black river in scene 27.
WHY: the prism already glints on his arm in the still, so a real cut-glass dome breaking sunlight into colour makes the price of bread feel like a trick of the light.

=== SCENE 27 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera, the frame split at the waterline. The mayor in his sinking rowing boat under the iron bridge tips another bucket of soaked banknotes over the side, then scoops again; with each pour the river slops higher over the gunwale. Under the surface the wet notes bleed thick clouds of dye, like ink dropped in water on a light-show projector: purple, green and burnt orange billow, fold into one another and bloom outward through the lamp beam that slants down from the bridge, the light turning the clouds translucent. The sunk bowler hat turns slowly in the current. The camera sinks slowly below the waterline over the eight seconds, until the surface is a rippling ceiling above. In the last two seconds an orange dye cloud rises and swells up through the beam, filling the frame in roiling orange and violet. Sound: sloshing water, the bucket's clank, a dull underwater hum. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: a billowing tongue of orange and violet filling the frame, matching the tall orange-and-violet furnace flame in scene 28.
WHY: the dye clouds are already in the still, and liquid-light-show ink in water is the most physical psychedelia there is for money going under.

=== SCENE 28 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera. One frame split by the brick chimney: on the left the gangster in braces drives his shovel of coal into the open firebox and the flames roar up, sparks spitting; on the right grandma presses her cheek and palm against the cold bricks while her stub of candle gutters and shrinks. On the furnace side the heat shimmer is the layer: the air above the fire ripples and the bricks, the men and the steam wobble behind it, the flame's colour wheeling from orange through pink to gas-blue violet. On the attic side frost is the layer: ice ferns grow across the window pane and creep out over the floorboards and the brick toward her hand, catching the streetlamp in cold blue and lilac. The two layers meet at the chimney edge and push against each other. Over the eight seconds the camera dollies slowly back and rises, revealing more of each room. Sound: roaring fire, coal scraping, the creak of ice. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: the wide pulls back into a two-storey view of one wall dividing amber fire below from frost above, matching the cut-open building in scene 29.
WHY: heat haze and growing frost are opposite real physical distortions, and letting them fight across the chimney stages the warm-mint, frozen-attic gap without any added effect.
