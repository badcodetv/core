=== SCENE 6 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, Chicago, January 1928: a tenement attic under the slates, seen through a small attic window pane so thick with frost that only a ragged thawed patch in the lower corner stays clear. Through that patch, close and low, an old woman with a deeply lined face and a mole on her cheek, a grey wool shawl over her head, cups her cracked hands round one stub of tallow candle on an upturned crate. Her breath hangs as a lit cloud in front of her mouth. Icicles hang from the rough beams behind her; frost furs a cold iron stove, a chipped enamel jug, a hook with a man's old coat. The candle is the only light, and the black around it is total. The ice ferns on the glass catch that one flame and break it apart: every frost crystal throws a spectral starburst, violet, green and amber needles fanning across the frame, a ring of rainbow halo around the flame like a cold moon, the colour strongest where the ice is thickest. The camera sits slightly tilted, too close to the glass, as if someone is peering in from the roof. Any paper in the room is blank.
WHY: The cold itself becomes the psychedelic layer, the frost on the pane splitting her one candle into a halo of colour, so the image is beautiful exactly where she is freezing.

=== SCENE 7 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, Chicago, 1928, night: looking straight up from inside a storm drain, through the cast-iron bars of its kerbside grate, crusted with coal grit and dead leaves. High above, on a stone balcony draped with limp bunting, a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks and a pencil-thin grey moustache, has just flung a handful of envelopes into the wind. The envelopes fall toward us. Press photographers on the balcony edge are firing flash-powder trays, and the frame is a stroboscopic multiple exposure: each burst freezes the falling envelopes at a different height, so every envelope leaves a ladder of ghost copies down the dark, and each flash comes through the red and blue bunting, staining its ghosts crimson, cobalt or bone-white. Flash smoke rolls in coloured clouds. At the grate's edges, hands in red scarves and blue scarves reach in, too late. The sky is starless sodium haze; the street is dry, gritty stone. The envelopes are plain and unmarked.
WHY: The stimulus money literally falls through the bars toward the drain, and the photographers' own flashes multiply it into a ghost ladder of colour, so the spectacle is the trick.

=== SCENE 8 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, Chicago, 1928: a hidden speakeasy back room, shot from table height, the lens pressed against a heavy cut-crystal whiskey decanter in the foreground so the far side of the room is seen through its facets. One hanging lamp with a green glass shade over a poker table; nothing else lit. The table is piled with clay chips, rubber-banded cash, and two whole smoked hams on greaseproof paper, cigar ash on the cigar-burned felt. Gangsters in chalk-stripe suits and fedoras, ordinary faces, one pockmarked, one with a toothpick, one with a scar through his eyebrow, have all frozen mid-hand and turned toward the door. Among them a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat, heavy gold chain of office round his neck, long narrow face, hollow cheeks, pencil-thin grey moustache. In the doorway, lit by a bare bulb in the corridor, a heavy-set Black man in his fifties, shaved head, broken nose, double chin, sweat-stained vest, flour on his forearms, holds one small loaf. The decanter's facets split him into a fan of six repeating copies, each edge fringed in amber, emerald and violet, the whisky staining half of them gold. Cards are face down.
WHY: The cut glass multiplies the one baker with one loaf into a crowd at their door, and the whisky they drink is the lens that sees him.

=== SCENE 9 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, Chicago, 1928: an underground pool hall under the elevated railway, brick vaults sweating, sawdust on the floor. The camera lies on the worn green baize at ball height, the white cue ball huge in the near corner of the frame, its curved surface holding a bent miniature of the whole room. Behind it, an old gangster with a white moustache, fedora pushed back, chalk-stripe waistcoat, leans down along his cue, one eye shut, his face cut off at the frame edge. A young red-haired gangster with freckles and a chipped tooth watches from the shadows, chewing a toothpick. Further back, through thick cigar smoke, a band of gangsters in pinstripes on a plank stage: trumpet, upright bass, a banjo. On the stage floor a projectionist's lantern shines up through a glass dish of coloured oil and dye, so slow swirling cells of magenta, saffron and turquoise crawl over the band's faces, the brass bells, the brick and the smoke itself, while the pool table keeps its single low green-shaded lamp. The rest is black. Music stands and walls are bare.
WHY: The liquid-light projection makes the room itself melt and swirl around the one steady thing, a careful old man teaching a young one how to take his shot.
