=== SCENE 10 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, late morning inside a marble bank lobby off LaSalle Street. The camera sits low and tilted at the edge of a loan desk, looking past a scuffed brass inkwell and a stack of blank, dog-eared forms. On the right, close and soft, a worker in his only suit, shiny at the elbows, frayed cuff, hat in his lap, leans forward. Across from him a thin clerk with round steel spectacles, celluloid collar and nicotine-yellow fingers. Far behind them, small and sharp, three gangsters in chalk-stripe suits and fedoras, one with a toothpick, one pockmarked, carry canvas sacks straight past a bored guard into an open round vault. The only light is noon sun falling through a cracked Tiffany stained-glass dome overhead: it lays heavy, saturated pools of ruby, cobalt and amber across the worn marble floor, the desk, the worker's lapels and the clerk's spectacles, each lens holding a tiny glowing coloured window. Dust hangs in the shafts, so the beams are solid coloured columns. The marble is grey-veined, chipped, scuffed by decades of shoes. Everything outside the pools falls into brown dimness. Every form, plaque and grille is blank, with no lettering anywhere.
WHY: The debt is signed in the coloured light of a cathedral to money while the real theft walks by in the background, small and unhurried.

=== SCENE 11 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, night, deep in a bank basement. The camera lies almost on the concrete floor, tilted, so the giant round steel vault door fills the upper two-thirds of the frame, its spun-metal finish cut in fine concentric rings. Two gangsters in sweat-soaked shirtsleeves and braces, smeared goggles pushed up on one forehead, crouch at the door with an oxy-acetylene torch; its needle of white-blue flame is the only light. Where the torch has cut, the steel has heat-tinted the way real steel does: a spreading rainbow of oxide bands, straw gold, bronze, plum, peacock blue, rippling outward through the machined rings so the whole door shimmers like oil on a hub. Molten beads drip and cool orange on the floor. In the left foreground, close to the lens, the third gangster stands guard, scarred cheek, crushed fedora, tommy gun held upright, muzzle to the ceiling, his face lit blue from below. Behind them, unlit black, a toppled chair and a coil of hose. Every dial and plate on the door is plain, no lettering anywhere.
WHY: The vault's own metal turns psychedelic under the torch, so the colour is literally the heat of the theft.

=== SCENE 12 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, deep night in a vast brick basement under a bakery, a speakeasy built around a secret mint. The camera hangs high in the corner among sooty iron steam pipes, looking down at a steep tilt across the whole room. Three black iron printing presses on the left throw sheets of uncut banknotes into the air like confetti; the sheets drift everywhere. On a plank stage at the back, a band of gangsters in pinstripes plays trumpet, banjo and upright bass. Dancers in fringed dresses kick on a sawdust floor. Dead centre of the swirl, the mayor dances: a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache. Above him turns a homemade mirror ball, hand-glued broken mirror shards on a wooden globe, lit by one hard carbon-arc spotlight. It throws hundreds of moving flecks of white, rose and lime light across the brick, the faces, the dancers' fringes and every falling banknote, so the air is full of glinting paper. Beyond the flecks, the room sinks into smoky darkness. Banknotes show only blank engraved swirls, no lettering anywhere.
WHY: The first drop is the party, and the mirror ball turns every printed note into a scattering spark of joy over the man who owns the presses.

=== SCENE 13 ===
A full-bleed frame from a 35mm feature film, Chicago, 1928, night, an intimate close shot inside the secret basement mint. The camera sits down inside the mouth of a press's open iron firebox, among glowing coals, looking up and out at a tilt. Filling the right of the frame, the mayor leans in with a coal shovel heaped toward the lens: a tall, skinny man in a bowler hat with a heavy gold chain of office round his neck, a long narrow face, hollow cheeks, a pencil-thin grey moustache, grinning, lit hot red from below, sweat on his lip. On the left rises the biggest press, its iron frame glowing dull cherry red at the seams, spitting banknotes. Its ink rollers spin wet: thick printer's inks in emerald, violet and copper green marbled together, the swirls stretching and folding like oil paint in water, catching the firelight in glossy ribbons, flicking drops that streak through the air. Behind, slumped against brick, an exhausted stoker in a torn undershirt, soot to the eyes, barely lit. The firebox edge frames the shot, rust-scaled and cracked. Beyond the fire's reach, black. The notes show only blank engraved swirls, with no lettering anywhere.
WHY: The press is a furnace the mayor feeds himself, and the marbling ink makes the money look like it is melting as it is made.
