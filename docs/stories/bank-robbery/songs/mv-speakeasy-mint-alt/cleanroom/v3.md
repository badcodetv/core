=== SCENE 9 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, filmed on a vintage film camera. The old gangster with the white moustache draws the cue back slowly, then strikes; the cue ball rolls away from us across the green baize. On the stage behind, the little projector at the band's feet is a liquid light show: a hand-held glass dish of coloured oil and water sits in its beam, and the projector's heat makes the dye bubble, so magenta, amber and teal blobs swell, split and slide across the brick arch and over the trumpeter's pinstripes. The red-haired lad keeps chewing his toothpick and follows the ball. Camera locked low at table height for four seconds, then pushes forward with the rolling ball. In the last two seconds the camera closes in on the polished ball, and the swirling oil colours, curved and bent in its surface, fill the frame as a glowing dome of colour. Sound: the crack of the cue, the ball rolling. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: The cue ball's curved, colour-filled reflection fills the frame, a round dome of light that cuts to the stained-glass dome over the bank lobby.
WHY: The projector already in the still gives the psychedelia a real, period-correct cause (the oil light show), and the ball gives a moving subject and a round shape to cut on.

=== SCENE 10 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera. The worker in the worn suit turns his hat slowly in his hands; the clerk with round spectacles keeps writing and does not look up. Behind them the gangsters walk steadily past with their sacks toward the open vault, at an ordinary walking pace. A cloud passes over the stained-glass dome: the coloured beams dim, then return brighter, and the pools of red, blue and gold light slide slowly across the marble floor and over the walking men's coats. In the foreground, the light catches the cut-glass inkwell and its facets split it into small rainbow prisms that crawl across the loan papers. Camera starts locked off, then after four seconds drifts left and pushes slowly past the desk toward the round steel vault door, as the coloured beams settle on its circular face in rings of colour. Sound: a pen scratching, footsteps echoing on marble. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: The round steel vault door washed in rings of stained-glass colour, matching the rainbow heat-tinted vault door that opens scene 11.
WHY: The dome and inkwell are already the light sources in the still, so the colour moves with a cause while the robbery walks past unnoticed in the background.

=== SCENE 11 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera. The two gangsters in shirtsleeves and braces crouch at the vault door, holding the blowtorch flame steady against the steel. As the steel heats, real temper colours bloom outward from the white-hot point: straw yellow, bronze, purple, deep blue, in rings that spread slowly across the polished round door like oil on water. Sparks pour from the cut and skitter across the wet floor, each one trailing a short streak of orange light. The guard on the left holds his tommy gun upright, muzzle pointed straight at the ceiling, and turns his head slowly to watch the dark doorway. Camera locked off for five seconds, then tilts down and pushes low along the wet floor into the bouncing sparks, so the frame becomes scattered points of light on black. Sound: the hiss of the torch, sparks hitting concrete. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: Scattered bright sparks on the black wet floor, matching the mirror ball's scattered dots of light across the dark brick of scene 12.
WHY: Heat-tint colours on steel are a real, physical rainbow caused by the torch itself, a different technique from the oil and stained glass before it.

=== SCENE 12 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, vintage film camera, looking down from above. The printing presses on the left thump and throw sheets of banknotes into the air; the notes flutter down over the dancers like confetti. The mayor in the middle dances a stiff, delighted Charleston; the couples around him swing and kick, the band keeps playing on the stage. The mirror ball turns slowly in the spotlight beam and throws hundreds of small dots of white, green and pink light that sweep across the brick walls, the falling notes and the dancers' faces, the dots stretching into short arcs as it spins. Camera slowly cranes down and drifts left. In the last two seconds it pushes in on the nearest press, whose rollers glow red from the heat underneath, the ink on them swirling green and purple, until the red-orange glow fills the edges of the frame. Sound: presses clanking, feet stamping on the floor. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: The red-hot press rollers with swirling green and purple ink, framed in orange glow, matching the furnace-mouth view of the biggest press in scene 13.
WHY: The mirror ball is a real 1920s light-scattering device already in the frame, and the push into the hot press drops the camera one level deeper into the mint.
