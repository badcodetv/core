=== SCENE 29 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, shot on a vintage film camera. The tenement stands cut open in the snow, floor by floor. The coloured patterns on its inner walls are a liquid light show: oil and dye pressed between hot glass plates on an overhead projector. Over the first four seconds the projector heat makes the oil bubble and creep upward, orange and gold from the furnace floor sliding up into the green of the middle floors, then thinning into cold violet at the attic beams, where grandma sits still beside her candle. The workers below keep turning the press handles in one steady rhythm. From 5 seconds the camera tilts slowly down off the building onto the snowy street, where steam rolls low across the frozen ruts. Sound: press machinery clanking, wind. No dialogue, no on-screen text, no captions.
HANDOFF: the tilt settles on low steam drifting across snow, which matches the breath plumes rising off the queue in scene 30.
WHY: the oil climbs from hot to cold, so the wealth gap reads as heat failing to rise, and every change in colour comes from a real projector.

=== SCENE 32 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, shot on a vintage film camera. The mayor stands backed against the furnace holding the tiered cake on its big silver tray, his hands trembling. Behind him the crowd lifts their red, green and blue railway lanterns one by one and steps closer. The tray works as a curved funhouse mirror: each raised lantern appears in its polished rim as a stretched blob of colour, and as the tray shakes the blobs slide, smear and orbit round the curve like a slowly turning colour wheel. The furnace grilles at right pulse orange. The camera pushes in slowly over the cake toward the rim of the tray. In the last two seconds one lantern reflection swells to fill the frame and flares into a hard white star-shaped glint. Sound: furnace roar, lantern handles creaking. No dialogue, no on-screen text, no captions.
HANDOFF: a white star-shaped glint on polished silver, matching the starburst street lamps through the lace curtain in scene 33.
WHY: the mayor's own silver tray turns the crowd's colours back on him, and the trembling hands drive every distortion.

=== SCENE 33 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, shot on a vintage film camera, camera locked off. We look down from an upstairs window, through a lace curtain, onto the empty street under the elevated railway. The street lamps shine through the lace, and the lace acts as a diffraction grating, throwing long rainbow starburst spikes. For the first five seconds a faint draught stirs the curtain, and the spikes slowly rotate and shimmer in spectral colours as the weave shifts. Below, the parked car stays where it is with its door hanging open. Steam drifts from the grate, a few playing cards lift and settle, and the orange glow of the open trapdoor pulses once and dims. From 6 seconds the curtain falls still, the starbursts freeze, and nothing moves at all. Sound: distant wind, steam hissing, then silence. No dialogue, no on-screen text, no captions.
HANDOFF: end of film.
WHY: the lace turns the bookend street into one last real optical effect, then the film stops on the stillness of a city that has gone downstairs.
