=== SCENE 13 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, shot on a vintage film camera from inside the firebox looking out. The mayor drives the shovel forward and tips the coal onto the glowing bed in front of us; sparks burst up and the coals flare orange. The press behind him keeps turning, its rollers rolling the green and purple marbled ink so the swirls stretch and fold like oil on water, and banknotes keep slapping out and fluttering down. The rising heat over the coals makes a shimmering haze that bends the whole room, the press and the mayor's grin ripple and warp as if seen through moving water. The slumped stoker stays still. After 6 seconds the camera pushes down into the coals as one lump splits open, and a fountain of white-hot sparks fills the frame and blooms to a white glow. Sound: coal rattling off the shovel, the press clanking. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: a white-hot spark burst from the splitting coal, matching the magnesium flash blooming top centre of scene 14.
WHY: the heat haze and the rollers' own marbled ink give the psychedelic layer a physical cause, and the spark burst is the same shape and light as the next frame's flash.

=== SCENE 14 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, filmed from behind the mayor on the bandstand. The magnesium tray in his raised hand flares; the flash pulses brighter and sparks spit upward, and the coloured smoke clouds, red, pink, yellow and teal, roll outward and billow over the crowd, lit from inside by the flash so each cloud glows its own colour. The crowd's faces flicker as the flash catches them, turning to stare up. On the right, behind the smoke, the black getaway car pulls away from the bank steps, heavy on its springs, wheels spinning on the snow, and rolls out of frame at a believable speed. The camera tilts slowly up and left as the smoke thins; after 6 seconds the flash dies to a single small point of light hanging in the drifting colour, glowing like a street lamp globe. Sound: a magnesium whoomph, an engine revving away. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: the dying flash shrinks to one warm point of light in thinning smoke, matching the street lamp at the top centre of scene 15.
WHY: coloured smoke lit from inside by a real flash is a light-show effect with a cause in frame, and the car's departure stays heavy and continuous.

=== SCENE 15 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed, looking out through the bakery window past the sweet jars on the counter. The calm gangster in the pinstripe suit walks steadily left to right between the two crowds with the sack over his shoulder, cigarette in his mouth, never looking at anyone; around him red-scarf and blue-scarf men shove and grab coats. The camera tracks slowly sideways along the counter so the sweet jars pass in front of the lens: through each curved jar the street bends, stretches and splits into red, green, violet and orange, the gangster's figure warping wide and then snapping back sharp in the gaps between jars. The street lamp flares through the glass lids. After 6 seconds the camera stops with a red sweet jar on the left and a violet jar on the right, framing the shouting men in the clear gap between them like three vertical panels. Sound: muffled shouting through glass. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: two curved jars split the frame into a red left panel, a clear centre and a blue-violet right panel, matching the three-panel layout of scene 16.
WHY: refraction through real sweet jars makes the psychedelic layer a found-glass lens effect, and it hands over the exact shape of the next frame.

=== SCENE 16 ===
Single continuous shot, no scene cuts, real-time speed. The frame keeps its three panels. In the centre the red-scarf man and the blue-scarf man stay nose to nose, shouting, foreheads pressed together, breath steaming, the same pay envelopes sticking out of their pockets; their faces stay as they are. On the left a man swings the burning red flare in a wide arc, and on the right a man swings the blue lantern; the camera's long shutter drags each light into long glowing ribbons of red and blue that trail through the air like light painting, and the ribbons spill across the black dividers into the centre panel, washing one face red and the other blue. After 6 seconds the camera pushes right into the blue lantern's glass chimney; the flame bends through the curved glass and the light breaks into sliding bands of green and magenta that fill the frame. Sound: the flare hissing, crowd roaring. No dialogue, no on-screen text.
HANDOFF: green and magenta light bands sliding through the lantern glass, matching the projector's green and magenta patterns on the ceiling of scene 17.
WHY: light-painted flare and lantern trails are a long-exposure in-camera technique driven by the props already in the frame, and the lantern glass hands its colours to the speakeasy projector.
