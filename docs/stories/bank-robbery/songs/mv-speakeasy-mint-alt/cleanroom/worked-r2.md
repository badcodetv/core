# What worked in scene 1 (the opening car chase), round 2 — the director loved this

Described from the images, so you know the target. Do not copy it scene for scene; each scene needs its own angle and its own psychedelic layer.

- **They look filmed, not generated:** dry or dusty streets instead of wet mirror-gloss; one real light source per frame with deep unlit black around it; worn, specific things (cracked horsehair seat, crushed felt hat, spilled playing cards, soot on iron girders, mud on running boards, split crates, a burst coal sack); an off-centre, tilted, odd camera position (low on a back seat, high on the elevated railway looking down, the passenger seat of the chasing car, low on a kerb at the mouth of an alley); a real place (Chicago, 1928, under the elevated railway).
- **The psychedelic layer was physical, and different each time:** prismatic bands of colour from old rippled window glass sliding over upholstery and coats; train-wheel sparks pouring past the lens through a prism; a half-wiped, oily, rain-hit windscreen smearing lamps into sprayed streaks; a coloured-gel lamp lighting kicked-up dust, plus an in-camera double exposure of a giant flame over the sky.
- **Every prompt opened by calling it a frame from a feature film, full-bleed** (this stopped the cream photo borders), named the year and city, and stayed around 150–220 words of narrative prose.
