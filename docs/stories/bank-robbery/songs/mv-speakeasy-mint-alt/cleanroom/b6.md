=== SCENE 22 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: Chicago, 1928, a low-ceilinged brick basement under a bakery, after midnight. The camera sits on the flour-dusted floorboards between two pairs of scuffed work boots, tilted hard to the left, looking up at the wooden stairs in the top right of frame. Two American police officers in peaked caps and long blue coats are halfway down, one with a walrus moustache, one with ginger sideburns, each holding a tommy gun with the muzzle pointed straight up at the joists, their faces creased with confusion. Along the left wall stand four squat cast-iron printing presses, turned round with their backs open and stuffed with black bread tins, coals glowing under them. In the foreground, the band from the club stand stiffly in white baker's hats over their pinstripes, one still with a trumpet half-hidden behind a sack. Forgotten above them, the club's mirror ball still turns on its chain, and its hundreds of hard light flecks drift across the flour hanging in the air, the officers' coats, the brass of the presses and the bakers' sweating faces, white, amber and cold violet. The only other light is the orange coal glow; everything beyond it is unlit black. No lettering anywhere.
WHY: The disguise is given away by the one club fixture nobody switched off, and its scattered flecks of light make the raid look like the party it interrupted.

=== SCENE 23 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a small Chicago bakery at dawn, 1928, seen from the dusty floor behind the shop counter. In the near bottom-left corner, the edge of a wooden trapdoor is lifted a finger's width, and a thin line of acid-green light leaks from beneath it across the worn boards and up the underside of the counter. Above, on the counter's edge, stand three tall glass jars of boiled sweets, lemon drops, red humbugs and blue aniseed balls, cutting across the upper half of the frame. Through the jars, the open street door and the dry, grey morning street beyond are broken into curved, magnified pieces of yellow, red and blue, like a cracked church window. In those coloured fragments, two American police officers in peaked caps and long blue coats, one with a walrus moustache, one with ginger sideburns, walk away down the pavement, each with a long loaf tucked under his arm, one turning back to tip his cap. Low pale-gold sunlight comes flat through the door and is the only light in the shop; the shelves stay in brown shadow, crumbs and a broom on the floor. The shop sign and any lettering stay blank.
WHY: The cops leave through a sweet-jar kaleidoscope, bribed with bread, while the green truth glows unseen a few inches from the lens.

=== SCENE 24 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: the deepest cellar under a Chicago bakery, 1928, seen from very high in the corner of the ceiling, looking straight down a riveted black iron heating pipe that runs from the camera to the floor far below. The pipe climbs the soot-crusted brick wall from a huge squat furnace and ends dead against the ceiling right beside the lens, capped with a rusted plate, going nowhere. Far below, a stoker in a sweat-soaked singlet, his arms black with coal dust, swings a flat shovel heaped with bundled banknotes into the open furnace door. The dyed paper burns strangely: the flames lick out in sheets of copper green, blue and violet among the orange, and that shifting coloured fire is the only light in the room, throwing the stoker's long shadow up the wall and lighting the floating ash, the heap of unburned notes on the dry stone floor, the split sacks and the coal shovel scratches. The rest of the cellar falls away into deep black. Heat ripples the air above the furnace mouth. No lettering on the notes or the furnace.
WHY: Money burns in impossible colours at the bottom of the building while the one pipe that could carry its warmth upstairs stops at the ceiling beside us.

=== SCENE 25 ===
A frame from a 35mm feature film, full-bleed, 16:9: a windowless counting room under a Chicago bakery, 1928, night. The camera rests on a long scarred wooden table, level with a brown rat in the right foreground, nibbling the corner of a rubber-banded bundle of banknotes, its whiskers sharp. Behind it, out of focus, rubber-banded stacks of cash climb in leaning towers to the low ceiling, and three gangsters in chalk-stripe suits and fedoras hunch over the table counting under a single hanging lamp, ordinary specific faces: one pockmarked, one with a scar through his eyebrow, one chewing a toothpick. Against the back wall stands a large glass aquarium of murky green water and fat goldfish; the lamp shines through it, and the water throws a living net of rippling, bright green-gold caustic light across the stacks, the gangsters' hands and cheeks, the cigarette smoke and the rat's fur, with the orange flash of a passing fish. Everything outside that wobbling light is black. The camera is tipped slightly off level, the rat larger than the men. Every note, wrapper and ledger is blank or illegible.
WHY: The rat at the money is the real counter, and the aquarium's caustic light makes the dull work of counting feel underwater and sickly.
