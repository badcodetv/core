# Round 2 stagings for the opening shot (2026-10-29) — one per sealed writer

Each writer gets ONE of these, plus Jack's words, the facts, the web research and the honest description of round 1.

1. **Back seat of the getaway car.** Camera low in the back seat looking forward and sideways. In the front passenger seat a gangster has the tommy gun up out of his side window at the sky. Through the small rear window behind, two police lamps chase. Outside, iron girders of an elevated railway strobe past. Psychedelic layer to build: coloured light coming *through* old rippled/bevelled window glass and splitting into bands across the upholstery, the men's coats and the gun, like a prism.
2. **From up on the elevated railway, looking down.** A strange, high, tilted angle through the iron sleepers and girders of the El, down onto the street below, where the getaway car swings round a corner with the gun up at the sky (so pointing toward the camera's height, not at anyone) and the police car's lamp just entering frame. Psychedelic layer to build: sparks from a passing train's wheels raining down past the lens, glowing streaks of molten colour in the foreground.
3. **The pursuing police car's windscreen.** Camera in the police car's passenger seat: the driver's gloved hands and dashboard in the foreground, the getaway car ahead through the glass with the tommy gun up at the sky. This composition worked in round 1; the street beyond it did not. Psychedelic layer to build: the rain and grime on the glass smearing the lights into sprayed paint, half the glass just wiped.
4. **Dusk, no rain.** Break the rainy-neon-night habit completely: the last blue of evening, dry dusty street, gas lamps just lit, shop windows warm. The getaway car tears out of a narrow alley between a bank's back wall and a warehouse, gun up at the sky, police car's lamp rounding the corner behind. Psychedelic layer to build: an in-camera double exposure / heat-haze or dust lit through a gel, so the light itself looks strange while the world stays real.
