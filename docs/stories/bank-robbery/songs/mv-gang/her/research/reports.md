## outlaws

OUTLAWS ANGLE: findings (5 searches, 2 fetches). Evidence is thinner than hoped on garment-level detail; caveats flagged.

PEARL HART (best-documented)
- Photographed after arrest 1899 in men's clothing "armed to the teeth with a Winchester rifle and a brace of revolvers". Source: https://lithub.com/the-true-story-of-pearl-hart-straight-shooting-poetry-writing-woman-bandit
- Outfit: man's dark shirt open at the neck, trousers held up by suspenders, "rough cowboys' clothing". Same source. (Hat, boots, belt not described there.)
- Long hair tied up in a bun, so even in menswear the silhouette stayed soft; one eyewitness in 1901 saw her in the same look plus coat and vest: https://frontiertimesmagazine.com/blog/pearl-hart-arizona-woman-bandit-j-marvin-hunter-sr
- Why it landed: before 1900 women almost never wore trousers, so trousers plus guns was the shock. Overview: https://time.com/4575757/pearl-hart-stagecoach-robber
- Takeaway: a female outlaw reads female partly through the contrast of menswear on a woman's frame (narrow waist, belted).

BELLE STARR (mostly legend, not verified from photos)
- Commonly described: black velvet riding habit/skirt, tight black jacket, high-topped boots, man's Stetson with ostrich plume, two pistols in cartridge belts, ridden side-saddle. Sources: https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Starr and https://petticoatsandpistols.com/2011/06/belle-starr-the-bandit-queen/
- Surviving photos: studio portrait (c. early 1880s, Fort Smith) in a black western-style dress and hat with revolver in hand (https://texashighways.com/culture/people/when-belle-starr-married-outlaw-jim-reed-her-legend-as-the-female-jesse-james-began/); 1886 photo seated side-saddle beside a deputy marshal.
- Also reported: buckskins and moccasins, rattlesnake-rattle necklace. The velvet-and-plumes look was partly her constructed public image (flag this as legend, not archive).
- Takeaway: long dark velvet skirt plus plumed hat plus crossed gun belts = most "female" and most "Wild West" silhouette available, and all of it works with a full balaclava (plume on the hat, gloves, high collar).

BONNIE PARKER (1930s bridge)
- Famous 1933 photo (Met Museum, gelatin silver print): one foot on the front fender of the stolen Ford V8, gun in hand, cigar in mouth, "style-conscious". https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/818137
- Beret tilted at an angle is widely claimed but only from weak sources; midi/ankle skirt NOT confirmed from the photo itself (only from TV costume comparisons). Treat beret and skirt length as period-plausible, not verified. Cigar was reportedly a joke pose she disliked: https://www.thecollector.com/bonnie-and-clyde-outlaws-great-depression/
- Takeaway: the pose is the asset: one boot up on the running board or bumper, weapon casually held, hip cocked. It bridges Wild West to the hot-rod/Prohibition world of the video directly.

ETTA PLACE / LAURA BULLION / OTHERS
- Searches returned no usable garment descriptions. Known only: Bullion's c.1901 Pinkerton photo (https://npg.si.edu/exhibit/frontier/pop-ups/04-15.html); Etta Place studio portrait c.1900 with the Sundance Kid, Fort Worth/NYC (https://www.legendsofamerica.com/etta-place/). Period studio-portrait convention for them: fitted high-necked blouse, tailored jacket, plain long skirt, hat with trim. I did not verify this, so use it as inference only. Poker Alice and Cattle Kate were not researched (search budget); treat as gaps.

GAPS AND CAVEATS
- No source verified specific fabrics/cuts in any single photo. Most useful for prompt work are the repeatable archetypes: velvet riding habit, plumed man's hat, crossed cartridge belts, menswear with suspenders, boot-on-fender pose.

10 PROMPT FRAGMENTS
1. Black velvet riding habit, nipped waist, long skirt over high boots
2. Wide-brim man's Stetson with a curling ostrich plume
3. Two cartridge belts crossed low over the hips, a pistol on each
4. Fitted black jacket, high stand collar, gloved hands, cuffs over wrists
5. Pinstripe waistcoat and suspenders over a tailored blouse, nipped waist
6. One boot propped on the car's front fender, revolver held loose
7. Hand on hip, weight on one leg, rifle crooked in the elbow
8. Posed side-saddle on a dark horse, pistol raised, studio-portrait stillness
9. Rattlesnake-rattle necklace worn over a buttoned high collar
10. Sepia cabinet-card portrait, painted studio backdrop, stiff theatrical outlaw pose

Sources used: Lithub, Time, Sharlot Hall/Frontier Times (via search), Wikipedia (Belle Starr), Petticoats and Pistols, Texas Highways, Met Museum, The Collector, NPG, Legends of America.

## costume

COSTUME RESEARCH: cowgirl x Prohibition moll (4 searches; sources are thin on gauntlets and Stetsons, so some points are my synthesis, marked as such)

SOURCED FINDINGS
- Prairie Rose Henderson (bronc champion 1917) designed her own rodeo outfits: knee-length bloomers, sequinned blouse, chiffon, ostrich plumes, broad-brimmed hat, cowboy boots. Her vest had heavy embroidery, overlays and cutouts. Sources do not confirm fringed bloomers; fringe in photos is from later riders such as Vera McGinnis. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Rose_Henderson ; https://truewestmagazine.com/?p=40181 ; https://texashillcountry.com/?p=49201
- Bloomers were common on cowgirls by the 1920s for mobility. Cowgirls often made their own costumes because none were manufactured. https://centerofthewest.org/?p=16797 ; https://www.folkwear.com/products/242-rodeo-cowgirl-jacket
- Split/divided riding skirt: split up the middle so she rides astride but it still reads as a skirt (Fanny Sperry Steele, c. 1900). It is the garment most associated with the rise of the cowgirl. Roughly 1890-1910; after WWI many women switched to breeches. https://fabricationsottawa.com/products/231-big-sky-riding-skirt-sewing-pattern ; https://rangemagazine.com/features/winter-10/wi10-bronc_busters.pdf ; https://vintagedancer.com/?p=51746
- V&A example, 1910s: a convertible skirt with a buttoned front panel hiding wide trousers. https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1811228/sporting-ensemble-national-cloak
- Lucille Mulhall was described as wearing a riding habit and a "picturesque Mexican sombrero". There is no confirmed source for gauntlets or a Stetson on her. https://petticoatsandpistols.com/2016/04/27/lucille-mulhall-first-cowgirl/
- 1920s women's fashion: cloche (the signature hat), also beret and bucket hats. Winter coats had large fur shawl collars. Long continuous pearls were common. A wool two-piece tailored suit was standard for travel and motoring. https://vintagedancer.com/1920s/1920s-womens-fashion/ ; https://vintagedancer.com/1920s/1920s-style-cloche-hats/ ; https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/1920-1929/
- Gangster style: expensive suits, gold watches, good shoes. Molls were the women around the gangs; Bonnie Parker did her own shooting. https://gatsbyflappergirl.com/how-did-gangsters-dress-in-the-1920s
- Costume reference: 1890s female outlaw riding looks. https://silhouettescostumes.com/?p=23446

WHERE THE TWO WARDROBES OVERLAP (my synthesis)
- Wide-brim hat versus cloche: a Stetson with a low crown and a bandana sits over the balaclava, or a cloche-style felt with a Stetson crease.
- Divided skirt versus 1920s tailored skirt: a mid-calf pleated or split skirt in the same cloth as the gang's suits, with boots underneath.
- Fur shawl collar versus bandana: a fur collar closes the neck gap, so no skin shows.
- Long pearls versus gunbelt: pearls worn over the vest, a gunbelt slung over a drop-waist jacket.
- Gauntlet gloves cover the wrists (the no-skin rule), and fringed cuffs add motion.
- Tailored riding jacket (nipped waist, peplum) versus the 1920s skirt-suit jacket.
- T-strap heels versus Cuban-heel riding boots: use a T-strap or buttoned boot with a Cuban heel.
- Femininity cues that work with no face: nipped waist and peplum, a skirt hem swinging, heels, pearls, jewelled hatpin or brooch, a fur stole, a gun on the hip with a hand on the hip.

10 PROMPT FRAGMENTS
1. split riding skirt in mustard wool, pleated, mid-calf, tall buttoned boots
2. fitted tailored riding jacket, nipped waist, peplum flare, brass buttons
3. long strand of pearls draped over embroidered Western vest
4. wide-brim felt Stetson with cloche-style dip, hatpin brooch, over knitted balaclava
5. fringed leather gauntlet gloves, fringe swinging mid-gesture
6. big fox-fur shawl collar tucked tight at the neck
7. silver gunbelt slung low over drop-waist jacket, pearl-handled revolver
8. silk bandana knotted at throat, ends trailing in the wind
9. T-strap Cuban-heel boots, one planted forward, hand on hip
10. sequin-and-chiffon trim on vest, ostrich-plume hat band, rodeo-queen swagger

COMPOSITION AND COSTUME PRINCIPLES (my inference from the above, not sourced)

- Pale or very dark long coat with tails that move; a feather, fan or sash for a soft accent.
- Place her one stride ahead, centre frame, gang fanned behind her toward camera, low angle.

FASHION ANGLE FINDINGS (5 searches; results were thin on exact 1920s-western-womenswear, so some points are my inference, marked INFER)

FULLY MASKED / BALACLAVA COUTURE
- Marine Serre made full-face coverings a recurring runway device (AW19 onward): knit intarsia balaclavas, latex moon-print, lace-embroidered and bedazzled masks that completely obscure faces as models walk. Serre's masks are paired with tailored or body-skimming clothes, so the garment under the mask carries gender. https://showstudio.com/news/mask4mask-when-high-fashion-goes-incognito , https://www.papermag.com/marine-serre-face-masks-apocalypse , https://voguescandinavia.com/articles/the-8-best-balaclava-runway-looks-to-replicate-this-winter

- Simone Rocha (Fall '22): alpaca-wool balaclava with brooch-like crystals and pearls, sold with matching gloves; also an ivory wool balaclava with pearls and white crystals worn over a ruffled dress. This is the clearest "feminise a balaclava" precedent: pearls, brooches, ruffle dress. https://www.modaoperandi.com/women/p/simone-rocha/beaded-alpaca-wool-balaclava/539870

HOW TO MAKE A MASKED FIGURE READ FEMALE AND AS THE STAR (synthesis, mostly INFER)
- Hourglass built by tailoring: nipped, belted waist; peplum or fitted jacket flaring at the hip; long skirt or wide-leg trousers with a high waist (Bonnie/Rocha precedent).
- A hat on top of the balaclava breaks the "head sock" and adds a period cue (tilted beret, or a wide-brim Stetson with a ribbon band).
- Pearls and brooches pinned on the knit or collar (Rocha); long pearl rope or a ribbon bow at the neck hides where neck meets collar.
- Leading-lady cues: long dramatic coat or duster with fur or feather collar, longer than the men's jackets, a single saturated colour the six men do not wear (e.g. emerald, oxblood, bone white), a cinched belt with gun holster.
- Hands and feet carry femininity too: long gauntlet gloves in kid leather, heeled lace-up boots or Cuban-heel Western boots, seamed stockings (no skin, so opaque or dark).
- Posture: weight on one hip, one hand on hip, chin up; stands a half-step ahead of the men in a V formation.

## silhouette

SILHOUETTE ANGLE: research findings (5 searches, no fetches)

CAVEAT: no source I found tests whether viewers read gender from a masked or covered figure. What follows is general silhouette and readability craft plus period-costume facts. The femininity read comes from my inference, built on these cues.

READABILITY CRAFT
- Silhouette test: fill the figure solid black, shrink it to thumbnail size and drop the colour. If she still reads as a distinct character, the design works. https://www.creativebloq.com/art/character-design/how-to-design-instantly-recognisable-characters-using-strong-silhouettes
- A design that relies on colour or face markings alone is weak. Her ring and triangle will not carry the "woman" read, so the outline has to. https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/appeal-2
- Hourglass, triangle and circle are the recurring female body-shape tropes in character design. They are shorthand, not guaranteed, and untested. https://skyryedesign.com/art/drawing/oc-art-female/
- Hat, hair shape, cape or coat give a figure a signature outline. Van Helsing's hat is the example given of reading at long-shot distance. https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/157653
- Lineups: designs that work alone can fail in a group, so test the seven-figure lineup in black thumbnail first. https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465 and https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/character-design-and-team-makeup
- To separate the leader, vary height, costume mass and hat shape against the six men. Six similar boxy suit shapes plus one clearly different outline works. https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/character-design-and-team-makeup/notes
- The six men share a boxy broad-shouldered suit silhouette, so her contrast is structural (my inference):
  - cinched waist against their straight torsos
  - flared or draped lower half against their trouser tubes
  - heels adding height
  - a hat brim wider or shaped differently from the men's fedoras

1920s CUES (period fashion)
- Dropped-waist, straight, slender line. Hemlines rise to the knee. Note this is a loose, tubular shape, so it will not by itself show a waist. https://glamourdaze.com/2010/05/1920s-fashion-womens-dress-and-style.html and https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/1920-1929/
- Cloche hat: close-fitting, brim pulled low over the eyes, trimmed with ribbon, bows or embroidery. It is a strong female-coded shape and sits over a balaclava naturally. https://vintagedancer.com/1920s/1920s-womens-fashion/
- Wrap-over coats, T-strap or Mary Jane shoes, and pleated skirts are period women's items. https://vintagedancer.com/1920s/1920s-day-dresses
- Practical fix: to get a visible waist in the 1920s, use a belted or cinched coat or jacket with a wrap, or a fitted jacket over a flared pleated skirt, instead of a flapper shift. This is my inference.

WILD WEST CUES
- Costume pieces that recur: long duster coat, vest, gun belt and holster (a hand-tooled belt is a historically noted item), corset-style bodice, wide-brim hat. https://historicalemporium.com/pages/gunslinger-fashion-for-men-how-to-dress-like-a-gunslinger and https://www.halloweencostumes.com/womens-annie-oakley-cowgirl-costume.html

This is a historical outlaw reference, so it supports a hat-low, armed-woman pose, but the clothes themselves were masculine and so do not help femininity. https://www.legendsofamerica.com/we-pearlhart/ and https://lithub.com/the-true-story-of-pearl-hart-straight-shooting-poetry-writing-woman-bandit (some accounts are partly fictionalised)

GANG CONTEXT
- Suits: mustard, brown, charcoal, cream, black, maroon. Balaclavas: plum, bottle-green, red, navy, mustard, white, grey. The red triangle and white ring are fixed.
- To stand apart she needs a hue the gang lacks (teal, jade, saddle tan, dusty rose, ice or powder blue) or a texture the gang lacks (velvet, suede, fringe, beads, fur). Texture matters most because the men are all wool and chalk-stripe.

5 DISTINCT PALETTES

1. Midnight velvet and gold (the queen)
 - Balaclava: deep black-teal or ink navy knit. The white ring pops and the red triangle glows.
 - Outfit: black silk velvet duster-coat with ermine or fox collar and cuffs; gold lame lining flashing at the hem; antique gold buttons.
 - Why: nobody in the gang wears velvet, so she reads as expensive and female. Black reads as the leader without competing with charcoal.
 - Refs: Shrimpton black velvet coat, Fountainhead chestnut velvet and gold lame.

2. Jade and saddle tan (the Western outlaw)
 - Balaclava: bottle-green is taken, so go jade or teal, a lighter blue-green.
 - Outfit: saddle-tan suede fringed jacket or bolero over a cream silk blouse; tan fringed gauntlets; tooled-leather belt with silver conchos; jade silk wild rag knotted at the neck.
 - Why: warm tan is absent from the gang, jade matches the 1920s coat colours, and fringe gives movement and a clearly Western read.
 - Refs: David Morgan fringed gauntlets, Vintage Dancer western wear.

3. Raspberry and oxblood (the showgirl gunslinger)
 - Balaclava: dusty rose or raspberry knit; keep it clearly different from the plain red and maroon in the gang.
 - Outfit: raspberry velvet riding coat, cinched waist, split skirt over high black boots; black gloves; red-brown tooled holster and bandolier.
 - Why: a warm jewel tone is feminine without looking like a costume, and it links to the red triangle.
 - Refs: cherry-red velvet and sable coat, Peekaboo devore velvet coat.

4. Ivory duster and silver (the ghost in the dust)
 - Balaclava: ice or powder blue, or bone white (white is already used, so powder blue is better).
 - Outfit: long ivory or pale-oat oilskin duster flapping open, silver-beaded vest, silver conchos on a pale belt, pale grey suede gauntlets, silver-grey silk scarf.
 - Why: the only light, cool-silver silhouette next to six dark and mustard suits; it reads from far away and in motion.
 - Refs: Historical Emporium duster guide, beaded cocoon coat.

5. Black and oxblood tooled leather (the hardest)
 - Balaclava: charcoal-plum or deep aubergine, darker than the existing plum.
 - Outfit: oxblood tooled-leather riding jacket with floral carving, black velvet corset waistcoat, long fringed gauntlets in black, a wide concho belt, a silk neck scarf in mustard to echo the gang.
 - Why: it gives Wild West toughness with a visibly female cut (waist, corset, skirt) and ties to the gang's maroon and mustard.
 - Refs: Cowboys and Indians accessory piece, Silhouettes outlaw page.

Recommendation: palette 2 or 4 for contrast against the men, palette 1 for authority. A mix works: ivory duster over a jade or tan base.

## promptcraft

PROMPTCRAFT FINDINGS (Nano Banana / Gemini image). Note: 4 searches + 2 fetches. Only the Google blog is official. The other sources are community guides, and I did not open them in full.

A. GOOGLE'S OFFICIAL PATTERNS (source: https://developers.googleblog.com/en/how-to-prompt-gemini-2-5-flash-image-generation-for-the-best-results/)
- Add-to-image template: "Using the provided image of [subject], please [add/remove/modify] [element] to/from the scene. Ensure the change is [how it should integrate]." Our use: "Using the provided image, add a seventh figure standing front and centre... Ensure she matches the scene's lighting, grain and perspective."
- Inpaint/freeze template: "Using the provided image, change only the [element]... Keep everything else in the image exactly the same, preserving the original style, lighting, and composition." This is Google's own wording for keeping the scene unchanged. It suits "same prompt, stitch her in".
- Multi-image composite template: "Take the [element from image 1] and place it with/on the [element from image 2]. The final image should be a [description]." Use the character sheet as image 1 and the existing shot as image 2.
- Aspect ratio: editing usually keeps the input's ratio. If it drifts, state it. With several inputs, the output takes the LAST image's ratio, so put the scene image last.
- Best practices: be hyper-specific (materials, patterns, shapes); write a narrative paragraph, not keyword soup; state purpose; iterate in small follow-up turns; use semantic negatives (say what IS there, not "no X"); use camera terms (low angle, wide shot, Dutch angle).
- Consistency drift: when features drift over many edits, restart with a full, detailed character description (same Google page).
- The API docs moved to https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-generation (the ai.google.dev URL redirects there). I did not read that page.

B. CHARACTER REFERENCE SHEET CRAFT (community guides)
- One canonical anchor image first: a front or three-quarter view is the most reusable. Then attach it for every later view and change ONLY the view line (https://blog.picassoia.com/ai-character-sheet-generator-prompts-from-an-image).
- The wording pattern is repeated "identical" and "locked" lines. Example from https://martini.art/en/prompts/image/consistent-character-prompts: "Same character as referenced canonical portrait, three-quarter back view, identical wardrobe, identical lighting, neutral studio backdrop, locked features."
- Sheet layout guides: front / three-quarter / side / back full-body plus close-up detail panels, on a plain backdrop. Examples: https://budgetpixel.com/blog/character-sheets and https://selfielabstudio.com/blog/nano-banana-pro-consistent-character-sheets-guide-20260220. A 3-column layout (detail portrait, full front, full back) at 3:2 is in https://skills.sh/shinchven/nano-banana-skills/character-reference-sheet.
- The Morphic prompt library names distinctive marks (scar, notched ear) to anchor identity (https://morphic.com/resources/prompts/character-design-prompts). Our equivalent: the white face ring, the small red triangle on the wearer's left eye side, and the balaclava's colour and stitch texture. List these in every prompt.
- Nano Banana Pro is reported to accept up to 14 reference images and render 4K (Picassoia link above). Treat that as unverified and check the current model in Flow.
- Pipeline advice for many shots: https://signals.aktagon.com/articles/2026/03/generating-consistent-imagery-with-gemini-a-practical-guide-to-building-a-prompt-based-generation-pipeline/ (not read in full).
- Two more community pages on keep-unchanged wording: https://www.promptzone.com/darcy_reddy/nano-banana-prompt-patterns-for-gemini-25-flash-image-447f.md (add a literal freeze clause on local edits; one sentence per person in a multi-subject prompt) and https://www.cometapi.com/ultimate-guide-to-nano-banana-how-to-use-and-prompt-for-best/ ("Preserve the dog.", "Keep facial features identical to the reference."). Both are community practice, not official syntax.

C. COSTUME CONSISTENCY ACROSS MANY SHOTS (synthesis)
1. Write one fixed "costume paragraph" (garment, fabric, colour, cut, accessories) and paste it unchanged into every prompt. Never paraphrase it.
2. Attach the sheet as a reference every time, and say "wearing the exact outfit from the character sheet".
3. Change one variable per prompt (pose, angle, or scene).
4. Say the hard constraints positively: "fully covered head to toe: knitted balaclava with a stitched white ring around the face, no visible eye, nose or mouth openings; leather gloves; high collar". Avoid "no skin"; the semantic-negative tip says the model may render the thing you name.
5. Femininity must come through the silhouette. Use words like "nipped waist, defined hourglass silhouette, fitted jacket, long skirt or high-waisted trousers, heeled boots, hat angle, hip-shot pose".
6. Because she is added into existing frames, describe the six men in the source image once as "the six men already in the image, unchanged".
7. For video, hand the animation step the approved composite still, not a new text prompt.
8. Put the scene image LAST among the inputs to keep its aspect ratio.
