# The Bank Robbery: the gang leader, costume research brief

For the director and the image-prompt writer. Eight research angles merged: outlaws, costume, film, fashion, grime, silhouette, palette, promptcraft. Evidence flags: SOURCED means a page said it, INFER means synthesis, LEGEND means popular history that is not archive-verified.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

She is a Belle Starr-style bandit queen who stepped off a rodeo poster into a 1925 speakeasy. The six men are all boxy wool chalk-stripe, so she carries what they cannot: a waist, a flared hem, a hat brim of a different shape, velvet, fringe or fur, and a hue or texture none of them wear. Wild West gives her the duster, the crossed cartridge belts, the gauntlets, the wild rag at the throat and the plumed hat. Prohibition gives her the cloche, pearls, fur collar, T-strap or Cuban heel, the tailored skirt-suit and the Bonnie Parker boot-on-the-fender pose. The knitted balaclava with its white ring and red triangle is her face. A hat on top of the knit breaks the "head sock" and does the period work. Everything below the chin has to say "woman" and "boss" at once. Marine Serre and Pussy Riot both show that a fully masked figure reads female through clothes, not faces.

## 2. Six directions

Each is built on a different silhouette. The thumbnail test is to fill each solid black and check that no two match.

**A. The Bandit Queen** (silhouette: long bell, tall plume)
- A black velvet riding habit with a nipped waist and a skirt sweeping to the boot. Crossed cartridge belts, one pearl-handled pistol each side.
- Garments, head to toe: Stetson with a curling ostrich plume over the balaclava, high-collared fitted jacket, black kid gauntlets with buttoned cuffs, velvet skirt, high laced boots.
- Palette: black velvet and gunmetal. Balaclava is deep aubergine or ink navy, so the white ring and red triangle glow.
- Signature accessory: a rattlesnake-rattle necklace over the buttoned collar (LEGEND).
- Signature pose: stands a half-step ahead with one pistol raised, still as a studio portrait.
- Reference: Belle Starr's velvet-and-plumes look, partly her own invented image. https://petticoatsandpistols.com/2011/06/belle-starr-the-bandit-queen/ ; https://texashighways.com/culture/people/when-belle-starr-married-outlaw-jim-reed-her-legend-as-the-female-jesse-james-began/

**B. The Dietrich Marshal** (silhouette: tall vertical column, wide-leg trouser, sharp peak lapels)
- A high-waisted, wide-leg trouser suit in pinstripe or plain wool, cut to an hourglass. She is the one woman in trousers among six men in trousers, so the cut and the waist do the work.
- Garments: low-crowned Stetson with a ribbon band, peak-lapel jacket, waistcoat, silk scarf at the throat, long leather gloves, Cuban-heel boots under the trouser break, tooled gunbelt over the jacket.
- Palette: near-black navy with tonal variation. The "black" on screen is navy and brown (Johnny Guitar trick). Balaclava is charcoal-plum.
- Signature accessory: an ornate holster on one hip with a gloved hand resting on it.
- Signature pose: hand on hip, weight on one leg, a finger jabbed at the lens.
- Reference: Polly Gray's Dietrich-based trouser suit in Peaky Blinders S5 (https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/peaky-blinders-costumes-designer-interview) and Joan Crawford's all-black gunfighter look as Vienna in Johnny Guitar (https://parterre.com/2020/08/20/do-you-ever-dream-of-vienna/).

**C. The Ghost in the Dust** (silhouette: long A-line coat flaring to the boot, hat-less or low hat)
- A long ivory or pale-oat oilskin duster, nipped by a belt, flapping open to a silver-beaded vest. She is the only light, cool figure among six dark and mustard suits.
- Garments: pale duster, beaded vest, tooled pale belt with silver conchos, grey suede gauntlets, silver-grey silk scarf, laced heeled boots.
- Palette: ivory, silver and powder blue. The balaclava is powder blue, since white is already used by the ring.
- Signature accessory: the duster's split back and coattails.
- Signature pose: strides toward camera with the coat streaming behind, gang fanned out behind her.
- Reference: Basic Instinct's cream silk duster (https://filmcostumecollection.omeka.net/items/show/1267) and the Historical Emporium duster guide (https://historicalemporium.com/how-to-dress-gunslinger.php). The cream duster is a verified costume. The oilskin version is INFER.

**D. The Rodeo Queen** (silhouette: short and wide, a fringed, flared, shoulder-heavy shape with a broad hat)
- Prairie Rose Henderson-style rodeo glamour: sequin, chiffon, plume and fringe, with a knee-length divided skirt or bloomers over tall boots. Fringe gives motion that a face cannot.
- Garments: broad-brimmed hat with an ostrich plume hatband, saddle-tan suede fringed bolero with a six-inch fringe, cream silk blouse, fringed gauntlets that fully cover the hands, concho belt, split skirt, tall buttoned boots.
- Palette: saddle tan, cream, jade and gold. Balaclava is jade or teal (bottle-green is taken). Tan is a hue the gang lacks.
- Signature accessory: a silk wild rag or jade neckerchief knotted at the throat.
- Signature pose: a roping or whip-crack gesture with the fringe swinging mid-gesture.
- Reference: Prairie Rose Henderson, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Rose_Henderson ; https://truewestmagazine.com/?p=40181. Fringe on the bloomers is not confirmed, and the fringe in photos is from later riders. Gauntlets: https://www.davidmorgan.com/shop/product/422/western-fringed-gauntlet

**E. The Knit Bonnie** (silhouette: narrow pencil column, a beret or cloche crown, a boot propped up)
- Soft knit and tweed read as outlaw glamour, and the knit echoes the gang's knitted masks. A long, slim line with the hat doing the work at the top.
- Garments: tilted knitted beret-style crown over the balaclava, yellow or camel knit vest, belted windowpane or tweed jacket, tan tweed pencil skirt, long gloves, T-strap or buttoned Cuban-heel boots, a loose neck scarf.
- Palette: mustard-yellow knit, tan tweed, with a clearly different balaclava (dusty rose).
- Signature accessory: a pistol in a gloved hand, held loose.
- Signature pose: one boot up on the hot rod's front fender or running board, gun held casually, hip cocked. This is the clearest bridge from the Wild West to the hot rods in the video.
- Reference: Bonnie Parker's 1933 photo (https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/818137). Bonnie and Clyde (1967), Theadora Van Runkle (https://silverscreenmodes.com/bonnie-clyde/). The beret and skirt length are period-plausible and not verified from the photo itself.

**F. The Fur-Collar Boss** (silhouette: round cocoon, wide shawl collar tapering to a narrow ankle; heavy top)
- A 1920s cocoon opera coat in a jewel colour. This reads as feminine armour and expensive. Nobody in the gang wears velvet, fur or beads, so texture is her tell.
- Garments: cloche hat with one feather pulled low over the balaclava, raspberry or deep emerald velvet coat with a fox or ermine shawl collar and cuffs, a long strand of pearls draped over the coat, drop-waist beneath, gold lame lining flashing at the hem, gold-studded kid gloves, T-strap heels.
- Palette: raspberry or oxblood, with gold. Balaclava is dusty rose or raspberry, clearly different from the plain red and maroon in the gang. The fur collar closes the neck gap, so no skin shows.
- Signature accessory: a pearl-handled revolver, or a pistol hidden in the muff.
- Signature pose: leaning back, one gloved hand on the hip, chin up, backlit in a saloon or vault doorway.
- Reference: 1920s flapper and opera coats, https://www.shrimptoncouture.com/collections/sold-1920s-antique/products/wonderful-1920s-unlabeled-black-velvet-coat-w-ermine-collar-cuffs ; https://peekaboomarketplace.com/collections/all/products/1920s-devore-velvet-flapper-coat-with-mink-trim. Brooch-and-pearl knit: Simone Rocha, https://www.modaoperandi.com/women/p/simone-rocha/beaded-alpaca-wool-balaclava/539870

**Recommendation:** B or C for the strongest group contrast, A for the most Wild West, D if the video wants movement. A hybrid works, for example an ivory duster over the B base.

## 3. What makes her read as female with no skin, ranked

No source tested gender reading on a masked figure. The ranking below is INFER built on silhouette craft and period costume.

1. **Cinched waist against their straight torsos.** A belted duster, nipped jacket, peplum, corset waistcoat or cinched gunbelt over a fitted jacket. This is the strongest lever. Note that 1920s drop-waist and shift dresses hide the waist, so use belted or tailored pieces instead.
2. **Flared or draped lower half against their trouser tubes.** A skirt, split riding skirt, bias-cut skirt, or a coat hem that swings.
3. **A different hat shape.** Plumed Stetson, cloche or beret against six fedoras. The hat is where the six-men shape breaks.
4. **Heels and boots.** T-strap, Cuban-heel and buttoned boots lengthen the leg. Her height is a half-head above the men.
5. **Hands.** Long kid gauntlets, pearl-button cuffs, an elegant wrist angle and a pointing finger. This also covers the no-skin rule.
6. **Texture the men lack.** Velvet, fur, fringe, beads, pearls, a feather. The men are all wool and chalk-stripe.
7. **Accessories pinned on the knit.** Pearls and a brooch on the balaclava or collar (Rocha precedent), a ribbon bow at the neck, a long pearl rope.
8. **Posture and movement.** Weight on one hip, chin tilted up, head tilt, a heel-toe walk with a hip sway, a skirt or coat hem that swings.
9. **A colour or hue the men do not wear.** This sets her apart as the leader but does not by itself read as female. Pussy Riot's balaclavas over bright dresses and tights are the closest masked precedent (https://theartstory.org/artist/pussy-riot/).

## 4. Leading the gang

- Place her at the point of a V, one stride ahead, with the six men fanned behind. Centre frame, low-angle hero shot (INFER).
- Make her the brightest or the only warm figure, or the only pale coat among dark suits. Vienna in Johnny Guitar inverts the palette of her crowd (https://parterre.com/2020/08/20/do-you-ever-dream-of-vienna/).
- Vary height, costume mass and hat shape. Six similar boxy suit shapes plus one clearly different outline is the rule (https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/character-design-and-team-makeup/notes).
- Test the seven-figure lineup as a black thumbnail first, because a design that works alone can fail in a group (https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465).
- Crew behaviour (INFER from grime staging): the men look at her, not the camera, one hand on a hat brim or a gun. A half-step back, spread wide.
- Her rapping gestures: finger jab to the lens, flat palm chopping the air, a hand cupped to the ear. Brat energy shows in the head tilt and the shoulder shrug, and she leans into the camera while the men lean away. Little Simz's "Woman" (crew covering the lead's back) is the stated reference: https://www.dstngr.com/article/2021/5/14/little-simz-woman-video
- Lighting: saloon-doorway backlight, dust haze, hot-rod headlights rim-lighting velvet and beads.

## 5. Prompt craft

Official Google patterns: https://developers.googleblog.com/en/how-to-prompt-gemini-2-5-flash-image-generation-for-the-best-results/

**Answer to "keep the same prompt, just stitch her in":**
- Yes. Keep each existing shot's prompt and its approved still, and add her as an edit on that still. The edit pattern is Google's own: "Using the provided image, add [element]. Ensure the change [integrates how]. Keep everything else exactly the same, preserving the original style, lighting and composition."
- Use the multi-image template with the character sheet as image 1 and the existing shot as the LAST image, because the output takes the last image's aspect ratio.
- Describe the six men once as "the six men already in the image, unchanged".
- Hand the video step the approved composite still, not a new text prompt.
- Caveat: I have no confirmation of whether prompts exist for every video already made. That is a repo question, and the recipe cards in `docs/provenance/cards/` should hold them.

**Character sheet:**
- Make one canonical anchor image first, a front or three-quarter full-body view. Then attach it for every later view and change ONLY the view line. Example pattern: "Same character as referenced canonical portrait, three-quarter back view, identical wardrobe, identical lighting, neutral studio backdrop, locked features." (https://martini.art/en/prompts/image/consistent-character-prompts)
- Layout: full-body front, three-quarter, side, back, plus close-ups of the balaclava (ring, triangle, stitch texture), gloves and boots, on a plain grey backdrop. https://budgetpixel.com/blog/character-sheets ; https://skills.sh/shinchven/nano-banana-skills/character-reference-sheet
- Name the identity anchors in every prompt: the white ring, the small red triangle on her left eye side, and the balaclava's colour and knit texture.

**Wording patterns:**
- Write one fixed costume paragraph and paste it unchanged into every prompt. Never paraphrase it.
- Change one variable per prompt: pose, angle or scene.
- State the constraints positively. Say "fully covered head to toe: knitted balaclava with a stitched white ring and no visible eye, nose or mouth openings, leather gloves, high collar". Avoid "no skin", because naming it can make the model render it (the semantic-negative tip).
- Write a narrative paragraph, not keyword soup. Be hyper-specific about materials. Use camera terms.
- Get femininity from silhouette words: "nipped waist, defined hourglass silhouette, fitted jacket, heeled boots, hip-shot pose".
- If features drift over many edits, restart with the full description.
- Run the repo's same-prompt check (`scripts/variations/fresh.py`) on every prompt, since several directions here share fragments.
- Community guides are unverified. The claim that Nano Banana Pro accepts 14 reference images is unconfirmed, so check the model in Flow.
