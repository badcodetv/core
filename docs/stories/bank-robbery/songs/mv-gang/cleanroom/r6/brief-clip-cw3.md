# The job

You write image-to-video motion prompts for a glossy 1920s Prohibition music video: a gang of masked robbers in sharp suits rapping to camera over a cockney woman's rap about money printing and inequality. Each prompt animates one approved still for 6 seconds; the job is the performance: big, sarcastic stage-rapper moves straight at the lens, with the deadpan bystander carrying on.

# Facts about these clips

- Each still becomes the FIRST frame of a 6-second clip (Google Flow, its fast video model, 16:9). The editor cuts fast on the beat (about 92 beats a minute, a beat every 0.65 s) and may use any 0.6 to 2.6 seconds of it, so the performing must start in the first half second and never stop: a new move every beat or two, no standing still, no slow build.
- The man (or men) in the still are rapping at the camera. They wear knitted balaclavas with NO mouth hole, so there are no lips to sync: the rapping is all in the body, head, shoulders and hands. Big stage-rapper moves done sarcastically, mock-serious, too much: see the gesture research below. More than pointing.
- Name the moves in order, on the beat ("on the first beat… on the next…"), and make them big, sharp and readable at a glance. Nothing he holds may vanish or turn into something else; if he holds something, he keeps it.
- Masks keep their shape: a white ring round the face and a small red triangle high on the RIGHT side of the picture (the man's left eye). Gloves stay on. Suits, hats and masks never change.
- The camera stays still (locked off); the editor adds camera moves later. Say so.
- No speech or singing; the song goes on top. Sound effects are fine (stamps, claps, cloth, the room).
- Nothing new enters the frame. Other people in the still stay where they are and react little; no new people appear.

# Web research: the stage moves

## 1. Early-2000s Eminem on stage: body language as plain physical description

### 1a. What the sources support

- **Most rap gestures are beat gestures.** A Masaryk University study of rappers' gestures found that gestures marking the beat were the most common type (about half of all occurrences), followed by iconic gestures that act out the words. A French study adds "metalinguistic/phatic" gestures that mark the start or end of a bar or signal "this line is the one". **MEDIUM** ([Masaryk, via search abstract](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116?kod=SBA399); [French clip study](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116/en?lang=en)). *Use:* the free hand should move on the beat, and once or twice per frame it should **illustrate** a word.
- **Theatrical staging and crew.** Anger Management 2002 used a circus set (Ferris wheel, fireworks, a tuxedoed performer), a montage of C-SPAN critics, an effigy he mocked on stage, a four-song D12 segment, and hits cut down to a verse and chorus. **STRONG** ([Jim DeRogatis, 2002](https://www.jimdero.com/News2001/Aug4AngerMgmt.htm); [Wikipedia: Anger Management Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anger_Management_Tour)). One review said he looked bored for much of the show **MEDIUM** ([Washington Times, 2002](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2002/jul/24/20020724-034747-1009r/)).
- **2002 VMAs "White America":** a mock presidential podium, with costumed politicians whispering to each other behind him while he rapped. **MEDIUM** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/eminems-best-live-performances/)). *Parody gold: the deadpan background extras.*
- **2000 VMAs:** about 100 bleached-blond lookalikes in white tees marched in from the street outside Radio City, bobbing along behind him. **STRONG** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_MTV_Video_Music_Awards); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/remember-when-eminem-and-hundreds-of-stans-invaded-the-2000-mtv-vmas-with-the-real-slim-shady); [Festival Sherpa](https://www.festivalsherpa.com/throwback-thursday-eminem-brings-his-own-army-of-100-slim-shady-doppelgangers-to-the-vmas)). *Use:* a gang of six identical silhouettes moving in unison is the same joke.
- **2001 Grammys "Stan":** a dark stage with a single light on him alone for his verse. At the end the two performers embraced and raised joined hands. **STRONG** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/today-hip-hop-history-eminem-performs-stan-elton-john-43rd-grammy-awards/); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/elton-and-eminem-perform-stan/)). *Use:* one hard pool of light, darkness around it.
- **Proof as hype man:** on stage for most of the show, a charismatic presence, visibly having fun with Eminem. The two did **synchronised moves** (the "My Band" send-up, and a 2000 Anger Management clip in which they moved in sync). **MEDIUM** ([PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/116198-eminem-live-from-new-york-city-2496125906.html); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/alex-siber/amazing-eminem-performances-we-found-on-youtube)).
- **2003 Grammys "Lose Yourself":** with Proof, moving under lime-green stage light. **MEDIUM** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/eminems-best-live-performances/)).
- **Critics on his body language:** "spastic movements", "rapid pace and explosive energy", crotch-grabbing and pinching gestures (*out*, they are obscene). **WEAK** (one magazine-blog, [Medium](https://medium.com/@poppulsenewsmag/eminems-audacious-stage-presence-ignites-debate-on-relevance-and-artistic-expression-a9667cfb8b19)).
- **Crowd call-outs:** he led the crowd in chanted, insulting call-and-response, and crowds raised lighters on the ballad. **STRONG** ([DeRogatis](https://www.jimdero.com/News2001/Aug4AngerMgmt.htm); [PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/116198-eminem-live-from-new-york-city-2496125906.html)).
- **8 Mile final battle:** he went first, then the opponent froze, dropped the mic and stood motionless. Eminem said the frozen opponent mirrored his own earlier choke. **MEDIUM** ([Slashfilm, citing Grantland](https://www.slashfilm.com/1077205/how-eminem-made-fun-of-anthony-mackies-real-life-to-expose-character-in-8-miles-final-rap-battle)). *Use:* a robber raps while a rival stands dead still with a dropped microphone.

### 1b. Move list: 26 prompt-ready sentences (all **WEAK**: from general knowledge of the footage, not sourced)

Each line gives one move and the camera angle that shows it best. Costume is not copied.

1. **Flat-palm chop:** free hand held flat, edge down, chopping down through the air on each stressed syllable, elbow tucked. *Low angle, 24mm, waist-up; the hand at the peak of its arc.*
2. **Sideways-palm wave:** the free hand turned palm-down and rocked side to side at chest height in time with the bar, like patting an invisible dog. *Medium shot, eye level, slight 3/4.*
3. **Finger jab at the lens:** index finger stabbing straight at the camera, arm half-extended, the fingertip big and foreshortened in the foreground. *Close, 21–24mm, the lens at chest height.*
4. **Two-finger point down:** index and middle together, pointing down at one person in the front row, the head tilted with the point. *Over-the-shoulder from the crowd, looking up.*
5. **Fist to chest:** a gloved fist pressed flat to the breastbone on a sincere line, the shoulders squared. *Medium close-up, frontal.*
6. **Mic hand tight:** the microphone held right against the mouth, gripped by the head with the cord looped once round the wrist, the elbow high and out to the side. *Tight close-up, low.*
7. **Mic cord loop:** the free hand flicking slack mic cord behind him mid-stride. *Wide, stage lip, low.*
8. **Hunched forward:** torso folded forward from the hips, knees bent, the head pushed toward the audience, like a sprinter at the blocks who decided to talk instead. *Low wide-angle from the stage floor.*
9. **Bounce:** knees flexing on the beat, both heels lifting, the shoulders dropping with each downbeat. *Full figure, slight motion blur only in the hem of the coat.*
10. **Pacing the lip:** walking sideways along the front edge with one foot on the very edge, the eyes never leaving the crowd. *Long lens from the side, or wide from below the edge.*
11. **Leaning over the front row:** one foot up on the monitor or edge, one forearm across the raised knee, the head craned out over the crowd. *Over-the-shoulder from the crowd, looking up into him.*
12. **Chin down, eyes up:** the head dipped, the gaze up through the brows straight into the lens: the threatening look. *Close-up, the lens slightly above eye line.*
13. **The snarl:** upper lip pulled back on one side mid-word. *(Our gang wear balaclavas, so the snarl must be carried by the head tilt and the eyes behind the knit.)*
14. **Head nod on the one:** a deep, slow nod on each downbeat, chin to chest. *Profile or 3/4.*
15. **The shrug:** both shoulders up, palms open and turned up, the head cocked: "what did you expect?" *Medium, frontal, deadpan.*
16. **Mock bow:** a theatrical deep bow with one arm sweeping across the waist, held a beat too long. *Wide, low, centred.*
17. **Finger gun (non-obscene):** thumb up, index out at the lens, a tiny recoil flick of the wrist. *Close, wide lens.*
18. **The "shh":** a gloved index finger raised to the knitted mouth of the balaclava, eyes wide. *Close-up.*
19. **Ear cup:** a hand cupped behind the ear, leaning toward the crowd: "I can't hear you." *Medium, from the crowd side.*
20. **Hand up for call-and-response:** the free arm straight up, palm open or a raised fist, the mic swung out toward the crowd. *Wide from behind the performer, the crowd beyond.*
21. **Both arms out:** arms spread wide, palms down, crucifix-cocky, at the end of a verse. *Low wide, symmetrical.*
22. **Counting on fingers:** the free hand counting off a list, one finger at a time. *Medium close.*
23. **Throat-cut swipe** (careful with tone): a flat hand drawn across the throat to cut the beat. *Medium.*
24. **Head-tap:** two fingers tapping the temple: "think." *Close-up.*
25. **Back to the crowd, look over the shoulder:** turned away, the head snapping round to the lens on the punchline. *Medium from behind.*
26. **Crew behaviour:** the hype man one step behind and to the side, mouthing the last word of every bar, hand on the lead's shoulder or arm pumping. The rest of the crew are planted wide-legged at the back, arms folded, nodding, or doing one synchronised move together. *Wide, the lead sharp, the crew slightly soft behind.*

**What made it aggressive-but-funny rather than generic "rapper pointing"** (WEAK, interpretive; it fits the sourced facts above). The aggression is **close and specific**: one person, one finger, the head pushed into their space. The comedy is **theatrical over-commitment**: mock-bows, mock-politicians, clones, effigies, the sincere fist undercut by the next sarcastic shrug. The **wiry, restless body** (bouncing, hunched, never still) works against the stillness of a "cool" pose. A generic AI still gives you a straight-backed man pointing calmly at mid-distance. Ask instead for a hunched body, bent knees, the move caught at its peak, aimed at the lens, too close.

# Web research: parody touches

## 4. Ten parody touches (deadpan, not cartoon)

Ideas, so ungraded. Each is one physical detail.

1. **The backing crew in the wrong mood:** while the lead raps furiously into the lens, the other five stand behind him with folded arms, one stifling a yawn, one checking a pocket watch (after the bored-looking headliner and the 2002 whispering extras, §1a).
2. **Synchronised move, one man late:** five gloved hands up in unison, the sixth still halfway up.
3. **The hype man's prop:** instead of a mic, the hype man holds a ledger, a ticker-tape strip or a bank's brass desk bell, raised like a mic.
4. **Bystanders who don't care:** a barber keeps shaving his customer, a bank teller keeps counting, a Pullman porter holds out a tray, all deadpan, while a masked man raps at the lens.
5. **The hidden-bar reflex:** a barman behind the rapper has his hand on the bottle-chute lever, poised (the 21 Club mechanism, §2a).
6. **Gesture meets the wrong object:** the "flat-palm chop" lands across a stack of banknotes; the finger-point lands on an adding machine.
7. **Polite crime:** one robber holds a raised finger to the lens saying "one moment", another wipes his feet on the doormat on the way into the vault.
8. **Over-formal stage business:** a mock bow in a bank vault to an audience of safe-deposit boxes; a lit cigar balanced on the vault wheel.
9. **The frozen rival:** a single uniformed guard or rival gangster, mic dropped at his feet, standing dead still and expressionless behind the gang (8 Mile's frozen opponent, §1a).
10. **The lighter wave, wrong era:** the stood-up crowd hold up lit matches or candles to the ballad bar, as a stadium crowd holds lighters (§1a).

# Web research: writing image-to-video prompts

## 1. Writing the prompt

- Image-to-video: the start image sets character, lighting and style. Describe **only the motion**. Re-describing subject, background or lighting "confuse[s] the model and lead[s] to poor results" [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/best-practice
- Call subjects by general terms ("the man", "they", "the figure on the left") rather than re-describing them [vendor] (same URL).
- Three motion kinds, which can be combined: camera move over a static scene, subject animation, environment animation [vendor] (same URL). In practice, pick one primary [reported, below].
- One focused moment per clip. Chaining several events "often leads to muddled or incomplete videos" [vendor] (same URL).
- Text-to-video formula: [Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style & Ambiance]. In image-to-video, keep cinematography and action, and drop the rest because the image supplies it [vendor + inference] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- Camera vocabulary Veo documents: static, pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, zoom, crane, aerial, handheld, whip pan, arc. Zoom changes focal length and "the camera itself doesn't move." Some advanced angles and lenses are "not officially supported" [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide
- Short clips: aim for "subtle evolution rather than dramatic change" [vendor] (same URL).
- Length: aim for one storyboardable change, not more text. Stacks of adjectives and do-everything action lists produce "wiggle" and drift [reported] https://www.imaginary.app/blog/image-to-video-that-actually-moves-not-just-wiggles-a-9-step-troubleshooting-che
- Write the action as start → end → hold ("one action only: X, then stop") so the model knows when to stop. The start image must show the start state, or the model oscillates between states [reported] (same URL).
- Don't combine a big subject motion with a big camera move: they compete [reported] (same URL).
- Avoid "handheld" and "shaky": they produce jitter. Use a locked tripod or one slow, stabilised dolly or slider [reported] (same URL).
- Omni Flash: one blog says to put camera direction at the end, in 3–8 words [reported, thin] https://framia.converge.ai/page/en-US/news/gemini-omni-flash-prompt-guide
- Omni Flash: write a director's shot brief, not keywords, and state the audio every time, because sound is generated in the same pass [reported] https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide-2026
- Timestamps: Google's Veo 3.1 guide documents `[00:00-00:02] shot…` blocks for **multi-shot** sequences in one generation [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1. For a one-action clip from a still, timestamps add cuts and beats, which is the opposite of what's wanted. Untested on Omni Flash [inference].
- Negative phrasing: Veo says "describe what you don't want to see" as a plain list ("wall, frame"), and avoid "no" and "don't" [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide. The 3.1 guide prefers positive restatement: "a desolate landscape with no buildings or roads" over "no man-made structures" [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- Negatives are weaker than positives in general (researcher quoted) [reported] https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/. Omni Flash has no negative-prompt field, so phrase constraints positively [reported] https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide-2026
- Audio: "Clearly specify if you want audio" and put audio in sentences separate from the visuals [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide. Use the labels `SFX: …` and `Ambient noise: …` [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- No speech: there is no documented "no dialogue" switch. Name the soundtrack fully (SFX, ambience, music) and include no quoted line or speaker [inference]. Google Labs support said speech can trigger burnt-in subtitles, so silence also protects against text [reported] https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/
- If a figure must "rap" in silence, the model may invent vocals to match the mouthing. A mask with no mouth reduces this. Untested [inference].

## 2. Identity, costume, small details; stopping morph, extras, teleporting, text

- Add short locks: "Background stays the same; no new objects appear", "Wardrobe unchanged", "Keep [X] consistent" [reported] https://www.imaginary.app/blog/image-to-video-that-actually-moves-not-just-wiggles-a-9-step-troubleshooting-che
- Localise the motion ("Primary motion: right hand. Torso and background remain static") to cut warping elsewhere [reported] (same URL).
- Morphing mostly comes from too much motion. Use one primary motion and at most one subtle secondary [reported] https://blog.segmind.com/veo-3-image-to-video-guide/
- Logos and emblems warp, hands distort and shapes morph "after about two seconds" in single-image animation generally [reported, thin, vendor blog] https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/gemini-omni-flash-1.1-image-to-video. Keep the emblem large, front-on and sharp in the still, and keep the mask's surface out of the motion (no head turns) [inference].
- Omni Flash consistency "can drift during large scene changes or fast camera moves". Use a reference image for important subjects [reported] https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-gemini-omni-flash-prompting-guide-2026
- For reuse across clips, Google advises repeating the same verbatim character description and changing only the action [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/best-practice. This conflicts with the image-to-video "don't re-describe" rule. Resolve it by repeating only a short identity lock line, not the full look [inference].
- Ingredients/reference images exist to "maintain a consistent aesthetic across multiple shots" [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1
- In one 9-shot test, Omni Flash's recurring flaw was an "abrupt zoom or framing jump". Hands and object physics mostly held, with no morphing [tested, small sample] https://community.trypencil.com/public/blogs/we-put-gemini-omni-flash-to-the-test-2026-07-13. Write "locked-off camera, framing holds" to counter it [inference].
- Text: Veo adds garbled subtitles even when told not to, mostly when there's speech [reported] https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/. Blogs report "no subtitles / on-screen text" lines helping only partly. The fallback is cropping the bottom 12–18% [reported, thin] https://www.veo3ai.io/blog/veo-3-remove-subtitles-captions-fix-2026
- In the dialogue format, a colon with no quotation marks keeps words from being rendered as on-screen text [vendor] https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/best-practice. Irrelevant if there's no speech.
- Extra people: no source found with a tested fix beyond the "no new objects / background stays the same" lock. Frame the still with every figure already in it, and don't ask anyone to enter [inference].
- Teleporting vehicles: no source addresses it directly. Likely causes are a vehicle half out of frame in the still, plus a camera move with no stated direction. Fix: the vehicle starts fully in frame, travels one stated direction ("moves left to right across frame and exits frame right"), and the camera is locked or does a single pan following it [inference].

## 3. Hand-gesture performance with a covered face

- No verified source on beat-timed gesture prompting was found (**thin**). A Veo prompt page shows chained big gestures with hand tension [reported, thin] https://apidog.com/vi/blog/google-veo-3-prompt-theory
- In Omni Flash tests, finger snaps hit contact with audio in sync, and typing rhythm looked "natural and varied, not looped" [tested] https://community.trypencil.com/public/blogs/we-put-gemini-omni-flash-to-the-test-2026-07-13. This suggests rhythmic hand action is within reach.
- The same test saw hands "alternated between a loose fist and an open palm instead of snapping" on one shot [tested] (same URL). Keep the gesture vocabulary small: one repeated move.
- Describe the gesture as a physical cycle with a count, not a mood. Example: "his right hand chops down on each beat, four times, forearm stays level, left hand stays at his chest" [inference, built from the "one action, then stop" rule above].
- Tie motion to sound in a separate SFX/music sentence ("a steady boom-bap drum beat; each hand chop lands on the kick drum"). Veo documents sound synced to actions [vendor] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1. Beat-to-gesture sync is untested [inference].
- No face means the head and shoulders carry the rhythm: a nod on the beat, shoulders rolling, leaning into the lens on the last bar [inference].
- The mask can't "speak", so don't ask for mouthing. Ask for the body to deliver it ("rapping" may invent a mouth or vocals) [inference].
