=== SHOT h13 ===
A frame from a 1920s feature film: six masked robbers perform at the lens on the grass airfield from the attached frame, the silver corrugated-metal trimotor parked behind them with its propellers still, green farmland under a summer sky. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached line-up image, masks copied from its close-ups: PLUM, BOTTLE, CRIMSON, MARINE, SILVER, and RUST. Each is a white-ringed knitted balaclava, a small red felt triangle over the man's left eye, on the right of the picture; black leather gloves, collars up, no skin. Packed in a tight wedge, CRIMSON at the point leans into the lens, knees bent, right index finger jabbing at us, large and soft in the foreground; the four behind him bounce mid-beat, flat palms chopping down. Just behind, RUST polishes a propeller blade with a white handkerchief, ignoring them. Low wide-angle shot from knee height, 24mm, a metre from CRIMSON, tilted a few degrees. Hard midday sun from high left. Shot on 35mm film, fine grain. 16:9.
WHY: A low drill-style wedge on the grass makes the plane loom while the polishing driver gives the one deadpan beat.

=== SHOT h14 ===
A frame from a 1920s feature film: the cramped radio studio from the attached frame, a little bigger, quilted maroon padding on every wall, lit only by the one deep-red lamp. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached line-up image, masks copied from its close-ups: PLUM, BOTTLE, CRIMSON, MARINE, RUST and SILVER. Each wears a white-ringed knitted balaclava with a small red felt triangle over his left eye, on the right of the picture; black leather gloves, collars up, no skin. All six crowd shoulder to shoulder round the chrome microphone, heads pushed together, performing past it into the lens: PLUM's gloved fist pressed to his chest, SILVER's arm hooked over BOTTLE's shoulder, the others' free hands rocking palm-down on the beat. MARINE politely lowers the microphone stand two inches with finger and thumb. Their shadows rise huge and horned across the padded wall. Camera slightly above eye line, 28mm, the microphone soft in the lower foreground. Deep inky shadows, no fill. 35mm film grain. 16:9.
WHY: A huddle round one microphone shot from just above gives the claustrophobic crew-in-the-booth energy and a different angle from the others.

=== SHOT h16 ===
A frame from a 1920s feature film: the newspaper press hall from the attached frame, black iron rotary presses running on either side, wide white paper webs racing overhead. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached line-up image, masks copied from its close-ups: SILVER, RUST, CRIMSON, PLUM, MARINE and BOTTLE. Each wears a white-ringed knitted balaclava with a small red felt triangle over his left eye, on the right of the picture; black leather gloves, collars up, no skin. They stand in a diagonal line receding from the camera, SILVER nearest at the right edge, waist-up, both arms flung wide, palms down; behind him RUST and CRIMSON point two fingers down at the lens; PLUM and MARINE bounce mid-step, coat tails swinging. Last in line, BOTTLE reads a freshly printed sheet held open, its print too small and soft to read. Wide-angle, 21mm, from chest height close to SILVER, the frame tilted ten degrees. WHY: A tilted receding diagonal under the racing paper gives the most kinetic, music-video angle of the four, with the reader at the back as the deadpan.
