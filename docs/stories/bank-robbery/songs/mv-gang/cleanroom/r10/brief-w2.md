# Web research from an earlier round: stage moves of one solo rapper

## 1. Early-2000s Eminem on stage: body language as plain physical description

### 1a. What the sources support

- **Most rap gestures are beat gestures.** A Masaryk University study of rappers' gestures found that gestures marking the beat were the most common type (about half of all occurrences), followed by iconic gestures that act out the words. A French study adds "metalinguistic/phatic" gestures that mark the start or end of a bar or signal "this line is the one". **MEDIUM** ([Masaryk, via search abstract](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116?kod=SBA399); [French clip study](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116/en?lang=en)). *Use:* the free hand should move on the beat, and once or twice per frame it should **illustrate** a word.
- **Theatrical staging and crew.** Anger Management 2002 used a circus set (Ferris wheel, fireworks, a tuxedoed performer), a montage of C-SPAN critics, an effigy he mocked on stage, a four-song D12 segment, and hits cut down to a verse and chorus. **STRONG** ([Jim DeRogatis, 2002](https://www.jimdero.com/News2001/Aug4AngerMgmt.htm); [Wikipedia: Anger Management Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anger_Management_Tour)). One review said he looked bored for much of the show **MEDIUM** ([Washington Times, 2002](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2002/jul/24/20020724-034747-1009r/)).
- **2002 VMAs "White America":** a mock presidential podium, with costumed politicians whispering to each other behind him while he rapped. **MEDIUM** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/eminems-best-live-performances/)). *Parody gold: the deadpan background extras.*
- **2000 VMAs:** about 100 bleached-blond lookalikes in white tees marched in from the street outside Radio City, bobbing along behind him. **STRONG** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_MTV_Video_Music_Awards); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/remember-when-eminem-and-hundreds-of-stans-invaded-the-2000-mtv-vmas-with-the-real-slim-shady); [Festival Sherpa](https://www.festivalsherpa.com/throwback-thursday-eminem-brings-his-own-army-of-100-slim-shady-doppelgangers-to-the-vmas)). *Use:* a gang of six identical silhouettes moving in unison is the same joke.
- **2001 Grammys "Stan":** a dark stage with a single light on him alone for his verse. At the end the two performers embraced and raised joined hands. **STRONG** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/today-hip-hop-history-eminem-performs-stan-elton-john-43rd-grammy-awards/); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/elton-and-eminem-perform-stan/)). *Use:* one hard pool of light, darkness around it.
- **Proof as hype man:** on stage for most of the show, a charismatic presence, visibly having fun with Eminem. The two did **synchronised moves** (the "My Band" send-up, and a 2000 Anger Management clip in which they moved in sync). **MEDIUM** ([PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/116198-eminem-live-from-new-york-city-2496125906.html); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/alex-siber/amazing-eminem-performances-we-found-on-youtube)).
- **2003 Grammys "Lose Yourself":** with Proof, moving under lime-green stage light. **MEDIUM** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/eminems-best-live-performances/)).
- **Critics on his body language:** "spastic movements", "rapid pace and explosive energy", crotch-grabbing and pinching gestures (*out*, they are obscene). **WEAK** (one magazine-blog, [Medium](https://medium.com/@poppulsenewsmag/eminems-audacious-stage-presence-ignites-debate-on-relevance-and-artistic-expression-a9667cfb8b19)).
- **Crowd call-outs:** he led the crowd in chanted, insulting call-and-response, and crowds raised lighters on the ballad. **STRONG** ([DeRogatis](https://www.jimdero.com/News2001/Aug4AngerMgmt.htm); [PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/116198-eminem-live-from-new-york-city-2496125906.html)).
- **8 Mile final battle:** he went first, then the opponent froze, dropped the mic and stood motionless. Eminem said the frozen opponent mirrored his own earlier choke. **MEDIUM** ([Slashfilm, citing Grantland](https://www.slashfilm.com/1077205/how-eminem-made-fun-of-anthony-mackies-real-life-to-expose-character-in-8-miles-final-rap-battle)). *Use:* a robber raps while a rival stands dead still with a dropped microphone.

### 1b. Move list: 26 prompt-ready sentences (all **WEAK**: from general knowledge of the footage, not sourced)

Each line gives one move and the camera angle that shows it best. Costume is not copied.

1. **Flat-palm chop:** free hand held flat, edge down, chopping down through the air on each stressed syllable, elbow tucked. *Low angle, 24mm, waist-up; the hand at the peak of its arc.*
2. **Sideways-palm wave:** the free hand turned palm-down and rocked side to side at chest height in time with the bar, like patting an invisible dog. *Medium shot, eye level, slight 3/4.*
3. **Finger jab at the lens:** index finger stabbing straight at the camera, arm half-extended, the fingertip big and foreshortened in the foreground. *Close, 21–24mm, the lens at chest height.*
4. **Two-finger point down:** index and middle together, pointing down at one person in the front row, the head tilted with the point. *Over-the-shoulder from the crowd, looking up.*
5. **Fist to chest:** a gloved fist pressed flat to the breastbone on a sincere line, the shoulders squared. *Medium close-up, frontal.*
6. **Mic hand tight:** the microphone held right against the mouth, gripped by the head with the cord looped once round the wrist, the elbow high and out to the side. *Tight close-up, low.*
7. **Mic cord loop:** the free hand flicking slack mic cord behind him mid-stride. *Wide, stage lip, low.*
8. **Hunched forward:** torso folded forward from the hips, knees bent, the head pushed toward the audience, like a sprinter at the blocks who decided to talk instead. *Low wide-angle from the stage floor.*
9. **Bounce:** knees flexing on the beat, both heels lifting, the shoulders dropping with each downbeat. *Full figure, slight motion blur only in the hem of the coat.*
10. **Pacing the lip:** walking sideways along the front edge with one foot on the very edge, the eyes never leaving the crowd. *Long lens from the side, or wide from below the edge.*
11. **Leaning over the front row:** one foot up on the monitor or edge, one forearm across the raised knee, the head craned out over the crowd. *Over-the-shoulder from the crowd, looking up into him.*
12. **Chin down, eyes up:** the head dipped, the gaze up through the brows straight into the lens: the threatening look. *Close-up, the lens slightly above eye line.*
13. **The snarl:** upper lip pulled back on one side mid-word. *(Our gang wear balaclavas, so the snarl must be carried by the head tilt and the eyes behind the knit.)*
14. **Head nod on the one:** a deep, slow nod on each downbeat, chin to chest. *Profile or 3/4.*
15. **The shrug:** both shoulders up, palms open and turned up, the head cocked: "what did you expect?" *Medium, frontal, deadpan.*
16. **Mock bow:** a theatrical deep bow with one arm sweeping across the waist, held a beat too long. *Wide, low, centred.*
17. **Finger gun (non-obscene):** thumb up, index out at the lens, a tiny recoil flick of the wrist. *Close, wide lens.*
18. **The "shh":** a gloved index finger raised to the knitted mouth of the balaclava, eyes wide. *Close-up.*
19. **Ear cup:** a hand cupped behind the ear, leaning toward the crowd: "I can't hear you." *Medium, from the crowd side.*
20. **Hand up for call-and-response:** the free arm straight up, palm open or a raised fist, the mic swung out toward the crowd. *Wide from behind the performer, the crowd beyond.*
21. **Both arms out:** arms spread wide, palms down, crucifix-cocky, at the end of a verse. *Low wide, symmetrical.*
22. **Counting on fingers:** the free hand counting off a list, one finger at a time. *Medium close.*
23. **Throat-cut swipe** (careful with tone): a flat hand drawn across the throat to cut the beat. *Medium.*
24. **Head-tap:** two fingers tapping the temple: "think." *Close-up.*
25. **Back to the crowd, look over the shoulder:** turned away, the head snapping round to the lens on the punchline. *Medium from behind.*
26. **Crew behaviour:** the hype man one step behind and to the side, mouthing the last word of every bar, hand on the lead's shoulder or arm pumping. The rest of the crew are planted wide-legged at the back, arms folded, nodding, or doing one synchronised move together. *Wide, the lead sharp, the crew slightly soft behind.*

**What made it aggressive-but-funny rather than generic "rapper pointing"** (WEAK, interpretive; it fits the sourced facts above). The aggression is **close and specific**: one person, one finger, the head pushed into their space. The comedy is **theatrical over-commitment**: mock-bows, mock-politicians, clones, effigies, the sincere fist undercut by the next sarcastic shrug. The **wiry, restless body** (bouncing, hunched, never still) works against the stillness of a "cool" pose. A generic AI still gives you a straight-backed man pointing calmly at mid-distance. Ask instead for a hunched body, bent knees, the move caught at its peak, aimed at the lens, too close.

# Web research: the film-still look

## 3. Making it look like a high-budget film still in Nano Banana 2

Nano Banana 2 is reported to be Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. **MEDIUM** ([AI/ML API](https://aimlapi.com/blog/banana-prompt-guid); the Google Cloud guide names "Gemini 3.1 Flash Image Preview" separately).

### 3a. Google's own guidance (STRONG: primary)

From the [Google Cloud prompting guide](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana), the [Google Developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/how-to-prompt-gemini-2-5-flash-image-generation-for-the-best-results/) and the [Google Keyword tips](https://blog.google/products/gemini/image-generation-prompting-tips/):

- **Prose, not keyword lists:** "Describe the scene, don't just list keywords." The formula is subject + action + location/context + composition + style, in full sentences.
- **Think like a photographer:** name the shot type, lens, aperture, angle ("low-angle shot with a shallow depth of field (f/1.8)", "wide-angle lens"), and name the light ("chiaroscuro lighting with harsh, high contrast"). A film-stock or camera-body phrase sets the look ("as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy").
- **Materials, not categories:** "Don't just ask for a suit jacket; ask for 'navy blue tweed.'" This is directly useful for the suits: name the cloth (chalk-stripe worsted, herringbone, barathea), the knit of the balaclava (chunky cable, fine rib) and the glove leather (black kid, oiled pigskin).
- **Positive framing:** say "an empty street", not "no cars". Negative words can summon the thing.
- **References:** up to 14 reference images. The pattern is [reference] + [relationship instruction] + [new scenario], naming each image's job ("using the attached image as the costume…").
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 is supported natively. With several input images the output takes the ratio of the **last** image, so attach a 16:9 image last, or state the ratio.
- **Text:** put the exact words in quotes and specify the font; the model "may occasionally misspell words".
- **Consistency drifts** over repeated edits: start fresh with the full description rather than chaining edits.

### 3b. Practitioner guidance (MEDIUM/WEAK: secondary)

- **The first ~15 words get the most weight; end with the output spec ("16:9").** Use one style family and one camera phrase only. Stacking camera bodies or styles makes "a smeared hybrid". **MEDIUM** ([Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide)).
- **"Realistic" is a feeling, not an instruction:** replace it with a camera, a lens and a lighting direction. State the image's purpose (e.g. "a still frame from a feature film"). **MEDIUM** (Fliki).
- **Slop triggers:** "hyperrealistic", "8K", "ultra-detailed", "masterpiece" push toward a rendered, video-game look. The slop look itself is flat, over-lit, waxy skin, teal-and-orange, symmetrical. **MEDIUM**: several blogs plus a January 2026 Reddit thread agree, but nobody ran a controlled test ([Cloudinary](https://cloudinary.com/guides/image-generation/how-to-make-ai-images-look-real); [alimagelab, 2026](https://alimagelab.blogspot.com/2026/01/why-your-ai-images-look-fake-and-how-to.html)). Add **WEAK**: "cinematic", "epic", "stunning", "dramatic lighting" said without a source, and "moody" also drift toward the glossy average. Name a source of light instead.
- **Hands:** anchor the anatomy (state which fingers are extended, show the thumb), minimise occlusion, avoid motion blur near the hands, and name specific failures rather than "bad anatomy". **WEAK** (vendor blogs: [Curify](https://www.curify-ai.com/blog/10-prompting-tips-nano-banana); Sider, which blocked fetching). Add **WEAK**: **gloves help.** A black leather glove reads as a single shape with fewer creases to get wrong. Give each hand **one** job ("the right hand grips the microphone; the left hand points with only the index finger extended, the other three fingers curled and the thumb on top").
- **No source found on foreshortening.** **WEAK** suggestion: describe it optically, not anatomically: "the pointing hand is very close to the lens and appears large, slightly soft, filling the lower-left of the frame, while his masked face behind it is sharp". The model knows the lens physics behind "24mm lens a foot from the hand".

### 3c. Applied to this job (WEAK: synthesis, test it)

- **Body caught mid-gesture:** write the *peak*, not the motion: "frozen at the top of a downward chop, palm flat, the elbow high", "mid-bounce, both heels off the floor". Motion blur is allowed only on the coat tails or a swinging mic cord, never on hands or the head.
- **One motivated light and deep blacks:** name the single source and where it sits: "lit only by a single bare bulb hanging just above and in front of him; everything beyond the bulb's reach falls to near-black; the bulb's glare is visible at the top of the frame". Then a positive line for the blacks: "the shadows are deep and inky, with no fill light".
- **Film feel:** one stock or camera phrase (e.g. "shot on 35mm motion-picture film, fine grain, slight halation round the bulb"), an anamorphic or spherical lens choice, and the sentence "a frame from a feature film".
- **Stopping readable fake text:** describe signs by shape and light only ("a hand-painted sign, out of focus, its lettering unreadable"), keep them soft behind the subject, or turn them away. Or give a real short word in quotes if text must be read. Don't write "no text" (positive framing).
- **Person very close to a wide lens in 16:9:** "a close, low, wide-angle shot from about a metre away, 24mm, the lens at chest height looking slightly up; his body fills the left two-thirds of the 16:9 frame; the room stretches away behind him in sharp perspective; the five other members of the gang stand behind him, smaller, in a loose line". Placing the lead off-centre fights the symmetrical slop default.
- **Length and structure:** about 80–150 words of prose in this order: subject + action (the gesture at its peak) → place → composition and lens → light → texture/stock → 16:9. More than that and the camera and light lines get diluted (**WEAK**, consistent with the guidance that early tokens dominate).
- **Reference for costume consistency:** say exactly what the image is for: "Using the attached image only for the masks, suits and gloves of the six men; new place, new pose, new light". Attach the 16:9 image **last** so the ratio holds. Because masked faces carry no identity, the costume reference is doing all the consistency work, so keep wording identical across prompts for the costume itself (Fliki: "reuse the exact same physical description").

# Web research: parody touches

## 4. Ten parody touches (deadpan, not cartoon)

Ideas, so ungraded. Each is one physical detail.

1. **The backing crew in the wrong mood:** while the lead raps furiously into the lens, the other five stand behind him with folded arms, one stifling a yawn, one checking a pocket watch (after the bored-looking headliner and the 2002 whispering extras, §1a).
2. **Synchronised move, one man late:** five gloved hands up in unison, the sixth still halfway up.
3. **The hype man's prop:** instead of a mic, the hype man holds a ledger, a ticker-tape strip or a bank's brass desk bell, raised like a mic.
4. **Bystanders who don't care:** a barber keeps shaving his customer, a bank teller keeps counting, a Pullman porter holds out a tray, all deadpan, while a masked man raps at the lens.
5. **The hidden-bar reflex:** a barman behind the rapper has his hand on the bottle-chute lever, poised (the 21 Club mechanism, §2a).
6. **Gesture meets the wrong object:** the "flat-palm chop" lands across a stack of banknotes; the finger-point lands on an adding machine.
7. **Polite crime:** one robber holds a raised finger to the lens saying "one moment", another wipes his feet on the doormat on the way into the vault.
8. **Over-formal stage business:** a mock bow in a bank vault to an audience of safe-deposit boxes; a lit cigar balanced on the vault wheel.
9. **The frozen rival:** a single uniformed guard or rival gangster, mic dropped at his feet, standing dead still and expressionless behind the gang (8 Mile's frozen opponent, §1a).
10. **The lighter wave, wrong era:** the stood-up crowd hold up lit matches or candles to the ballad bar, as a stadium crowd holds lighters (§1a).
