=== SHOT h17 ===
A frame from a 1920s feature film: six masked robbers in sharp suits rap straight down the lens on the granite steps of the columned bank at dusk, the place in the attached frame from the film, keeping its stone, its dusk blue and the orange fire glowing in the doorway behind them, banknotes still drifting down through the air. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached line-up card, masks copied exactly from its close-ups: Plum, Bottle, Crimson, Marine, Rust and Silver. Each mask is hand-knitted rib wool with a white ring round the face and one small rounded red felt triangle high over the man's left eye, on the right side of the picture; black leather gloves, no skin. They are packed in a tight wedge descending the steps, Crimson at the front leaning down over the lens, frozen at the bottom of a flat-palm chop, the five behind crowding his shoulders with arms swung forward. Low, close wide-angle shot from two steps below, 24mm, tilted slightly. Silver calmly brushes a banknote off his lapel. Shot on 35mm film, fine grain, 16:9, edge to edge.
WHY: The crew-wedge pushing down at a low lens is the classic street-video formation, and the steps give it height without anyone growing out of scale.

The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached line-up card, masks copied exactly from its close-ups: Plum, Bottle, Crimson, Marine, Rust and Silver. Each mask is hand-knitted rib wool with a white ring round the face and one small rounded red felt triangle high over the man's left eye, on the right side of the picture; black leather gloves, no skin. They are packed in a tight cluster at the foot of the water tower, heads tipped back, all looking up into the lens, arms raised toward it: Marine and Crimson pointing with index fingers, Silver's flat palm stretched upward, the others' fists up. WHY: A high wide angle down at a crowd reaching up is the other crew-video staple and gives the set a fourth angle none of the others use.
