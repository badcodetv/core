=== SHOT k05 ===
A side-on tracking shot at dusk on a wide cobbled avenue in 1920s Chicago, the camera racing level with two speeding black Packard sedans, 35mm lens, the gas lamps and shop windows behind smeared into streaks while the cars stay sharp. SILVER, the tall one, is caught mid-leap in the gap between the two running boards, legs split wide, coat tails flying, body perfectly straight. His mask and oxblood-red suit are exactly as in the attached reference image: rib-knitted wool with no holes, a white wool ring round the face, and a small red felt triangle over his LEFT eye, which puts it on the right side of the picture. Both hands are in black leather gloves; one is out for balance, the other holds only the end of his bow tie as if that were keeping him alive. Low warm side light from the lamps rims him against a cooling violet sky. Lacquered paint reflects the street, chrome headlamps glint, cobbles are wet and worn. The picture fills the 16:9 frame edge to edge.
WHY: The leap is played dead straight and heroic, and the joke is the tie he clutches like a lifeline, which suits a "dyspraxic flash".

=== SHOT k06 ===
A low wide-angle shot from pavement height, 24mm lens, on a bright morning market street in 1920s New York, hard sunlight raking between brick tenements and fire escapes. In the foreground a black police Model T lies flipped on its roof, wheels still spinning, crushed into a wooden fruit stall with a striped awning torn loose; dozens of oranges hang in the air, frozen mid-bounce, catching the sun. A uniformed policeman, an adult man with a bushy moustache and his helmet knocked sideways, climbs out of the upturned car unhurt and shakes his fist. Behind him, a long black running-board sedan streaks past in a blur. Through its window RUST is at the wheel and CRIMSON leans back casually, his masks and suits exactly as in the attached reference image: knitted balaclavas with no holes, a white wool ring round each face, the red triangle over each man's LEFT eye, on the right side of the picture as they face the lens. Black leather gloves, no skin. No signs or lettering anywhere. 16:9, full frame.
WHY: The car flip lands as pure slapstick consequence, the outraged fist is the "call the police" line acted out, and the gang barely glance at it.

RUST is at the wheel, arms crossed over hard on the steering, and BOTTLE leans far out of the passenger window, gun held straight up at the sky. Their masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image: hand-knitted rib wool with no eye or mouth holes, the white wool ring round each face, the small red felt triangle over each man's LEFT eye, which puts it on the right side of the picture whenever they face up toward the lens. Edge-to-edge 16:9.
WHY: The handbrake turn is the getaway driver doing his job beautifully, which fits "work can get paid though", and the top-down angle makes the smoke arc read as one graphic sweep.

=== SHOT k08 ===
A long-lens shot from far along the riverbank at sunset, 200mm, the low sun directly behind the scene so everything is backlit in gold and the river glitters. A steel bascule bridge is rising, its two leaves tilted up like a ramp, and the gang's black sedan hangs in the air over the widening gap, nose high, wheels spinning, the river far below. CRIMSON leans out of the rear window holding his grey fedora on top of his mask, and PLUM is in the front, arms raised as if on a fairground ride; the masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image: knitted balaclavas with no holes, a white wool ring round each face, the red triangle over each man's LEFT eye, on the right side of the picture as they turn toward camera. Black leather gloves on every hand. At the near edge of the bridge three police cars have stopped short, policemen standing on their running boards staring up. Iron girders, rivets and the sedan's chrome rim-lit by the sun. No signs or readable words. 16:9, full frame.
WHY: The impossible jump is the film's crowning stunt for the line about a hungry revolution, and the stranded police watching it is the deadpan payoff.
