A long, narrow vault corridor in a 1920s American bank, nickel-plated steel walls, a terrazzo floor with a brass inlaid chevron, one bare bulb in a wire cage at the far end throwing hard light and long shadows toward us through a thin haze. Five taut white threads cross the corridor at different heights, each with one small brass bell. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image; this shot shows MARINE and BOTTLE. In the foreground MARINE arches backwards beneath the lowest thread like a ballet dancer, one leg pointed high, gloved arms flung wide, cream linen jacket flaring. Behind him BOTTLE walks straight through the threads at a stroll, two snapping against his tweed chest, bells swinging. Each balaclava is ribbed hand-knitted wool over the whole head, no holes, a white wool ring round the face, one small rounded red felt triangle over the man's left eye, on the right side of the picture. Black leather gloves, no skin. Low side angle, 35mm lens, film grain, 16:9 edge to edge, no lettering anywhere.
WHY: The perfect contortion and the shrugging walk-through turn the laser-grid cliche into the line about rules the usual people simply ignore.

The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image; this shot shows CRIMSON, RUST and SILVER. Each balaclava is ribbed hand-knitted wool covering the whole head and neck, no eye, nose or mouth holes, a white wool ring round the face, one small rounded red felt triangle high over the man's left eye, on the right side of the picture. WHY: A debt bomb that two loud men fight over while the quiet one simply stops the clock fits "insistent on debt's existence" and plays the wire cliche as plain prop comedy.
