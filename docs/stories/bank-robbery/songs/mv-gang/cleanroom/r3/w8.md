=== SHOT k09 ===
A wide 24mm frame taken from just above the black water at night: a varnished mahogany rum-runner speedboat leaps a white wake, its bow high, wooden crates of bottles bouncing loose in the air above its deck. A Coast Guard patrol boat's searchlight hits it from the left as the only light, hard cold white, throwing a long beam through sea spray and leaving the sky pitch black. PLUM stands braced in the stern like a captain, one leg up on a crate, one arm flung forward in a grand stage-rapper point at the horizon, the other hand calmly checking a pocket watch. The mask and suit are exactly as in the attached reference image: hand-knitted rib wool, no holes, white ring round the face, red felt triangle over his LEFT eye, on the right side of the picture. Black leather gloves, no skin. Spray beads on the chrome and the chalk-stripe wool. No readable words anywhere. 16:9, edge to edge, film grain.
WHY: The searchlight makes it a straight chase shot, and the man timing it on his pocket watch is the joke, while asset inflation is the cargo flying off the boat.

=== SHOT k10 ===
A long-lens side view, about 200mm, from a hillside level with a high timber trestle in late afternoon. A black steam express crosses it with a big white plume, and the lens squashes the trestle legs, the gorge and the far ridge into flat layers. Low amber sun from behind the train rims everything with hard gold edges. On the carriage roof BOTTLE and MARINE sprint toward the right of frame, each clutching a canvas cash sack, their fedoras tumbling off behind them in the wind. MARINE twists back mid-stride with a sarcastic two-handed point at the camera. The masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image: knitted rib wool, no holes, white ring round each face, red triangle over the LEFT eye, on the right of the picture for anyone facing the lens. Black leather gloves, no skin. A lone steel radio mast stands on the distant ridge. No readable words. 16:9, edge to edge.
WHY: It is the classic train-roof run done by two men who stop to show off, and the radio mast on the ridge sets up the devil's radio station.

=== SHOT k11 ===
A low wide 35mm frame from the grass of a farm field at dawn. A corrugated-metal tri-motor plane lifts off toward the camera, wheels just clear of the ground, its three propellers blurred, and the rising sun behind it throws a pale backlight that turns the dust and morning mist to haze. A rope ladder swings from the open cabin door, and SILVER hangs off its lowest rung with one hand, giving a slow, bored, regal wave with the other. The mask and suit are exactly as in the attached reference image: knitted rib wool, no holes, white ring, red triangle over his LEFT eye, on the right of the picture. Black leather gloves, no skin, cuffs over the wrists. Far behind across the field, three small black police sedans bounce through the furrows, kicking up dust and much too late. No readable words. 16:9, edge to edge, soft halation round the sun.
WHY: Flying off into the sunrise is the film's own "the end" shot, and SILVER's royal wave is the parody.

=== SHOT k12 ===
A low 50mm frame from the crowd on a city boulevard at dusk, looking up as an open cream roadster with chrome headlamps and white-wall tyres speeds past. Theatre marquees glow down the street, and their bulbs plus the warm streetlamps are the only light, with deep shadows between them. CRIMSON stands on the back seat flinging fistfuls of banknotes from a split canvas sack, his other arm thrown up in a big raise-the-roof. The wind has caught a blizzard of plain green notes with no legible print. RUST drives, both gloved hands on the wheel, staring straight ahead. Their masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image: knitted rib wool, no holes, white ring, red triangle over the LEFT eye, on the right of the picture. Black leather gloves, no skin. The adult crowd, dressed in varied period coats, hats and cloches, grabs at the notes with delight. No readable words on the marquees or anywhere else. 16:9, edge to edge, film grain.
WHY: The cash rain plays "stacking up the cash by the grand" straight and big, while RUST's flat stare at the road keeps it deadpan.
