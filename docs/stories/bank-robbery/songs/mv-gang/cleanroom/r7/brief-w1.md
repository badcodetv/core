## 1. Pacing a rap video so the shots breathe

### What measured data exists

- **STRONG.** Bordwell's "Intensified Continuity" (Film Quarterly, 2002) puts classical Hollywood at an ASL of 8–11 s. The fastest features, the ones influenced by music video, ran at 3–4 s, with extremes around 2.7 s (*The Crow*, *U-Turn*, *Sleepy Hollow*). Even the cutting style borrowed from music video averaged two to three times longer shots than our cut 6. ([Bordwell via Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/310344009/Bordwell-Intensified-Continuity); [Senses of Cinema](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2007/book-reviews/david-bordwell/))
- **MEDIUM.** A shot-by-shot academic analysis of Mike Posner's "I Took a Pill in Ibiza" (Pérez Rufí & Rodríguez López, 2017) found mostly close shots and an ASL of **about 2 s**. That is a pop video cut for the internet era. I only saw the abstract in a search snippet; the full text was blocked. ([ariasmontano.uhu.es](https://ariasmontano.uhu.es/entities/publication/7b058a26-32fb-4329-b388-8a0822f99aa8))
- **MEDIUM.** Sedeño Valdellós et al. (2016, *Revista Latina de Comunicación Social*) measured ASL across 40 post-television music videos and found ASL varied by video type: concept-led videos differed from conventional performance or narrative ones. I could not read the per-category figures (the file was an EPUB that would not parse), so I quote no numbers from it. ([Revista Latina](https://nuevaepoca.revistalatinacs.org/index.php/revista/article/download/807/1229/3966))
- **Not found.** I found no published ASL study specific to rap or hip-hop videos, and no Cinemetrics set for them. Any rap-specific ASL figure below is my inference.
- **WEAK (inference).** Taken together, a modern music video sits around **2–3 s ASL**. An ASL of 1 s is flurry pace sustained for the whole song, so nothing reads as fast any more. That matches Jack's note that the cut is "so many cuts". It also matches editor Max Windows's warning: "Often good editing is judged on the frequency of the edit, but this does not always constitute a quality cut." ([shots.net](https://shots.net/news/view/cut-the-tempo-how-to-edit-for-music-video))

### How editors decide when to hold and when to cut

- **MEDIUM.** Cutting on the first beat of every bar works, but done every bar it becomes predictable. Editors shift where the cut falls so the viewer can't anticipate it. ([Muzines](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/doing-the-video/12894); [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/from-music-videos-to-feature-films-editing-lessons-that-translate))
- **MEDIUM.** Skilled editors break the rhythm on purpose: they hold a shot longer than expected to build tension, or cut just *before* a drop to sharpen the anticipation. ([journalism.university](https://journalism.university/media-information-literacy/deconstructing-elements-music-video/))
- **MEDIUM.** Editors describe moving from cutting on every beat towards "more interesting rhythms" that let certain shots breathe. Jamie Hodgson says this in LBB's *Finely Sliced* series. ([LBB, Jamie Hodgson](https://lbbonline.com/news/finely-sliced-taking-inspiration-from-the-arts-with-jamie-hodgson))
- **STRONG (theory).** Carol Vernallis, the main scholar of music-video form, says editing follows **musical phrases** as well as the beat. She also says it works so that no single element (narrative, performer, lyrics) "gains the upper hand". Phrases in rap are bars and couplets, so cutting on the phrase means cutting on the bar or the 2-bar line. Seen through summaries only, not the primary text. ([Vernallis summary](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/carol-vernalliss-music-video-theory/73507931); [UWL editing dossier](https://repository.uwl.ac.uk/4706/1/EditingDossiermsmi.2017.6.pdf))
- **WEAK (inference).** A working rule from the above:
  - A **story shot** with real action holds until its action completes, usually **1–2 bars (2.6–5.2 s)**. A cool shot that is cut before its action resolves feels like a trailer.
  - A **performance shot** holds a whole **line (one bar) or couplet (two bars)**. Cut on the bar line or the punchline, never mid-line.
  - **Fast cuts** go on the beat (0.65 s) or the half-beat (0.33 s) in short bursts only.
  - Vary where the cut lands: on the downbeat for weight, on beat 4 (the "and" before the bar) to pull into the next line, and on a snare or ad-lib for a hit.

### Flurries: few, and placed

- **WEAK (inference).** A flurry is felt because it contrasts with held shots. Two to four short bursts in a 3.5-minute song, each 1–2 bars long, read as events. Jack's "sparingly cut fast" asks for exactly this.

### A recipe for this song (~92 BPM, ~80 bars), WEAK (inference from the above)

Adjust the bar counts to the real song map; the proportions are what matter.

| Section | Shot length | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro (~8 bars) | 2 bars a shot (5.2 s), so ~4 shots | Establish the world: the glossy wide, the gang assembling. Let the 6 s clips nearly play out. |
| Verse (16 bars each) | Average 1 bar (2.6 s); performance shots 1–2 bars, story shots 1–2 bars | Story carries the verse, with performance on the punchlines (see §2). About 10–14 shots a verse. |
| Hook (8 bars) | Mostly 1 bar; the **last 1–2 bars of the hook** can be a flurry (shots on the beat) | The hook is where the gang performs together. Give one hook a flurry, not every hook. |
| Break / bridge | Long holds: 2–4 bars (5–10 s); two 6 s clips back to back is fine | The breath before the final push. The single longest shot of the video belongs here. |
| Final hook | 1 bar, with the **biggest flurry** (2 bars on beats, or half-beats for 1 bar) leading into the last line | The climax. |
| Outro | 2 bars a shot, ending on one held hero shot | Let the last gesture land; see §4. |

- **Target totals:** about **70–90 cuts** (ASL 2.3–3 s), down from 214.
- **Flurries:** **3, maybe 4**: (1) the end of the first hook, (2) one violent story beat mid-song (the shoot-out or the safe), (3) the run into the final line. Each is 1–2 bars, and each is followed by a **held** shot so the burst stops dead.

## 2. Mixing performance and narrative

- **STRONG (theory).** Vernallis: music video "is not a slave to continuity editing", and story can run as fragments in montage rather than as one continuous sequence. Repetition and recurring shots are part of the form. Narrative need not be balanced against performance; the edit keeps either one from dominating. ([Vernallis summary](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/carol-vernalliss-music-video-theory/73507931); [UWL dossier](https://repository.uwl.ac.uk/4706/1/EditingDossiermsmi.2017.6.pdf))
- **MEDIUM.** No fixed ratio is published. The split follows the song's goal: personality singles lean on performance, cinematic songs lean on story. A useful test: if removing a story shot wouldn't damage the progression, it is atmosphere, not story. Performance earns its place when it gives the viewer something the story can't. This is vendor content aimed at AI music videos. ([Jack Righteous / Atlabs](https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/ai-art-visuals-creatives/atlabs-performance-vs-story-scenes-ai-music-video))
- **MEDIUM.** Editors often lay the performance in first, then see where the story beats and b-roll belong; the artist stays the star. ([No Film School, speed up your edit](https://nofilmschool.com/speed-up-music-video-edit); [Ideta](https://fr.ideta.io/blog-posts-english/how-to-edit-music-videos-an-ultimate-guide-to-creating-effective-music-videos))
- **MEDIUM.** Ian McLaughlin (LBB) calls performance videos "a uniquely challenging sub-genre" and builds several stacked clips when cutting a band, because cutting a performance on the beat goes stale fast. I saw this in a search snippet; the page itself refused the fetch. ([LBB search result](https://lbbonline.com/news/more-than-just-cutting-to-the-beat-the-nuance-of-editing-music-videos))

### Pattern for this video, WEAK (inference)

- **Ratio:** about **35–40 % performance to 60–65 % action** by screen time. That gives "a good mix" without overpowering, which is Jack's wording.
- **Where performance goes:**
  - Performance on the **first line of each verse** (the rapper is introduced).
  - Performance on the **punchlines**, the bar that ends a couplet, where the viewer should hear the words.
  - The **whole gang on the hook**.
  - Story **under the descriptive lines** in between.
  - Go back to performance at least every 4–6 bars so it runs "throughout".
- **Six performers without repetition:**
  - Give each robber **one home location and one framing** (one in the speakeasy booth, one at the safe, one on the truck, and so on). The viewer learns who is who without faces.
  - Rotate **by verse ownership**: whoever's voice is heard is on screen. On group lines, use a 2-shot or a 6-shot.
  - Vary the size of the performance shot (wide, medium, close on the hands) rather than repeating the same medium.
  - Don't show the same performer twice in a row unless the second shot is a different size.
- **Masked, lip-less performance:**
  - **MEDIUM:** MF DOOM rapped through a mask and said it kept the focus on the lyrics, not the face. He even sent stand-ins in the mask to shows. Identity was carried by the costume and the voice, not the mouth. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/952716733); [Seattle Weekly](https://www.seattleweekly.com/?p=69338))
  - **MEDIUM-STRONG:** A semiotic study of 50 French and Czech rap videos (Chodakova & Podhorná-Polická) catalogued 136 rap gestures with over 3,000 occurrences. **Beat-marking gestures were the most frequent.** There were also iconic gestures (miming sleep, writing), pointing gestures, and a category of "metalinguistic/phatic" gestures aimed at the viewer. ([muni.cz](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116/en?lang=en), abstract via search)
  - **WEAK (inference):** So the masked bars are carried by:
    - the head and shoulders bouncing on the beat (the flow made visible);
    - hand chops on the stressed syllables;
    - pointing into the lens on "you" lines;
    - one iconic mime per punchline;
    - leaning into the lens on the hard line.
  - Masks hide lip-sync errors, so a performance clip only has to be **on the beat**, not on the syllable.

## 3. Fixing image-to-video failures (Veo-family, Flow)

### General rules that apply to every fix

- **STRONG (Google).** For image-to-video, "Your source image already provides the subject, scene, and style. Focus your prompt on the motion." Re-describing the character, background or lighting makes prompts redundant that "confuse the model and lead to poor results". Refer to the character as "the subject", "he", "they". ([Google Cloud, Veo best practices](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/best-practice))
- **STRONG (Google).** Camera movement with a static scene is "the simplest and most reliable way to add dynamism". Subject animation is "best for subtle, lifelike actions". (same page)
- **STRONG (Google).** "A sharp, clear, and well-composed" source image gives a more coherent video, because the model takes character detail from it. (same page)
- **MEDIUM.** Characters drift because the model puts motion ahead of consistency once the prompt gets complex. A character can look right for the first frames, then slowly change. Keep prompts short, and change one variable per retry. ([veo3gen.app](https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/veo-31-image-to-video-this-week-the-first-frame-lock-workflow-for-consistent-cha); [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/consistent-character-ai/))
- **STRONG (research).** Google DeepMind's Physics-IQ benchmark found video models' physical understanding "severely limited" and *unrelated* to how realistic they look. Sora scored highest on realism but only about 10 % on physics. Realistic-looking destruction is exactly where these models fail. ([arXiv 2501.09038](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09038v3))

### 3a. A mask pattern that drifts, vanishes or changes mid-clip (k02, k06, k11)

- **MEDIUM.** Lock the camera (tripod / locked-off) or use only a slow dolly-in. These are the most stable options. ([veo3gen.app](https://www.veo3gen.app/blog/veo-31-image-to-video-this-week-the-first-frame-lock-workflow-for-consistent-cha))
- **MEDIUM.** One guide recommends explicitly asking the model to keep the same face shape and outfit throughout and to avoid morphing during camera moves. ([Veo 3 negative prompt guide](https://veo3-ecru.vercel.app/blog/veo-3-negative-prompt-guide-2026))
- **WEAK (inference, from the drift mechanism above):**
  1. **The still must show the mask clearly**: large in frame, facing camera, the ring and the triangle sharp. A tiny mask in a wide car shot (k02, k06) gives the model too few pixels to keep. For car interiors, frame **medium-close through the windscreen**, not a wide of the car.
  2. **Keep the head turn under about 30°.** The triangle sits over one eye. A big turn hides it, and the model re-invents it on the way back. Favour shoulders, hands and the body for the motion.
  3. **No occlusion**: no hand passing over the face, no wind-whipped scarf, no smoke across the mask. Anything that covers the pattern lets the model redraw it.
  4. **Slow and small**: one action per clip ("he grips the wheel and glances to the side, then back to the road"). Don't stack actions.
  5. **Motion prompt names the mask only as something to keep**, without describing it, e.g. "his knitted mask stays exactly as it is". This follows Google's "don't re-describe" rule while flagging the item to keep.
  6. **The plane hang (k11):** wind and violent movement attack a soft knitted mask. Shoot it **from above, looking down at the gripping gloves and the top of the masked head**, with the camera moving and the body swaying, not thrashing. Or hold the mask face-on with only the background (clouds) moving.
  7. For a shot that must be wide and fast, accept that the mask is unreadable. **Cut it short (under 1 bar)** so the drift never shows. Cutting before the drift is a legitimate fix.

### 3b. A character "teleporting" in a spin (v05)

- **MEDIUM.** Practitioner guides say a turnaround should be a *gentle* orbit from the front to a slight side angle, and suggest wording like "moves like a smooth dolly, not a teleport". Interpolation breaks on chaotic motion. ([Veo 3 camera control guide](https://veo3-ecru.vercel.app/blog/veo-3-camera-control-prompts-2026); [Veo 3 negative prompt guide](https://veo3-ecru.vercel.app/blog/veo-3-negative-prompt-guide-2026))
- **WEAK (inference) on why:** during a full 360° the model loses sight of the reference face and costume for most of the turn. On the way round it re-generates the figure from scratch and lands it in a slightly different place, size or pose. That is the "teleport".
- **WEAK (inference) on fixes, in order of reliability:**
  1. **Half turn, then cut.** He turns his back to camera (180°) and the clip ends there. Cut to a second clip that starts with his back to camera and turns to face. The cut hides the hidden side.
  2. **A partial pivot**: a quarter turn of the shoulders with the head staying on camera (a swagger turn, not a spin).
  3. **The camera orbits slowly** round a planted figure instead of the figure spinning. Describe it as one continuous move ("the camera slowly circles a quarter of the way round him while he keeps rapping").
  4. **Fake the spin in the edit**: a whip pan or flash-frame transition between two clips (facing away, then facing camera). This is a classic music-video spin and needs no generation of the turn at all.

### 3c. People at the wrong scale in the still (p08, the giant in the alley)

- **MEDIUM (forum).** Nano Banana 2 users report wrong proportions even with references. The advice is explicit proportion wording, or pinning scale with a reference that already has the right layout. One user got accurate scale by roughing a composite first and using it as a reference. ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rxkwfn/snapshots/2026-03-20T21%3A15%3A59.791656Z); [Skool post](https://chat.skool.com/pro-grade-ai-training/scaling-products-in-an-image))
- **STRONG (Google).** Describe the scene in rich, specific prose, and use the "shot from a [camera angle] with a [lens]" pattern. ([Google AI Studio image docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-generation))
- **WEAK (inference) on wording that fixes scale:**
  - **Give the camera a height and distance:** "shot at eye level from across the alley, about six metres back, 35 mm lens". A defined lens and distance gives the model a perspective to obey.
  - **Anchor each figure to the architecture:** "all three men stand on the same cobbles, the tops of their heads level with the bottom of the first-floor window; the doorway behind them is a head taller than them". Real objects of known size (doors, windows, a lamp post, a barrel) are the scale ruler.
  - **"Three men of the same ordinary height, shoulder to shoulder in one line"**, rather than "the leader in the middle". "Leader", "central" and "dominant" invite the model to make him bigger.
  - **Fewer people in the shot.** Three in an alley is safer than six. The rest of the gang can be in a separate shot.
  - **A different staging that sidesteps the problem:** one performer framed medium with the others soft behind, or a low angle that makes the alley loom. Scale errors are hardest to see when figures stand at clearly different depths.

### 3d. Destruction that looks real, not "AI slop"

- **MEDIUM (practitioners).** Generators have no rigid-body physics; they reproduce patterns. A vague prompt gives a "screensaver" look. Describing the *specific material and its failure* works better ("the beam buckles" rather than "debris flies"). Short prompts beat stuffed ones. Plan on 2–3 rerolls per physics shot, and a complex multi-object success rate of about 50 % in one vendor's tests. ([Hailuo field notes](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/simulating-impact-prompting-debris-gravity); [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1stsfx4/snapshots/2026-04-24T22%3A04%3A53.789389Z); [invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-accurately-do-ai-video-generators-simulate-physics/); [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/ai-video-physics-realism-whats-fixed-and-whats-broken))
- **STRONG.** Models that look realistic still fail physics (Physics-IQ, above). The best fix is to **stage destruction so the model needs little physics**:
  - one material;
  - one direction of motion;
  - a camera close to the detail;
  - the cause off-screen or implied;
  - the rest left to the edit and the sound.

**WEAK (inference) on stagings, most reliable first:**

**Bullets through a wooden door (k26):**
1. **From inside the room, camera locked on the door.** Beams of hard daylight punch through new holes one after another, so dusty light shafts appear across the dark room. The light *is* the bullet hole, and light shafts are something the model renders well. Small splinters puff from each hole towards the camera. Two or three holes, not twenty.
2. **The reaction.** A masked robber presses flat against the wall beside the door, and splinters spit past his shoulder. The destruction stays small at the frame edge, and the performance carries it.
3. **The aftermath.** A slow push-in on the door riddled with holes, smoke drifting through the light beams, then it swings open on its own with a creak.
4. **Avoid:** a wide shot with the door exploding into planks, any axe or tool stroke (repeated tool contact is where it looked fake), and splinters flying in every direction.

**Barrels off a truck / down stairs:**
1. **One barrel, close.** A single barrel rolls off the tailgate in the foreground, at low angle, and the camera tracks it a short way. One rigid object on one path is the most reliable.
2. **The aftermath plus one detail.** The barrels are already down, one is split, and liquor glugs out across the cobbles, with the camera low in the spreading puddle. Liquid spreading on a surface is a strong pattern for these models; tumbling multi-body physics is not.
3. **The reaction.** The driver in the cab glances at the mirror, which shows the barrels bouncing away, small and soft in the reflection.
4. **Down stairs:** shoot from the bottom step looking up, with **one** barrel thumping down two steps towards the lens, then cut. Don't ask for a cascade of many barrels.

**The safe from the wall:**
1. **Through the hole, from inside the wall, looking out.** The chain is hooked on and goes taut, the plaster cracks in one line, and dust trickles. Then **cut** to the truck pulling away, then to a wide of the empty hole. The pull happens in the cut.
2. **The blown door instead of the whole safe.** Close on the safe door, a puff of smoke from the hinges, and the door sags open a few inches. Small, one part, slow.
3. **The aftermath.** A clean, neat rectangular hole in the wallpaper, the dangling chain swinging, and a robber dusting off his gloves to camera. That is a joke as well as an image.
4. **A practical action.** The robbers simply wheel the safe out on a sack truck, straining, ironically calm. It needs no destruction physics at all.

- **WEAK (inference).** For all three, the **sound does half the work**: splinter cracks, wood thuds, a groaning chain. A short clip on the beat with a hard sound effect reads as violent where a long clip of AI debris reads as slop. These are good candidates for a flurry (§1).

## 4. Gestures that match specific lyrics

- **MEDIUM-STRONG.** Rap performers use beat gestures most, then iconic gestures (miming the line's image), pointing gestures, and gestures aimed at the viewer. ([Chodakova & Podhorná-Polická](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1686116/en?lang=en))
- **WEAK (inference).** Sarcasm reads through an iconic gesture done **too politely or too grandly**, a beat of stillness, then a look into the lens. A masked face can't smirk, so the irony goes into a **head tilt** and **timing**: the gesture lands, then he holds still for half a beat. Every idea below is staged for a locked camera with the mask kept face-on (§3a).

### "don't just eat cake" (the closing line, after "it's time to give, not just to take, but for heaven's sake")

1. **The refused slice.** He holds a dainty slice of cake on a china plate at chest height and lifts it to the mouth hole of the mask. He stops, tilts his head to the lens, and slowly turns the plate round to offer it to the camera, to the viewer. "Don't just eat it: give it." It closes the give/take couplet.
2. **The Marie Antoinette.** One gloved hand on his chest, the other pointing with a cake fork like a lecturer, a tiny wagging fork on "don't". He finishes by dropping the fork onto the plate with a clink. The cake line is the famous "let them eat cake" misquote, and the posh gesture sends it up.
3. **Cutting it fairly.** A big tiered cake on the table. On "for heaven's sake" he looks up to the ceiling; on "don't just eat cake" he cuts it into many even slices with a knife and slides one towards camera. That is redistribution as a gesture. It also suits the camera locked over the table, hands only, with the mask framed above.

### "hungry people feed a revolution"

1. **The empty plate to the raised fist.** He taps an empty tin plate twice on the beat on "hungry people". On "feed a revolution" the hand that held it rises into a slow, deliberately theatrical clenched fist. One continuous motion, no turn.
2. **The bread roll as a grenade.** He tosses a bread roll up and catches it like a grenade, then mimes pulling a pin with his teeth at the mask's mouth hole. A deadpan look to camera.
3. **Spoon-feeding.** He spoon-feeds an imaginary person below frame, mock-tender. On "revolution" he flips the spoon and raps it on the table like a gavel.

### "the right and the left both witness the theft"

1. **The two hands.** He holds both gloved hands out at shoulder height, palms up like weighing scales, and his head turns slightly to each: right, then left. On "theft" both hands snap shut together, and he opens one to show it is empty. A pickpocket's trick. Keep the head turns small.
2. **Hands over eyes.** He covers the right eye-hole with one glove ("the right"), then the left eye-hole ("the left"), then makes a slow "I saw that" point into the lens with two fingers to his own eyes and then out. **Caution:** a hand over the face risks the mask drifting (§3a). Use it only in a close, slow clip, or split the line across two cuts.
3. **Two witnesses.** Two other robbers stand frame left and frame right, each with a hand over their mouth in mock shock. The rapper in the middle opens his coat to show it lined with banknotes, and shrugs. It plays the line as a group gag and keeps any one head from moving much.

- **WEAK (inference) on the cut.** These lines are punchlines, so per §2 they go to **performance shots held for the whole bar** (2.6 s, or the whole 6 s clip for the closing line). The final "don't just eat cake" is the outro's hero shot: hold it, then cut to the logo or to black.

# Web research: the film-still look

## 3. Making it look like a high-budget film still in Nano Banana 2

Nano Banana 2 is reported to be Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. **MEDIUM** ([AI/ML API](https://aimlapi.com/blog/banana-prompt-guid); the Google Cloud guide names "Gemini 3.1 Flash Image Preview" separately).

### 3a. Google's own guidance (STRONG: primary)

From the [Google Cloud prompting guide](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana), the [Google Developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/how-to-prompt-gemini-2-5-flash-image-generation-for-the-best-results/) and the [Google Keyword tips](https://blog.google/products/gemini/image-generation-prompting-tips/):

- **Prose, not keyword lists:** "Describe the scene, don't just list keywords." The formula is subject + action + location/context + composition + style, in full sentences.
- **Think like a photographer:** name the shot type, lens, aperture, angle ("low-angle shot with a shallow depth of field (f/1.8)", "wide-angle lens"), and name the light ("chiaroscuro lighting with harsh, high contrast"). A film-stock or camera-body phrase sets the look ("as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy").
- **Materials, not categories:** "Don't just ask for a suit jacket; ask for 'navy blue tweed.'" This is directly useful for the suits: name the cloth (chalk-stripe worsted, herringbone, barathea), the knit of the balaclava (chunky cable, fine rib) and the glove leather (black kid, oiled pigskin).
- **Positive framing:** say "an empty street", not "no cars". Negative words can summon the thing.
- **References:** up to 14 reference images. The pattern is [reference] + [relationship instruction] + [new scenario], naming each image's job ("using the attached image as the costume…").
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 is supported natively. With several input images the output takes the ratio of the **last** image, so attach a 16:9 image last, or state the ratio.
- **Text:** put the exact words in quotes and specify the font; the model "may occasionally misspell words".
- **Consistency drifts** over repeated edits: start fresh with the full description rather than chaining edits.

### 3b. Practitioner guidance (MEDIUM/WEAK: secondary)

- **The first ~15 words get the most weight; end with the output spec ("16:9").** Use one style family and one camera phrase only. Stacking camera bodies or styles makes "a smeared hybrid". **MEDIUM** ([Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide)).
- **"Realistic" is a feeling, not an instruction:** replace it with a camera, a lens and a lighting direction. State the image's purpose (e.g. "a still frame from a feature film"). **MEDIUM** (Fliki).
- **Slop triggers:** "hyperrealistic", "8K", "ultra-detailed", "masterpiece" push toward a rendered, video-game look. The slop look itself is flat, over-lit, waxy skin, teal-and-orange, symmetrical. **MEDIUM**: several blogs plus a January 2026 Reddit thread agree, but nobody ran a controlled test ([Cloudinary](https://cloudinary.com/guides/image-generation/how-to-make-ai-images-look-real); [alimagelab, 2026](https://alimagelab.blogspot.com/2026/01/why-your-ai-images-look-fake-and-how-to.html)). Add **WEAK**: "cinematic", "epic", "stunning", "dramatic lighting" said without a source, and "moody" also drift toward the glossy average. Name a source of light instead.
- **Hands:** anchor the anatomy (state which fingers are extended, show the thumb), minimise occlusion, avoid motion blur near the hands, and name specific failures rather than "bad anatomy". **WEAK** (vendor blogs: [Curify](https://www.curify-ai.com/blog/10-prompting-tips-nano-banana); Sider, which blocked fetching). Add **WEAK**: **gloves help.** A black leather glove reads as a single shape with fewer creases to get wrong. Give each hand **one** job ("the right hand grips the microphone; the left hand points with only the index finger extended, the other three fingers curled and the thumb on top").
- **No source found on foreshortening.** **WEAK** suggestion: describe it optically, not anatomically: "the pointing hand is very close to the lens and appears large, slightly soft, filling the lower-left of the frame, while his masked face behind it is sharp". The model knows the lens physics behind "24mm lens a foot from the hand".

### 3c. Applied to this job (WEAK: synthesis, test it)

- **Body caught mid-gesture:** write the *peak*, not the motion: "frozen at the top of a downward chop, palm flat, the elbow high", "mid-bounce, both heels off the floor". Motion blur is allowed only on the coat tails or a swinging mic cord, never on hands or the head.
- **One motivated light and deep blacks:** name the single source and where it sits: "lit only by a single bare bulb hanging just above and in front of him; everything beyond the bulb's reach falls to near-black; the bulb's glare is visible at the top of the frame". Then a positive line for the blacks: "the shadows are deep and inky, with no fill light".
- **Film feel:** one stock or camera phrase (e.g. "shot on 35mm motion-picture film, fine grain, slight halation round the bulb"), an anamorphic or spherical lens choice, and the sentence "a frame from a feature film".
- **Stopping readable fake text:** describe signs by shape and light only ("a hand-painted sign, out of focus, its lettering unreadable"), keep them soft behind the subject, or turn them away. Or give a real short word in quotes if text must be read. Don't write "no text" (positive framing).
- **Person very close to a wide lens in 16:9:** "a close, low, wide-angle shot from about a metre away, 24mm, the lens at chest height looking slightly up; his body fills the left two-thirds of the 16:9 frame; the room stretches away behind him in sharp perspective; the five other members of the gang stand behind him, smaller, in a loose line". Placing the lead off-centre fights the symmetrical slop default.
- **Length and structure:** about 80–150 words of prose in this order: subject + action (the gesture at its peak) → place → composition and lens → light → texture/stock → 16:9. More than that and the camera and light lines get diluted (**WEAK**, consistent with the guidance that early tokens dominate).
- **Reference for costume consistency:** say exactly what the image is for: "Using the attached image only for the masks, suits and gloves of the six men; new place, new pose, new light". Attach the 16:9 image **last** so the ratio holds. Because masked faces carry no identity, the costume reference is doing all the consistency work, so keep wording identical across prompts for the costume itself (Fliki: "reuse the exact same physical description").
