=== SHOT v12 ===
A music-video still from a 1920s Prohibition film: a huge black cast-iron cylinder printing press thunders in a whitewashed cellar, its rollers flinging sheets of green banknotes up into the air, hundreds of notes fluttering and tumbling everywhere. A robber called BOTTLE, three-quarters toward the camera, feeds a stack of blank paper into the press with black leather gloves; he wears a brown tweed suit, collar turned up, and a chunky hand-knitted bottle-green rib wool hood covering his entire head and neck, smooth knit where the face would be, a large white wool ring stitched round the face from brow to chin, one small rounded red felt triangle high over his left eye, on the right side of the picture at about two o'clock. His mask and suit are exactly as in the attached reference image; he is BOTTLE, the only man in this shot. Hard noon daylight slants down through a grated pavement window, lighting the flying notes. Low side angle, 35mm lens, fine film grain, ochre walls, oily brass gears. The banknote engraving is soft and blurred.
WHY: The line "it prints all the dollars" is shown literally with one robber working a press that sprays money into the air, in cold daylight to set it apart from the red and fire shots.

=== SHOT c03 ===
A music-video still: six masked robbers crammed shoulder to shoulder in a low red-brick basement round a black iron printing press, all facing straight into the lens, gloved fists punched up, chanting. One bare bulb with a red glass shade hangs above them, the only light, throwing deep red over the brick and hard shadows under the arches. Each wears a hand-knitted rib wool hood covering head and neck, smooth knit where the face would be, a large white wool ring stitched round the face from brow to chin, and one small rounded red felt triangle high over his left eye, on the right of the picture at about two o'clock. All six men are in the shot, PLUM, BOTTLE, CRIMSON, MARINE, RUST and SILVER, and their masks and suits are exactly as in the attached reference image. Left to right: PLUM, plum hood, chalk-striped mustard suit; BOTTLE, bottle green, brown tweed; CRIMSON, crimson, charcoal pinstripe, grey fedora; MARINE, marine blue, cream linen; RUST, rust-gold, black suit, white tie; SILVER, silver-grey, oxblood suit, bow tie. Black gloves, collars up. Low fisheye lens, grainy colour film.
WHY: "Debasement in da basement" is a whole-crew chant in a literal basement round the press, framed fisheye from below under one red bulb.

He wears a cream linen suit with two-tone shoes, cuffs down over the wrists, collar up, and a chunky hand-knitted marine-blue rib wool hood covering head and neck, smooth knit where the face would be, a large white wool ring stitched round the face from brow to chin, one small rounded red felt triangle high over his left eye, on the right side of the picture at about two o'clock. His mask and suit are exactly as in the attached reference image; he is MARINE, the only man in this shot. The fire backlights him with a hot orange rim; his front falls into cool shadow. Close, 24mm wide lens at chest height, grainy film.
WHY: "Printer is burning" plays literally behind a calm rapping MARINE, lit only by the fire from behind.

=== SHOT d01 ===
A music-video still: a scarred oak table in a cellar counting room piled high with bricks of green banknotes, each brick bound with a red rubber band, the stacks leaning and spilling. A broad robber called PLUM sits behind it and fans one brick of notes out toward the camera like a magician spreading cards, the notes splayed in a sharp arc close to the lens, his other gloved hand flat on the pile. He wears a chalk-striped mustard-yellow three-piece suit, black leather gloves, collar up, and a chunky hand-knitted plum rib wool hood covering head and neck, smooth knit where the face would be, a large white wool ring stitched round the face from brow to chin, one small rounded red felt triangle high over his left eye, on the right side of the picture at about two o'clock. His mask and suit are exactly as in the attached reference image; he is PLUM, the only man in this shot. A single green-shaded banker's lamp low on the table lights the money from below and to the side; teal-painted walls fall into shadow. 50mm lens at table height, shallow focus on the fanned notes, soft grain.
WHY: "Stacks and stacks of elastic cash" is a literal table of rubber-banded bricks, with PLUM's card-trick fan giving the line its swagger.
