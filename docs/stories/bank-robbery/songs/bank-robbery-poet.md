# The Bank Robbery — punk-poet sweep

**Kai, 21 October 2026:** ska "is not the right style… One style I can't get out of my head is
just like John Cooper Clarke, just like spoken poetry… with a kind of vague, occasional orchestral
instrument flourish." Then: five to ten style variations, chosen with a quick research pass.

**Words and cues:** the **poet cues** block in [`score.md`](./score.md) § Lyrics. The words are Kai's
and the same as the ska block. The front man's label is `punk poet, breathless deadpan rant`,
repeated identically. The chorus keeps a **small gang shouting back**, because the gang was what Kai
loved in the ska take (Claude's default; Kai didn't choose).

## The rules this sweep runs on

- **Here, "performance poetry" is the right word.** The house rule bans `spoken word` from every
  box because it summons a performed poem. That is a narration rule: for narration we wanted a man
  *talking*. This time a performed poem is the target, so the pool word does the casting.
- **The voice is a translated reference** (`suno-voices.md` §2c): John Cooper Clarke described by
  scene, era and voice, never named. That sentence is identical in all six, so only the music moves.
- **No `rapid-fire`.** Musical speed words pull the voice into rap (`suno-v6.md` §8b), so the speed is
  described as a `breathless rant`, and `rap, hip hop` are excluded.
- **Melody makes him sing.** A sustained pitched bed hands the voice a key, so the orchestra is
  written as **events between lines**, not a pad under them. The one exception is style F, where
  the strings sustain as dread, which tests that rule.
- **Bold, not meek:** apart from the voice sentence, every style's production is its own. Each
  Exclude list bans the other styles' sounds.

## The six styles

Research: a quick agent pass (11 searches) on the traditions around punk and performance poets,
including John Cooper Clarke's own Martin Hannett records, Sleaford Mods, Kae Tempest's *Brand New
Ancients* (poetry with violin, cello and tuba), Peter and the Wolf (narrator and orchestra taking
turns), film-noir voiceover, and Scott Walker's late string clusters. Every practitioner claim
about spoken delivery in Suno was blog-grade and untested.

| Style | The idea | Tradition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E-turns** | the voice alone; the full orchestra crashes in only in the gaps | Peter and the Wolf |
| **E-mcr79** | cold drum machine, echoing bass, delay haze, one string-quartet chord on punchlines | Clarke's own 1978–80 records |
| **E-loop** | one two-bar loop that never changes, voice louder than the music, a tuba blurt | Sleaford Mods |
| **E-noir** | brushes, upright bass, smoky room, muted trumpet sting on each reveal | film-noir confession |
| **E-chamber** | violin, cello, tuba and a floor tom, short dry stabs | *Brand New Ancients* |
| **E-dread** | near-silence, huge dissonant string clusters, timpani shocks | late Scott Walker |

Dropped from the shortlist: music-hall funk (too close to the Ian Dury ska voice Kai is moving away
from) and bedroom two-step (a UK-garage beat would pull the voice into rapping).

### E-turns

Style:

```
British punk performance poetry with a symphony orchestra taking turns: the voice alone in a quiet hall, and only in the gaps between lines the full orchestra crashes in with a short fanfare, a pizzicato scurry or a timpani roll, then stops dead. The voice: a 1978 Salford punk poet, a breathless nasal Mancunian deadpan rant, machine-gun rhymes, dry and close to the mic; a small gang shouts the chorus back. Played completely straight.
```

