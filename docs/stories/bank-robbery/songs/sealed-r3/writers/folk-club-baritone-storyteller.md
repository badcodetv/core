I wrote one prompt for this experiment. It's in my own words from the plain brief, and I didn't reuse wording from earlier prompts or the listening pass.

**Where the ideas came from**
- **The Best one page.** Suno's page only shows the tags "psych folk, funk". They tell you nothing about how the take actually sounds, so I took the music line from the listen description in the brief instead. That line is 29 words and doesn't mention sitar, psych or anything Indian. ([Suno page](https://suno.com/song/024b82e8-378c-47bd-bf73-bb5eafea2ed5))
- **Describing the voice.** Two guides recommend describing the singer as their own thing (range, tone, diction, wobble) and not leaving the genre to imply the voice. If you don't name the voice type, Suno picks one, and often badly. So the Style box puts the singer first, then a separate short music line. ([folk tenor guide](https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/how-to-make-suno-preserve-a-natural-folk-tenor-suno-v5-5-voice-guide), [Roo's prompt list](https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/the-suno-ai-prompts-list-that-actually-works-2026-genre-artist-style-and-use-case))
- **The kind of singer.** I based him on real English folk-club singers. Fred Jordan's lightly ornamented singing marked him as a storyteller. Roy Harris and Harry Boardman came up through the clubs from the late 1950s, often singing unaccompanied. That's why I asked for an older, untrained man "telling a tale" rather than performing. ([Harry Boardman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Boardman), [Roy Harris, Sing Out!](https://singout.org/english-folk-singer-roy-harris-passes/))
- **Avoiding shouting.** The chorus direction asks for the room to hum and sing along, not shout. That's how a folk club sounds, and it avoids the gang-shout and screaming risk. The long held words are tagged to waver.
- **Settings.** I used v6 with Variety off, Max Mode off, a male voice, style influence 75 and weirdness 25. I set the length to 190 s, the top of the allowed 170–190 s range, because Kai's lyrics are 428 words and the Best one take was only 170 s. Even at 190 s, Suno may cut off the last verse, so check that first when you listen.

Kai's words are unchanged, including the curly apostrophe in "heaven’s". I only added tags in square brackets.
