**What the research found**
- **Suno v6 is new.** It came out on September 9, 2026, in three versions: v6, v6-wild and v6-mini. All three can make songs up to 8 minutes long, so this full set of lyrics can fit in one Create. There's a new "Variety" slider that changes how strongly v6 reworks your style prompt. Setting it to 0 keeps your style words intact. One early tester says it rewrites prompts into shorter, weaker ones, but Suno hasn't confirmed that. Suno also says v6 understands more of the words musicians use for vocals, instruments, structure and mood. ([Suno help: What's new in v6](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/13924801), [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/radar/suno-v6-everything-you-need-to-know-about-sunos-new-ai-music-model/), [AI Musicpreneur](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-tools-news/suno-v6-v6-wild-v6-mini-launch/))
- **Put the most important thing first and name the voice type.** Guides say the vocal style is one of the strongest controls you have, and that a detailed classical brief beats just writing "classical". ([Jack Righteous 2026 prompt guide](https://jackrighteous.com/ja/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/best-prompts-for-suno-ai-2026-guide-to-better-results), [Jack Righteous Mozart test](https://jackrighteous.com/es/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/testing-suno-classical-music-mozart-inspired-prompts), [Soundverse](https://www.soundverse.ai/blog/article/how-to-write-effective-suno-ai-prompts-1314)). I couldn't load the Mozart test page itself (the site blocked repeated requests), so that point comes from the search summary.
- **Where tags go.** Section tags go on their own line, and you can pair one with a delivery tag, for example `[Chorus] [Operatic]`. Useful lyric tags include [Operatic], [Vibrato], [Melisma], [Legato], [Staccato], [Choir], [Head Voice] and [Chest Voice]. Instruments and the sense of space (orchestra, harpsichord, church organ, harp, cathedral, hall reverb) belong in the style prompt. Things to exclude go at the end of the style prompt. ([HookGenius metatag list](https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-metatags-complete-list/))
- **These are community tips, not official rules.** One guide's test used "dramatic operatic soprano solo with heavy vibrato, grand Italian opera, reverb". Another mentions an [opera solo] tag and a spaced "s a tb" tag for stacked choir parts. Guides agree that tags are suggestions, not guaranteed commands, and I found no official Suno list of opera tags. ([Jack Righteous special tags](https://jackrighteous.com/es-us/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/how-to-use-suno-ai-special-tags), [FrankX playbook](https://frankx.ai/guides/suno-prompt-playbook), [apipass](https://apipass.dev/blogs/7-pro-suno-prompt-hack), [Scribd cheatsheet](https://www.scribd.com/document/766592783/suno-ai-prompting-cheatsheet), [ai-made cheat sheet](https://ai-made.beehiiv.com/p/the-suno-cheat-sheet-make-great-ai-songs-every-time), [Medium guide](https://medium.com/@abhisheksd2003/the-ultimate-suno-ai-prompt-guide-with-clear-tested-examples-2d827ffe8b3a), [r/SunoAI](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/SunoAI/comments/1dg88p8/super_suno_prompts))
- **What it means for these lyrics.** The stretched words ("FREEEEEEEZING", "sooooo", "waaaay") work naturally as held high notes and runs, and "burrrrrrrrring / occurrrrrrrring" works as a rolled operatic R. Since tags are only suggestions, I avoided [Shouted], [Belted] and anything rap-like so the takes stay within Jack's rules.
