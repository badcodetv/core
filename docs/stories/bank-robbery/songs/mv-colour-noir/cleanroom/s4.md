=== SHOT safe1 ===
The instant a small safe leaves a wall, seen from across a narrow back alley at night in 1928, the camera at chest height against the opposite wall with a rusted iron drainpipe cut off by the left edge of the frame. An egg-yolk yellow sedan with black mudguards stands at a hard angle on the right, its rear wheels spinning on wet-dark cobbles and throwing thick grey tyre smoke, lit deep red by its two small tail lamps. From its rear axle a heavy chain runs dead straight, rigid as a bar, across the alley and into a ragged hole in an oxblood brick wall with a flaking bottle-green painted band. The safe, a squat cast-iron box in chipped forest-green enamel with a brass dial, hangs tilted in the air a foot outside the hole with the chain wrapped around it, while bricks, mortar and pieces of green wall fall around it through a cloud of pale dust. One bare amber bulb in a tin shade above a wooden back door, high on the left, is the only other light, so the dust glows amber on one side and red on the other, and the far end of the alley drops into true black. A man leans far out of the sedan's rear window toward the camera, big enough in the frame to read clearly, dark overcoat collar turned up, both leather-gloved hands gripping the door, his head turned back over his shoulder to watch the hole so that the front of his mask faces us at three-quarters. His whole head is inside a hand-knitted balaclava of marine-blue wool, slightly uneven, with a ribbed neck tucked into the collar and continuous knit running unbroken across the place where eyes, nose and mouth would be. One thin ring of cream wool, chain-stitched by hand, circles the whole face from brow to chin. High inside that ring, above the eye nearer the camera and pushed up close against the cream stitching, sits one small triangular patch of solid red felt with rounded corners, the whole patch filled with flat red cloth and its hand-cut edge sewn down with tiny black running stitches. The rest of the ring's interior, including its centre, is bare blue knit. Falling bricks are slightly blurred, while the chain, safe and mask are sharp. Rich saturated colour film with visible grain and hard shadows on the brick, the picture running edge to edge across the whole 16:9 frame.
WHY: Keeps the alley, angle and yellow-red-green colour Jack liked, and brings the man further forward so the marine-blue mask's ring and solid off-centre felt triangle are large enough to read as a period hand-made piece.

=== SHOT safe4 ===
Inside the back of a fleeing 1928 sedan, the camera low behind the rear seat's far corner and looking toward a small oval rear window, the interior in near darkness: buttoned plum velvet upholstery, curved brass grab handles, a brown blind with a tasselled cord swinging sideways on the left. A heavily built man kneels backward on the rear seat in a dark overcoat with its collar turned up, the right side of the frame cutting one shoulder, both leather-gloved hands spread flat against the glass. He has twisted his head back over his shoulder so the front of his mask faces the camera at three-quarters. His whole head is inside a hand-knitted balaclava of silver-grey wool, slightly uneven, with a ribbed neck disappearing into the collar and continuous knit running unbroken across the place where eyes, nose and mouth would be. One thin ring of black wool, chain-stitched by hand, circles the whole face from brow to chin. High inside that ring, above the eye nearer the camera and pushed up close against the black stitching, sits one small triangular patch of solid red felt with rounded corners, the whole patch filled with flat red cloth and its hand-cut edge sewn down with tiny black running stitches. The rest of the ring's interior, including its centre, is bare grey knit. The only light comes from outside: pulses of orange pass through the window, strike the plum velvet and the door panel, and bounce up onto the grey wool and the gloved knuckles, so the mask glows warm against the dark car. Through the oval, a taut chain runs back from below the sill to a cast-iron safe skidding along a wet waterfront road, tilted up on one corner and throwing a fan of orange sparks from the corner that touches the ground. Behind it, low harbour fog has swallowed everything except the two large soft white headlamp discs of a pursuing car and, higher and off to one side, the small green glow of a dock lamp on a post. The rear glass is old and slightly rippled, with a crack across one corner, so sparks and headlamps bend a little as they pass through it. Focus sits on the worn wool of the mask and the stitched felt; the view through the window is softer and blurred by the car's shaking. Warm orange against deep plum and black, cold white only inside the window, coarse film grain, a full picture reaching every edge of the frame.
WHY: Keeps the plum interior and the safe sparking through the oval window, and turns the man's head toward the camera so the silver-grey mask's ring and felt triangle catch the orange bounce light and can be seen.
