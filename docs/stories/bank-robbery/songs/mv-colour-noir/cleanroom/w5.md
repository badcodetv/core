=== SHOT 1 ===
The instant a small safe leaves a wall, seen from across a narrow back alley at night in 1928, the camera at chest height against the opposite wall with a rusted iron drainpipe cut off by the left edge of the frame. An egg-yolk yellow sedan with black mudguards is parked at a hard angle on the right, its rear wheels spinning on the cobbles, thick grey tyre smoke lit deep ruby by its two small tail lamps. From its rear axle a heavy chain runs dead straight, rigid as a bar, across the alley and into a ragged hole in an oxblood brick wall painted with a flaking bottle-green band. The safe, a squat cast-iron box in chipped forest-green enamel with a brass dial, hangs in the air a foot outside the hole, tilted, chain wrapped twice around it, with bricks, mortar and a section of green wall still falling around it and hanging in a cloud of pale dust. The only other light is one bare amber bulb in a tin shade above a back door, high on the left, so the dust glows amber on one side and ruby on the other, and the far end of the alley falls away to true black. A figure in a plain black knitted balaclava leans out of the sedan's rear window, looking back at the hole with both arms hooked over the door. Falling bricks are slightly blurred, the chain and safe are sharp. Rich, saturated colour film with visible grain, hard shadows on the brick, and the picture running edge to edge across the whole 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: The chain snaps out of frame to the right as the safe is yanked away and the camera whip pans with it, landing on the next moving shot.
WHY: Everyone imagines the dragging, but the frozen second of a wall giving birth to a safe is the funniest and most violent image of the lot, and it has all the light it needs from a tail lamp and one bulb.

=== SHOT 2 ===
The camera is bolted almost at road level, a hand's width above dry granite setts on a wide city avenue at night, facing the way the car is fleeing, and the safe fills the lower left of the frame so close that its corner is cropped by the edge. It is a dented cast-iron box in dull forest-green enamel, on its side and mid-bounce, one corner still grinding on the stone. Where that iron corner touches the granite a hard spray of white-orange sparks fans backward past the lens, some of them long streaks crossing the frame, and they throw a flickering orange pool on the setts directly beneath. A chain, shaking and slightly slack, climbs from the safe up the centre of the frame to the rear bumper of an egg-yolk yellow 1928 sedan thirty feet ahead, small and off to the right, leaning hard into a turn with its body rolled and its front wheels cut over. The main light comes from behind the camera: the two headlamps of a pursuing police car, out of sight, rake low along the road and throw the safe's enormous black shadow and the shadow of the chain forward up the avenue, right to the sedan's rear wheels. Either side, shopfronts in lacquer red, plum and mustard smear horizontally into streaks of colour, with rows of bare yellow bulbs on a theatre canopy drawn out into lines, all their signs too blurred to read. The safe's edge in the foreground is the sharpest thing in the picture. Heavy film grain, deep dark between the lights, colour filling the frame to all four edges with nothing around it.
WAY OUT: The safe bounces up and fills the lens with dark green iron, and the next shot starts on a dark shape of similar size moving out of the frame.
WHY: Giving the safe the foreground and the car the distance makes the loot the monster of the shot, and a shadow thrown by unseen police headlamps puts the cops in the picture without showing them.

=== SHOT 3 ===
Seen from the catwalk of a rooftop water tower, looking steeply down over a corroded iron rail that crosses the lower right corner close to the lens and out of focus, a 1920s city an hour after sundown, with the last dull violet still in the sky off the top edge of the frame and only a sliver of it visible. Far below, a black canal cuts diagonally through crowded blocks of uneven height, and a narrow iron girder bridge crosses it. On that bridge a car no bigger than a grain of rice, its yellow paint only just readable under a passing lamp, is dragging a single thin bright thread of orange sparks that starts two streets back, follows the curve of the road around a corner and over the bridge, and fades to dull red as it cools. The canal holds a broken double of the thread: below the bridge the sparks and the lamps are shattered into short shaking bars on the water, dimmer than their sources. Behind the thread come three pairs of small white headlamps, closing, spaced unevenly. The surrounding rooftops are mostly dark, tar and water tanks and chimney stacks, with a few long skylights glowing amber, one rooftop painted in faded jade, and a row of bulb signs seen from behind so they show only as scaffolding and rows of dots, no letters. Thin coal smoke lies across one block and catches the light of a street below it. Nothing else in the city has noticed. A long-lens view with flattened layers of buildings, deep plum shadows, saturated film colour, a fine grain, filling the entire 16:9 frame without any margin.
WAY OUT: A chimney stack on a nearer rooftop slides across the lens as the camera drifts along the catwalk, and the cut hides inside that passing black.
WHY: At this scale the chase turns into a single line of light drawn across a whole sleeping city, which is the rare picture of it that nobody would expect after a run of close-ups.

=== SHOT 4 ===
Inside the back of a fleeing 1928 sedan, the camera low on the floor behind the front seats and looking back and slightly up, with the interior in near darkness: buttoned plum velvet upholstery, a brass grab handle, a tasselled blind swinging sideways on its cord. A heavily built figure in a plain black knitted balaclava kneels backward on the rear seat with both gloved hands flat on the glass of the small oval rear window, seen from behind and in partial profile, one shoulder cut by the frame edge. Their face and hands are lit only from outside, in pulses of orange that come through the window and bounce off the velvet. Through the oval, small and centred on nothing in particular, a taut chain runs back from below the sill to a cast-iron safe skidding along a waterfront road, tilted up on one corner, throwing a fan of sparks to one side as the road bends. Behind the safe, low fog off the harbour has swallowed everything except two large soft white headlamp discs of a police car and, higher up and off to one side, the faint green glow of a dock lamp. The rear glass is old and slightly rippled, with a crack across one corner, so the sparks and the headlamps bend a little as they pass through it. Sharp focus sits on the worn wool of the balaclava and the gloved knuckles; the view through the window is softer and blurred by the car's shaking. Warm orange against deep plum and black, with cold white only inside the window, coarse film grain and a full picture reaching every edge of the frame.
WAY OUT: The passenger's dark shoulder swings across the lens as the car lurches, wiping the frame to black for a clean cut to anywhere.
WHY: The most thrilling view of a dragged safe may be from the person who just realised it is still attached, framed like a small bright cinema screen in a very dark room.
