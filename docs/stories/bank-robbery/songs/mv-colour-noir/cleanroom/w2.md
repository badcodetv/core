=== SHOT 1 ===
Seen from the roof of a coal tug moored out in the middle of a wide black river at night, about four metres above the water, with the tug's soot-black funnel and a loop of wet hawser cropped hard against the left edge of the frame. Off to the right a colossal riveted steel arch bridge sweeps upward and leaves through the top of the picture, standing on one stone pier as big as a church. In the cold shadow beneath that arch, on the far bank, three long 1930s sedans, one oxblood, one cream, one bottle green, are parked nose-in on a cobbled quay, and their round tungsten headlamps meet in a single small warm pool of light. Inside that pool, tiny against all that iron, stand five people in sharp dark suits and hats: a very tall thin man with an upright bass, a stout older man with a trumpet, a young man with a clarinet, a grey-haired woman behind a small drum kit, and a broad woman at a period microphone. Thin river mist makes the headlamp beams visible as they cross the cobbles and die within a few metres. Behind the bridge a prohibition-era city climbs in uneven steps, some blocks entirely dark, others strung with bulb signs and coated neon tubes in ruby red, uranium green, pale gold and midnight blue, every sign too far away to read. The river takes those lights and breaks them into long trembling horizontal bars that run toward the camera, dimmer than their sources, with true black water between them. The picture runs to all four edges of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: The tug's funnel slides across the lens as the boat swings on its mooring, wiping to black and into any shot that opens on a dark shape leaving the frame.
WHY: Five people and a pinprick of headlamp under an enormous bridge gives the band the scale of a legend and shows the whole coloured city doing its reflecting at once.

=== SHOT 2 ===
The lens sits a hand's width above the river at the very foot of an old stone slipway, where small ripples lap over the first wet step and one blurred ripple crosses the bottom of the frame. The slipway climbs away from us beneath the low, rust-stained iron belly of a bridge, its flaking paint a faded oxblood. At the top of the slope two 1930s cars are parked side by side, aimed straight down at us, their big bowl headlamps blazing warm white directly behind the band and flaring into the lens. So the five musicians are backlit: edges of hot light around a hat brim, a suit shoulder, the curve of an upright bass held by a very tall thin man, the bell of a stout older man's trumpet, a young man's clarinet, the rim of a cymbal behind which a grey-haired woman sits at a small drum kit. Nearest the water, a broad woman in her forties stands at a chrome spring-hung period microphone with her head lifted and her mouth closed, drawing a breath, the light catching one silver streak in her black hair and the deep plum wool of her double-breasted jacket. Their dark reflections stretch down the wet stones toward the camera, sharp in the standing puddles and smeared on the damp stone between, and the two headlamps run down the slope as long gold streaks that end at our feet. At the far left of the frame the open river carries broken bars of ruby and green from a city we cannot see. Grain sits in the deep shadow, and the image fills the frame edge to edge.
WAY OUT: A ripple rises over the lens and the frame dissolves in black water, cutting on the splash to the next shot's wet road or spray.
WHY: Shooting into the headlamps from water level doubles the band in the wet stone and turns the backlight into the whole picture.

=== SHOT 3 ===
A long brick vault running under the approach to a river bridge, seen at standing eye height from behind two parked 1930s cars, whose rounded black rear ends and spare wheels fill the bottom corners of the frame, their plates blank. A man's hatted head and one shoulder stand in silhouette at the lower left, and his cigarette smoke curls up through the beams. Twenty metres ahead the headlamps strike the five musicians from the front and throw their shadows enormous across the curved wall behind them. The wall was painted long ago in sea-green and mustard, now peeling in big continents, and the colour glows wherever the hard light lands. The shadows are razor-edged and three storeys tall: the neck of an upright bass bends over the crown of the vault, a trumpet the length of a bus reaches across the bricks, and a drum kit's cymbal hangs overhead like a dark moon. The people casting them look small and very sharply dressed in the pool of light: a very tall thin bass player in charcoal chalk-stripe, a stout older trumpeter in a tan three-piece, a young clarinettist in a narrow navy suit, a grey-haired woman at a small drum kit in a burgundy waistcoat, and a broad woman in her forties with a silver streak in her black hair standing at a period microphone in deep plum, lips together, gathering breath. Off-centre at the far end, the vault opens through a tall arch onto the river, where a second arch beyond it frames a slot of night water full of gold and red light. Everything between the headlamp pool and that slot falls away into true brick-black. The image fills the whole frame, corner to corner.
WAY OUT: The man's shoulder steps across the lens and the shot cuts out of his dark coat into the next picture.
WHY: The band's shadows become the monster in the room, a giant dark mural in a coloured tunnel that nobody expects from five people and a couple of cars.

=== SHOT 4 ===
Close and long-lens, about an 85mm view from low beside the front wing of a parked car: a huge chrome bowl headlamp burns out of focus at the extreme right edge of the frame, cut in half by the frame, and it is the only light on the woman filling the left two-thirds of the picture. She is a broad woman in her forties at a period microphone, black hair set in hard waves with one silver streak running back from her temple, wearing a deep plum chalk-stripe jacket with wide peaked lapels and a dark red lip. The light rakes across her from the right and low down, showing the pores, the small lines around her eye, the weave of the wool, the scuffed chrome of the microphone and the rings on the hand wrapped around its stand; the left side of her face falls away into near black. Her mouth is closed, her nostrils slightly flared, her chest lifted, eyes shut: the held instant before the next line. The night is cold, and the breath she has just let go hangs in the headlamp beam as a lit drift of vapour beside her cheek. Far behind her, gone soft, a stone bridge pier rises and four out-of-focus musicians stand in the outer spill of the beam: a very tall thin shape with a bass, a stout shape with the glint of a trumpet, a slim young man with a clarinet, a grey-haired woman behind a small drum kit. Beyond them the river is pure blur, big overlapping discs of ruby, pale gold and bottle green hanging in the dark. The picture fills the frame to every edge.
WAY OUT: A headlamp clicks off and her face drops to black, or a match on the round headlamp carries the cut into the headlight of the next car.
WHY: After the wide shots this is one face and one lamp, and the breath lit in the beam tells you the song without a single open mouth.
