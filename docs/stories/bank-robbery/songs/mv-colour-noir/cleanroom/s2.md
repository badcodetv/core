=== SHOT bridge2 ===
The lens sits a hand's width above the river at the very foot of an old stone slipway, where small ripples lap over the first wet step and one blurred ripple crosses the bottom of the frame. The wet cobbled slipway climbs away from us beneath the low, rust-stained iron belly of a bridge, its riveted plates painted a faded oxblood that flakes to pale green. At the top of the slope two 1930s cars are parked side by side, aimed straight down at us, their big bowl headlamps blazing warm white behind the band and flaring into the lens. So the five musicians are backlit, with edges of hot light around a hat brim, a suit shoulder and the curve of an upright bass held by a very tall thin man, then the bell of a stout older man's trumpet, a young man's clarinet, and the rim of a cymbal behind which a grey-haired woman sits at a small drum kit. Nearest the water, closer to us than any of them, the singer stands at a chrome spring-hung period microphone: a broad man in his forties with a thick neck and a heavy jaw shadowed with stubble, bareheaded, his black hair combed straight back and going silver at one temple, his nose once broken and set crooked under heavy eyebrows. He wears a deep plum wool double-breasted suit jacket with wide lapels, a dark tie and big square shoulders, and he has his chin lifted and his lips together, drawing a breath through that crooked nose while the headlamp light rims his ear and the silver in his hair. The dark reflections of all five stretch down the stones toward the camera, sharp in the standing puddles and smeared on the damp cobbles between, and the two headlamps run down the slope as long gold streaks that end at our feet. Beyond the stone edge of the slipway, on the side away from the drum kit, the open river carries long broken bars of red and green light from a city out of sight, and the water between them is black. Grain sits in the deep shadow, and the image fills the frame edge to edge.
WHY: The same low slipway, backlit band and red and green river stay exactly as Jack liked them, while the singer becomes a man described by jaw, neck, nose and tailoring so nothing pulls the engine back to the last round's woman.

=== SHOT bridge3 ===
A long brick vault runs under the approach to a river bridge, seen at standing eye height from behind two parked 1930s cars, whose rounded black rear ends and spare wheels fill the bottom corners of the frame, their number plates bare metal. A man's hatted head and one shoulder stand in silhouette in the near corner beside one car, and his cigarette smoke curls up through the dark. Twenty metres ahead the cars' headlamps strike the five musicians from the front and throw their shadows enormous across the curved wall behind them. The wall was painted long ago in sea-green and mustard, now peeling in big continents to the bare brick, and the colour glows wherever the hard light lands. The shadows are razor-edged and three storeys tall: the neck of an upright bass bends over the crown of the vault, a trumpet the length of a bus reaches across the bricks, a cymbal hangs overhead like a dark moon, and the singer's shadow stands among them as a great block of shoulders beside the thin line of a microphone. The people casting them look small and very sharply dressed in the pool of light on the cobbles: a very tall thin bass player in charcoal chalk-stripe, a stout older trumpeter in a tan three-piece, a young clarinettist in a narrow navy suit, a grey-haired woman at a small drum kit in a burgundy waistcoat, and at a period microphone the singer, a broad man in his forties in a deep plum double-breasted suit, bareheaded, with black hair going silver at one temple, a broken nose and heavy eyebrows, his lips together and his chest filling as he gathers breath. Off-centre at the far end, the vault opens through a tall arch onto the river, where a second arch beyond it frames a slot of night water full of gold and red light. Everything between the headlamp pool and that slot falls away into true brick-black. The image fills the whole frame, corner to corner.
WHY: Jack had no note on this scene, so the vault, the giant shadows and the peeling sea-green and mustard wall hold, and the singer's change to a man also reshapes his shadow on the wall into a block of shoulders.

=== SHOT bridge4 ===
Close and long-lens, about an 85mm view from low beside the front wing of a parked car: a huge chrome bowl headlamp burns out of focus at the extreme edge of the frame, cut in half by that edge, and it is the only light on the man who fills the other two-thirds of the picture. He is the singer, a broad man in his forties at a period microphone, thick through the neck and heavy in the jaw, his black hair combed back hard with brilliantine and going silver at the temple nearest the lamp. His nose was broken long ago and set with a bump and a slight lean, and heavy black eyebrows sit low over his closed eyes. He wears a deep plum chalk-stripe jacket with wide peaked lapels over a white collar and a dark knotted tie. The light rakes across him from the lamp and from low down, showing the pores, the grey in his stubble, the small lines around his eye, the weave of the wool, the scuffed chrome of the microphone and the one worn gold ring on the big hand wrapped around its stand; the far side of his face falls away into near black. His mouth is closed, his nostrils slightly flared and his chest lifted: the held instant before the next line. The night is cold, and the breath he has just let go hangs in the headlamp beam as a thin lit drift of vapour beside his cheek. Far behind him, gone soft, a stone bridge pier rises and four out-of-focus musicians stand in the outer spill of the beam: a very tall thin shape with a bass, a stout shape with the glint of a trumpet, a slim young man with a clarinet, a grey-haired woman behind a small drum kit. Beyond them the river is pure blur, big overlapping discs of red, pale gold and bottle green hanging in the dark. The picture fills the frame to every edge.
WHY: The half-cut headlamp, the raking light, the breath and the coloured blur on the river all stay, and this close the broken nose, brow, stubble and big ringed hand carry the man Jack asked for.
