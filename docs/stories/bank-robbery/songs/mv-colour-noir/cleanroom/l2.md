=== SHOT l03 ===
A weathered, clean-shaven helmsman of about 60 with deep creases and a flat wool cap grips a spoked wheel low at his chest, in the open cockpit of a long 1920s rum-runner's speedboat racing dark through night fog. The lens is mounted on the foredeck, 35mm, slightly low, his face three-quarter and filling almost half the frame height. A small amber instrument lamp lights his mouth and jaw. Behind him a patrol cutter's searchlight drives an ice-blue shaft through the fog, spray burning white inside it. The varnished mahogany deck between him and the lens mirrors the amber lamp and the blue beam as long wet streaks. Everything else falls to true black.
MOTION: Camera locked to the boat. The ice-blue searchlight beam slides slowly across the fog behind him. He says: it is state level manipulation. He talks at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: The fog run puts the chase and the state's searchlight behind one calm face, so the words land as a shrug.

=== SHOT l04 ===
Long telephoto compression, 85mm, along the deck of a low grey rum boat at night. A heavy man of about 70 with sagging jowls, clean-shaven, a battered black fedora pushed back, stands full front and chest-up, filling half the frame height, looking just past the camera. Directly behind his shoulder a warning shell from a distant cutter has just landed: a column of water stands taller than the mast, lit blood red by the gun's flash on the horizon. An emerald starboard lamp beside him lights his cheek and mouth green. The wet steel hull plate at his side mirrors red and green in hard bands. Sky and sea are black.
MOTION: Locked camera. The tall column of water behind him slowly collapses back into the sea. He says: it is the devil with a radio station. He talks at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 6
WHY: A warning shot from the authorities towers over a man who doesn't turn round, which gives the longest line a big slow image to sit on.

=== SHOT f3 ===
Water-level view from a rowing skiff at night, 24mm, very close: a gaunt man of about 65 with grey stubble and hollow cheeks, hatless, in a soaked dark overcoat, sits at the oars with his hands low, his face three-quarter and filling the top half of the frame. Behind him the crew's own speedboat, set alight to destroy the evidence, burns against a timber pier, and the pier has caught: tall orange flames, sparks climbing. The black harbour water mirrors the whole blaze upside down in one long smear. A violet mercury tube on the next wharf, out of frame right, lights the near side of his face and his mouth; orange rims his wet hair.
MOTION: Locked camera. The orange flames on the pier keep burning and their reflection trembles on the water. He says: that drives the situation. He talks at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: Burning your own boat to kill the evidence is real rum-runner practice, and it gives a two-second line an instant, doubled picture.

=== SHOT f4 ===
High angle from the rigging of a schooner anchored offshore at night, 50mm, looking down on a broad man of about 58, clean-shaven with a broken nose and a tweed cap pushed back, who looks up just past the lens, his face filling a third of the frame height. Below and behind him, buyers' motorboats crowd along the hull with engines running, burlap sacks of bottles hanging mid-swing over the rail. At his elbow, a tin-topped table is stacked with bundles of banknotes. A yellow lantern just above the lens lights his mouth. Teal lamps on the boats tint the sea and the deck. His black oilskin coat and the tin table mirror yellow points and teal streaks.
MOTION: Locked camera. The yellow lantern light sways gently across his face and the banknotes. He says: working people get paid in money. He talks at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: The offshore mother ship is where bootleg cash visibly changed hands, so the line about money has money stacked beside it.

=== SHOT e4 ===
A rain-wet freight yard at night, seen from low between the rails, 40mm, tilted a few degrees. A long-faced man of about 72 with white stubble, a black derby tipped back, sits in the open door of a 1920s boxcar, three-quarter and chest-up, filling a third of the frame height, a revolver hanging pointed at the ground. Behind him the boxcar's false floor is torn up, whiskey cases packed in the cavity. Beyond, a locomotive vents steam. A magenta gelled lamp on the yard office paints his face and mouth; a lime-green signal lamp streaks down the wet steel rails and the polished black boiler, with the locomotive's headlamp burning white through the steam.
MOTION: Locked camera. Steam drifts slowly from the locomotive through the magenta and green light. He says: ill gotten gains, no need to explain. He talks at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 6
WHY: Liquor in a hidden floor is a real smuggling trick, and it sits right behind a man calmly declining to explain it.

=== SHOT c2 ===
Inside a timber dock warehouse at night, 28mm, straight-on at chest height and close: three clean-shaven men of about 60 to 75 in three-piece suits and fedoras tipped back stand shoulder to shoulder, faces large and bare, mouths open wide mid-shout, drum-fed submachine guns held out at arm's length and pointing up at the rafters. Behind them a fourth man with his back to us has a fireman's axe sunk into a barrel in a long stack, whiskey pouring from the split staves. Ankle-deep spilled whiskey covers the floor like a mirror. A string of coral-pink bulbs lights their mouths and doubles in the flood; cobalt moonlight slants through the loading doors onto the rain-wet dock.
MOTION: Locked camera. Whiskey keeps pouring from the split barrel into the flood on the floor. The men shout together: Call the police, it's a robbery! They shout at an unhurried pace, and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: Old men gleefully announcing their own crime while an axe drains the stock is the joke of the chorus, in one wide, wet frame.
