=== SHOT d1 ===
Stage-floor level in a burgundy 1920s lounge, wide lens almost touching the row of footlights. The singer stands alone just above us, facing the lens, his head and shoulders filling half the frame, mouth wide open on a word. Hot yellow footlights rake up under his chin and light his lips and teeth clearly. Far behind him the burgundy velvet curtain has caught fire along its hem, soft and out of focus, and he pays it no attention. Deep shadow above, burgundy and yellow only, polished stage boards gleaming. No lettering anywhere, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera creeps in slowly in one unbroken shot. Only the distant curtain flames flicker while his head stays nearly still. He sings: Robbery, robbery! Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: Footlights under the chin give the redraw tool a brightly lit mouth while the fire stays far enough back to be ignored.

=== SHOT d2 ===
Three-quarter view at chest height with a 35mm lens, in a burgundy mob lounge beside a small footlit stage. The singer fills the left foreground, his face a third of the frame high, turned slightly to the right, shouting with his jaw dropped, a cool teal light across his mouth. Further back and smaller, a slim woman drummer of about 35 with bobbed black hair and a tubby young Italian American trumpeter of about 25, trumpet lowered, shout with their heads back. Behind them the mirrored bar is bursting, bottles and glass hanging mid-air in teal and burgundy. No lettering, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera holds on a tripod in one continuous shot. Flying glass behind drifts in slow motion, everything else keeps its shape. The men shout together: Call the police, it's a robbery! Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: A big clear lead face with two smaller shouters makes the gang chant read without a crowd of mouths to fail.

=== SHOT d3 ===

On the left a furnace door hangs open, bundles of banknotes burning in the fire. On a bench behind him, small and soft: a Black upright bass player of about 60, tall and white-haired; a slim woman drummer of about 35 with bobbed black hair; a tubby young Italian American trumpeter of about 25; a wiry fingerpicking guitarist of about 70, bald. All shout. No lettering, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera drifts a few inches left without stopping, single shot. Furnace flames lick at the door; the bench stays still and soft. The men shout together: Debasement! Debasement! Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: The wide lens makes the singer huge and shrinks the bench, so the four others stay out of the tool's way.

=== SHOT d4 ===
From down on the floor beside an open basement furnace, looking up at a three-quarter angle through a 50mm lens, with blurred fire glowing in the lower left corner. The singer's head and shoulders take up half the frame against old brick and rusty pipes. Orange furnace light burns along one side of his face and hard green light from the corridor's far end cuts the other, meeting across his open, clearly visible mouth. Above and behind him a burst pipe blasts a jet of steam that catches the green. He sings as if it were a quiet evening. No lettering, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera rises an inch or two, very slowly, in one take. The steam jet keeps blowing behind him and his head barely moves. He sings: It happened in da basement! Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: The split orange and green light is the shot's whole idea and both colours land on the mouth.

=== SHOT b1 ===
Night under a riveted iron bridge, shot at eye level with a long 100mm lens. A big round car headlamp blazes just out of frame on the right, throwing hard white-gold light straight across the singer's face. He is close, three-quarter to us, his face half the frame high, mouth wide open, every tooth lit. Behind him the river is pure black, dissolving city lights into soft blue and gold discs, and out on the water, blurred, a rum barge burns. Cold blue shadow falls on the side of his face away from the lamp. No lettering, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera sways almost nothing, as if handheld by a steady operator, one shot. The soft fire on the distant barge flickers. He sings: Robbery, robbery! Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: A single hard headlamp on a long lens is the plainest possible light on the mouth, with the burning barge reduced to a colour behind him.

Behind them a stopped 1920s car's two round lamps glow violet through the haze, its windscreen shattered and radiator steaming. Orange and violet only, his breath just visible. No lettering, no black bars.
MOTION: Camera orbits a few degrees very slowly, one unbroken take. Brazier flames flicker; the drummer and the wrecked car stay as they are. He sings: own the printer and you don't fear winter. Nobody else sings.
SECONDS: 6
WHY: The brazier gives a practical front light for the mouth and a warm answer to the word winter.
