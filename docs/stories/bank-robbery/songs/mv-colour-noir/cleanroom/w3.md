=== SHOT 1 ===
Seen from a camera bolted to the front bumper of a pursuing police car, one foot above the road and looking dead ahead, on a night street that runs beneath an elevated railway in 1931. Eight feet in front of the lens an oxblood sedan swings left around a riveted iron pillar, rolled hard over on its outer springs and cut off by the right edge of the frame; its spare wheel, chrome bumper and two small ruby tail lamps are sharp, and pale smoke curls off the rear tyre. The police car's own headlamp beam, coming from just below the lens, lands flat and white on the sedan's boot. Iron pillars rush past on both sides, pulled into long horizontal smears of black and rust-green. Between them, amber light from bare-bulb shopfronts falls in bars across granite setts and streetcar rails, and the setts at the bottom of the picture are dragged into streaks by the speed. Overhead a train is crossing at the same moment: a spit of white-violet sparks from its pickup shoe falls through the ties and catches the sedan's roof. The shopfronts behind the pillars are painted jade and tomato, their sign boards plain blocks of colour with no lettering. The gutter is damp, the crown of the road dry, and one puddle holds a ruby tail-lamp streak stretched toward the camera. Everything between the lights stays truly dark, with coarse film grain and deep dye colour, and the scene fills the whole 16:9 frame to its edges.
WAY OUT: The next iron pillar passes inches from the lens and blacks out the frame, and the cut happens inside that black.
WHY: The camera is locked to one car and chasing another, so the only sharp things are the two cars and the speed is in everything else.

=== SHOT 2 ===
High on a city rooftop at dusk, looking steeply down past the rusted iron leg and dark timber staves of a water tank, which take up the left quarter of the picture, huge and close and out of focus. Thirty storeys below, a broad avenue meets a street coming in at a sharp angle, and the whole chase is tiny down there: a single butter-yellow roadster sliding sideways across the streetcar tracks with its rear wheels flinging a fan of pale grit, and three black police sedans strung out behind it, the last one overshooting the corner and bumping up onto the kerb. A green streetcar has stopped dead across one lane, and small dark-hatted figures scatter off the crossing. Headlamps have just come on and each draws a short bright dash along the road, while the roadster's tail is smudged along its slide. The buildings between here and there are uneven in height: terracotta, soot-brown brick, verdigris copper roofs, plain faded colour blocks on the gable walls, some whole blocks already dark with no lit windows. A few strings of bulbs are starting up along the avenue, amber against the last of the sky, which runs from dense peach on the right down to bruised violet behind the far towers, with the skyline pushed off to the right third. Roof steam drifts through the middle distance. Rich, dense colour with visible grain, and the view runs uninterrupted to every edge of the frame.
WAY OUT: Whip pan down and along the line of the water tank's leg, landing at street level on a shot travelling in the same direction as the roadster's slide.
WHY: Most chases sit on the bumper, so this one makes the city the giant and the crime a yellow speck that you still can't look away from.

=== SHOT 3 ===
Night on a frozen river in winter 1930, with the camera almost lying on the ice and panning hard to follow a bootlegger's sedan as it crosses from right to left. The sedan is deep teal with cream wheels, squatting low on its rear springs, wooden crates stacked to the roof behind its side windows; it is sharp while its spokes have blurred into solid discs, its nose is dipped, and a tall plume of ice dust and snow tears off the back wheels, lit from inside by its own tail lamp. Its front is cut off by the left edge of the frame, with its headlamps throwing a hard beam ahead that shows blown snow crossing it. Thirty yards behind, in the plume, a police motorcycle and sidecar is leaning into the chase, its single headlamp a hot white point with a crouched rider in a long dark coat and peaked cap as a soft shape behind it. The far shore fills the upper third of the picture: a whole city's bulb signs and coated neon, dragged by the pan into horizontal bars of ruby, gold and uranium green. The black wind-polished ice below takes those same bars and stretches them toward the lens, longer and dimmer than their sources, broken up wherever white drift crosses them. A slab of pressure ridge rips past the bottom of the frame as a pale blur. The sky above the city is plain dark with a little of its glow. Fine grain and saturated dye colour, the image reaching all four edges with nothing around it.
WAY OUT: The sidecar's headlamp swings straight into the lens and flares the frame white, and the cut lands on that flare into the next shot's brightest light.
WHY: A real rum-running route nobody expects to see, it puts a whole neon city in the reflection with no rain and no street.

=== SHOT 4 ===
From inside the back of a getaway sedan at dawn, looking backward through its small oval rear window as the car tears down a narrow wholesale fruit market lane. The interior is sharp and close: plum mohair seats, a brass ashtray, a tan window blind flapping on its cord, and a man in a rumpled cream suit kneeling backward on the seat at the left of the frame, cropped by the edge to one shoulder, one ear and thinning hair, his hand clamped on the window ledge. The whole car is rolled over so the horizon outside tilts steeply. Oranges are loose across the parcel shelf, two of them in the air and blurred. Out through the oval everything is streaked along the line of travel: awnings striped tomato, mustard and bottle green, a stack of burst crates, porters in waistcoats flattened against a wall, cabbages and oranges still bouncing in the road, and straight behind, coming through the spray of fruit, a police motorcycle with a sidecar, the rider crouched low in goggles and peaked cap. The low sun comes from behind the motorcycle, straight down the lane and in through the rear window, hard and gold, turning market steam into a bright wall, laying one oval of light over the man's shoulder and the plum seat, and leaving the rest of the car's interior deep and brown. Colour-negative grain with rich reds, and the car's interior carries the picture out to all four frame edges with no border.
WAY OUT: The sedan dives under a stone railway arch, the oval window drops to black, and the cut comes on that black.
WHY: It swaps night for hard morning sun and turns the chase into a frame within a frame, with the passenger's view of what the car has just wrecked.
