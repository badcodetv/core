=== SHOT den2 ===
One continuous shot. The fist hauls the iron lever upward and the left end of the shelf lifts, tipping it steeper. The bottles slide down it one after another and drop into the black hatch, heavy glass rattling against glass, while the spilled liquor runs along the bar. The camera starts low, slides past the big amber bottle in the foreground and follows the last small clear bottle along the shelf. It tips over the lip and falls into the hatch, and the camera keeps pushing in until the hatch's black fills the whole frame. The coloured light on the wall stays still.
ENDS ON: Full black inside the hatch after the last bottle falls, ready to cut to a round lamp or headlamp opening out of the dark.
WHY: The camera chases one object through a chain reaction, which gives the shot speed and makes the bottle's fall the reason for the dark.

=== SHOT den3 ===
Camera locked overhead, one unbroken shot. Something out of sight bumps the green lamp shade at the top of the frame and it starts to swing on its cord, wider with each pass. The pool of light and the shadows of the hats sweep back and forth across the felt and the checkered floor, and the smoke tears around the moving shade. The six men stay seated with their hats down and faces hidden, their hands almost still. At about seven seconds the dark rim of the shade swings straight at the lens and covers the whole frame in black-green.
ENDS ON: The shade's dark rim blocks the lens at the peak of its swing, with the moving shadow carrying the cut.
WHY: All the motion comes from one swinging light, so the camera holds still and the men's hands have little chance to morph.

=== SHOT city1 ===
One continuous sideways move, gathering speed. The camera slides left along the stone bank under the bridge, so the riveted oxblood girder sweeps across the view until its dark steel covers the whole frame. Mist creeps over the water and the bars of light ripple. The tower, windows and lamps stay steady, and every building keeps its shape.
ENDS ON: Dark girder steel across the whole frame, with the cut hidden on it.
WHY: The prompt is kept short because the city is the weak spot, and parallax against a near girder gives speed without asking the skyline to do anything.

=== SHOT safe2 ===
Single tracking shot, camera low to the cobbles and moving with the safe at street speed. First three seconds: the yellow car pulls hard away, its rear squatting under the load, the chain snapping taut and the green iron safe jerking after it, scraping on its lower edge so the sparks spray from that edge only. Next three seconds: the safe slams over the bumps, the chain goes slack then taut, and the car rolls on its springs with its wheels on the ground, while the headlights far behind it grow closer. Last two seconds: the safe hits a raised ridge of cobbles, kicks up and lurches back at the lens until dark green iron fills the frame. Shop signs and the marquee stay soft with motion blur.
ENDS ON: Dark green iron filling the lens at the top of the safe's bounce, ready to cut to a dark shape of similar size moving out of the frame.
WHY: Timed beats build the weight through the chain's slack and pull, so the bounce at the end feels earned by the load.

=== SHOT safe3 ===
High on the rusted catwalk, watching from far away. Down below, the small car crosses the iron bridge from right to left, pulling a thin line of orange sparks behind it, and the headlights on the wet road behind it chase after it at a true, unhurried-looking distance speed, all of them staying on the road. Smoke rises slowly from the roofs and the city lights hold steady. The camera moves right along the catwalk in one continuous drift, the roofs shifting gently with parallax, and a dark steel upright of the railing passes close to the lens and covers the whole frame in black.
ENDS ON: The railing's dark upright blacks out the frame, replacing the suggested chimney because the railing is already in the start frame and nothing new has to appear.
WHY: The chase plays as a small detail seen from a distance, so the shot gets its scale from an onlooker's view rather than from action up close.
