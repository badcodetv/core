=== SHOT 1 ===
Seen from a driver's eye height, behind the wheel of a 1933 sedan tearing along a cobbled street beneath an elevated railway at night, the whole picture one full 16:9 film frame running to every edge. The bottom of the frame is the car: a thin three-spoke steering wheel with a wire-sprung centre, two hands in oxblood leather driving gloves hauling it hard left, the right wrist showing a finger's width of mustard check shirt cuff, each hand with four fingers curled over the rim and the thumb along the spoke. A painted metal dash runs beneath the windscreen with an oval cluster of cream-faced gauges, their needles shaking and their numerals too small to read. Thirty yards ahead through the flat upright glass, a flatbed truck has snapped its ropes and is shedding oak barrels: one hangs in mid-air, one has burst on the cobbles and thrown a fan of amber beer that the car's headlamps light from behind, and three more roll straight at the bonnet, the nearest big enough to fill a quarter of the windscreen. Riveted girders painted bottle green stand in a row down both sides of the road, the closest smeared into horizontal streaks by the car's movement while the barrels stay sharp. Slats of hard yellow light fall from the tracks overhead, plum and oxblood shopfronts glow under strings of bare bulb signs whose letters blur to nothing, and the gaps between pillars drop to true black. The horizon leans ten degrees with the swerve. Heavy film grain, rich saturated dye colour, deep shadow.
WAY OUT: The next green girder passes close to the lens and blacks out the whole frame, and the cut happens inside that dark.
WHY: Motion and danger coming straight at the viewer's own hands give the thread its pure chase hit, with the colour held in painted steel and shopfronts instead of coloured light.

=== SHOT 2 ===
The driver's head has turned left, and we look sideways out of the open side window of a stationary 1930s coupe, waiting at a city crossroads at night in the middle of a theatre crowd. The picture is one complete frame of colour film, filling the wide 16:9 shape edge to edge. The dark chrome-edged window opening frames the whole scene, and resting on its sill at the bottom left is one gloved hand in oxblood leather, the fingers tapping, a sliver of mustard check shirt cuff at the wrist. Barely two feet away, filling the right half of the frame from the chest up, a heavyset motorcycle policeman sits astride his idling machine in a dark navy tunic with brass buttons and a peaked cap, looking dead ahead and chewing a matchstick, entirely unaware of us. The tank of his motorcycle gleams in the low foreground, with a long smear of gold bulb light bent across its curve. Behind him, soft with distance, people in evening clothes pour out beneath a lacquered vermilion theatre canopy edged with hundreds of small bare bulbs: a woman in a jade satin coat laughing with her head thrown back, a man in a white scarf lighting a cigarette. All the canopy's letters are out of focus and illegible. The bulbs light the policeman from behind and from his far side, a warm gold rim along his cap and jaw, while the side of his face nearest us sits in soft shadow lifted only by a dim glow from the dash. A long lens feel, shallow depth, dense grain, a still and held moment.
WAY OUT: The crossroads light changes and the driver's eyes snap forward, so a fast whip pan right from the policeman's profile blurs across the windscreen pillar and lands in the next shot's movement.
WHY: The one quiet frame in the thread, it gets its tension from a policeman two feet away who hasn't looked round yet.

=== SHOT 3 ===
From behind the wheel, a 1930s roadster is airborne at the top of its leap across the gap of a raised bascule bridge, just before dawn, and the world is tipped. The image is one uninterrupted frame of colour motion picture, reaching all four edges of the wide 16:9 shape. At the very bottom, a pair of oxblood leather gloves grip a slim steering wheel at ten and two, knuckles straining through the hide, a thin line of mustard check cuff at the left wrist, and the long louvred bonnet in deep cream paint pitches downward past them with its round chrome headlamp shell at the far corner. There is no roof and only a low raked screen, so the view is enormous. Straight ahead and below, the far leaf of the bridge rises to meet the car, a huge slope of black riveted steel and timber decking with its tram rails shining, still many car lengths away. Directly under the bonnet, a long drop down to the river: broad jade-black water with a coal barge and its tiny lit cabin, the lamps along the embankment broken by the current into long wavering bars of rust and gold. To the left, cropped by the frame, the massive stone bridge tower with one lit window looms close. Far off on the right, an uneven skyline of stepped towers stands in dark silhouette against a sky of rose and dull violet, the sun not yet up, with a few high windows still burning. The horizon runs at a steep fifteen-degree tilt. Cold pale light from the sky lies along the bonnet and gloves, and no lamp reaches this high. Fine grain, deep colour, huge scale.
WAY OUT: The nose keeps dropping until the dark steel deck fills the frame and the cut lands on the slam of the car hitting it, matched to a hard jolt at the start of the next shot.
WHY: This is the big, stop-and-look scale shot, the one frame that leaves the streets for open sky, drop and skyline in a colour nobody expects from a getaway.

=== SHOT 4 ===
Late afternoon, hard low sun, and the driver has twisted right round in the seat of an open touring car to look backward down the narrow alley the car is tearing out of, the picture running full frame to all four edges of the wide 16:9 shape. Closest to the lens along the bottom edge is the top of an oxblood leather seat back and the folded canvas hood behind it, gripped by a single gloved hand in darker oxblood leather, its fingers dug into the seam, mustard check cuff pushed up the wrist. The rest of the frame is the wreck the car has left. A tall canyon of soot-brown and faded ochre tenements runs away from us at an angle, fire escapes zigzagging up one side, and the car has torn through every washing line strung across the gap. Bedsheets and shirts in saffron, crimson, indigo and bleached white are still falling through the shafts of sun in a slow storm, some lit through like stained glass, one enormous crimson sheet billowing only a few feet from the lens and cropped by the top of the frame. On the cobbles, a toppled barrow has spilled lemons that bounce along the gutter, and a heavy woman in a floral apron stands in the middle of the alley, furious, both fists raised, while three men lean out of upper windows shouting. Further back, a black police sedan noses into the mouth of the alley with a pale shirt snagged across its radiator grille, and its driver can't see. The low sun comes from behind the tenements at the far end, throwing everyone's long shadows towards us and hanging dust in the air. Everything beyond the foreground has the slight sideways smear of a jolting car. Grainy saturated dye-transfer colour, shadows full and dark.
WAY OUT: The big crimson sheet falls across the lens and fills the frame with red cloth, and the cut comes out of that cloth on a red shape or light in the next shot.
WHY: The only daylight in the thread, and the only time we look back, it plays the chase as comic chaos with a whole street's colour falling out of the sky.
