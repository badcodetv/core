=== SHOT 1 ===
A night on the Chicago River in 1928, seen from a hand's breadth above the water, from the shadow beneath a double-deck bascule bridge. The riveted underside of the bridge, painted oxblood red, crosses the top of the frame and runs down the left edge as a huge cropped girder close to a 28mm lens, its rivets catching wavering light thrown up off the river. Beyond it, a tall white terracotta tower with a clock at its crown stands to the right of centre, lit from below by banks of floodlights on the roofs opposite, so that it glows cream against a deep plum sky and its upper storeys fall away into dimness. Lower warehouses along the bank stay as dark blocks with only three or four amber windows. A moored tug, its hull bottle green and its wheelhouse cardinal red, sits against the far wall under one brass lamp. The water is almost-black green. Far off it holds a smooth pale column of the white tower; nearer the lens the same column breaks into long horizontal bars of cream and amber that stretch toward the camera, dimmer than the lights that make them. Thin mist lies on the surface. Three-strip colour film with fine grain, the image running to every edge of the 16:9 frame. All signs and painted lettering are too far off and too dim to make out.
WAY OUT: As the camera drifts sideways the oxblood girder passes the lens and blacks out the frame, and the cut happens on that dark.
WHY: From water level the river becomes a mirror for a skyscraper lit like a stage set, and the bridge overhead gives a slow pan something huge to slide past.

=== SHOT 2 ===
Pittsburgh at the end of a winter dusk, 1927, seen from the top landing of an inclined railway cut into a steep snow-covered hillside, some four hundred feet above the river. At the bottom left, close to the lens and cropped by the frame, is the sloping nose of a funicular car painted crimson, with brass handrails and two lit windows, its cable and rails plunging out of frame beneath it. Below, the Monongahela curves away from the lower right into the distance, broad and the colour of slate. On the far bank a steel mill runs for a mile along the water: a row of black sheds and tall stacks, and at two points converters throw white and tangerine flame straight up, lighting the underside of a long flat bank of smoke in rose and ochre from beneath. A single long copper streak lies on the river below each flame, and nowhere else. Three iron truss bridges cross at uneven distances, small and dark. The snow on the rooftops of the near neighbourhood falls in lilac and pale blue, with a scatter of gold kitchen windows between dark terraces. A small knot of downtown towers sits at the far right edge, half lost in haze. Above the smoke the last of the sky is ultramarine going to violet. A 50mm lens, everything sharp to the horizon, soft film grain and rich saturated colour, filling the 16:9 frame edge to edge. No lettering can be read anywhere.
WAY OUT: The crimson funicular car rolls forward past the lens on its cable and wipes the whole frame with red paint and brass, and the next shot begins as it clears.
WHY: An industrial furnace is the prettiest light in the country here, and its great width across a snow-lit valley gives the pan a mile of fire to travel along.

=== SHOT 3 ===
Coney Island at the last moment of dusk, summer 1925, seen from ankle height on the wet sand at dead low tide. The ebb has left a wide, still sheet of water on the beach, so thin that the ribbed sand shows through it near the lens. Far across it, spread along the whole width of the frame, is the amusement park: hundreds of towers, minarets and domes painted coral, mint and butter yellow, every edge outlined in strings of warm white light bulbs, with the tallest tower well left of centre and a long dark gap to its right where the unlit timber lattice of a roller coaster climbs against the sky. A black barnacled pier piling stands in the right foreground, cropped by the top and bottom of the frame, slick and dripping, with a thin gold edge of light down one side. The sky is a low band of salmon pink at the horizon rising into deep violet-blue, with a first few stars. In the still water the park appears upside down, sharp and complete in the far distance, a little dimmer than the real thing; closer to the lens, where the ribs of sand break the surface, the bulbs smear into vertical threads of gold that run toward the camera. Colour film at its most saturated, faint grain, a 35mm lens held nearly at the waterline. The picture fills the 16:9 frame to all four edges. Any signs on the park are tiny and illegible.
WAY OUT: Hold the brightest cluster of bulbs on the tallest tower and match cut to a pair of headlamps sitting in that exact spot of the frame.
WHY: A million bulbs doubled in a sheet of seawater looks invented but is simple physics, and the long unbroken horizon is made for a slow sideways pan.

=== SHOT 4 ===
Downtown Los Angeles on a warm, dry night in 1929, looking steeply down Broadway from a roof ten storeys up, from behind a giant vertical theatre sign. The back of the sign fills the left third of the frame close to the lens: a raw steel frame painted rust red, tar-black conduit, and rows of bare bulb sockets spilling hot yellow light around their edges onto the girders. Past it the street drops away and leaves the frame toward the upper right at a sharp diagonal, a canyon of theatre marquees stacked one behind another. Their undersides are packed with white and amber bulbs that flood the pavement below; above them hang upright signs in ruby red neon, uranium green, cool blue argon and noviol gold, their shapes all seen edge-on so that the lettering collapses into unreadable slivers of colour. Striped awnings in plum and mustard are folded back along the shopfronts. Two pairs of polished streetcar rails run down the middle of the empty road, and they are the only hard reflections in the scene: thin bright lines carrying the colour of each sign they pass. A lone butter-yellow streetcar sits stopped at the far end with its interior lit, and a few parked sedans in maroon and black carry small smears of neon along their roofs. Above the marquees the office floors go abruptly dark, and the clear sky is near-black navy. Lens around 40mm, deep focus, glowing film colour with a light grain, running to every edge of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: A hard whip pan left along the back of the sign turns its rows of bulbs into yellow streaks, and the cut hides in the blur.
WHY: Seeing the sign from behind puts the viewer somewhere nobody normally stands, and a completely dry street proves the colour comes from the city rather than from weather.
