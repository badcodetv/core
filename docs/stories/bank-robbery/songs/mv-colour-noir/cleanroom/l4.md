=== SHOT e1 ===
Night hold-up of an armoured money truck on an American city street in the 1920s, 16:9, filling the frame. A lean man of about sixty, grey stubble, deep creases, pearl-grey fedora pushed back, stands in the foreground at chest-up, three-quarter to the lens on a 50mm, looking just past camera. Behind him the riveted truck's rear doors hang open, canvas sacks split, banknotes hanging in the air too soft to show any print. Amber bulbs strung over the road warm his cheek and mouth; a blue neon tube out of frame right cuts a hard edge along his jaw. The waxed black door of a touring car beside him mirrors the bulbs as a row of amber points and the blue as one long streak. Shopfronts carry no lettering. Shadows fall to true black.
MOTION: Locked camera, one continuous shot. Banknotes drift slowly down behind him while everything else holds its shape. He says: stacking up the cash by the grands. He talks at an unhurried pace and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: The money-truck job stays readable with loose cash as its only motion, while the mirror-black door supplies the gloss around a large, well-lit mouth.

=== SHOT e2 ===
A rain-soaked iron fire escape five floors up a brick tenement during a police chase, late 1920s, widescreen. Seen from just beyond the railing at eye level on an 85mm lens, a heavyset man near seventy, clean-shaven, jowls running with rain, soaked black homburg, fills the frame from chest to crown, facing almost front and glancing down past us. His right arm is flung wide with a revolver aimed at the sky, far from his face. The window beside him is mid-burst, glass hanging in the air. Far below, an unlettered theatre marquee of icy white bulbs burns out of focus, while magenta light from a sign across the alley washes his face and mouth. Backlit rain falls in white threads; wet iron slats and the window shards shine magenta and white. Everything outside the lamps is black.
MOTION: Locked camera, one continuous shot. Rain keeps falling straight down past the iron railing and nothing else changes. He says: standard plans are for the working man. He talks at an unhurried pace and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: A fire-escape chase puts the danger on every side of him, and steady rain gives Omni one simple thing to animate while his face stays still.

=== SHOT f7 ===
Inside a grand marble bank hall at midnight in 1926, the round vault door has just been blown off its hinges and smoke pours from the dark opening. A thin, white-haired man of about seventy-five, clean-shaven, hollow cheeks, dark bowler, stands close to a long lens at eye level, his head and shoulders filling the left half of the frame, three-quarter front, staring across the room. One hand holds a Thompson far out at his side, muzzle to the ceiling. Green glass desk lamps along the bent brass teller grilles light his face and lips emerald; a crimson alarm bulb above the vault rims his ear and colours the smoke. The polished floor doubles it all, green pools and a red smear, scattered notes lying blurred and unreadable upon it.
MOTION: Locked tripod, one continuous shot. Smoke rolls slowly out of the vault behind him and the room holds still. He says: hungry people feed a revolution. He talks at an unhurried pace and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 4
WHY: A blown vault is the biggest bank-job image there is, and placing it behind a still, front-lit face keeps the lip sync clean.

=== SHOT f8 ===
A tar rooftop above a Prohibition-era city, the moment after a burst of gunfire has riddled a wooden water tank. Shot from low down on a 28mm lens, close enough that a broken-nosed, clean-shaven man of about sixty-five, heavy brow, black overcoat and wide hat, looms large in the lower right, looking straight past the lens with his chin level. He holds a drum-fed Thompson in his far hand, pointed at the sky. Behind him a dozen jets of water arc from the tank's holes under a steel sign frame seen from the back, its bulb clusters blazing orange. A violet argon tube on the next building lights his face and mouth. The flooded roof at his feet reflects violet and orange ripples, and a far-off skyline of warm points runs along the horizon.
MOTION: Locked camera, one continuous shot. Water keeps spraying in steady arcs from the holes in the tank behind him. He says: then it's retribution, perhaps some execution. He talks at an unhurried pace and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 6
WHY: Shooting up property rather than a person lets the line sound menacing while the framing stays inside the rules, and the longer take leaves room to choose the best 3.6 seconds.

=== SHOT c5 ===
A low cellar print shop, 1924: a counterfeiters' flatbed press is on fire and three aging printers have only just turned to run. All three, between fifty-five and seventy-two, clean-shaven or stubbled, shirt sleeves and ink-black aprons, no hats, crowd shoulder to shoulder at a slightly high angle on a 35mm lens, their faces large in the frame and turned toward each other and front, mouths wide and clear. Behind them, the press burns in sulphur-yellow flame, and sheets of curling, unprinted-looking paper blaze on drying lines. Turquoise fire licks from the copper plates racked on the wall. Yellow light hits their faces from one side, turquoise from the other. Spilled ink spreads across the floor like a black mirror, holding both colours and every flame.
MOTION: Locked camera, one continuous shot. Flames keep climbing the press behind them and nothing else moves much. The men shout together: Printer is burning, now now what's occurring? They shout at an unhurried pace and nobody else speaks.
SECONDS: 6
WHY: Three lit faces against a burning press deliver the crew's panic literally, and the coloured copper fire and black ink make it unlike the other shots.
