=== SHOT 1 ===
A jeweller's shop in the 1920s on a hot, bright afternoon, seen from counter height across a glass case at about arm's length, on a 40mm lens. A broad robber in a chalk-striped mustard suit and a knitted plum balaclava has set a ruby tiara on top of his mask and is turning his head to admire it. Sewn on the forehead of the balaclava, below the tiara and large enough to read across the room, is a thin pale ring with one small red triangle inside it, set off centre toward 2 o'clock, its corners rounded. The elderly jeweller, in a bottle-green waistcoat with sleeve garters and a loupe pushed up on his brow, is holding up a silver hand mirror for him with both hands, his mouth tight, a man giving the worst fitting of his career. In the robber's gloved left hand hangs a fistful of pearl ropes. Behind them a second masked figure, this one in a bottle-green knit with the same sewn emblem on the forehead, sweeps a velvet tray of rings into an open doctor's bag. Hard sun comes from high on the right through a striped lemon and white awning outside the window, throwing slanted bars of shadow across the walnut cabinets, the emerald velvet and the side of the robber's face, and every stone in the glass case catches it with small white points. The walls are a deep painted peacock green, the air is dusty, and the shop's window glass is a bright blur of the street. Film grain, rich saturated dye colour, deep shadow inside the bars. The picture runs right to the edges of the frame.
WAY OUT: The robber snaps the hand mirror aside across the lens so its flash of sun whites out the frame, matching to a headlamp or a bright window in the next shot.
WHY: A dangerous man trying on a tiara while his victim politely holds the mirror is a joke you can read in one second, in full daylight, which nobody expects from a noir.

=== SHOT 2 ===
A grand hotel ballroom in the 1920s, seen from directly above, the camera hanging among the crystal drops of a huge electric chandelier and pointing straight down the long fall to the floor. Very close to the lens, cropped by the left edge of the frame, a robber in a white dinner jacket stands at the top of a tall painter's ladder, leaning back with his face tipped up toward us, unhooking strings of crystal into a pillowcase. He wears a knitted saffron-yellow balaclava, and on its forehead, sewn on and sharp in the chandelier's glare, is a thin pale ring with one small red triangle inside it, off centre toward 2 o'clock, with rounded corners. Far below him the ladder narrows to a point on a polished parquet floor inlaid with a great star. Sixty guests in evening dress stand around the edge of the star in a neat wide ring with their hands raised: gowns of turquoise, tangerine, violet and hot pink seen from above like spilled sweets, the men's black shoulders between them. Three more small figures in saffron balaclavas stroll across the empty middle of the floor, one holding out a silver champagne bucket to a dowager who is dropping her necklace into it, her head turned away in disgust. The only light is the chandelier itself, burning warm and brightest near the lens and falling off toward the corners of the room, where the crimson walls go nearly black and the long shadows of the guests point outward from the centre like the spokes of a wheel. The chandelier drops close to the camera are large, soft and out of focus, with hard flares of light in them. Film grain, deep colour. The picture fills the frame edge to edge.
WAY OUT: A big soft crystal drop swings across the lens and fills the frame, and the cut comes on its passing, into any shot opening on a round light or a wheel.
WHY: The straight-down view turns a hold-up into a piece of choreography, a colour wheel of frightened rich people with a man stripping the ceiling in the foreground.

=== SHOT 3 ===
A mail train stopped dead at night in a deep pine cutting in falling snow, in the 1920s. The camera stands low in the snow beside the track, a few feet from the locomotive's front wheel, looking back along the length of the train on a wide 24mm lens. On the right, enormous and cropped by the frame, the black wet flank of the engine leaks white steam across the ground. The carriages behind it are painted deep maroon with mustard-yellow lining, unlettered, and curve away into the dark, and the sliding door of the third car stands open with a block of yellow lamplight pouring out onto the snow. From that door a long line of figures in knitted balaclavas and heavy coats passes grey canvas mail sacks hand to hand down the track toward us, a human chain with a jaunty rhythm, one sack in mid-air between two of them. Nearest the lens, filling the left third of the frame from the chest up, the last man in the line has a sack on his shoulder and has paused to look straight at us. His balaclava is knitted in burnt orange wool crusted with snowflakes, and sewn on its forehead, big and clear, is a thin pale ring with one small red triangle inside it, off centre toward 2 o'clock, with rounded corners. Along the embankment behind him, five mail clerks in dark waistcoats and peaked caps stand in a row with their hands up and snow gathering on their shoulders. A railway flare stuck in a drift on the far side burns a raw magenta-pink, lighting the falling flakes, the steam and one side of every figure, while the yellow door lights the other side, and between those two lights the pines stay truly dark. Falling snow close to the lens streaks soft and white. Coarse film grain, saturated colour. The image extends to every edge of the frame.
WAY OUT: A rolling bank of the locomotive's steam blows across the lens to white, and the cut happens inside it, coming out through smoke, fog or dust in the next shot.
WHY: Snow, a pink flare and a bucket-brigade of sacks give the compilation an epic outdoor scale, and the one man stopping to look at us makes it cheeky rather than grim.

=== SHOT 4 ===
Dusk on a wide river in the 1920s, the camera almost touching the water, riding in the wake of a small varnished mahogany speedboat as it pulls away from a towering paddle steamer that has been turned into a floating casino. The sky has gone rose and plum, with one long bar of apricot at the horizon. The steamer fills the back of the frame at a steep angle, cut off by the top and the right edge, its tiers painted cream and tomato red, with strings of amber bulbs along every deck that drop down into the water as long broken bars of gold, stretched toward the camera and dimmer than the bulbs above. Along the rail of the middle deck, a crowd of gamblers in furs and evening clothes lean over and stare, and several of the men have been relieved of their jackets and trousers and stand in sock suspenders and shirt-tails with their hands raised, utterly dignified. Two masked figures on the lower deck are still lowering a full-sized roulette table over the side on a rope, tilting it, chips sliding off it and falling toward the water in a bright scatter of pink and green. In the near foreground, large and off to the left, sits the stern of the speedboat with a robber lounging back against an open sack of banknotes, one elbow on the transom, his legs crossed, holding a fat cigar, in a cream linen suit and a knitted teal balaclava. Sewn on its forehead, bold enough to read clearly at this size, is a thin pale ring with one small red triangle inside it, off centre toward 2 o'clock, with rounded corners. The last of the sky lights him softly from behind, and a small brass lamp on the boat warms the side of his chest and the sack. The wake peels up in a glassy curl right at the lens, sharp in the foreground, spray hanging in the air. Fine film grain, painted saturated colour. The picture fills the whole frame, edge to edge.
WAY OUT: The camera dips under the wake's curl so a dark wall of water crosses the lens, and the cut comes out on any shot that starts from darkness, a tunnel or a passing wall.
WHY: Stealing a whole roulette table by boat while half-dressed gamblers watch at dusk is pure swagger, and the water-level vantage gives the broken gold reflections their best chance of looking real.
