=== SHOT 1 ===
A cut-down 1932 roadster in oxide-red primer tears across a dry alkali lakebed in 1940, seen from a camera lying almost on the ground beside the course, panning hard with the car as it passes three metres away. The car has no fenders, no hood and no windscreen, only a chopped body, a bare chromed flathead V-8 with finned heads, and four short exhaust stubs spitting pale heat. The car stays sharp while everything else is dragged into long horizontal streaks: the cracked pinkish-white lake surface, the far line of violet mountains, a parked truck reduced to a smear of mustard yellow. Its wire wheels have blurred into solid discs, the front tyres are turned a few degrees against a skid, the body rolled toward the camera so the inside rear wheel is barely touching. The driver is a sunburnt man in his thirties in a grease-black undershirt, leather cap and scratched goggles, no helmet and no belt, both fists locked high on a thin wooden wheel, teeth showing, knuckles white. A blank white circle where a number has been sanded off sits on the door. Low sun from behind the camera, an hour before dusk, rakes across the car and lights every rivet; a tall wall of churned chalk-white dust boils up behind the car and rolls over the back third of the frame, and small stones hang in the air off the rear tyre. The nose of the car is about to leave the right edge of the frame. Coarse colour film grain, dense saturated reds and yellows. The picture runs to all four edges of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: The wall of dust rolls across the lens to full white, and the next shot opens as its camera clears a cloud of steam, smoke or fog.
WHY: A real panning blur on one bright, filthy car with its wheels fighting the ground makes speed a physical fact, in hard daylight colour that the night shots of the film will not have.

=== SHOT 2 ===
Seen from high up on the catwalk of a rusted water tower at the edge of a city in 1946, at night, a long straight road runs diagonally across black farmland from the bottom left corner toward the top right. Forty or so parked jalopies line both sides of it in two ragged, uneven rows, nose in, headlamps on, so that the road is a narrow corridor of yellow light with total darkness either side of it. Grown men and women in work clothes and hats stand on running boards and car roofs, tiny at this distance, their long shadows thrown across the tarmac by the headlamps. Down the middle of the corridor two roadsters run side by side at full throttle, the size of a thumbnail, one sulphur yellow, one deep plum, each dragging a low streak of grey exhaust that glows where the headlamps catch it; the plum car has its back end stepped out a little and is a door's width from the row of bumpers. A single iron girder of the water tower, rust-orange and slightly out of focus, cuts down the left edge of the frame close to the lens, lit from beneath by a faint reflected glow. Far off in the top right corner the city sits low and off to one side, an uneven scatter of amber windows and one red-lit radio mast, with big unlit dark blocks between them, the haze over it a dull rose. The sky holds no moon. Deep shadows, heavy grain, hot yellow against pure black. The picture runs to all four edges of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: A hard whip pan down off the distant cars along the rusted girder, landing on a matching vertical in the next shot, such as a lamp post, pillar or exhaust stack.
WHY: From this far away the whole outlaw meet becomes a single stripe of light in the dark, a shape nobody expects from a car race, and the crowd tells you how illegal and how loved it was.

=== SHOT 3 ===
The camera is bolted to the tail of a speeding roadster, looking straight backward at bumper height along a downtown avenue at night in 1938, and filling the frame is the car chasing it, barely a metre and a half behind. The pursuing hot rod is bottle green with a cream-painted, cut-down radiator shell and no fenders; its two round headlamps are mismatched, one dead and one blazing white, and short open exhaust pipes along its flank throw a hand-length of orange-blue flame. Its nose is dipped hard under braking, its dropped front axle scraping a steel streetcar rail set in the road, and a fan of white-yellow sparks sprays backward from that one point of contact, lighting the cobbles and the car's underside from below. Behind the windscreen frame with its glass gone, the driver, a lean man in a tweed cap and oil-streaked shirt, leans forward with one hand clamped on the wheel and the other hand hauling on an outside gear lever. The street behind him is dragged into streaks by the speed, the sharpness falling away fast past the green car: a theatre front of bare amber bulbs in a sunburst with no lettering, a shop awning in crimson and gold, a tall tube of uranium-green neon bent into a plain arrow, a tilted horizon. The road is damp in patches: each sign lays a long smeared stripe of its colour down the asphalt toward the camera, sharp only in one puddle, while the crown of the road stays dry and dark. Near the bottom edge of the frame, the blurred dark rim of the tail of the camera car and one shaking exhaust pipe intrude. Rich saturated film colour, deep blacks between the lights. The picture runs to all four edges of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: The dead-and-live headlamp flares as the green car lunges at the lens, and the white flare matches into a bright round lamp in the next shot.
WHY: A chasing car seen from the car it is chasing puts the viewer in the danger, and the sparks off a streetcar rail are a detail no default car picture would ever think of.

=== SHOT 4 ===
A long telephoto view straight up a vast concrete flood channel at the end of a summer dusk in 1948, taken from the shallow water in the middle of the channel, looking through the arches of three concrete road bridges that the long lens has stacked up tight behind each other like a set of nested gateways. Under the nearest arch, two cut-down roadsters are coming straight at the camera, flat out, the channel otherwise empty. One, in chalky turquoise, stays on the flat floor, ploughing through a thin sheet of water so that each front wheel throws a tall curved wing of spray. The other, in mustard yellow with a bare engine, has climbed the sloped concrete bank on the left and rides it at a steep angle, body tilted nearly forty-five degrees, its two inside wheels light, a line of grit and water falling off it downhill. The drivers are grown men, goggled, bare-headed, heads low behind the cowls. The sky behind the bridges has gone to a deep bruised magenta with one band of hot apricot at the horizon, and that band lies on the wet channel floor as a single long stripe of colour running toward the lens, broken into bars where the cars have torn the water up. On the far bridge a lone iron lamp has just come on, its small pool of sodium-yellow light falling on the concrete below it. High on the bank at right, a few silhouetted men lean on a railing with their hats on. The concrete is stained with long dark streaks of rust and water, the near arch's underside nearly black. Heat shimmer and spray soften the far distance; dense film grain and saturated dusk colour. The picture runs to all four edges of the 16:9 frame.
WAY OUT: The turquoise car reaches the lens and its dark flank passes at arm's length, a real wipe that opens on the next shot as the car clears frame.
WHY: Hot rodders in a concrete flood channel have a period precedent (an article title on the L.A. River, seen in search and not read), and the stacked arches with a car riding the wall give the frame a geometry and a sky that look like nothing else in a car sequence.
