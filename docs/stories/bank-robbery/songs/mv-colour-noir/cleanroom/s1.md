=== SHOT den1 ===
We sit on the plank floor of a cramped basement bandstand, at hip height behind a small drum kit, looking out past the musicians into the room they are playing to. Filling the left third of the frame, close and slightly soft, are the shoulder and pinned-up grey hair of a woman in a charcoal waistcoat, her brushes blurred over a plain white calfskin snare head, a brass cymbal edge cutting across the lower corner. Beyond her, in hard rim light, a very tall thin man in a double-breasted chalk-stripe suit bends around an upright bass; a stout older man in a mustard three-piece lowers his trumpet; a young man in a too-big bottle-green suit holds a clarinet. At centre-right, nearest the microphone, stands the singer: a broad man in his forties in a double-breasted garnet wool suit with wide lapels, black hair going silver at one temple, a nose broken long ago and set crooked, heavy eyebrows. He turns his head in profile to a heavy chrome period microphone, lips closed, barrel chest lifted, drawing a breath before the next line, one big hand loose at his side. A row of bare bulbs in tin reflectors along the lip of the stage throws amber light up and back toward the camera, edging every figure in gold while their backs fall into dark. Past them, through cigar haze, the den burns in painted colour: plum walls, emerald leather booths, small crimson-shaded table lamps, men in wide-brimmed hats turned toward the stage. Between the lamps the room falls into true black. The picture runs to all four edges of the frame, with fine film grain.
WHY: The singer becomes a heavy, broken-nosed man whose garnet suit takes over the gown's colour, so the red mass at the microphone that Jack liked stays where it was.

=== SHOT den4 ===
A long, low service tunnel of sooty vaulted brick behind the club, seen through a long telephoto lens from the far end, so that the depth is squeezed flat and lagged pipes run overhead toward a brick arch. Five musicians in sharp 1930s suits wait in single file along the right-hand wall between sets, stacked close by the compression. Nearest, big in frame and cropped at the knee, leans the singer: a broad man in his forties in a long dark green velvet overcoat hanging open over a near-black double-breasted suit, black hair going silver at one temple, a broken nose, heavy eyebrows, an unlit cigarette between his thick fingers, jaw set, mouth closed, looking at nothing. Behind him a stout older man polishes a trumpet bell on his sleeve, then a young man sleeps on his feet with a clarinet under his arm, then a grey-haired woman in shirtsleeves and a waistcoat taps sticks against her thigh, and at the back a very tall thin man stands folded almost in half under the pipes, holding the neck of an upright bass. In the left-hand wall an open coal furnace door burns, and its orange firelight rakes across the bricks and up one side of each face. At the far end of the tunnel, a whole section of the brick wall has been rolled aside on iron rails, a hidden door standing open, and through the slot pours a hard blade of magenta and emerald light from the room beyond, laying a long coloured stripe down the wet flagstones toward us, broken by puddles. Steam drifts through the firelight. Dark brick swallows everything between the two light sources. The image carries all the way to the edges of the frame, with visible grain.
WHY: Only the foreground figure changes, to the same male singer in the same green velvet, and the furnace and hidden door stay in frame as they worked in the picked take.

=== SHOT bridge1 ===
Seen from the roof of a coal tug moored out on a wide black river at night, about four metres above the water, with the tug's soot-black riveted funnel cropped hard against the left edge of the frame and a coil of wet hawser on the deck below it. A colossal riveted steel arch bridge sweeps overhead from the top of the picture and comes down on the right onto one stone pier as big as a church. In the cold shadow beneath that arch, on the near bank to the lower right, long 1930s sedans in oxblood, cream and bottle green are parked nose-in on a cobbled quay, and their round tungsten headlamps meet in a single small warm pool of light on the wet stones. Inside that pool, tiny against all that iron, stand five people in sharp dark suits and hats: a very tall thin man with an upright bass, a stout older man with a trumpet, a young man with a clarinet, a grey-haired woman behind a small drum kit, and at a period microphone the singer, a broad man in his forties with black hair going silver at one temple, mouth closed, chest lifted on a breath. Thin river mist makes the headlamp beams visible as they cross the cobbles and die within a few metres. Beyond the arch to the left, a prohibition-era city climbs in uneven steps, some blocks entirely dark, others strung with bulbs and coated glass tubing, every glowing shape far too distant to be more than a soft plain bar or block of light in deep red, acid green, pale gold or midnight blue. The river takes those lights and breaks them into long trembling horizontal bars that run toward the camera, dimmer than their sources, with true black water between them. The picture runs to all four edges of the frame.
WHY: The tiny figure at the microphone becomes the broad male singer, and the city's lights are now plain shapes of colour so the engine has no lettering to print.
