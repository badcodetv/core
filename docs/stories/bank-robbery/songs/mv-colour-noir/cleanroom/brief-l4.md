## B. Period light and gloss

**What actually lit a 1920s street**
- **Correction to the brief: arc lamps were mostly on the way out.** Largely abandoned in the 1920s for harsh light and noise [reported, snippet, https://landscape.uvm.edu/landscape_new/dating/street_lighting/early_arc_lights.php]. Incandescent had largely replaced them by a 1925 Edison catalogue [reported, snippet, https://www.architectmagazine.com/practice/a-visual-history-of-streetlighting-from-the-19th-and-20th-centuries_o]. Gas lingered: Newark's last went in 1926 [tested https://knowingnewark.npl.org/spectrum-of-street-lighting-techniques-watched-over-newark]. Warm bulbs on poles and strung over the road is the correct default [inference].
- **Neon in 1923 is the standard account** [tested https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_sign, https://iaeimagazine.org/magazine/features/origins-of-neon-light], with loose ends: a 1923 San Francisco photo of the same dealer's signs may be earlier, lighting type unclear [tested https://signsofthetimes.com/an-exploration-neon-sign-origins/]. It spread to big-city licensees [reported, snippet]. Rare and new: one or two neon signs per street, not a Blade Runner wall [inference].
- **Neon colours:** neon gas is orange-red; argon with mercury is blue [tested https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/neon-lights-made]. Greens and pinks from coatings are probably later, so colour them with gel-like light instead [inference].
- **Bulbs:** Broadway "spectaculars" were hundreds of bulbs on animation circuits [tested https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/the-man-who-invented-times-square-o-j-gude-and-the-birth-of-the-spectacular]. Colour from dipped or coloured bulbs [inference].
- **Headlamps:** electric, not carbide. Acetylene was the majority "up until 1912" [reported https://blog.1000bulbs.com/history-of-the-headlight]; dynamos commonplace from 1920 [tested https://www.louwmanmuseum.nl/en/news/the-history-of-automobile-lighting]. 1920s-30s projector lamps sometimes had tinted lenses [tested https://atexpa.org/exhibit/headlights]; a snippet says amber or green on the upper half (source page unconfirmed), a usable colour accent.

**Getting the gloss**
- **Wet-down:** spray the street, rain optional; place practicals so reflections fall in frame, keep the camera low [reported https://melies.co/cinematic-techniques/atmosphere/wet-down, a tool vendor's glossary]. Night reflections hold contrast and colour because sources are directional [reported https://abduzeedo.com/node/88975].
- **Rain:** rain is lit from behind with back-crosslight; on the rain-shootout film, Hall hid lamps behind trees [reported, operator quote, snippet from an ASC page I could not reopen].
- **Colour against colour:** Storaro stacked gels, three layers for one yellow, to reach saturated primaries [tested https://spectrum.rosco.com/index.php/2020/06/vittorio-storaro-reveals-the-origin-of-the-storaro-selection-in-dick-tracy]. Seven locked colours, one colour per character, layered light keeping each separate, deep focus, static panel-like frames, and notably no haze [reported https://nofilmschool.com/dick-tracy-visual-language]. "Red against cyan" as his rule: snippet only. Haze and hard colour fight each other, so choose per shot [inference].
- **For the still model [inference]:** name each source and where its colour lands, not an adjective. "A red neon tube, out of frame left, paints the left side of his face and a long red streak across the wet cobbles; hard white backlight from a bulb sign behind him rims his hat and shoulders and lights the drifting haze; the polished black car door mirrors the bulbs as a row of warm points." One warm and one opposite colour per frame. Deep blacks kept: "shadows fall to true black, colour only where a lamp reaches."

## C. Lip sync in Omni Flash (little is Omni-specific)

**Known**

**Pace**
- One speaker per clip is the most repeated rule: shot-reverse-shot is "the most reliable path to clean sync"; a two-shot drops with rapid turns [reported https://www.veo3ai.io/blog/veo-3-two-character-dialogue-guide-2026]. - Line length: 3-6 s is about 8-15 words, start at 5-10 [reported https://artificial-intelligence-wiki.com/best-ai-tools/ai-image-and-video-generators/veo-lip-sync-tutorial/]; 6-10 per line [reported, untested, https://www.imaginary.app/blog/veo-31-dialogue-sfx-prompts-that-actually-sync-a-creator-workflow-for-clean-mout]. - Numbers: those guides imply about 2.5 words/s; sustained fast rap is 4.3-4.7 words/s [tested https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/the-fastest-rapper-in-the-game]. A 2-4 s cut at 4.5 w/s holds 9-18 words, over the guides [inference].

**What wrecks it** (mostly other lip-sync tools' vendors, not Omni)
- Extreme profile; frontal or near-frontal is best [vendor, its own tool, https://sync.so/docs/compatibility-and-tips/improving-lip-sync-quality]. The three-quarter-to-profile turn is the hardest moment [reported, snippet, https://ai.sync.so/lip-sync-video-profile-analysis].
- Hands, microphones, hair, sunglasses over the mouth [vendor, same]; add cigars [inference]. Facial hair hides the lip line [reported, snippet, https://ai.sync.so/beard-mustache-lip-sync].
- Walking, gestures and props while talking; crowded words; overlap; wrong person's mouth moving [reported Imaginary, untested].
- **Face size:** medium (chest-up) and close-up work best; wide shots lose sync because the mouth has too few pixels [reported AI wiki link above]. "Locked medium close-up" is the example framing, and "a closer shot" fixes wrong-speaker errors [reported Magic Hour]. Expect several takes: 5-10 tries is that guide's estimate [reported].

**How to stage "in the middle of action" [inference]**
- Make the head still and the world violent: he is fixed on the running board, the street streaks past; he stands still while barrels burst behind him. Action sits behind and beside the face, and moving light does the rest.
- Chest-up to head-and-shoulders, near-frontal, mouth lit. Noir shadow goes on eyes and hat brim, never the lower face.
- "To each other": over-shoulder with the listener's back to camera, so only one mouth exists. Bystanders out of focus or turned away. Add "only he speaks; the others keep their mouths closed."

## D. The outro: the sign shot up

**Gun**
- Rate, sources conflict: M1928 about 600-725 rpm [https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/ground/m1928.htm]; M1921 800 (snippet) or about 1000, M1928 about 700, drums of 50 or 100 [https://modernfirearms.net/en/submachine-guns/thompson-1921-tommy-gun-eng/]. Call it 10-16 rounds/s, so a 50-round drum lasts about 3-5 s [inference]. At 24 fps, a flash every 2 frames or so [inference].
- Cutts compensator on the muzzle vents gas upward to hold the barrel down, so expect flame jetting up from the top [reported https://www.sandboxx.us/news/the-cutts-compensator-and-the-thompson-smg-adoption-a-tale-of-marine-corps-corruption/; flame inference]. Forum posters disagree on whether blanks flash more than live .45 [reported, snippet, https://thefedoralounge.com/threads/public-enemies.24513/page-18]; a blank's flash is very brief and can be missed on camera [reported, snippet, https://theasc.com/blog/filmmakers-forum/filming-with-firearms].
- No tracers: not in gangster use [inference]. Casings: fat short brass spraying right from the top of the receiver [inference; my searches did not confirm].
- Real pattern: seventy rounds in three sweeps at set heights, marked on brick; bricks now at the Mob Museum [tested SV]. A sweep along one height is the historical shape; use it across the sign [inference].

**Sign build**
- **Bulb sign:** sheet-metal letter troughs with sockets, on animation circuits [tested Gude link; troughs inference].
- **Porcelain enamel:** powdered glass fired onto iron, one firing per colour, so few colours and a gloss [tested https://antique-collecting.co.uk/2021/06/07/enamel-advertising-signs-the-essential-guide/].
- **Neon:** glass tube, electrodes, roughly 15 kV transformer [tested https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/neon-lights-made]; on a painted or enamel face. Blade sign projecting off a brick corner, or rooftop on steel frame, gives a sky behind it and a place to rain sparks down [inference].

**How it fails [all inference, except where linked]**
- **Enamel:** glass chips off in a ring around the hole, exposing dark iron. Big white or coloured craters, the best-reading damage.
- **Painted sheet:** a clean dent-edged hole, paint flaking. With a lit bulb or can behind it, the holes glow: beams through haze.
- **Bulbs:** each one pops, glass puff, goes dark. The letter dies bulb by bulb, a great way to show progress. Circuits fail in blocks; flicker before death.
- **Neon:** a broken tube lets gas escape and goes dark, glass falling; a spark may jump at the break (15 kV). General knowledge, unconfirmed.
- **Sparks:** a slow .45 on thin sheet would barely spark in life. Film makes it: pros use zirconium hits for metal [reported vendor https://rogergeorge.com/blogs/special-effects-guides/top-5-ways-to-create-bullet-hit-effects]. Fine white-orange fans and drifting embers.

**Camera [inference]**
- Keep it simple: locked frontal to read the word, then cuts. Slow motion shows casings in the air, glass, sparks; a muzzle flash in slow motion becomes rare, so ramp to real speed for firing. Gun in the foreground, out of focus, sign sharp behind; or low shot of shooters' backs, rim-lit, wet street mirroring the sign. Finish: the last letters flicker, one bulb left, darkness, smoke drifting across the holes lit from behind. Rain or haze makes each beam visible.

# How these get used

A quick-cut music video. Each shot plays for two to four seconds. The song's vocal is laid over it afterwards, so the man's mouth has to make the song's words.

The voice is one old man, low and gravelly, unhurried. He is not a young man. So every speaker is a man of about 55 to 75 with a lived-in face. Each shot has a different man.

For the talking to read: the speaker's face fills about a third of the frame height or more, three-quarter or full front, in clear light on the mouth, clean-shaven or stubble (no moustache or beard over the lips), no hand, cigar, collar or gun in front of the mouth. One speaker only unless the shot says a crew. Speakers look at each other or just past the camera, or at what's happening, and they talk through it as if it's nothing.

Action fills everything round the face. The face being big doesn't stop the frame being wild: it's foreground, and the danger is behind it, beside it, reflected in the car door or a mirror.
