=== SHOT mask1 ===
A jeweller's shop in the 1920s on a hot, bright afternoon, seen from counter height across a glass case at about arm's length, on a 40mm lens. In the middle of the frame a broad man in a chalk-striped mustard suit, collar turned up and black leather gloves on, has set a ruby tiara on top of his mask and turned his head three-quarters toward us, tilted to admire himself. His whole head is in a hand-knitted balaclava of plum wool, slightly uneven knit-two-purl-two rib, its long neck tucked into his collar, with unbroken knit running across the place where his eyes and mouth would be. A thin cream wool ring is chain-stitched around his face, as wide as the face from brow to chin. High inside that ring, over his left eye and on the right of the picture, pressed up against the ring's inner edge, sits one small triangle cut from a single piece of solid red felt, its corners rounded, sewn flat with tiny stitches round its edge. The middle of the ring is bare plum wool. On the right the elderly jeweller, in a bottle-green waistcoat with sleeve garters and a loupe pushed up on his brow, holds up a silver hand mirror with both hands, mouth tight, a man giving the worst fitting of his career. A fistful of pearl ropes hangs from the masked man's gloved hand. Behind them at the back cabinet a second man, in the same hand-knitted mask made the same way but in bottle-green wool with an oatmeal ring and the same red felt triangle over his left eye, gloved, sweeps a velvet tray of rings into an open doctor's bag. Hard sun comes from high on the right through a striped lemon and white awning, throwing slanted bars of shadow across walnut cabinets, emerald velvet and the plum wool, and every stone in the case catches small white points. The walls are deep painted peacock green, the air dusty, the window a bright blur of street. Film grain, rich saturated dye colour, deep shadow inside the bars. The picture runs right to the edges of the frame.
WHY: Keeps the shop, sun and colours but moves the logo from forehead to the over-the-eye position, using a stated left-eye-on-picture-right anchor, a one-piece filled felt patch and bare knit at the centre to fight both the drift and the outline faults.

=== SHOT mask3 ===
A mail train stopped dead at night in a deep pine cutting in falling snow, in the 1920s. The camera stands low in the snow beside the track, a few feet from the locomotive's front wheel, looking back along the train on a wide 24mm lens. On the right, enormous and cropped by the frame, the black wet flank of the engine leaks white steam across the ground. The plain carriages behind it, deep maroon with mustard-yellow lining, curve away into the dark, and the sliding door of the third car stands open, a block of yellow lamplight pouring onto the snow. From that door a line of men in heavy coats with collars up and leather gloves on passes grey canvas mail sacks hand to hand toward us, a human chain with a jaunty rhythm, one sack in mid-air. Nearest the lens, filling the left third of the frame from the chest up, the last man has a sack on his shoulder and has stopped to face us square on. His whole head is in a hand-knitted balaclava of burnt orange wool, uneven rib crusted with snowflakes, its long neck tucked into his collar, with unbroken knit running across the place where his eyes and mouth would be. A thin black wool ring is chain-stitched around his face, as wide as the face from brow to chin. High inside it, over his left eye and on the right of the picture, pressed against the ring's inner edge, sits one small triangle cut from a single piece of solid red felt, its corners rounded, sewn flat with black stitches round its edge. The middle of the ring is bare orange wool. Behind him each man's mask is made the same way in its own dye, with the same red triangle over the left eye: next a Windsor green with a cream ring, then rust gold with a black ring, then marine blue with a cream ring, then dull mauve with a cream ring, and silver grey with a black ring in the doorway. Along the embankment five mail clerks in dark waistcoats and peaked caps stand in a row with hands up, snow gathering on their shoulders. A railway flare in a drift beyond them burns raw magenta-pink, lighting the flakes, the steam and one side of every figure; the yellow door lights the other side, and between the two the pines stay truly dark. Near flakes streak soft and white past the lens, clear of the nearest man's face. Coarse film grain, saturated colour. The image extends to every edge of the frame.
WHY: Holds the cutting, the two lights and the near man's orange while giving every man in the chain his own period dye tied to his place in the line, with the logo facing us square on over the eye and black stitching to lift the red off the orange.

=== SHOT mask4 ===
Dusk on a wide river in the 1920s, the camera almost touching the water, riding in the wake of a small varnished mahogany speedboat as it pulls away from a towering paddle steamer turned floating casino. The sky has gone rose and plum, with one long bar of apricot at the horizon. The steamer fills the back of the frame at a steep angle, cut off by the top and right edges, its tiers painted cream and tomato red, with strings of amber bulbs along every deck that fall into the water as long broken bars of gold, stretched toward the camera and dimmer than the bulbs above. Along the middle rail a crowd in furs and evening clothes lean over and stare, and several men, relieved of jackets and trousers, stand in sock suspenders and shirt-tails with hands raised, utterly dignified. On the lower deck two masked figures, one in mustard wool with a black ring and one in dull mauve with a cream ring, still lower a full-sized roulette table over the side on a rope, tilting it, chips falling toward the water in a bright scatter of pink and green. In the near foreground, large and off to the left, a man lounges at the stern against an open sack of banknotes, elbow on the transom, legs crossed, in a cream linen suit with the collar up and tan leather gloves, a fat cigar held idle in his fingers at shoulder height. He has turned his head back over his shoulder to look straight at us. His whole head is in a hand-knitted balaclava of teal wool, slightly uneven rib, its long neck tucked into his collar, with unbroken knit running across the place where his eyes and mouth would be. A thin cream wool ring is chain-stitched around his face, as wide as the face from brow to chin, big enough to read clearly. High inside it, over his left eye and on the right of the picture, pressed against the ring's inner edge, sits one small triangle cut from a single piece of solid red felt, its corners rounded, sewn flat with small stitches round its edge. The middle of the ring is bare teal wool. The last of the sky lights him softly from behind, and a small brass lamp on the boat warms his chest, the sack and the front of the mask. The wake peels up in a glassy curl right at the lens, sharp in the foreground, spray hanging in the air. Fine film grain, painted saturated colour. The picture fills the whole frame, edge to edge.
WHY: Keeps the river, steamer and dusk but turns the lounging man's head to the lens so the over-the-eye logo can actually be seen, lets the lamp light it, and dyes the deck figures their own colours instead of black.
