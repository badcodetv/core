=== SHOT 1 ===
We sit on the plank floor of a cramped basement bandstand, at hip height behind a small drum kit, looking out past the musicians into the room they are playing to. Filling the left third of the frame, close and slightly soft, are the shoulder and pinned-up grey hair of a woman in a charcoal waistcoat, her brushes blurred over a snare, a brass cymbal edge cutting across the lower corner. Beyond her, seen from behind and in hard rim light: a very tall thin man in a double-breasted chalk-stripe suit, bent around an upright bass; a stout older man in a mustard three-piece lowering his trumpet; a young man in a too-big bottle-green suit with a clarinet. At the centre-right a broad woman in her forties in a garnet satin gown, a silver streak through her black hair, turns her head in profile to a heavy chrome period microphone, lips closed, chest lifted, taking a breath. A row of bare bulbs in tin reflectors along the lip of the stage throws amber light back at the camera, so every figure is edged in gold with their backs falling into dark. Past them, through cigar haze, the den burns in painted colour: plum walls, emerald leather booths, small crimson-shaded table lamps, men in wide-brimmed hats turned toward the stage. Between the lamps the room falls into true black. The picture runs to all four edges of the frame, with fine film grain.
WAY OUT: The drummer's shoulder leans across the lens and wipes the frame to black, and the next shot opens as another dark shape slides clear of the camera.
WHY: Standing inside the band looking out puts the viewer among the musicians, and it lets the footlights carve the five figures out of a room full of painted colour.

=== SHOT 2 ===
Extreme low angle along the top of a mahogany back bar, the camera resting among the bottles at the very moment the shelf gives way. A hidden iron lever has been thrown, and the long shelf has tipped down to the left on a hinge, so forty unlabelled bottles of ruby, amber, absinthe-green and cobalt glass are sliding in a tight, jostling line toward a square black hatch in the wall at the far end. The nearest bottle fills the right side of the frame, huge and motion-blurred along the direction of the slide, liquor tilting hard inside it. The farthest are tumbling into the hatch, one already gone to its neck, a spray of gin hanging in the air above the opening. A single brass lamp mounted behind the shelf shines through all that coloured glass from the back, casting long bars of red and green light across a sweat-damp plaster wall painted deep tobacco yellow. In the upper left, cut off by the frame at the wrist, a barman's thick hand in a rolled white shirtsleeve grips the lever. Everything further than the lever drops into darkness. Spilled liquor runs along the tilted wood in a thin bright line that catches the lamp. The image fills the frame edge to edge, with heavy shadow and rich, saturated colour held in the glass.
WAY OUT: Follow the last bottle down: it drops into the black hatch, the screen goes dark for a beat, and the next shot opens on a round shape of the same size, such as a headlamp or a hanging lamp.
WHY: A real Prohibition trick, shelves that dumped the stock down a chute when the law came in, gives a stopped-time image of coloured glass that belongs to no other kind of bar.

=== SHOT 3 ===
Looking straight down from the ceiling, the camera directly above a round poker table in a windowless back room, the whole composition a graphic pattern of circles. One green enamel lamp hangs just under the lens, its rim forming a dark curved arc across the top edge of the frame, and it drops a hard pool of light onto billiard-green baize. Around the table, seen only from above, are the crowns of six wide-brimmed fedoras in cream, oxblood, dove grey, violet, mustard and black, each with its shoulders of loud patterned tailoring pushed forward into the light, and a pair of heavy hands resting at each place: signet rings, a cigar, face-down cards, uneven towers of red, ivory and teal chips. One man, off to the lower right, has leaned back so far that his hat has dropped out of the light and only his hands remain. Cigar smoke rises straight up toward us in slow flat sheets, softening the top of the picture and glowing green where it crosses the lamp. A black-and-white checker linoleum floor is barely visible past the shoulders, and it falls away into complete darkness at the corners. The colour is all in the hats, the cloth and the chips. The picture fills the entire frame, with fine grain and deep, velvety blacks.
WAY OUT: The dark lamp rim swings through the lens as someone knocks it, and the moving shadow carries the cut into the next shot.
WHY: Looking directly down turns a mob card game into an almost abstract wheel of coloured hats, a vantage that ordinary gangster pictures rarely use.

=== SHOT 4 ===
A long, low service tunnel of sooty vaulted brick behind the club, seen through a long telephoto lens from the far end, so that the depth is squeezed flat. Five musicians wait in single file along the right-hand wall between sets, stacked close by the compression. Nearest, big in frame and cropped at the knee, stands a broad woman in her forties in a dark green velvet coat over a satin gown, silver streak in her black hair, cigarette unlit between her fingers, jaw set, mouth closed, looking at nothing. Behind her is a stout older man polishing a trumpet bell on his sleeve, then a young man asleep on his feet with a clarinet under his arm, then a grey-haired woman in shirtsleeves tapping sticks against her thigh, and at the back a very tall thin man folded almost in half under a run of lagged pipes, holding the neck of an upright bass. Along the left side, an open coal furnace door is out of frame, and its orange firelight rakes across the bricks and up one side of each face. At the far end of the tunnel, a whole section of the brick wall has been rolled aside on iron rails, a hidden door standing open, and through the slot pours a hard blade of magenta and emerald light from the room beyond, laying a long coloured stripe down the wet flagstones toward us, broken by puddles. Steam drifts through the firelight. Dark brick swallows everything between the two light sources. The image carries all the way to the edges of the frame, with visible grain.
WAY OUT: A stagehand crosses very close to the lens, his dark coat wiping the frame, and the next shot opens as another figure or a passing car clears the camera.
WHY: The band waiting in a furnace-lit tunnel with the party a blade of colour behind a sliding brick wall shows the den's secret plumbing and gives these five people a tired, human moment.
