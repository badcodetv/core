I wrote one prompt, **axelrod-baroque-strings-chorus**. Its Style box is 950 characters, so it fits under Suno's 1,000-character limit.

**What shaped it**
- **Both takes drifted towards rap.** One AI listener heard a "UK rap vocal" over "boom-bap" drums. The other heard "spoken-word/rap" with the tin whistle reduced to "accents." So I kept "half-crooned, never rapped" word for word and added "boom bap" and "shouting" to the Exclude box. I also put "never shouted" next to the hook, so "sings out big" doesn't turn into yelling.
- **The new Axelrod drum sound.** Axelrod's *Song of Innocence* (1968) has Earl Palmer, Carol Kaye and Al Casey of the Wrecking Crew playing under big strings. It's listed as psychedelic R&B and baroque pop, and hip-hop producers have sampled its drums heavily. That's why I paired breakbeat drums with baroque strings and harpsichord. I couldn't find a source on how the drums were miked, so "close-miked" is my description, not a documented fact.
- **Choir and strings stay in the background.** Prompting guides say to keep one main genre and treat things like a choir as secondary touches, not a second genre. So the classical parts come last in the Style box, after the funk and dub layers. They're repeated as short bracketed directions in the Lyrics box, written so Suno won't sing them.
- **Settings.**
  - **Variety off** so Suno doesn't rewrite the Style box.
  - **Weirdness 40**, up from 35. Guides treat 50 as the normal middle setting, so this gives the strings and choir a little room to blend in without losing the hook.
  - **Style influence 75**, same as the reference.
  - **Duration 270 s**, same as before.
- **Unchanged.** The rest of the box's wording is kept, and every lyric word, spelling and line is as you gave it. Suno's public pages for both takes only show "psych, folk, funk" and Model V6, which matches the record.
