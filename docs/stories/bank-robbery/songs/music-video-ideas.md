# The Bank Robbery song — music video ideas

Jack, 2026-10-25: *"instead of going to suno, i want to start thinking about the music video. we have
enough songs i want to show kai, then we will decide. that being said, the lyrics and subject matter
remains the same. so what could the music video depict, it doesnt have to do with the bank robbery
story video, it can be its own creative thing."*

Lyrics: Kai's (`sealed-r5/lyrics.txt`). The song itself is not picked yet, so nothing here depends on
the sound; every idea works over psych funk, cop-show funk or the Brixton take. Nothing ruled,
nothing generated.

## What the lyrics give us to film

- **The printer in the basement** ("It happened in da basement", "Printer is burrrrning"): the hook
  is a place and a machine. Everything can hang off it.
- **"Call the police, it's a robbery!"**, but nobody broke in. A robbery with no break-in.
- **Grandma freezing, landlords needing, the shitty little job**: who it lands on.
- **Left and right both witness the theft, blaming each other**: the distraction. The film's own
  idea (left and right carrying one pallet) in song form.
- **Elastic cash, Pablo Escobar, "don't get high off your own supply"**: the gangster frame.
- **"Own the printer and you don't fear winter"**: who it lands on *well*. The beneficiary line.
- **"Don't just eat cake"**: the ending, hunger and revolution.

## The ideas

### 1. The Basement Party (upstairs / downstairs)

A split frame for the whole song: the ceiling is the line across the middle. **Downstairs**, under
the bank, a 1970s psychedelic funk party: suits dancing in the colour wash, the printer as the DJ
deck, notes falling like confetti. **Upstairs**, the same moment in a British flat: grandma in her
coat by a dead radiator, a red rent letter, frost on the window. Each chorus the party gets bigger and
the flat gets colder. At the end the ceiling cracks and the notes fall through as snow, worth nothing.

- **Why it works:** it *is* the hook ("it happened in da basement") and it names the beneficiary
  in every frame without a word. Grand, fun to watch, and the joke has a victim on screen, which the
  reader rules want (sarcasm paired with an undeniable consequence).
- **References:** Tame Impala's *Elephant* (colour bleeding over the performers, by Yoshi Sodeoka);
  Jungle's choreographed dance videos for the party half.
- **Cost:** two sets, both repeat, so Flow can keep them consistent. The split screen is a Premiere
  job, not a generation.
- **My pick for the strongest.**

### 2. Follow the Fiver (one long shot)

One brand-new note comes off the printer and the camera follows it, never cutting, as it changes
hands down the chain: banker → fund manager → landlord → estate agent → shop till → a worker's wage
packet. Each handover lands on a snare. Everyone it passes gets richer; prices rise behind it. By the
time it reaches the worker it buys a meal deal.

- **Why it works:** it shows *who gets the new money first* (the Cantillon effect, an 18th-century
  idea) without explaining it. The plainest version of the argument, and the hardest to dispute.
- **References:** Gondry's *Star Guitar* for the Chemical Brothers (things pass the window exactly on
  the beat; planned on graph paper); our own `continuous-shot` skill chains Omni clips into one.
- **Cost:** the most technical one. It is a run of chained clips with hidden joins.
- 🔴 **Careful with the claim:** the Bank of England's own evidence supports "big cash gains to people
  who were already wealthy", not "QE made inequality worse" (`research.md`). The video shows who got
  it first, and never puts a "QE caused…" caption on screen.

### 3. Sabotage, but the cops arrest the wrong people

A fake 1970s cop-show title sequence (*The Sweeney*, *Starsky & Hutch*): freeze frames with names,
car chases, flares, sideburns. "Call the police, it's a robbery!" sends two detectives screaming
across London. They kick in doors on a benefits claimant, a small-boat arrival, a bloke with a
telly. The real job is going on in the basement the whole time, and the cops drive past it in every
chase.

- **Why it works:** the cop-show funk is already one of the song's possible sounds; the chase is
  the distraction, made literal.
- **References:** Spike Jonze's *Sabotage* for the Beastie Boys (1994), cut as the credits of a made-up
  show; ours swaps LA for a grey 1970s London.
- **Cost:** cheap and very Flow-friendly (stylised, no lip-sync needed).
- 🔴 **Reader rule:** the boat arrival can't be the punchline. Make the cops look stupid, not the
  people they grab, and never deny the real costs of immigration (proportionality).

### 4. The Foreground Fight

A pub, a high street, a TV debate set: a left bloke and a right bloke brawl in the foreground, in
slow motion, beautifully lit, the whole song. In the background, out of focus, a man in a suit wheels
barrow after barrow of cash out of the bank and into a car (the BMW X8, plate `BAD C0DE`, if wanted).
Neither fighter ever turns round. One shot at the end pulls focus to the back.

- **Why it works:** "distraction IS the attraction / the intention is tension between the factions".
  It mocks both sides equally, like the film.
- **Reference:** Childish Gambino's *This Is America* (2018), which keeps the eye on the dancing
  while the background carries the real content. We borrow the depth trick, not the content.
- 🔴 **Reader rule:** never "you've been duped". The fighters are angry for real reasons; the joke is
  on who profits from the fight, not on them.

### 5. Weimar Wallpaper (Britain, 2026)

Real 1922–23 photos exist of German children building with worthless banknotes, flying a kite made
of them, using bundles as bricks. Restage them in a British estate today: kids building a fort of
fivers, grandma papering the hall with twenties, a dad feeding notes into the stove for heat
("grandma FREEZING", "own the printer and you don't fear winter").

- **Why it works:** it is the strongest single image for "debasement", and it is history, not a
  prediction. Very BadCode: the future that already went wrong.
- **Archive option:** cut real 1923 photos *next to* our shots, by the cut only (never AI people
  inside real footage). The photos need a licence check through `find-footage`; the "Germans used
  money as wallpaper" story I only found as a trivia claim, so it is unverified.
- 🔴 **Accuracy:** Britain is not in hyperinflation. Played as a warning from the future, not a
  report on now.

### 6. Elastic Cash (the Pablo verse as the whole video)

Pablo Escobar's brother Roberto, his accountant, wrote in his memoir that they spent about $2,500 a
month on rubber bands and wrote off around 10% of the cash every year as "spoilage": rats and damp.
Video: a vault so full nobody can count it. Rubber bands snapping, rats nesting in bundles, a
man buried up to his chest in notes, still printing.

- **Why it works:** "elastic bands not plastic hands" and "don't get high off your own supply" are
  in the lyrics already, and the facts are funnier than anything we'd invent.
- **Facts:** the $2,500 and the 10% are from Roberto Escobar's own book only, unconfirmed elsewhere.
- 🔴 **Two traps:** don't show a real Escobar or Biggie face (a stylised "Pablo" figure only). And
  **never make the bankers into rats**: bankers-as-vermin is an old antisemitic image. The rats eat
  the money; they never stand for the people.

### 7. The Ending, for any of these: cake

The last verse goes to "hungry people feed a revolution" and "don't just eat cake". Marie
Antoinette almost certainly never said "let them eat cake"; it traces back to Rousseau, written when
she was a child. An ending card that knows that: a crowd at the gates, a tiered cake on a silver
tray, and a caption from the narrator correcting the history. It suits the voice: a narrator from
the future with total authority.

### 8. The Speakeasy Mint (Jack, 2026-10-25) — idea 3 grown up

Jack: *"parody USA's prohibition speakeasys and we do it in the style of Cuphead the game, where
basically gangsters print money in a speakeasy, the mayor is there and it is kind of like the fat
tonys basement in the simpsons but with money printing machines … following police cars and protests
above and it keeps panning down seeing the side profile of a building (maybe a government building
or bank or something, maybe have it disguised as something else though)."*

**The shot:** the building as a cross-section, like a doll's house with the side wall off. The camera
starts at street level (sirens, protest, left and right crowds fighting) and drops one floor every
chorus. Verses ride back up to the street. The last chorus finally reaches the bottom of the basement.

**The front: a bakery.** "Earning that dough", ovens that burn like the printer, bread for the street
and cake downstairs, and the cake ending (idea 7) comes free. Other fronts if not: a pawnbroker, a
funeral parlour, a church hall.

**Gags it gives us:**
- The password at the door slot is "quantitative easing".
- The raid: a cop knocks, someone pulls a lever, the presses flip round into bread ovens. The cops
  leave with a free loaf. Real Prohibition trick: the 21 Club in New York had a button that sent every
  bottle down a chute into the sewer, and a 2½-ton cellar door that looked like a wall.
- 🔑 **The 21 Club's best trick:** its cellar was really the basement of No. 19 next door, so the
  staff could truthfully say there was no liquor in the building. That is our joke exactly: "we're
  not printing money, it's quantitative easing."
- **The leaders of the left crowd and the right crowd dance together downstairs** while their
  supporters fight on the street. "The right and the left both witness the theft" and "divide and
  conquer", in one picture, and it mocks both sides the same.
- A furnace down there burns banknotes for heat and the heat never rises. Grandma freezes on the
  top floor (it's the Basement Party, idea 1, turned on its side).

**Why it works:** the US setting suits lyrics that say "the Fed" and "dollars" (and Pablo and Biggie
are American too); a cartoon is a step away from real politics, so the satire can go harder;
1930s cartoon jazz and psych funk share a swing.

**The style: 1930s rubber-hose, not Cuphead.**
- Cuphead is a homage to Fleischer Studios (Betty Boop, early Popeye) and early Disney, and its makers
  are famous for hand-drawing every frame. An AI video "in the style of Cuphead" is exactly what sparked
  the 2025 Ghibli backlash: AI copying a living studio's hand-drawn look. 🔴 It hands the anti-AI crowd
  the story about us (`docs/using-ai.md`). Prompt for the 1930s source, never the game's name, logos
  or title cards.
- 🔴 Watch the caricatures. 1930s cartoons carry minstrel-style faces, and a fat cigar banker can drift
  into an antisemitic caricature. Check every frame for both.
- ⬜ **Unverified:** whether Nano Banana and Omni can hold a rubber-hose look (bouncy limbs, ink line,
  sepia film grain) through motion. Nothing in the repo tests it. One still would answer it.

**How it would be made (lanes):** stack the floors as tall stills; Premiere moves the camera down the
side of the building (exact and smooth, `hybrid-method.md`); each window plays its own Omni loop.
All of it is AI, so the no-AI-in-real-footage rule doesn't come into it.

## Lines we must not cross (from the repo)

- **No AI people inside real footage.** Real archive and AI shots join by the cut only.
- **Flow video is silent, no dialogue.** Singing lip-sync would be a new exception for Jack to rule;
  every idea above works without it.
- **Name the beneficiary** in every idea (who owns the printer), and mock left and right equally.
- **Don't claim QE caused the inflation or the inequality** (`research.md`, "the three things").
- **The song says "the Fed" and "dollars"; the reader is British.** The pictures can be
  British (the Bank of England, a UK flat) without changing a word. Worth Kai's view.
- "Money printer go brrr" is a 2020 internet meme with crypto and gold-bug overtones. If our printer
  says *brrr*, people will read it as that camp.
