# The crew — the eleven

**First pass, 6 October 2026, not ruled.** One sheet for all eleven until the cast settles; split
into a file each when it does.

**They are types, not people.** No one here is a named living person. The house ruling is to name
institutions, mechanisms and public figures only in their public, checkable acts
([`principles.md`](../../../story-craft/principles.md), ruling 13). Whether any of the eleven
should look like someone real is an open call in [`story.md`](../story.md).

**Each one gets one moment of being right** (principle 4: every antagonist gets a scene of real
interiority). They are not evil. They each have a good reason, and the reasons add up to a
robbery.

## The Ocean's Eleven jobs

| # | Name | The heist job | Who they are | Recruited | Their good reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Ex** | The mastermind | A former Chancellor, just "out" | He recruits the rest | "The alternative was worse." He may even be right |
| 2 | **The Fixer** | The right-hand man, always eating | A lobbyist who used to be an adviser | At lunch. It is always lunch | "I just introduce people" |
| 3 | **The Donor** | The bankroll | Owns the houses and the shares already | By his pool. Says no, then asks what the return is | "I'm the one taking the risk" |
| 4 | **The Governor** | The inside man | Runs the bank. Has the key | In his own office, where he pretends not to know them | "My job is the number, not who gets it" |
| 5 | **The Proprietor** | Explosives | Owns newspapers. Headlines are his charges: big bang, lots of smoke, no structural damage | In a print hall, testing a front page | "I print what sells. You bought it" |
| 6 | **The Presenter** | The face, the con man | A broadcaster famous for balance. Always hears both sides of the wrong argument | In a make-up chair | "It's not my job to have a view" |
| 7 | **The Platform** | The tech | Owns the feed. Does not cut the cameras, points them | Never in the room. A face on a laptop | "I just show people what they want" |
| 8 | **The Accountant** | The grease man | Can fit through a loophole the size of a letterbox | Found folded inside a filing cabinet | "It is all perfectly legal." It is |
| 9 | **Mr Blue** | Driver | A politician of the right | In a TV studio, mid-row with Mr Red | "Somebody has to say it" |
| 10 | **Mr Red** | Driver | A politician of the left | Same studio, same row, same taxi home | "Somebody has to say you can't say that" |
| 11 | **Mr Turquoise** | Nobody asked him | The outsider who is not like the other robbers. Same suit, bigger cut | Invites himself. Films it | "I'm the only one telling the truth about this robbery" |

**Mr Blue and Mr Red are the bickering brothers** from Ocean's Eleven (the two drivers who never
stop needling each other). They went to the same school. They share a taxi. They drive one van.

## Taking the piss equally (the rule from 6 October)

Every joke on one of them is paid for with a joke on the other, in the same scene.

| | Mr Blue (right) | Mr Red (left) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why he does not tip Denise | "She should get a better job" | "Tipping lets her employer off the hook. I support her structurally" |
| What Denise gets | Nothing | Nothing |
| His objection to the robbery | The crew is too big | The crew is not diverse enough |
| How he argues | Louder | Longer |
| What he says about the other | "Your lot were in charge for thirteen years" | "Your lot were in charge for fourteen" |
| In the vault | Carries the left-hand end of the pallet | Carries the right-hand end of the same pallet |
| What he does when a camera appears | Starts the row | Starts the row |
| His cut | The same | The same |

The two "in charge" lines are checkable: Labour governed 1997 to 2010 and the Conservatives
2010 to 2024.

**The media get the same treatment.** The Proprietor makes the noise. The Presenter gives the
noise two chairs and a clock, and calls it balance. Neither points a camera at the vault.

**Mr Turquoise is the third target, not a referee.** He mocks both of the others and takes his
share. The reader this work is for may be drifting toward his party
([`the-reader.md`](../../../marketing/the-reader.md)), so the joke is always on the man in the
suit, never on anyone who votes for him. Open call in [`story.md`](../story.md).
