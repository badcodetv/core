## Why the money goes in (approved by Jack, 6 October 2026)

Jack: "I like this, add it to the storyboard."

- **A normal robbery takes money out.** Someone notices, and someone gets arrested.
- **This one makes new money and puts it in.** Nobody's account goes down by a penny, so nobody
  can say they were robbed.
- **But with more money about, each pound buys less.** Denise's savings are the same number and
  no longer cover the flat she was saving for.
- **The new money reached the crew first.** They used it to buy houses and shares before prices
  rose, which is why keys come out.
- **That is the debasement,** shown as a picture instead of explained.

## Scene by scene

### 1. Breakfast

- **Borrowed:** the opening of Reservoir Dogs, where the crew argues about tipping.
- **We see:** a greasy-spoon café, morning. Eleven people in black suits crammed round pushed-
  together tables, full English breakfasts, a red sauce bottle and a brown one. Denise clears
  plates round them. A television on the wall shows a small boat, on a loop.
- **We hear:** the bill arrives. Nobody reaches for it.
  - Mr Blue: "I don't tip. She wants more money, she can get a better job."
  - Mr Red: "That is a disgusting thing to say. I don't tip because tipping lets her employer
    off the hook. I support her structurally."
  - Mr Turquoise: "See, this is what's wrong with both of you." He does not tip either.
  - The Ex, to Denise, warmly: "Could we get separate receipts?"
- **The laugh:** two opposite speeches and one empty saucer. Hold on the saucer.
- **Denise:** counts the plates under her breath. "Eleven."
- **Why it is here:** this is the whole film in one scene. Everything after is the same thing,
  bigger.
- **To decide:** does anyone say what they do for a living? Better if not.

### 2. The spoiler

- **We see:** Denise locks the café and walks to her second job, down a high street where half
  the shops are shut. Behind her, small, eleven suits get into one van.
- **We hear:** music. Then **N**: "They got away with it. I'm telling you now so you can stop
  hoping. I'm from after. Nobody goes to prison in this one."
- **Moved:** the slow walk in black suits used to be here. It is now scene 9, where it has
  somewhere to walk.
- **The laugh:** the narrator spoils the film in the titles.
- **The title card.**
- **Why it is here:** the audience knows the ending, so every scene after is watched for *how*
  (story-craft principle 10: tell them about the bomb).
- **To decide:** the music. Reservoir Dogs uses a real 1970 record; ours has to be made or
  cleared.

### 3. Getting out

- **Borrowed:** Ocean's Eleven opens on Danny Ocean's parole hearing.
- **We see:** The Ex in a chair facing a panel we do not see. Card: THREE WEEKS EARLIER. It is
  shot like a prison parole board. Pull back: it is a select committee room, and he is resigning.
- **We hear:**
  - Panel: "If you were released, what do you think you would do?"
  - The Ex: "Spend more time with my family." Beat. "And some consultancy."
- **Then:** he walks out of the building holding a clear plastic bag of his belongings, as
  prisoners do. In it: a phone, a tie, a red ministerial box.
- **The laugh:** leaving office looks exactly like leaving prison, except the car is nicer.
- **To decide:** how much of his past we show. Suggestion: none. He is "The Ex".

### 4. The recruiting

- **Borrowed:** the Ocean's Eleven montage. Each specialist is found doing their trade, with a
  freeze-frame and a name card.
- **How each card works:** NAME, then THE JOB in heist language, then one line of what they
  really do. Fast. About five seconds each.

| Card | Found | The line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| THE FIXER — "knows everyone" | At lunch. He is at lunch in every shot of the film | "I don't do anything. I introduce people" |
| THE DONOR — "the bankroll" | By a pool | "No. Absolutely not." Beat. "What's the return?" |
| THE GOVERNOR — "the inside man" | In his office at the bank | "I can't be seen with you." He is talking to them through his open door |
| THE PROPRIETOR — "explosives" | A print hall. He holds up tomorrow's front page like a stick of dynamite | "Big bang. Lots of smoke. No structural damage" |
| THE PRESENTER — "the face" | A make-up chair | "I couldn't possibly take a side." "There's two sides." "Lovely. I'll have both" |
| THE PLATFORM — "the tech" | A face on a laptop that someone carries about | "I don't cut the cameras. I just choose what's on them" |
| THE ACCOUNTANT — "the grease man" | Unfolds himself out of a filing cabinet | "It's all perfectly legal." "We haven't told you what it is." "It will be" |
| MR BLUE and MR RED — "the drivers" | A television studio, shouting at each other across a desk | See below |

- **The drivers, the best gag in the montage:** they are mid-row on live television, properly
  furious. The floor manager calls the ad break. They stop, in the middle of a word. One passes
  the other his coat. They share a taxi. In the back, The Ex is waiting.
  - The Ex: "I need two people who can start a fight in an empty room."
  - Mr Red: "We're not together."
  - Mr Blue: "We've never been together."
  - They have the same school tie in their pockets.
- **Mr Turquoise** is not recruited. He is simply in the next shot, filming himself. "They
  didn't want you to see this."
- **Why it is here:** this is the trick being run where we can see it, on somebody else. Nobody
  in the audience is accused of falling for it.
- **To decide:** eleven is a lot of faces. If it has to shrink, the Fixer and the Accountant
  merge first.

### 5. The plan

- **Borrowed:** the heist briefing. (The "plan over a model" is my memory of the genre, not a
  confirmed Ocean's Eleven scene.)
- **We see:** a lock-up garage, one bare bulb, a corkboard with red string (*Seed*). On a
  trestle table, a model. Not of the bank: of the whole town. Little houses, each with a price
  tag on a cocktail stick. A tiny café with a tiny flat above it. One tiny figure with a hoover.
- **We hear:**
  - The Ex: "The biggest vault in the city. Everyone's life savings. Right there." (*Seed*)
  - The Governor: "The security is not the problem. I am the security."
  - The Ex: "The problem is eyes. Too many people looking at it." (*Seed, shortened*)
  - He turns to the Proprietor, the Presenter and the laptop. "So we give them something else
    to look at." He turns to Mr Red and Mr Blue, who are already arguing. "And somebody to
    watch."
  - The Donor: "And how much do we take?"
  - The Ex: "Nothing. We don't take anything out. We put it in."
  - Silence. The Accountant starts, very slowly, to smile.
- **Then:** the Fixer points at the tiny figure. "And her?" The Ex: "She keeps every penny."
  The Donor: "You bastard." It is a compliment.
- **Then, the distraction is made in front of us** (borrowed from Wag the Dog, where a fake war
  is produced in a studio):
  - The Proprietor pins up one photograph: a small boat on a grey sea. It is the picture from
    the café television.
  - He types a headline under it: **THEY'RE LETTING THEM IN.** Send. On the model, the
    little houses on one side of the street light up blue.
  - The Platform, from the laptop, puts a second headline under the same photograph: **WE
    DESTROYED THEIR HOMES. NOW YOU TURN THE VICTIMS AWAY.** Send. The other side of the street
    lights up red.
  - Mr Red: "Which one's true?"
  - The Platform: "Doesn't matter. I don't need them to believe it. I need them to reply."
  - The Ex moves two little crowds of figures along the model's high street until they meet
    outside the bank. Then he runs his finger down the gap between them, to the door.
- **The headlines are Jack's, 6 October 2026:** "they are letting them in" for the right, and
  for the left "we destroyed their home, now you turn the victims away". The wording above is
  tidied from that and is his to change. Same picture, two furies, and each one is aimed at the
  other crowd. The pairs considered before are under "The two headlines" below.

### 6. The colours

- **Borrowed:** the Reservoir Dogs codename scene, where Mr Pink objects to his name.
- **We see:** the same garage. The Ex hands out names from a list.
- **We hear:**
  - The Ex: "Mr Blue. Mr Red." They both nod. They like their colours.
  - "Mr Turquoise."
  - Mr Turquoise: "Why am I Mr Turquoise?"
  - The Ex: "Because blue was taken."
  - Mr Turquoise: "This is exactly what the establishment does. I'm the only one here who isn't
    a robber."
  - Mr Blue: "You're in the van."
  - Mr Turquoise: "I'm in the van *reluctantly*."
- **Then the real joke:** Red and Blue begin rehearsing the row and cannot stop. "Your lot had
  thirteen years." "Your lot had fourteen." The Ex says "cut". They carry on. The others leave.
  The light goes off. In the dark: "Thirteen." "Fourteen."
- **The laugh:** they were hired to fake a row and they have forgotten it is fake.
- **Rule check:** the row has no content. Numbers of years, "your lot", and nothing about any
  group of people.

### 7. The night before

- **Borrowed:** Killing Zoe. The night before the job, the crew gets wrecked instead of
  planning.
- **We see:** a summer party in a marquee on the Donor's lawn. Champagne, a string quartet, a
  chocolate fountain. Fast, ugly, hand-held, the only scene shot this way. By two in the
  morning Mr Red and Mr Blue have their arms round each other, singing their school song.
- **Cut against:** Denise, alone, hoovering the empty banking hall. Tidy, still, quiet. She
  counts the floor tiles as she goes.
- **We hear:** almost nothing we can make out. One clear line, from the Presenter to the
  Proprietor: "The thing is, off the record, I agree with all of you."
- **The laugh:** off camera they get on beautifully.
- **Why it is here:** it proves the row is a job. And it puts Denise in the bank before they
  arrive.
- **To decide:** how squalid. Killing Zoe is heroin in a cellar. Ours is canapés, which may be
  funnier.

### 8. The march

- **New, 6 October 2026.** The distraction, working.
- **We see:** card: BANK HOLIDAY MONDAY. The high street from scene 2. From one end, a march
  with red placards. From the other, a march with blue. They are the café's regulars: we
  recognise faces from scene 1's background.
- **Then:** police carry metal barriers in and build two lines down the middle of the road. An
  empty strip of tarmac is left between the crowds. It runs to the bank's front door.
- **Hold on two placards,** one each side of the gap. Both start with the same line: WE CAN'T
  AFFORD TO LIVE HERE. The last line is different on each, and the barrier hides it.
- **On two small stages,** Mr Blue and Mr Red, one per crowd, each with a megaphone. We hear
  the same half-sentence from both: "And I'll tell you whose fault it is—". Then each points
  across the gap at the other crowd.
- **The Presenter,** live to camera with his back to the bank: "Tensions are running high here.
  We'll be hearing from both sides."
- **The laugh:** the two signs.
- **Rules for this scene:**
  - The crowds are right to be angry. Nobody in them is stupid, ugly or drunk.
  - Same number of people each side, same lens, same time on screen.
  - A protest with shoving, not a riot. Nothing burns.
  - No real march, town, group or event is shown or named.
- **To decide:** do we ever read the last line of either sign? Suggestion: no.

### 9. The walk down the gap

- **Borrowed:** the slow walk in black suits from Reservoir Dogs, and the holiday job from
  Killing Zoe.
- **We see:** an officer lifts a barrier. The crew walks down the empty strip towards us, in
  slow motion, black suits, pushing pallets of new banknotes. Shouting crowds either side,
  every face and every phone turned to the other crowd. Nobody looks at the pallets.
- **Then:** Mr Blue and Mr Red climb down off their stages, step over the barriers into the
  gap, and each take one end of the same pallet. Still arguing.
- **We hear:**
  - The officer: "Mind how you go, sir. It's mad out here."
  - **N**: "In the films, this is the part with the lasers."
- **At the door:** they pull on carnival masks, having just walked barefaced past two thousand
  people. The masks are of each other. The security guard holds the door open. "Morning,
  Governor."
- **Inside, it falls apart anyway:** Red and Blue carry on the row in the empty lobby, for
  nobody. The Presenter sets two chairs between them and times them with a stopwatch. Mr
  Turquoise fires into the ceiling for attention, then checks his phone to see if it got any. A
  pallet tips over. The Fixer is eating.
- **The laugh:** the officer lifts the barrier.
- **Why it is here:** this is the distraction in one picture. The police are guarding the gap,
  so they are guarding the robbers.
- **Rule check:** money is coming *in*. Every pallet in shot is going towards the vault.
- **The noise carries on through scenes 10 to 12,** muffled, through the bank's walls.

### 10. The hoover *(midpoint)*

- **Borrowed:** in Killing Zoe, Zoe turns out to work in the bank.
- **We see:** a door opens at the back. Denise, in a tabard, pulling a hoover. Eleven people
  swing round. Eleven guns on her. Silence.
- **We hear:**
  - Denise looks at the masks. Looks at the shoes. "Full English, no tip." She knows exactly
    who they are.
  - Nobody moves.
  - Denise: "Lift your feet."
  - They lift their feet. She hoovers under them.
- **Then:** Red and Blue each try to sign her up.
  - Mr Blue: "You know whose fault this is."
  - Mr Red: "Don't you dare speak for her."
  - Denise: "Our road's changed. Rents went up. Some of that's real." She holds a finger and
    thumb a little apart. "It's about that much." She looks past them at the pallets going down
    the corridor. "What's all that, then?"
  - *(The receipt: the government's migration advisers found the effect on wages "generally
    small" and that migration "has increased house prices". See [`research.md`](./research.md).)*
  - Nobody answers her. They go back to arguing with each other.
- **Then:** she watches the bags go past. Her lips move. She is counting. She frowns: they are
  going the wrong way.
- **The laugh:** eleven armed robbers lifting their feet.
- **What turns:** it looked like they were caught. They were not even seen as unusual. She has
  cleaned round rooms like this for twenty years.
- **Why it is here:** the one line of proportion on immigration, from the hero, in her own
  words. Not denial, and not a lecture.
- **To decide:** this is the most delicate line in the film. It needs Jack's and Kai's eyes,
  and the size she shows with her fingers has to be honest.

### 11. The vault

- **We see:** the vault door, enormous. The Accountant is drilling it, in goggles, sweating.
  The Governor leans past him and opens it with a key from his own pocket.
  - The Accountant: "Why am I drilling?"
  - The Governor: "It looks better."
- **Then:** the pallets go in. New notes, still warm, stacked next to the old. The Donor walks
  along the safe-deposit boxes with a carrier bag and collects one thing from each: a house
  key.
- **Cut to the model, in the empty garage:** every price tag on every little house flips over
  to a bigger number, one after another, like a departures board. The tag on the flat above the
  café flips last.
- **We hear:** the guard, from the door: "You're putting it *in*?" The Ex: "Count it in the
  morning. It'll all be there."
- **N**: "And it was."
- **The laugh:** "It looks better."
- **Kept from the seed:** the reveal that they are not robbing the bank, they *are* the bank.
  Here it is one key in one pocket, not a twist.

### 12. The standoff *(lowest point)*

- **Borrowed:** the three-way standoff in Reservoir Dogs, and its question: who is the rat?
- **We see:** an alarm goes off. Panic. Red has a gun on Blue. Blue on Red. Turquoise has one
  on each of them and is filming with his chin. The Proprietor has one on the Presenter. The
  Presenter is pointing two guns, one at each side, for balance.
- **We hear:** "Who talked?" "He talked." "Your lot always talk."
- **Then:** nothing. No sirens. The alarm stops. It was Denise's hoover, tripping a sensor.
  - Mr Blue: "So nobody's coming?"
  - The Governor: "Why would anybody come? It's policy."
  - They lower the guns. They look slightly disappointed.
- **Cut to Denise,** in the staff room. Through the window, across the road, a SOLD sign is
  going up on the flat above the café. *That is the lowest point.*
- **Then, a separate beat, with no sound at all:** she takes the passbook from her apron and
  counts it. Every page. It is all there. She counts it again.
- **The laugh:** "It's policy." There is none after that.
- **Removed 6 October 2026 (Jack):** the narrator's line naming who gained, £3,000 against
  £350,000. The scene now ends on Denise counting, in silence.
- **What that leaves open:** nobody in the film now says who gained. The picture still shows it
  (the Donor's bag of keys in scenes 11 and 13, the SOLD sign here). The house rule is that the
  beneficiary is named once in words ([`principles.md`](../../story-craft/principles.md), 16), so
  this is a known gap for Jack and Kai to rule on, not an oversight.

### 13. The fountain

- **Borrowed:** the ending of Ocean's Eleven. The crew watches the fountains in silence and
  leaves one by one.
- **We see:** the eleven in a line, looking at a fountain. It is the small municipal one in
  the town's shopping precinct, and it is switched off. A pigeon. Piano music anyway.
- **Then:** they leave, one at a time, without a word. The Donor is carrying the bag of keys.
  Last to go: Mr Red and Mr Blue, who get into the same car. A man with a camera comes round
  the corner. Both windows go down. "Your lot—"
- **The laugh:** the fountain is off.
- **Why it is here:** the one beat of scale the film allows itself. Without it the piece goes
  from the vault straight to the café.

### 14. One bill

- **Borrowed:** Reservoir Dogs' breakfast, again.
- **We see:** the same café, the same table, the same eleven, the next morning. Denise is
  rubbing out the prices on the chalkboard and writing new ones. The television shows the small
  boat. The bill arrives.
- **We hear:**
  - Mr Blue: "I don't tip."
  - Mr Red: "Nor do I, but for completely different reasons."
  - The Ex: "Separate receipts, please."
  - Denise: "No."
  - She puts down one bill. It is long. At the bottom, in biro, one extra line. We do not see
    the number.
  - Denise: "I counted it last night. It's all there. That's how I know it was you. Burglars
    take things."
  - The Governor: "In fairness, everyone went up by the same percentage."
  - Denise: "Ten per cent of fuck all is fuck all."
  - Beat.
  - Denise: "One bill. You were all at the same table."
- **Then:** she reaches up and turns the television off. Behind her the table erupts. For the
  first time they are arguing about who pays.
- **N**: "In my timeline nobody counted. She counted. I notice that is new."
- **N**, last line: "Enjoy arguing about immigration, gentlemen. I'm sure it'll fix her rent."
  (*Seed, turned to face the crew instead of the viewer.*)
- **Last picture:** the saucer. This time there is a bill on it.
- **Why it is here:** the first picture was an empty saucer. The story ends on the row, still
  going, now pointed at the right thing.
- **The Governor's line is the Bank's real defence** (its 2018 paper: gains were "broadly
  similar" in percentage terms, so inequality as measured did not rise). Jack, 6 October 2026:
  "a ridiculous false equivalency". So it is in the film as the excuse, and Denise answers it.
- **To decide:** do we see the number on the bill? Suggestion: no.

## The two headlines (scene 5): options

Same photograph every time. **Chosen by Jack, 6 October 2026:**

- **To the blue feed:** THEY'RE LETTING THEM IN
- **To the red feed:** WE DESTROYED THEIR HOMES. NOW YOU TURN THE VICTIMS AWAY

The pairs below were offered first and are kept for the record.

| | To the blue feed | To the red feed | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | WHY WON'T THE LEFT ADMIT IT? | WHY WON'T THE RIGHT ADMIT IT? | One word changed, so the trick shows instantly. Says nothing about migrants | Does not say "immigration" out loud |
| B | THEY'RE LETTING THEM IN | THEY'RE LETTING THEM DROWN | Spells the subject out. Equal on both sides. "They" is the other side each time | Closest to real headlines, so the most likely to start the real argument in the comments |
| C | LOOK WHAT THEY'RE CALLING YOU NOW | LOOK WHAT THEY'RE CALLING YOU NOW | Identical. The funniest version of "it doesn't matter what it says" | Loses the red and blue difference |
| D | YOU WON'T BELIEVE WHAT ⬛⬛⬛⬛ DID | YOU WON'T BELIEVE WHAT ⬛⬛⬛⬛ DID | From the seed: "everyone fills in their own them" | A blank is harder to read at speed |

**Not offered:** any headline that states a claim about migrants, true or false. Repeating one,
even as a joke, makes it more familiar.

## Pictures to get right later (notes for `shot-craft`, not prompts)

- **Every wide shot of the crew needs a cost in it:** the shut shops behind the crowds in scene 9, Denise small in
