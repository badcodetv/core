## What was asked for (6 October 2026)

- The powerful are debasing our currency whilst stealing it: a bank robbery.
- A Reservoir Dogs scenario with politicians and powerful media people preparing for the job.
- Ocean's Eleven: they are recruited in the cheesy heist-movie way.
- Left and right taken the piss out of equally, squabbling over immigration and other
  distractions whilst they literally rob a bank.
- Also like Killing Zoe.

## Key concept

**The robbery works because the row is louder than the vault.**

Stated once here and never in the story. The opposite idea, which the story has to let breathe
before it loses: *we are poorer because of each other.*

## Background (what is true, and how sure we are)

Full receipts in [`research.md`](./research.md). The five that carry the story:

- The Bank of England created £895bn and bought assets with it.
- The Bank's own 2012 paper: the first £325bn was worth "around £10,000 per person if assets
  were evenly distributed". They were not. The top 5% of households held 40% of them.
- The Bank's own 2018 paper: the poorest tenth gained about £3,000; the wealthiest tenth about
  £350,000.
- Three companies control 90% of national newspaper circulation.
- Immigration is the country's top worry (42%) and the economy is second (32%). The official
  advisers found its effect on wages real, small, and falling on the lower paid.

## The twist: nothing is taken

**They break into the bank to put money in.** Bags go in. Nothing comes out except a bag of
house keys.

In the morning every account holds exactly what it held the night before. That is how the
cleaner knows it was not burglars. Burglars take things.

This replaces the seed's reveal ("the garage was always a boardroom, they are the bank"). The
seed's reveal is kept as a smaller beat: the Governor has the key and they drill anyway.

## The one object

**Denise's passbook.** She is saving the deposit for the flat above the café. The number in the
book is the same on the last page as on the first. The key to the flat leaves in the Donor's bag.

Two real numbers stand behind it: £3,000 and £350,000. **They are background only and are not
spoken or shown** (Jack, 6 October 2026).

## The three films, and what each gives us

| Film | What we take | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reservoir Dogs** | The breakfast and the tip. Colour codenames, which here are party colours. The slow walk in black suits. The standoff over who the rat is | Scenes 1, 6, 9, 12 |
| **Ocean's Eleven** | The mastermind just "out". The recruitment montage with name cards. The plan over a model. The silent fountain | Scenes 3, 4, 5, 13 |
| **Killing Zoe** | The night before, spent getting wrecked instead of planning. A job with no discipline at all. The woman who turns out to work in the bank. A bank holiday | Scenes 7, 9, 10 |

Only Killing Zoe is set in a bank. The other two lend their shape, not their target.

## Beats (joined by "but" and "therefore")

1. Eleven people in suits have breakfast and argue about why they will not tip the waitress.
   Left and right have different reasons. She gets nothing.
2. **Therefore** we are told what kind of people these are, and the narrator tells us the
   ending: they got away with it.
3. **So how?** Three weeks earlier, a man leaves office and starts recruiting.
4. He has a plan, **but** the problem is not the locks. It is that everyone is looking at the
   bank.
5. **Therefore** he hires the people who decide what everyone looks at, and two men who can
   start a row anywhere.
6. They are given colours. **But** the two men are so good at the row that they cannot stop,
   even with nobody watching.
7. **Therefore** the night before the job is not a briefing, it is a party.
8. The two headlines work. Two marches meet outside the bank, **therefore** the police build a
   barrier between them, and the empty strip between leads to the bank's door.
9. **Therefore** the crew walks down it, hung over, with pallets, and nobody looks. **But** the
   cleaner walks in with a hoover. *(The midpoint. It looks like they are caught.)*
10. **But** she has cleaned round meetings like this for twenty years, and asks them to lift
    their feet. **Therefore** they carry on, and she is the only one who sees which way the
    bags are going.
11. The Governor opens the vault with his own key. The money goes in. On the model, every
    price goes up. The keys come out.
12. An alarm. Everyone pulls a gun on whoever they already hated. **But** nobody comes. It is
    not a crime. *(The lowest point: Denise's flat is sold. Then, separately, her grief: she
    counts the book again, in silence.)*
13. **Therefore** they walk out of the front door and watch the fountain.
14. Breakfast again. They argue about the tip again. **But** this time Denise brings one bill
    for the whole table.

**The last beat was fixed first:** "One bill. You were all at the same table."

## The ending

- **Denise acts.** She does not make a speech. She puts down one bill, and the crew starts
  arguing about who pays it. The row is now about the right thing.
- **Nobody names who gained in words.** The narrator's line with the two figures was removed
  on 6 October 2026 (Jack). The Donor's bag of keys carries it in pictures.
- **The hope line** (the house ruling says it may be dry but may not be absent): "In my
  timeline nobody counted. She counted."
- **The closer,** the seed's line turned to face the crew instead of the viewer: "Enjoy arguing
  about immigration, gentlemen. I'm sure it'll fix her rent."

## The rules this story runs on

1. **The crowd is right to be angry, and the crew is who we laugh at.** Changed 6 October 2026
   (Jack): the public row is now on screen, as two marches. We watch the crew make it (scene 5)
   before we watch it work (scene 8), so the trick is shown being built, never being fallen for
   ([`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md), rule 2). Both crowds have been robbed and
   both know it.
2. **Left and right are mocked in the same scene, to the same depth,** and so are the two kinds
   of media. The table is in [`characters/the-crew.md`](./characters/the-crew.md).
3. **The row has no content.** We hear "your lot" and "thirteen years" and "fourteen years".
   We never hear a claim about immigrants stated so it can be knocked down (rule 10).
4. **Immigration is never called fake.** Denise says her street has changed and says how big
   that is next to the vault (rule 4).
5. **Money in, not money out.** Every picture of the robbery obeys it.
6. **Nobody says "inequality rose because of QE".** We say who got how much. See
   "What we may not say".
7. **No real living person is named or drawn.**
8. **Nobody is killed.** Killing Zoe's body count becomes something worse for this crew: nobody
   cares enough to stop them.

## What we may and may not say

| We may say | We may not say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Bank made £895bn and bought assets with it | "They printed £895bn and gave it to the rich" | It bought bonds. The gain came through prices |
| The poorest tenth gained about £3,000 and the wealthiest tenth about £350,000 | "QE made inequality worse" | The Bank's paper found similar gains in percentage terms and says it did not |
| It would have been £10,000 each if evenly shared | "You were robbed of £10,000" | It is the Bank's own illustration, not a debt |
| Pay is about £11,000 a year below where the old trend would have put it | "Your pay fell by £11,000" | It is a gap against a trend |
| The Lords said the Bank was "widely perceived" to be funding the deficit | "The Bank funded the government" | "Perceived" is their word |
| Her street has changed, and that is the smaller part | "Immigration has no cost" | The official advisers found small real costs |
| A pound buys less | Any figure for how much less | Not checked yet |

**The "same percentage" defence is a target, not a limit** (Jack, 6 October 2026: "a ridiculous
false equivalency"). We do not repeat the Bank's conclusion as ours. We put it in the Governor's
mouth and let Denise answer it (scene 14).

**The receipts since Covid** were researched on 6 October 2026 and are in
[`research.md`](./research.md), second pass. They change what we may say:

| We may say | We may not say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything costs a third more than in 2020. Food nearly 40%. Rent 37% | "Pay has not kept up with prices since Covid" | Average pay has now passed prices. It fell behind in 2022 to 2023 |
| Average pay is about 5% above 2008, eighteen years on | "Wages are lower than 2008" | Only the middle earner's figure says that, and it is not opened yet |
| Destitution more than doubled from 2017 to 2022 | "Everyone is suffering" as a statistic | It is a feeling the story can show, not a number |
| The banks made about £45bn a year and are paid interest on the money that was made | "The rich got richer since 2021" | Measured wealth fell by £2.1 trillion when rates rose |
| They have stopped taking the money back, and are keeping £120bn of it for good | "They are doing stealth QE" | The Bank is not buying. It is selling more slowly |
| The Bank's own staff put about 2 points of the 2021 inflation on QE | "QE caused the inflation" | The same paper puts 3 points on food and energy |
| Three in four people back a 2% tax on wealth over £10m, worth about £24bn a year | That it is costless | The tax experts warn about people leaving |

**What it means for the film:** the robbery (the money made, and who it reached first) is
2009 to 2021. What Denise lives with now is the bill: a third on everything, rent up 37%, and
the banks paid interest on the proceeds. The story should keep those two apart.

## Open calls (for Jack and Kai)

1. **Does this contradict The Magic Money Tree?** That story says making money is fine if you
   plant it. This one could be heard as "making money is theft". The reading used here keeps them
   on the same side: the crime is not making it, it is **who gets it first**. In the three verbs,
   this is a film about shaking. Needs a ruling before any script.
2. **Types or real faces?** Written as types. Real public figures are allowed only in their
   public, checkable acts (ruling 13), and a heist is not one.
3. **Mr Turquoise.** The reader may be drifting toward his party. He is in because leaving him
   out would not be equal. The joke stays on the man, never his voters. Keep, soften or cut?
4. **"Debasing the currency."** The story shows it (the same number buys less) and never
   argues it. The word itself is contested among economists. Is showing it enough?
5. **Money in, or notes trimmed?** The other literal picture of debasement is shaving every note
   and keeping the shavings. Money-in was chosen because it is closer to what happened.
6. **How they talk.** This is a talking film. The house rule is no dialogue in Flow videos,
   with Money For Something as the one exception. This needs the same exception, or recorded
   voices.
7. **The title.** "The Bank Robbery" is the working name. Floated: "Bank Holiday", "Count It",
   "One Bill". None checked.
8. **Medium and length.** Storyboarded as a film of about five minutes. It would also cut as a
   comic.
9. **The seed's "Liverpool versus Everton" distraction** is not used. It is still in the idea
   file.
