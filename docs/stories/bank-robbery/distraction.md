# The Bank Robbery — the distraction (ideas and research, 6 October 2026)

**Asked for by Jack, 6 October 2026:** the story "lacks the distraction part, where the left and
right people riot and protest or something. It used to be a metaphor of football hooligans, but
we want to spell it out."

**Ideas 1 and 2 were chosen by Jack on 6 October 2026 and are now in
[`storyboard.md`](./storyboard.md) (scenes 5, 8 and 9).** Idea 4, the same sign, came in with
the march. The rest are still ideas. Scene numbers below are the old thirteen-scene ones.

## What the storyboard has now, and what is missing

- The crew *hires* the people who make the row (scenes 4 and 5) and the two drivers *rehearse*
  it (scene 6).
- **We never see it work on anyone.** There is no crowd, no street, no police looking the other
  way. The distraction is talked about and never shown.

## The one rule the ideas all obey

**The crowd is right to be angry, and the crew is who we laugh at.** Both crowds have been
robbed and both know it. What the crew supplies is the direction to face.

Why this matters, from the research below:

- People taught a manipulation trick *on someone else* got better at spotting it. People told
  they fell for it did not ([`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md), rule 2).
- Satire is read through what the viewer already believes. Viewers who agreed with Alf Garnett
  admired him.
- Brass Eye's "Paedogeddon" played its mob as grotesque, and the tabloids read it as contempt
  for ordinary people.

## Nine ideas

Each is a device a film has already proved, turned to our job.

### 1. The walk down the gap *(recommended as the centrepiece)*

- **From:** Die Hard with a Vengeance (a fake bomb at a school pulls every police officer away
  from the gold) and The Italian Job (an engineered jam, and a getaway dressed as football fans).
- **Ours:** two marches are routed to meet outside the bank. The police put up a line of metal
  barriers to keep them apart, an empty strip of road between two crowds. The strip leads to the
  bank's front door. The crew walks down it, in suits, with pallets, escorted.
- **Why it is good:** it is the Reservoir Dogs slow walk, moved to where it means something. And
  it is true to life: police really do build a "sterile zone" between rival marches.
- **The line:** an officer lifts the barrier for them. "Mind how you go, sir. It's mad out here."

### 2. We watch it being made

- **From:** Wag the Dog, where a fake war is produced in a studio in front of us.
- **Ours:** in the garage, the Proprietor and the Platform take one photograph (the small boat
  from the café television) and write two captions for it. One goes to the red feed, one to the
  blue feed. Same picture, two furies.
- **The line:** the Platform: "I don't need them to believe it. I need them to reply."
- **The rule it keeps:** we never read either caption. We see two phones light up.

### 3. One printer, one invoice

- **From:** Inside Man and Money Heist, where robbers and hostages wear the same overalls so
  nobody can tell who is who.
- **Ours:** both crowds' placards come off the same press in the Proprietor's print hall, red
  ones and blue ones in alternate stacks. Both coaches are on one invoice, signed by the Fixer.
- **The laugh:** the lorry driver delivers to the wrong side and nobody notices for an hour.

### 4. The same sign

- **From:** Life of Brian, where the People's Front of Judea hates the Judean People's Front more
  than the Romans.
- **Ours:** hold on one red placard and one blue placard, either side of the barrier. The first
  line is the same on both: WE CAN'T AFFORD TO LIVE HERE. Only the last line differs.
- **Why it is honest:** in a YouGov poll of July 2025, a 2% tax on wealth over £10m was backed
  by 88% of Labour voters and 55% of Reform voters. They agree on the money.
- **Denise knows both crowds.** They are her breakfast regulars. She serves tea across the
  barrier.

### 5. Nobody is looking

- **From:** Black Mirror's "The National Anthem". The princess is released half an hour early
  and nobody notices, because the streets are empty and everyone is watching a screen.
- **Ours:** the robbery happens in daylight, on the pavement, pallets and all. Nobody films it.
  Every phone in the street is pointed at the barrier.
- **The laugh:** a pallet truck crosses behind the Presenter during his live report on "tensions
  outside the bank". He leans to let it pass.

### 6. The derby

- **From:** the original football idea, and football films like Green Street.
- **Ours:** the day is policed like a match. Red scarves one side, blue the other, mounted
  police, burger vans, a man selling both colours off one stall.
- **The line:** the Proprietor: "It's a derby. Nobody asks who owns the ground."
- **Why keep it:** it carries the old metaphor without replacing the spelled-out version.

### 7. What they call them at the party

- **From:** Joker, where a rich man calls protesters "clowns" and they take the mask as a badge.
- **Ours:** in scene 7, off camera, Mr Blue and Mr Red each say what they really think of their
  own supporters. It is the same word.
- **Why it is the equaliser:** the contempt belongs to the two politicians, equally, and never
  to the film.

### 8. The carnival

- **From:** Ace in the Hole and Network, where the media feeds a crowd and sells it back.
- **Ours:** the Platform has a counter on his laptop that ticks up with every angry reply. It
  looks exactly like the counter on the vault. The distraction is a second robbery.

### 9. The crowd turns round

- **From:** Dog Day Afternoon, where the crowd outside the bank picks a side.
- **Ours:** the ending. When Denise switches the television off in scene 13, the noise outside
  drops. One person each side of the barrier looks at the bank at the same time.
- **Keep it small.** Two heads turning, not a revolution. The narrator has already told us they
  got away with it.

## My recommendation

- **Use 1, 2 and 4 as the spine:** make it, march it, and show they agree.
- **Add 5 and 7 as single gags.**
- **Keep 6 as dressing.**
- **Make it a protest with shoving, not a riot.** A burning street asks the audience to judge
  the crowd, and it sits too close to real events in which people were hurt.

## What not to do

- **Do not show or name a real event.** The 2024 riots followed the murder of three children.
  Nothing in this film should point at it.
- **Do not write the false claim on screen,** even to expose it. Repeating it strengthens it
  (rule 10).
- **Do not say the internet caused the riots.** Parliament's own committee said how far
  misinformation drove the 2024 disorder "is not clear".
- **Do not make one crowd uglier than the other.** Same number of extras, same lens, same time
  on screen.
- **Do not let Denise pick a crowd.**

### Films

- **Dog Day Afternoon:** shot with 300 to 400 extras plus real neighbours, hand-held, to look
  like television news. [S] The crowd changing sides is [memory].
- **Money Heist:** hostages are put in the robbers' red overalls and masks, and the plan depends
  on the public's support. [snippet] Money is rained on a crowd as a diversion in Part 3.
  [snippet; the amount and episode are not confirmed]
- **Inside Man:** hostages are dressed identically to the robbers so police "cannot distinguish"
  them. [S]
- **Joker:** the insult "clowns" becomes the protesters' mask, and the crowd becomes cover.
  [snippet]
- **Wag the Dog:** a fake war, with studio footage and a theme song pressed on artificially aged
  vinyl. [S]
- **Die Hard with a Vengeance:** a fake bomb in a school empties the financial district of
  police while the gold leaves in dump trucks. [S]
- **The Italian Job:** the traffic system is sabotaged and the crew escape "disguised as British
  football fans". [S]
- **Brass Eye, "Paedogeddon" (2001):** about 3,000 complaints. Defenders said the target was
  media hysteria; the tabloids read it as an attack on decency. [S]
- **Black Mirror, "The National Anthem":** the princess is freed 30 minutes before the deadline
  and nobody sees, because everyone is watching a screen. [snippet]
- **Life of Brian, Network, Ace in the Hole, Green Street:** [memory].
- **No famous comedy scene of two crowds either side of a police line was found.** It may be
  open ground.

### Real events (background only: none of this goes on screen)

- **Policing rival marches:** on 16 May 2026 two opposing marches in London were kept apart by
  a metal-barrier "sterile zone", with over 4,000 officers, at a cost of £4.5m. [S]
  [Source](https://www.aol.com/articles/rival-protesters-were-kept-apart-201602600.html)
- **The 2024 disorder:** about 40,000 officer days and roughly £28m, the largest police
  mobilisation since 2011. [snippet] So "every officer is at the protest" is grounded.
- **How a false claim travelled in 2024:** posts carrying it had about 27 million views in 24
  hours (Amnesty, August 2025). [P]
  [Source](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/08/xs-design-and-policies/)
- **The regulator:** "virality and algorithmic recommendations" drove divisive content.
  [snippet] **Parliament's committee:** how far misinformation drove it "is not clear".
  [snippet]
- **Anger pays:** Facebook counted an "angry" reaction as worth five likes from 2017, and later
  set it to zero. [snippet]
- **The football link is real:** the English Defence League grew out of football firms in Luton
  in 2009. [snippet]

### They agree on the money

- **YouGov, July 2025:** a 2% tax on wealth over £10m. Labour voters 88% in favour, Liberal
  Democrat 83%, Conservative 61%, Reform 55%. [S, reported by a partisan outlet; the tables were
  not opened]
  [Source](https://leftfootforward.org/2025/07/revealed-overwhelming-majority-of-brits-support-wealth-tax-on-super-rich-according-to-new-poll/)
- **King's College London:** the two sides "dislike each other intensely" without necessarily
  disagreeing on the issues. [snippet]

### How to show people being wound up without insulting them

- **Teach the trick on someone else:** short videos showing manipulation techniques improved
  people's ability to spot them, across about 5.4 million viewers (Roozenbeek and van der
  Linden, 2022). [snippet]
- **Irony is read through belief:** viewers who shared a bigoted sitcom character's views
  admired him (Vidmar and Rokeach, 1974). [snippet]
