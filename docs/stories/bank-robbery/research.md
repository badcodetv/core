## The three things that change the story

1. **The Bank of England's own evidence supports "huge cash gains to people who were already
   wealthy". It does not support "QE made inequality worse".** The Bank says it did not. So the
   story says who got how much, in pounds, and never says "inequality rose because of QE".
2. **Only Killing Zoe is set in a bank.** Reservoir Dogs is a jeweller's and Ocean's Eleven is a
   casino vault. We borrow their shapes, not their targets.
3. **On immigration the official line is "real, small, and falling on the lower paid, plus house
   prices".** That is the proportionality line the reader rules ask for. Denial is not available
   and is not true.

## Part B — the money

### What the Bank of England did, and who it says gained

- **Quantitative easing peaked at £895bn** (£875bn of government bonds, £20bn of company bonds).
  [P] [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/quantitative-easing)
- The dates and sizes of each round (2009, 2011 to 2012, 2016, 2020) are **[memory]**.
- **The Bank's 2012 paper [P]:** asset purchases "have boosted the value of households'
  financial wealth held outside pension funds, but holdings are heavily skewed with the top 5% of
  households holding 40% of these assets."
  - The first £325bn raised household wealth by "just over £600 billion, equivalent to around
    £10,000 per person if assets were evenly distributed".
  - The median household held about £1,500 of those assets. The top 5% averaged £175,000.
  - The 40% is of financial assets outside pensions, not of all wealth.
  - [The paper](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/news/2012/july/the-distributional-effects-of-asset-purchases-paper)
- **The Bank's 2018 staff paper (Bunn, Pugh and Yeates) [P]:** "The 10% of least wealthy
  households are only estimated to have seen a marginal increase in their measured real wealth of
  around £3000 between 2006-08 and 2012-14, compared to £350,000 for the wealthiest 10%."
  - **It must travel with its other finding:** the gains were "broadly similar" as a percentage,
    and the Bank's line is that the policy did not lead to greater inequality.
  - It covers all the easing, including interest-rate cuts, not QE alone.
  - [The paper](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/working-paper/2018/the-distributional-impact-of-monetary-policy-easing-in-the-uk-between-2008-and-2014.pdf)

### The House of Lords, 2021

- Report: "Quantitative easing: a dangerous addiction?", 16 July 2021.
- "The Bank of England is widely perceived to be using QE to finance the Government's deficit
  during the COVID-19 pandemic." **The word is "perceived", not "proven".**
- The Bank should do more about "widening wealth inequalities that have resulted from rising
  asset prices caused by QE".
- Quotes read on [a mirror of the committee's press release](https://wired-gov.net/wg/news.nsf/articles/Bank+of+England+must+spell+out+the+risks+of+quantitative+easing+says+Lords+report+19072021113800?open=) [S].
  Parliament's own page refused the request.

### What it is costing now

- The Bank's QE fund paid the Treasury £123.9bn up to September 2022. The flow then reversed:
  about £103bn has gone back, and the lifetime result is projected at **minus £125bn** on market
  rates (minus £60bn if rates fall). [S]
  [Bank of England, 2026 Q1](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/asset-purchase-facility/2026/2026-q1)
- Four big banks received over £9bn of interest on their reserves at the Bank in 2023. [snippet]

### Wages, houses, wealth

- **Wages:** had pay kept growing as it did before 2008, the average worker would be about
  £11,000 a year better off (Resolution Foundation, March 2023). **This is a gap against a
  trend, not a pay cut.** [S]
  [Source](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/15-years-of-economic-stagnation-has-left-workers-across-britain-with-an-11000-a-year-lost-wages-gap/)
- **Wealth:** the richest 1% of households hold 10% of all household wealth, the same as the
  poorest half put together (ONS, 2020 to 2022). [S]
  [Source](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/bulletins/totalwealthingreatbritain/april2020tomarch2022)
- **Houses:** the median home in England cost 7.7 times median full-time pay in 2024 (ONS).
  [snippet]
- "House prices up 86%, pay up 65% since 2009" comes from an unofficial site. **Unverified.**
- **What a pound buys:** no solid figure was found. The Bank's own calculator has to be run by
  hand. **Do not quote a number until it is.**

### Immigration: how big it is as a worry, and what is measured

- **Ipsos, August 2026:** immigration is the top issue at 42%, the economy second at 32%. [S]
  [Source](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/immigration-remains-britains-biggest-issue-concern-about-climate-change-and-environment-rises)
- **Migration Advisory Committee, 2018 [P]:** migration "is not a major determinate of the wages
  of UK-born workers… lower-skilled workers face a negative impact while higher-skilled workers
  benefit, however the magnitude of the impacts are generally small". Also: "migration has
  increased house prices", more so where planning is tight.
  [The report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ba26c1de5274a54d5c39be2/Final_EEA_report.PDF)

### The Cantillon effect

New money enters at particular points. Whoever gets it first spends it before prices rise;
whoever gets it last meets the higher prices first. **No reputable academic source was found in
this pass**, only glossaries, so it is not citable yet. The story does not use the name.

# Second pass — since Covid (6 October 2026)

Asked for by Jack: "inflation has not matched wages, everyone is suffering… people are really
suffering since Covid", and "they are attempting to stealth quantitative ease". One more web pass
by a research agent. **Read, not tested.** Same tags as above, plus **[sum]** where the percentage
is the agent's own arithmetic on the official series named. Latest inflation month available:
August 2026.

## The short version

| Safe to say | True for 2022 to 2023 only | Cannot say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prices are up about a third since 2020, food nearly 40%, rents 37% | Pay falling behind prices | "Pay still has not caught up with prices" (average pay has) |
| Average real pay is only about 5% above 2008, eighteen years on | Double-digit inflation | "QE caused the inflation" |
| Destitution more than doubled between 2017 and 2022; very deep poverty is at a record | The "biggest fall in living standards on record" (it was a forecast; the outturn was milder) | "The rich got richer since 2021" (measured wealth fell when rates rose) |
| The four big banks' profits rose about 45% between 2022 and 2025 | Food bank use at its peak (now falling, still far above 2019) | "The Bank is doing QE again" |
| 75% of people support a 2% wealth tax | | "Firms profiteered", stated as fact |

**What this does to the story:** the suffering is real and sourced, but the honest shape is "a
third more expensive, and pay has only just crawled back to where it was", not "pay never caught
up". The strongest lines are prices, rents, food, destitution and bank profits.

## Prices against pay

- **Prices:** up **32.2%** from February 2020 to August 2026. [P][sum]
  [ONS series D7BT](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/d7bt/mm23)
- **Peak inflation:** 11.1%, October 2022. **Latest:** 3.1%. [P]
  [ONS, August 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/august2026)
- **Food:** up **38.6%** since February 2020. Peak rate 19.1%, March 2023. [P][sum]
- **Average regular pay in cash:** £512 a week (February 2020) to £705 (July 2026), up 37.7%.
  **That is ahead of prices.** [P][sum]
  [ONS earnings](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/averageweeklyearningsingreatbritain/september2026)
- **Average pay after inflation:** £471 a week in February 2020, back down to £472 at the low
  point in October 2022, £495 in July 2026. About 5% above February 2020 and 4.7% above its 2008
  peak. [P]
  [ONS series A2FC](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/timeseries/a2fc/lms)
- **The worst year:** real regular pay fell 2.8% in the year to mid-2022. [P]
- **The middle earner, not the average:** median full-time weekly pay in April 2025 was 2% below
  2008 after inflation. [snippet] **Worth opening**: it is the figure closest to what Jack means,
  because an average is pulled up by the top.
  [Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn02795/)

## Rents, houses, energy

- **Private rents:** £1,021 a month (February 2020) to £1,400 (August 2026), up **37.1%**.
  Faster than prices. [P][sum]
  [ONS rents](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/priceindexofprivaterentsukmonthlypricestatistics)
- **House prices:** £273,000 in July 2026, about 18% above February 2020, which is **a fall
  after inflation**. London is down 3.3% on the year. [P for the latest; snippet for 2020]
  **This cuts against "asset owners gained" since Covid, for houses.**
- **Interest rates:** 0.1% in 2020, peak 5.25%, **3.75% now**. [P for now; memory for the rest]
- **Energy cap:** about £1,042 in winter 2020 to 2021, government guarantee at £2,500 in the
  crisis, **£1,723 now**. [P for the guarantee and the latest; snippet for the rest] The
  definition of a typical home changed, so the comparison is rough.

## Hardship

- **Food banks:** Trussell gave out 2.6 million parcels in 2025, down 12% on 2024. [P] "45%
  above before the pandemic" is [snippet].
  [Trussell](https://www.trussell.org.uk/news-and-research/latest-stats/end-of-year-stats)
- **Destitution:** 3.8 million people in 2022, about a million of them children, more than
  double 2017. No newer study. [S]
- **Very deep poverty:** 6.8 million in 2023 to 2024, a record. 14.2 million (21%) in poverty.
  [S] [JRF, UK Poverty 2026](https://www.jrf.org.uk/understanding-poverty/uk-poverty-2026-the-essential-guide-to-understanding-poverty-in-the-uk)
- **The official forecaster in March 2022** expected the "biggest fall" in living standards
  "since ONS records began in 1956-57". [P] The outturn was milder; the outturn figures were not
  retrieved.
- **What comes next:** income per person forecast to grow 0.3% a year to 2030 (Resolution
  Foundation). [snippet]

## Who gained since 2020

- **Against us:** household wealth fell by £2.1 trillion between 2021 and early 2023, the
  biggest fall since the Second World War, because interest rates rose. [P]
  [Resolution Foundation](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/interest-rate-surge-delivers-record-biggest-wealth-fall-for-british-households-since-world-war-ii/)
- **Against us:** the number of billionaires on the Rich List has fallen since 2022 (157 in
  2026). [S for 2026; memory for earlier years]
- **For us, the banks:** the four big banks made £31.4bn before tax in 2022 and about £45bn in
  each of 2023, 2024 and 2025. [snippet]
- **For us, the interest on QE money:** the banks hold about £654bn at the Bank of England and
  are paid Bank Rate on it. One think tank puts the Treasury's bill to the Bank at £130bn over
  2025 to 2029. [snippet]
  [NEF](https://neweconomics.org/2025/02/treasury-to-hand-bank-of-england-130bn-in-next-five-years-in-stealth-subsidy-to-bankers)
- **Shares:** the FTSE 100 is up roughly 42% in cash since February 2020. [snippet and memory]
- **Profiteering:** contested. The Bank of England found "no evidence of a rise in overall
  profits" outside oil, gas and utilities. Unite says margins rose 30%. [S and snippet]

## Did QE cause the inflation?

- A Bank of England staff paper (May 2026, the authors' view, not the Bank's): the pandemic QE
  "contributed around 2pps to the above-target inflation rate of 5%" at the end of 2021, with
  "the remaining 3pps accounted for by food, energy and other shocks". [P]
  [The paper](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/working-paper/2026/are-the-effects-of-qe-and-tightening-state-contingent.pdf)
- **Fair:** energy and supply were the main cause, and the Bank's own staff put a real share of
  it on the money. **Not fair:** "QE caused the inflation."

## "Stealth QE"

- **The Bank of England has not started buying again.** It is still shrinking its pile, now
  £488bn. [P]
- **But it is slowing down.** The pace was cut from £100bn a year to £70bn in September 2025
  [snippet]. On 17 September 2026 it paused its sales, and plans a much slower run to 2034. [P]
- **And it is keeping some for good:** £120bn of the longest bonds, held permanently. [P]
  [Bank of England, September 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026)
- **In America:** the Federal Reserve stopped shrinking in December 2025 [memory] and bought
  short-term government debt at up to $40bn a month until August 2026, when it stopped. It says
  this was housekeeping. Market commentators and blogs are the ones calling it stealth QE. [S]
- **No named commentator was found calling the British moves stealth QE.**
- **The line we can stand behind:** "They have stopped taking the money back, and the Bank will
  keep £120bn of it for good." Not "they are printing again".

## Taxing the rich

- **A 2% yearly tax on wealth above £10m:** about **£24bn a year**, from about 20,000 people.
  [snippet] [Tax Justice UK](https://taxjustice.uk/blog/how-would-a-wealth-tax-work-in-practice/)
- **Support:** 75% for, 13% against (YouGov, 8 July 2025, 4,142 people). [P]
  [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/daily-results/20250708-3086a-1)
- **The rich pay a lower rate:** people earning £10m paid an effective 21%; one in ten of those
  over £1m paid under 11%, less than someone on £15,000 (Advani, Hughson and Summers, 2015 to
  2016 data). [snippet]
- **The other side:** the Institute for Fiscal Studies warns people may leave and that wealth is
  hard to value. [snippet]
