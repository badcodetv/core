```text
A photograph of an English market-town high street on a bank holiday morning, shot on 35mm motion-picture film through an anamorphic lens from a long way down the street on a long telephoto lens, so that everything stacks up tight: a line of men, two crowds, and shut shops close behind them.

Two lines of steel crowd barriers run down the middle of the road and leave an empty strip of tarmac between them. The film is tungsten-balanced stock exposed in daylight with no correcting filter, so the daylight turns cold steel blue-green while the white shirts stay clean white. The only warm colour is one orange street lamp left on above the crowd. Visible film grain, slightly soft at the edges of the frame, unposed.

Thanks.
```

## Round 2 — the same four looks, rewritten (6 October 2026)

**Asked for by Jack:** redo them, avoid "AI slop", optimise for Nano Banana in Flow, research both
on the web, and use the cinematography files.

- **Model:** Nano Banana 2. There is no "Nano Banana Pro 2"
  ([`nano-banana-2.md`](../../google-flow/nano-banana-2.md), fifth pass). Flow offers Nano Banana 2
  and Nano Banana Pro; 2 is free and is the standing choice.
- **Settings:** 16:9, two candidates, no reference.

### What round 1 got wrong, and the change

| Round 1 fault | Why it reads as AI | Round 2 change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera dead centre in the road, everything mirrored | "Too symmetrical" is the first named tell ([`symptoms.md`](../../cinematography/symptoms.md)) | The camera stands where a person could: in a crowd, in a doorway, at a first-floor window. Off the centre line in every look |
| The men in a row, facing us, identical | Posed, evenly spaced, same distance: a diagram (principle 21b) | Strung out in twos and threes, mid-stride, each doing something: eating, on a phone, leaning into a handle |
| Crowds watching the men | Breaks the joke | The camera looks across the strip, so the near crowd has its back to us and the far crowd faces us, and both look over the men's heads |
| Clean road, tidy street | A sterile setting is a named tell | Patched tarmac, a drain, chewing gum, a flattened cup, sagging bank-holiday bunting |
| Look A: blue daylight plus a lit street lamp | Two lighting ideas, and it read as dusk | One light, the sky. The lamp is gone |
| Estate agent's board | Lettering came back wrong | Removed. Shop signs are faded and blank |
| A long prompt with the look at the end | Earlier clauses win | Subject in the first line, then one camera phrase, then the scene, then the light. Shorter |

### What the research said (web, 6 October 2026; read, not tested)

- **Subject in the first fifteen words; full sentences; one camera phrase, never two; say what
  the picture is for.** `[community]`
  [Fliki, 29 September 2026](https://fliki.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide). The same guide
  suggests "anamorphic lens flare", which we do not take.
- **The slop look is smoothed faces, hazy default light, one shared palette, and symmetry. The
  cure is specifics: a named light, a named camera, an action.** `[community]` search summaries
  only: [OpenArt](https://openart.ai/blog/how-to-avoid-ai-slop/),
  [RedHub](https://blog.redhub.ai/ai-slop-look). Both already in `nano-banana-2.md`.
- **Nothing new against the repo.** The toolkit there already says all of it: one light with a
  direction, mid-action, off centre, something in the foreground, dirt in the world, no
  "cinematic" or "photorealistic".
- **Not used:** "rule of thirds" as a prompt word (rejected in `nano-banana-2.md`), and "slightly
  off level" (it returned black wedges on wide shots, 5 October).

## Round 3 — the protest, made real (6 October 2026)

**Asked for by Jack:** seven press photographs of real protests and riots as examples. Make the
protests "more real and gritty". Two crowds either side holding red and blue card "looks AI like".
Find a natural way to carry red and blue (masks, ties). Artistic and cinematic. Free to change
anything from the earlier rounds.

**Written from scratch.** Nothing is carried over from rounds 1 and 2 except the story.

🔴 **This breaks a storyboard rule on purpose.** Scene 8 says "a protest with shoving, not a riot.
Nothing burns." Jack's examples are smoke and burning bins. The rule was a first pass and unruled,
so round 3 follows Jack. The storyboard is not changed until he and Kai rule.
🔴 **Reader check owed:** hooded people setting fires can read as "the crowd are thugs", which
[`the-reader.md`](../../marketing/the-reader.md) warns against. Frames 1, 3 and 4 keep the crowd
as ordinary people; frame 2 is the risky one.

### What the seven photographs do (read off the pictures)

| In the photographs | In rounds 1 and 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Smoke hides half the picture; you see part of the event | Everything visible, edge to edge |
| People are caught mid-run, mid-kick, backs to the lens | People stand and hold things |
| Clothes are black, grey, navy: hoodies, puffers, trainers, rucksacks | Tidy anoraks |
| Colour arrives by accident: a flag, a scarf, a mask, a fire | Colour issued to each side as a card |
| Signs are bedsheets and cardboard, hand-painted, all different sizes | Identical blank rectangles |
| One light does the work: a fire, or sun through smoke | Even daylight |
| The crowd is clumps and gaps | The crowd is two even lines |
| The building in the haze is the subject (the Capitol picture) | The street is a backdrop |

The photographs were used as examples to describe, not uploaded: they are press pictures of real
people, and one carries a politician's name.

### Ways to carry red and blue without cards

1. **Smoke.** Hand flares and smoke canisters, red on one side and blue on the other. Real at
   British marches and football grounds. Used in frames 1 and 4.
2. **Scarves and masks.** Football scarves and surgical masks pulled up over faces. Frames 1 and 3.
3. **Paint.** Bedsheet banners, the same words on both, one in red paint and one in blue. Frame 3.
4. **Wheelie bins.** Councils issue them in colours; a red-lidded and a blue-lidded bin on fire
   together. Frame 2.
5. **Flags on poles,** plain, home-made, at all heights. Frames 3 and 4.
6. **The crew's ties.** Mr Red and Mr Blue in a red and a blue tie; every other tie black. Not
   tried in this round, because the crew is small in these frames.
7. **Police light.** Blue from a van's lamps on one wall, red from its tail lights on the other.
   Not tried.

### What the research said (web, 6 October 2026; search summaries only)

- Working press photographers shoot protests on two lenses, a wide and a long one; go for the
  front line, hands and faces; put a known landmark in the frame; and use silhouettes and smoke
  for depth. [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2018/12/04/pro-tips-for-photojournalism-at-riots-and-protests/),
  [StreetSnappers](https://streetsnappers.com/street-photography-blog/how-to-shoot-protests)
- Flares and face coverings are common enough at British protests that the government moved to
  ban both in 2024.
  [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/world/uk-news/2024/02/08/new-uk-laws-to-prohibit-face-coverings-and-pyrotechnics-at-protests/)
- Nothing useful found on prompting a crowd specifically.

### R3-1. Smoke

```text
Middle-aged men in black suits push a pallet of banknotes through drifting smoke on an English high street during a street protest. A news agency photograph, shot on a Canon EOS R3 with a 200mm lens from across the road in the late afternoon.

Thick white smoke from flares lies low across the road and hides the ground. On the left it is stained red by a hand flare burning in someone's raised fist. On the right it is stained blue by another. Where the two colours meet the smoke is pale, and out of that pale gap come five men in their fifties in black suits, white shirts and thin black ties, walking calmly. Two of them lean on the handle of a pallet truck stacked with shrink-wrapped banknotes. Nobody looks at the men in suits. A figure close to the camera, out of focus and cut off by the edge of the frame, runs across the foreground. Behind everything, shuttered shops and a stained rendered wall fade into the haze.

Low sun behind the smoke lights it from the back, so the people are dark shapes with bright edges. Slight motion blur on the runners. 16:9.

Thanks.
```
