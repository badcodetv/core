# hard-funk: what shaped it

- Funk grammar from Splice: bass on the one, muted 16th chicken-scratch guitar, horn stabs on snare accents, groove held by rhythm rather than chord changes. So the prompt uses one unchanging vamp, and the horns play hits, not tunes. https://splice.com/blog/what-is-funk-music/
- The chicken-scratch technique (squeeze the chord, then release it) and staccato stabs over syncopated gaps. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanking , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk
- Suno funk tagging advice: name the subgenre and the parts (slap bass, tight drums, horn stabs), add a production tag, write "instrumental" in the style box. https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-funk-prompts/
- Put negatives in the Exclude field, not the style box. https://github.com/mttkllr/suno-field-guide , https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-negative-prompting/
- v6 sliders: set Variety to off so v6 doesn't rewrite the tags, raise Style Influence for adherence, keep Weirdness near the normal 50. https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-v6-variety-style-influence-weirdness/
- The lyrics box is empty, so the picture's timeline goes in the style box as timed cues. The cues are the tap stabs (0-3 s), the stab on the last step (3-6 s), thinning out for the duck (6-8.5 s), the held hit for the prize, the dead stop with a needle-off at 12.5 s, the brighter return a step up at 14 s, and a one-hit ending at about 17 s. The thin sections and the open mid-range leave room for the spoken lines. The hope comes from the groove coming back higher with a climbing horn riff, plus handclaps.
- The risk of sounding like a period pastiche is offset by the "punchy modern low end" tag. "Spoken word" is left out of Exclude, because the voice is added in post and the bed only needs to leave space for it.
