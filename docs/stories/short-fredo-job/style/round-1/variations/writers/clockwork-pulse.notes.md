# clockwork-pulse: what shaped it

- The core texture comes from process-minimalism. Interlocking marimba patterns built up voice by voice (Six Marimbas), a piano ostinato laid over shifted copies of itself (Eight Lines), and a steady eighth-note pulse that a harmonic cycle moves over (Music for 18 Musicians, Violin Phase). That suits "the plan as a machine": layers that click in every two bars, then a harmonic climb for the jar section.

- 120 BPM puts the 12.5 s stop at about beat 25. Suno can't hit a timestamp, so the stop and silence are described as events and the edit lines them up. The 30 s length gives room to cut down to 18 s.
- The prompt follows v6 guidance: one main tradition, rhythm, two or three signature instruments, emotional direction, then production texture. It uses concrete instrument names, keeps under about 100 words, and avoids stacking genres. Exclude only lists audible traits: stray vocals (since the voiceover is added in post), drops, fade-outs and long intros. It's a boundary, not a guarantee.

- On the risk (clever rather than swaggering): one "sly off-beat bass slide" and a closed hi-hat add some crew cool without leaving the mechanism idea. "Uncluttered midrange, air between notes" keeps space for the four spoken lines without naming a voice, which could pull one in.
- Settings: v6 (not wild) with styleInfluence 72 so the instrument list holds. Weirdness 35 lets the wrong note land as wrong. maxMode is on for audio quality. vocalGender is null because it's instrumental.
