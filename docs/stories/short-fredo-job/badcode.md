## What it is
A BadCode short (9:16, about 15 s): a gang in BadCode balaclavas plans a heist-film job, blueprints, a laser grid, the
crew line-up, all for one small chocolate bar in a corner-shop jar that cost 10p. They pull it off, and the price has gone
up. "It was 10p when we started planning." The point: prices rise faster than ordinary people's plans. The way out (Jack, 2026-11-23):
money that keeps up: the crew's pay or savings ticks up with the price, so the next plan still works. Jack: "have heavy oceans eleven references": a parody of the slick heist film, played straight.

## Story-only rules
- Jack and Kai write the jokes: beats say what each moment conveys; joke lines stay blank for them. The pitch's line
  ("It was 10p when we started planning.") is on the shorts list, not ruled.
- Parody of the genre only: never a film's title, logo, score, characters or any actor's likeness. The chocolate bar and
  the shop show no real brand, wrapper or logo.
- The prices (10p then, today's price) are read at source before they are said or shown.

## Library pieces in use
- **Heist and caper** (look, copied in): clean, legible blocking (wide shots that show where everyone stands, then hands,
  tools and watches); maps, blueprints, ticking clocks and split screens explaining the plan; a crew line-up of distinct
  silhouettes, each member's trade shown by a prop; long wordless stretches of precise work cut to the beat; freeze frame,
  whip pan and snap zoom for comic punctuation. Flow tends towards a modern orange-and-teal grade and a crew of identical
  models in black suits: ask for distinct people and clear light instead. Plans, maps and clocks come out as gibberish
  lettering: make them as plain plates and add any words in post. One gesture per 8 s clip.
- **Real photo as the look:** find the layout on Unsplash (`python3 scripts/scene/unsplash-refs.py <scene-dir> "<query>"`),
  give the picked photo to Flow as the reference; the prompt says only what happens. Never an AI figure pasted into a
  real photo.
- **The BadCode balaclava:** anyone in it is BadCode in person. Fixed by the brand: a large white ring stitched round the
  whole face, the red triangle over the wearer's LEFT eye (on the right of the picture), no holes, no skin anywhere,
  gloves. Attach `docs/brand/balaclava/mask-reference.jpg` with `docs/brand/balaclava/shared-wording.txt` verbatim. In
  motion, every mask keeps facing the camera; check the triangle zoomed in at three points per clip. No mouths, so no
  lip-sync.
