# heavy-baritone-ledger-drawl — notes

What shaped it:

- **The beat, described rather than named.** Boom-bap guides advise building from the drum pocket, one sample/loop source, bass and texture, and warn against leaning on nostalgia words like "classic" alone. Hence the specifics: bare kick/snare with wide gaps, one dark minor loop, vinyl grit, no hook. https://jackrighteous.com/de-us/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/what-is-boom-bap-suno-ai-guide
- **Tempo ~88.** Analysis sites put the original near 90 BPM (marked as an AI estimate); prompt guides use 88 BPM for 90s East Coast boom bap. https://musiciwant.com/song/ten-crack-commandments-the-notorious-big , https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/the-suno-ai-prompts-list-that-actually-works-2026-genre-artist-style-and-use-case
- **Structure.** The original has no chorus and no standard 16-bar verse: a numbered list with a short gloss per item. So "no hook", and a [Scratch] tag between rules in place of the counting sample. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- **The voice without the name.** Community vocal guides use "male baritone" / "deep chest" wording plus delivery words like "laid-back", "understated", "conversational" to stop an over-animated read. https://sunohk.pages.dev/suno-tutorial/suno-timbre-vocal-prompt-guide/ , https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-prompt-guide-2026/
- **Behind the beat.** A how-to on the rapper's flow says his bars often start slightly behind the beat for an unhurried pocket. "Sits a hair behind the beat yet lands every syllable" comes from there (rough source). https://soundcy.com/article/how-to-sound-like-biggie
- **Unverified.** The "breathy wheeze" is from the brief. No source I found describes it.
- **Settings.** I used styleInfluence 70 so the boom-bap description sticks, and weirdness 30 to stay close to the target. Variety normal. Exclude bans singing and the modern trap tells (808, trap hats).
