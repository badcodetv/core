# closer-to-the-original-loop: notes

- The 1997 original's loop is a short guitar slide taken from a 1977 jazz record (a Les McCann tune), cut down and repeated. That is why the box now centres on "one brief crying jazz-guitar slide lifted from a 1977 jazz LP", with the Rhodes/organ moved down to a dim pad.
  - https://hiphopwired.com/291843/remember-big-21-samples-life-after-death
  - https://www.treblezine.com/les-mccann-tribute-scattered-sounds-20th-century/
- The numbers in the original are called by a sampled voice from another group's older record ("Shut 'Em Down"), which led to a lawsuit. So the caller is written as a separate rough voice cut from an old vinyl record that stabs in alone like a scratch, followed by silence before the MC.
  - https://www.mtv.com/news/504067/public-enemys-chuck-d-settles-big-copyright-suit/
  - https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Shut_%27Em_Down_(Public_Enemy_song)
- Flow: the quick-cluster/bebop wording is replaced with a checklist-style reading (rule, one clause of reasoning, stop), so the "said too fast" complaint is dealt with in the style box. Each rule has its own section tag and a [sampled shout] cue so every number lands on its own beat.
- Rewrite after the overlap check: the MC description now uses the box-to-keep's own vocal words unchanged ("enormous man, low velvet chest baritone, lazy Brooklyn vowels..."). The flagged new phrases ("deep smooth baritone", "big-bodied rapper", "baritone from the") are gone.
- Settings: v6, 75 s, male (all fixed). styleInfluence 70 so the loop and caller details carry. Weirdness 30 to stay close to jazz1.
