# slower-tempo-more-air: notes (redo)

- This redo rewords the parts the overlap check flagged: the genre opener, the description of the MC, the voice line, the lead-in to the punchline, and the barked-number sampled chant. Each one says the same thing in new words: boom-bap from the late nineties, a big-bodied rapper, a huge-framed man with a deep, smooth chest baritone, slurred Brooklyn vowels, and a gravelly chopped-up group shout that calls out the next number. The wheeze, the Rhodes-and-organ loop, the walking bass, the tape hiss, the 82 BPM tempo and "no hook" are still there.
- The tempo drops from 88 to 82 BPM, and the style line asks for relaxed pacing with room between lines. Suno's glossary treats tempo as BPM ranges, and guides warn that genre words and BPM should not contradict each other. So there are no words suggesting speed anywhere in the style.
- The lyric field gives each rule its own numbered [Verse] tag with [pause] after the punchline line, and a blank line between blocks. Community references say blank lines and break tags read as breaths or gaps, and that tags steer the output without dictating it. Each block uses only one or two tags, as the guides advise.
- The exclude field blocks rushed or crammed delivery as a backstop.
