# drums-first-dj-cuts: notes

The experiment: lead with the producer side. Most of the style box is the beat; the rapper gets one dense sentence.

What the research gave:
- The original beat was first made as a countdown for a NYC radio show ("Hot 5 at 9"), and the producer said he only scratched the numbers up to five, as a DJ choice. So the counting is a DJ on a turntable cutting a spoken number, radio-countdown style. That is why the prompt asks for a "turntablist" cutting "spoken numbers on vinyl" and why each rule gets a `[Scratched count: N]` bracket. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments, https://www.complex.com/music/2013/06/dj-premier-the-combat-jack-show-ten-crack-commandments, https://www.cheatsheet.com/news/notorious-big-10-crack-commandments-originally-came-from-radio-show.html
- The counting voice was a sampled vocal (and drew a lawsuit), and the keys came from a jazz record (WhoSampled via search results). We copy neither: dark minor Rhodes chords in our own two-bar loop, and the count is asked for as a generic scratched number. Source: https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- Tempo: one AI-analysed estimate of 90 BPM (https://musiciwant.com/song/ten-crack-commandments-the-notorious-big), unverified; the brief's 92 BPM sits within a hair of it, kept.
- Structure: no chorus, a numbered list, so "no chorus" in style and a sung hook in excludes.
- Voice: NPR calls it plummy, wheezy, humid, from deep in the chest; Rolling Stone, baritone and serene but menacing over a mellow sample; laid-back, conversational authority. Hence "chesty and slightly wheezy, slow, cool, conversational, behind the beat". Sources: https://www.npr.org/2010/08/02/128916682/biggie-smalls-the-voice-that-influenced-a-generation, https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/-38197/warning-1994-38238
- Suno tag guides: 90s boom bap works with dusty/sampler texture words, trap tags override vintage feel, and "turntable scratches, DJ cuts" are recognised. So trap/808/hi-hat rolls go in excludes. Sources: https://jackrighteous.com/en-us/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/what-is-boom-bap-suno-ai-guide, https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-hip-hop-rnb-tag-guide-v4-5

Choices: weirdness 45 (medium, as asked); styleInfluence 72 so the production words win; variety normal; male; 20 s; no max mode.

Risks (unverified until heard):
- Suno may SING or RAP the `[Scratched count: N]` brackets as words instead of making a scratch. If it does, the "Number one/two/three" lines still carry the order.
- The lyrics are dense for 20 s at 92 BPM (about 7-8 bars); the outro line may be cut off or the flow rushed against "slow".
- "Harder than nails" was written as "kick and snare harder than nails" plus "dry knock" so it reads as drum sound, not lyric.
