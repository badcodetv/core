# exclude-the-wrong-voice: notes (redo)

Experiment: get there by subtraction. A short positive box, plus a long exclude list built from what round 1 drifted into (gravel old man, female, young high voice, radio announcer, strings, spoken word, trap hats, lo-fi, autotune), with sung chorus, backing vocals, big reverb and cinematic swells added to keep the mix dry and bare.

Redo: the previous style box shared "in a shouted" with another writer. I also removed phrases that overlapped other writers (between the rules, behind the beat, dark minor, chest baritone, no chorus, heavyset Brooklyn, Turntablist). I reworded the count line as "A DJ chops in a hype-man voice barking each number before its rule" and rewrote the rest of the box in new words.

What the research shaped:
- No chorus and no standard 16-bar verse, just a numbered list with a short explanation per item. That gives "hookless, the track is a numbered list". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- The numbers are a cut-up sampled voice (the Chuck D "Shut 'Em Down" lawsuit, settled Nov 1998), plus a jazz sample (Les McCann "Vallarta") and the "Countdown" cut. That gives the scratched hype-man count and the dark jazz keys phrase. The phrase is described, not reproduced. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments, https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/ten_crack_commandments (secondary source, sample list unverified)
- Tempo is about 90 BPM. This is an AI-estimated figure, so it is unverified: https://musiciwant.com/song/ten-crack-commandments
- The voice (heavyset, wheezy chest baritone, laid-back and behind the beat) comes from the brief's target description. No source on the web measured it.

Settings: v6, variety off, weirdness 10, style influence 85, male, 20 s, maxMode off. These keep the engine close to a narrow target.
