# radio-promo-countdown (redo) — notes

Rewritten after the sameness gate flagged "behind the beat", "coast boom bap", "east coast boom" and the "beat drops out" direction. All four phrases are gone: the era is now "mid-90s New York hip-hop", the lag is "words dragged late against the snare", and the drop is "drums cut, piano chord rings alone".

What shaped it:
- The beat started as a radio promo for Hot 97's "Hot 5 at 9" countdown. The scratches counted to five right after the drop, and "ten" was added later from a spaceship countdown. Hence the angle: a 9pm FM countdown promo turned into a song, with one scratched announcer number per rule. Source: https://hypebeast.com/2011/1/jeru-the-damaja-10-crack-commandments-original-hot-97-promo
- The counting voice was a sampled rap voice, and its use led to a lawsuit (settled 1998). So the prompt asks for a generic "booming announcer voice", not a famous voice, to keep the copyright risk low. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- The keys come from a sampled jazz piano record. We do not copy them: the prompt asks for a single ominous low chord, once a bar. Source: https://amp.barstoolsports.com/blog/3365431/
- The tempo is about 90 BPM, an automated estimate, so unverified (from search results for the song).
- The song has no chorus, only a numbered list. Hence the exclusions for a sung hook and melodic rap (Wikipedia, above).
- Flow: an analysis found fairly regular rhythms that land on most beats, so the delivery is described as clear, lazy and confident rather than busy. Source: https://rapanalysis.com/?p=91 (seen only in a search summary, not read in full)
- No source described his timbre. "Deep, thick, raspy, nasal edge" is the brief's description plus common knowledge, and is unverified.

Settings: v6, 20 s, weirdness 58 so the scratch cuts have room, styleInfluence 72, male, variety normal.
