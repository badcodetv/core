# Round 2 (rap) — research notes for the ideas writer

Target: the 1997 Brooklyn countdown song and its rapper (names withheld; Suno refuses them).

## The song and the beat
- 1997, double album; 3:24; no chorus and no 16-bar verses: a numbered list of rules, each followed by a short explanation. Produced by the album's New York boom-bap producer. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- The beat began as a radio promo countdown ("top 5 at 9" on Hot 97); the scratched count originally only went to five. The count between rules is a cut-up, scratched sample of a shouting rap-group frontman's voice saying the numbers. https://www.cheatsheet.com/news/notorious-big-10-crack-commandments-originally-came-from-radio-show.html , https://www.funtrivia.com/trivia-quiz/Music/DJ-Premier-Vocal-Samples-153033.html
- The vocal-sample owner sued and settled in 1998: a real reason for us NOT to reuse any count sample — the count must be performed/scratched fresh. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Crack_Commandments
- Melodic bed: chopped jazz-piano/Rhodes keys from a 1970s soul-jazz pianist's record ("the sampling of the keys is unmistakable") — a short, dark, repeating keys stab. https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/ten_crack_commandments , https://amp.barstoolsports.com/blog/3365431/
- Producer's drum signature: "drums harder than nails", boom-bap kick+snare, drums chopped into tiny slices and re-layered, sparse arrangement. https://the-dowsers.com/the-dowser-posts/concrete-snare-drums-dj-premiers-25-best-beats , https://modeaudio.com/magazine/masters-of-sampling-dj-premier
- Tempo: ~90 BPM (AI-estimated, unverified); producer's typical range 85–95 BPM. https://musiciwant.com/song/ten-crack-commandments-the-notorious-big
- Unverified: exact drum break and musical key — no source found.

## The rapper's voice and flow
- NPR: voice "plummy, wheezy, humid", seems to come "from deeper in his chest than other people's voices"; learned phrasing from a jazz saxophonist who told him to slow down and phrase each note. https://www.npr.org/2010/08/02/128916682/biggie-smalls-the-voice-that-influenced-a-generation
- Rolling Stone (relayed): "thick, jaunty grumble" that grew deeper on the 1997 album; could flow "serenely" and still sound menacing. https://steemit.steemapps.com/biggie/@thebigbangtheory/biggie-smalls , https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/-38197/i-got-a-story-to-tell-1997-38236
- Secondary: deep, resonant, slightly raspy, warm, authoritative; laid-back, conversational, unhurried, deliberate pauses; precise enunciation; Brooklyn accent; understated not aggressive. https://soundcy.com/article/what-di-biggie-sound-like
- Flow analysis: lands on most beats (60 of 72 on one verse) — mostly ON the beat with light syncopation, not dragging behind. https://rapanalysis.com/?p=91
- Flow "like water: slow it up, pick it up, chop it up". https://www.npr.org/transcripts/128916682
