# The Telescope

**The only object that crosses the whole story intact.** Treat it as a character:
the *same physical tube* in every appearance, 1610 to now, never modernised,
never explained.

Galileo's actual instruments were modest — a wooden or paper tube bound in
leather, a convex objective, a concave eyepiece, maybe 20× at best, a field of
view you could cover with a thumbnail. **Keep it unimpressive.** The gap between
how ordinary it looks and what it did is the entire point: this is the object
that ended one world and started another, and it looks like a bit of plumbing.

## What it does in each beat

- **Beat 1** — pointed at Jupiter from a cold roof. Four lights.
- **Beat 2** — held out to [Cremonini](./cremonini.md), and not taken. **This is
  the story's central image.** The offered instrument and the folded hands.
- **Beat 9** — restaged as a stack of paper slid across a laminate table and not
  read. Same offer, same refusal, same framing. The audience should feel the
  brass tube in the paper.
- **Beat 10** — passed hand to hand down four centuries (roof → drawing room →
  lecture hall → lab bench) and finally **held out to the reader**, in the exact
  framing of beat 2. The last thing the story does is put the audience in
  Cremonini's chair and make them decide.

## Visual (house style: hyper-realistic documentary photography)

- About 18 inches. A tapered wooden tube wrapped in worn brown leather, brass
  fittings at both ends, tooled gilt decoration mostly rubbed away. Fingerprints
  and handling wear. Slightly crooked.
- Always held in a hand, never on a stand, never in a museum case. The wear
  should read as *used constantly by one person.*
- **Locked framing for the offer shot (beats 2, 9, 10):** the tube enters frame
  from the left, held out horizontally at chest height, the far end pointing at
  the refuser. Same lens, same distance, same eyeline, all three times.
- It never ages. Everything around it does.
