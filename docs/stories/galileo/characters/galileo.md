# Galileo

The one who looked, and then made the mistake of telling people.

**Not a saint.** This is important and it's the first thing to get right: he was
brilliant, stubborn, vain, and broke. He named Jupiter's moons the **Medicean
Stars** after Cosimo II de' Medici because he wanted the job, and he got it. He
was rude to people who could hurt him. He wrote his opponents' argument into the
mouth of a character called *Simplicio* and put it in a book, which is the kind
of thing that gets a man a trial.

He is a BadCode protagonist precisely because he isn't clean. The story doesn't
need him noble. It needs him **right, and punished for saying so.**

**The arc:** roof at night, four moving lights, a book, twenty-three years of
argument, then a room with a tribunal in it. He kneels. He reads out that the
thing he saw is not there. He signs it. He goes home and never leaves again.

He recanted **because he was made to** — under an institution that owned every
sentence he was allowed to say aloud. That's the whole point. The story does not
judge him for kneeling; it judges the room. And it rhymes his kneeling with a
modern person apologising before describing their own life
([Priya](./priya.md)) — one under threat, one for free.

## Visual (house style: hyper-realistic documentary photography)

Anchor figure — **the same man in every era-1 scene.**

- Late-40s to 60s, north-Italian, heavy build gone soft, a broad blunt face, a
  short greying beard, a high forehead going bald. Not the noble marble-bust
  Galileo — a working man with bad eyes and ink on his hands.
- Plain scholar's black, worn: a dark gown over a linen shirt going grey at the
  collar. Nothing ornamental. He dresses like someone whose money keeps arriving
  late.
- Demeanour: leaning in, always slightly too close to the thing he's looking at.
  Squinting. Caught mid-explanation with his hands moving, or caught being
  interrupted — never posed, never heroic.
- **The 1633 variant:** the same man, twenty years older and finished. Thinner,
  white-bearded, eyes gone bad. On his knees on stone in a plain shift, a page
  held in both hands, reading aloud. Shot from **behind and above** — we should
  see the room owning him, not his face performing grief.
- Light: candle and window only. Cold stone, warm flame, everything else dark.
