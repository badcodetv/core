# Dr. Priya Raghavan

Galileo's rhyme in the present day. Experimental physicist, postdoc, thirty-four,
on a contract that renews in March.

**The load-bearing choice: she is the department's designated skeptic.** She is
the one who takes apart the parapsychology paper at journal club and enjoys it.
She is the one who explains the base rate to relatives at Christmas. She has
publicly, repeatedly, and with some cruelty, been the person who says *that's not
evidence, that's a feeling.*

And then it happens to her.

The story never shows us what "it" is, and this is deliberate — the moment she
had is left entirely blank. Naming it would let the audience file it (*ah, a
ghost story / a psychedelic / grief*) and get out from under the beat. What we
show is only the **aftershape**: a person who knows exactly what she saw and knows
exactly what it will cost to say so, doing the arithmetic in real time.

**She recants like Galileo, except nobody asks her to.** At the table she opens
with *"I don't mean to sound spiritual, but—"* — an abjuration she wrote herself,
volunteered, unprompted, and is quietly proud of, because she thinks it proves
she's still rational. That's the whole story in one sentence.

**The apparatus that holds her:** her contract renews in March. Her funding sits
with the man in beat 9. There is no tribunal, no threat, no unpleasantness of any
kind. He is lovely about it. That's why it works.

**Her last line, alone, to nobody:** *"And yet it happened."* — the deliberate
rhyme with a sentence Galileo almost certainly never said. Four hundred years of
progress and the truth still has nowhere to live except a whisper with no witness.

## Visual (house style: hyper-realistic documentary photography)

- Mid-30s, British Indian, sharp-featured, hair up and coming loose by the end of
  every day. Tired in the specific way of someone who works late by choice. No
  makeup, or makeup put on four days ago.
- Institutional-casual and slightly cheap: jeans, a plain jumper with a stretched
  neck, trainers, a university lanyard she never takes off, a laptop covered in
  conference stickers. Everything about her says *smart, precarious, indoors.*
- Demeanour: arms crossed, head slightly tilted, mid-counterargument. Her default
  is amused disagreement — which is what makes the beat-7 collapse land, because
  it's the one moment the argument goes out of her.
- **Beat 7 variant (the apology):** at a table, mid-sentence, hands already going
  up in a *no-I-know* gesture before anyone has objected. Pre-emptive surrender in
  the body language. Others at the table are already looking at their drinks.
- **Beat 9 variant (the committee):** the mirror of Galileo's 1633 shot — shot
  from **behind and above**, the room owning her, the proposal and the pen on the
  table between her and the chair.
- **Beat 10 variant (the close):** alone, badly lit, somewhere ugly and ordinary —
  a strip-lit corridor at 2am, a car park, a stairwell. Speaking to nobody.
- Light: fluorescent, screen-glow, and the flat grey of British daylight through
  double glazing. Never warm. The only warmth in the whole story lives in 1610.
