# vocoder-trailer-braam: notes (redo)

Rewrite of the first draft after the sameness gate flagged "spoken word not" and "voice forward mix" as shared with other prompts. Both are gone. Speech is now asked for as "two talked voices with zero melody" and "[Talking, no melody: ...]" tags, and the mix as "dialogue dry and loud above the effects". The structure is unchanged.

What shaped it:
- Suno speech: describe the delivery in Style, use bracketed spoken cues, put anti-singing terms in Style and Exclude, and avoid hook/chorus words. Short sentences with periods. (https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-spoken-narration-workflow)
- Suno Sounds is strongest on cinematic hits and booms. Realistic physical detail, such as the car horn, may need layering in the edit. (https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-sounds-ai-sound-effects-guide)
- Braam: a distorted low brass or synth tone with a long reverb tail. Riser peaks land on the hit, and near-silence before a hit makes it land harder. That is why there is a vacuum before "There's only one." (https://violetrecording.com/sound-design-for-trailers/, https://www.rareformaudio.com/blog/sound-design-in-film-trailers)
- The ticking clock is a returning trailer staple. (https://www.rareformaudio.com/blog/sound-design-in-film-trailers)
- A braam clashes with the music's bass, so the bed is just riser, ticks and sub, with no bassline. (https://morphic.com/zh/resources/sounds/braam-sound-effects)

Unverified: I found no source on how Suno handles the word "vocoded". It may come back as a plain deep voice.
