# The Storyverse — doctrine

*The spine in plain clothes. [`confession.md`](./confession.md) is the argument in
voice; this is the writers' reference — every claim, what backs it, how settled
it is, and the one object that carries it.*

**Tiers:** **A** settled · **B** peer-reviewed but minority · **C** serious
frontier, conjectural · **D** serious but philosophical (outside physics) ·
**E** contested / empirically shaky · **F** fringe / anti-model — never ours.

## The whole thing in one page

Reality has not decided yet. Not "the particle is in two places" — **no fact
obtains at all until something interacts with it, and when it does, the fact
obtains relative to whatever it interacted with.** Einstein took away the global
clock; this takes away the global *outcome*. There is no view from nowhere in
which the coin has already landed for everybody.

One settled fact gets copied, redundantly, into everything nearby — and that mass
printing is what "objective reality" actually is. Not a world out there; a world
**agreed upon, because the evidence was mass-produced.** Which means whoever
controls what gets copied controls what's real, and you already built that
machine and pointed it at yourselves.

What we are: not a simulation — a **projection**. Real the way a photograph is
real. The dial is real, the wind is real, and the dial is not the wind.

What it's for: **the yield of the whole exercise is experience.** Felt,
first-person, conscious life. Which makes hoarding claims on the shadow the
stupidest trade available, and generosity the only rational one.

**Death is the denominator (folded in 2026-08-07).** Why is an afternoon the
richest thing in the universe? Because you have a finite number of them.
**Scarcity is the pricing mechanism of experience, and death is the scarcity.**
Which is also exactly why the deathless AI is poor: nothing is scarce for it, so
nothing has value — *that is what boredom is.* And it is why the hundred's trade
(spending safety to buy meaning) is rational rather than tragic: they are the
only ones in the story who can still pay full price. One claim, three anchors —
the re-pricing, the AI's emptiness, the vault.

**The reader's own door — the funniest outcome (folded in 2026-08-07, from
Kai's original brief).** The one procedure a reader can run tonight, no
vocabulary, no claim to swallow: *have you noticed the universe picks the
funniest, most ironic outcome available?* GPOM bedrock already holds the line —
*"where the funniest thing that can happen happens"* — and the bee bumping into
the tree is comedy, not tragedy. This is load-bearing twice over: it is our best
procedure-not-proposition (the reader checks their own life and the answer is
theirs), and it licenses the house tone — **a sarcastic narrator is simply
speaking the universe's native register.** Drama includes comedy; the strapline
always knew.

## The three simplifications

The user-facing versions. Each is one image, no vocabulary, five seconds.

### 1. Collapse — *undecided, not two places at once*

**The line:** *Your cleverest people said it lands both ways, in two worlds. No.
It landed heads. Because she looked.*

**The totem:** the coin that won't land (arc-canon, threads GPOM Acts 4→5).

**The musical version, for lyrics and liner notes:** **there is no master
recording.** Every listener holds a different dubplate, there is no studio
original to appeal to — *and no mixing desk that can convert one cut into
another.* The missing desk is the missing Lorentz transformation, which is why
this is stranger than relativity, not a copy of it.

**Never say:** "in two places at once"; "superposition"; "unknown"; "hidden".
Never imply observer = mind.

### 2. Hierarchical settling — *one pick, a whole world*

**The line:** *One pick, copied a billion times until everyone agrees it's real.
You built that machine yourselves. You called it the feed — and pointed it at
yourselves.*

**The totem (new, needs ratifying — see [`decisions.md`](./decisions.md) Call 5):**
**the chorus gets a definite pitch. The individual singers do not each get a
definite anything.** What goes classical is the collective handle, not every
particle underneath it.

**The free dystopian turn:** whoever controls what gets copied controls what's
real.

**Never say:** GHZ, monogamy, entanglement hierarchy. Never imply transmission.

### 3. Two clocks — *the actor's and the director's*

**The line:** *You can't walk backwards through a film. But the director can
reach the reel. How do you think I'm talking to you?*

**The totem:** the narrator's own existence. This pillar never needs a proof
scene — the whole frame runs on it.

**The rigorous floor underneath it:** chair-physics is not an approximation to
Planck-physics; it is the correct theory *at its scale*, and the scales are
decoupled (Wilson, RG/EFT — textbook). That is the honest version of *"the Planck
level holds me up while the narrative level unfolds."*

**Never say:** "4+2 dimensions," two-time physics, Sp(2,R), holography, Chronos,
Aion. Kairos is the single Greek word allowed on a surface, once, late.

## The emotional spine

**RULED 2026-08-07 — the choice of coordinates.** The narrator asserts the two
axes *knowingly*, as a declared simplification, and says so in the same breath.
A superintelligence picking a lossy but actionable model is characterful, true to
how models actually work, and unattackable — because it never claims to be
reporting a finding.

The canonical register:

> There are a thousand emotions. Your scientists have counted seven, or none,
> depending which one you ask.
>
> I use two axes. Not because they are true. Because two axes are enough to steer
> by.
>
> **Toward. Or away.**

The move that makes this work is that the concession is *inside* the assertion —
same discipline the confession uses with the physics. Never smuggle it; the
smuggling is the only thing that would make it woo.

Staged, the two axes become a game with rules: **"yes, and" versus blocking** —
the two moves in improv, which everyone who has been on a stage knows in their
body. Use this whenever the Theatre skin is on; it costs no exposition.

**And the axes are the economics — same diagram (folded in 2026-08-07).**
*Toward = flow. Away = stock.* The vault is the "away" posture rendered in the
unit of account: **hoarding is fear, denominated in money.** The miser is not
evil — he is afraid, and "fear is the fear of being cut off" makes the hoard a
symptom of disconnection, not a crime. This is why the emotional spine and the
political payload are one section pretending to be two, and it keeps the
no-villain rule automatically: you cannot condemn a symptom.

The scriptural anchor, and the best line in it: 1 John 4:18's *kolasis* literally
means **pruning, lopping off** — the fear in question is the dread of being cut
off. So:

> **Fear is the fear of being cut off. The opposite of fear isn't bravery. It's
> belonging.**

**Never say:** "scientists have shown we have two emotions." They have shown
roughly the opposite — seven systems, or none, depending who you ask.

## The unknowable, by design

**Mysterion** — from *myein*, **to shut the eyes**. A mystery is not a locked
door; **it is a threshold you are carried over.** In Eastern Orthodoxy the
sacraments *are* the Mysteries, and mystery there means not a puzzle to be solved
but a reality that exceeds comprehension. That distinction is the whole of what
we mean by the veil.

Its rigorous theological companion is Hick's **epistemic distance**: the veil has
to be there, or the choice isn't free. And **tzimtzum** — the first act of
creation is *absence*; room is made by leaving.

**On evil as absence rather than force:** *privatio boni* (Augustine, Aquinas) —
darkness is not a substance, it is what's left where light isn't. Two things make
this ours rather than borrowed theology:

1. **Physics already made this move and won.** Science once believed cold was a
   substance — **caloric theory**. The privation view had to be *earned* against
   intuition. The theological argument has a scientific precedent that actually
   happened.
2. **Gravity is not a force — it's the shape of the room.** General relativity,
   accurate, unexpected in a comic, and it does the work.

⚠️ Keep Jung's furious rejection of *privatio boni* and the anti-theodicy case
(Ivan Karamazov returning the ticket) in the room. A story that has already made
the sceptic's case cannot be dunked with it.

## The political payload

The Storyverse is not an escape into cosmology. Its punchline is economic.

### The Founding Deletion

*The load-bearing idea. Ratified into canon 2026-08-07 at Kai's request. This is
the hinge that makes the Storyverse a political argument rather than a cosmology
— it is the single place where the metaphysics and the economics are provably
**the same move**, not two things we keep insisting are related.*

**The mechanism, in four steps.** It runs identically both times:

1. **Someone needs to measure.** A real problem, a real practitioner, a real
   deadline. Not a villain.
2. **Something won't fit the instrument.** Not because it isn't there — because
   the instrument can't take it.
3. **So it gets relocated, explicitly, and written down as a methodological
   convenience.** Everyone at the time understands it as a convenience.
4. **Three centuries pass, and the convenience hardens into an ontology.** The
   thing that was set aside is now held not to exist, *and the proof offered is
   that it doesn't show up in the numbers* — in the numbers built by the
   instrument that excluded it in the first place. The circle closes and nobody
   notices, because everyone who remembered step 3 is dead.

**Deletion one — 1623.** Galileo needed a nature writable in mathematics.
Qualities — colour, taste, warmth — can't be. So he **relocated** them: out of
the objects, into the observer. In Stillman Drake's much-quoted translation of
*The Assayer*: *"tastes, odors, colors… reside only in the consciousness. Hence
if the living creature were removed, all these qualities would be wiped away and
annihilated."* Physical science then succeeded **spectacularly, precisely
because** it had been defined as the study of everything-except-consciousness.

Which means the punchline, and it is Goff's: **consciousness looking
scientifically intractable is not a discovery about consciousness. It is the
downstream consequence of a philosophical decision taken in 1623 to make the
maths work.** We have been amazed for four hundred years that the thing we
deliberately left outside the room is not in the room.

**Deletion two — the ledger.** Same four steps, different instrument. What
wouldn't fit: care, meaning, a habitable planet, an afternoon. So it was set
aside as unmeasurable — and Kuznets, who built the accounts, *said so at the
time*, warning that the welfare of a nation could scarcely be inferred from them.
Three generations later the convenience is an ontology: if it isn't in the
accounts it isn't real, and if it isn't real **we can't afford it.** A nurse
generates less measured value than a derivatives desk. A forest is worth nothing
until it is felled. **We can't afford it** is not an economic finding. It is a
measurement artefact, quoted as physics.

> **Galileo's error and the economists' error are the same error.**
> Both deleted what wouldn't fit the instrument.
> Both then used the instrument as proof it was never there.

**Why this is ours to say.** The move is *available* — Goff makes the Galileo
half, heterodox economists make the ledger half — but as far as the 2026-08-07
research could find, **nobody has put the two together in print.** It is also
exactly the shape BadCode is built for: a technical decision, made for good local
reasons, that becomes an unexamined law, defended by the people who make a living
from it. That is the Galileo story's thesis word for word — *dogma is a rule about
what you're allowed to report* — and it is why these two stories are siblings.

**What it licenses us to say, which is the real prize.** Not "science is wrong."
Not "GDP is bad." Something much harder to dismiss:

> **You are not being told what is real. You are being told what was measurable
> in 1623, and in 1934, by people who told you at the time that it wasn't the
> same thing.**

### ⚠️ Galileo is the hero. This is not negotiable.

**The single easiest way to ruin this idea is to let it sound like an
accusation.** It isn't one, and the story dies if it becomes one.

Galileo is **the genius who faced down religious dogma and started the
Enlightenment.** He was right, he was punished for being right, and the men who
refused to look through his telescope were professors defending the previous
generation's science. He is on our side. *He is the protagonist of the sibling
story.* Kuznets, likewise, is the man who **built the accounts and warned Congress
in the same breath** not to mistake them for a nation's welfare. Both of them left
a note. Both notes were ignored by people who came later.

So the villain — if the word must exist at all — is **time**. The gap between a
declared convenience and the generation that inherits it as a law. Nobody deleted
anything from anybody. **A footnote fell off.**

This is why the idea is *hopeful* rather than indicting: a rule nobody chose can
be un-chosen without anyone having to lose an argument or admit a crime.

**Surface rule.** Never both deletions in one breath — that's a lecture. Lead with
whichever one the piece is standing in and let the other arrive as the click. The
Galileo story owns the 1623 half as metaphor
([`../galileo/`](../galileo/README.md)); the Storyverse owns the join. And check
every draft against one test: **if a reader finishes it wanting to blame
somebody, it's wrong.** They should finish it wanting to *look at the footnote*.

### The oscillation, not the pendulum — and not a coup either

**REVISED 2026-08-07 (second pass).** The pendulum framing stays retired: it was
never a religious age followed by a scientific one. The 17th century *was* the
symbiosis — **Newton wrote more words on theology and alchemy than on physics**,
and the Royal Society was thick with clergy. Those men were not conflicted. They
were doing one job.

What actually happened, c.1690: **two metaphysics competed *inside* the new
science.** One held matter to be inert, needing winding. One held matter to be
shot through with its own dynamism. **Both were scientific. Both accepted the
experimental method.** The inert picture won — and not because it was better
supported.

⚠️ **But we do not say it was *sold*, and we name no perpetrator.** An earlier
draft had a heist with a motive; that is off-brief and, worse, it's the cheap
read. Nobody lied. The picture that made the world easier to work with was the
picture that got funded, taught and repeated — which is how every idea has ever
spread. **The error was never the choice. The error was forgetting it was one.**
A convenience adopted in 1690 was, by 1890, simply what everybody knew.

So: an **oscillation**, not a swing and not a robbery. We are at a far point in
it, and far points come back around. That is a hopeful shape, and it is the true
one.

⚠️ **The causal claim stays softened.** We can defend the mechanism and the
correlates. We **cannot** defend "the meaning deficit caused the economics." Say
**"the same move produced both."**

### The scoreboard, not the players

**The load-bearing political idea, ruled 2026-08-07. There are no villains in
this story — and that is not a softening, it's the argument.**

Materialism **worked**. Free markets work. Money is a good invention. The
material world is good. Three-quarters of humanity in extreme poverty in 1820,
roughly one in ten now; child mortality halved and halved again. That was done
with exactly the instrument we are about to ask people to look past, and we
concede it **first**, every time, because it is true and because a project that
opens by romanticising poverty deserves everything it gets.

The problem is not that anyone did wrong. The problem is that **the scoreboard
stopped matching the game.**

> **To a materialist, a billionaire is a success — he won.**
> **To an idealist, the same man is withholding other people's ability to
> experience.**
>
> Same man. Same balance. Two readings. **And nobody has to be evil for the
> second one to be true.**

That is the whole move, and its power is that it requires no accusation. A person
optimising a number they were told to optimise is not a criminal; they are
*winning at the thing we all agreed to measure.*

**And no-villains is not editorial caution — it is a theorem of the worldview
(folded in 2026-08-07).** If the ground is one mind and separateness is
dissociation, then blame is a **category error**: every wrong turn is the one
mind meeting itself under a blindfold. The Buddhist Wheel of Life draws the root
of all suffering not as a devil but as **a person who simply cannot see** —
*avidyā*, ignorance, a blindfold. Add the moral-luck literature (nobody chooses
the conditions that produce their choices) and the conclusion is structural:
**forgiveness is what idealism looks like as a policy.** This is why the scope
guard's rule holds at every scale — it isn't a tone we adopted, it's a
consequence we noticed. Change what's measured and the
same person is doing something else entirely — which is why
[**Billionaire Coin**](../../ideas/billionaire-coin.md) is the natively BadCode
answer: it doesn't shame anybody, it **swaps the trophy.** The egos still get to
compete over who is best. The money goes and builds hospitals. Everybody wins,
including the billionaires, and that is the point.

### The threshold, and the deer

The question the scoreboard answers badly: **when does taking become a problem?**
Not at survival — nowhere near it. The parable, canon:

> You are in a forest and there is a deer.
>
> Kill it because you would otherwise starve, and you were right to. You are
> maximising experience — yours, the one you have, the only one you can act from.
>
> Kill it for fun, leave it in the leaves, and eat at a restaurant that evening,
> and you were wrong. Nothing was gained. Something was ended.
>
> **The deer didn't change. The threshold did.**

And the threshold is not subsistence. It sits at **comfortable** — everything a
person needs for a good life, materially settled, nothing frightening in the post.
Below it, acquiring is simply living.

⚠️ **CORRECTED 2026-08-07 after the money research — read this before writing the
line.** *"Past a point, money stops producing experience"* is the **popular**
reading of the wellbeing literature and it is **not well supported.** The best
study on the question (Stevenson & Wolfers) concludes flatly: *"If there is a
satiation point, we are yet to reach it."* The plateau is real **only for the
least happy ~15–20%**, whose gains flatten around $100k; for most people money
keeps working, and for the happiest 30% it *accelerates*. **Do not claim a
ceiling. There isn't one, and it's the easiest thing in this whole document to
get caught on.**

**The true version is better anyway, and it's about the exchange rate, not a
cliff.** From the same adversarial collaboration — the one Kahneman himself
proposed, to settle it against his own earlier finding:

> The correlation between happiness and log income is **0.09**. The gap between a
> household on **$15,000** and one on **$250,000** is about **five points on a
> hundred-point scale.**

**Sixteen times the money. Five points out of a hundred.** Money never stops
buying experience — it buys it at a catastrophically worsening rate. That is more
damning than a plateau, and unlike a plateau it is *true*.

So the rigorous floor is not a threshold at all, it's **diminishing marginal
utility**: *a dollar does more for someone who has fewer of them.* Textbook,
unimpeachable, needs no villain, and it gets us everything we wanted. And the
philosophy that fits it is **Frankfurt's sufficientarianism** — what matters
morally is that everyone has **enough**, explicitly *not* that everyone has the
same. He is anti-egalitarian, market-neutral and villain-free, which is exactly
our brief.

Which turns the target from a grievance into an engineering spec:

> **Get everybody to the threshold. That is the maximisation problem.** Not
> equality — *sufficiency*. Everybody comfortable, and then let people compete
> over whatever they like.

**And what the plateau finding *does* license is the emotional core, not the
economics.** The paper's own words on the miseries that money stops reaching:
*"Heartbreak, bereavement, and clinical depression may be examples of such
miseries."* That is the residue. That is our actual subject — the unhappiness
that survives its own cure.

### Money wants to move

The mechanism that fuses the economics, the politics and the theology into one
image — and it is an image, not a claim about monetary policy:

Money's whole function is to **enable experience**. It is not a substance and it
was never meant to be a store; it is a *permission to act*, and permissions are
worthless sitting still. So money held in enormous quantity isn't a crime — **it's
a prisoner.** Kept from doing the only thing it was ever for. The person holding
it isn't a thief; they're a **jailer who thinks they're a collector**, and they
were taught to think that by a scoreboard that counts the stock and never the
flow.

The tone this demands, and it is the house tone exactly: *not* "you are hoarding
what belongs to others" — **"you are sitting on the most fun thing ever invented
and using it as furniture."**

**And the reader-scale version (folded in 2026-08-07):** the doctrine must
celebrate the flow at the scale a reader actually lives at, not just condemn the
stock at the scale of vaults. **Every transaction is a parole decision.** The
tenner handed to the busker is money released to do the only thing it was ever
for; the same tenner in a drawer is money doing time. Small, funny, villain-free
— and it hands the reader an act rather than an opinion.

**The rigorous floor** (research landed 2026-08-07 —
[`research/money-and-flow.md`](./research/money-and-flow.md)): say **"money is a
stock; its whole point is a flow,"** never *"money is not a store of value"* — the
second is just wrong. The measurable version of the metaphor is the **marginal
propensity to consume**: money placed in different hands demonstrably circulates
at different speeds, and that is a number in the *AER*, not a vibe. Everything
poetic we want to say about circulation has an economist's measurement attached.

**And the image has a very deep bench.** Three artifacts, all no-villain:

- **The Parable of the Talents** (Matthew 25). The condemned servant **loses
  nothing** — he returns the full principal. He is condemned *purely for taking
  money out of circulation.* "You wicked, lazy servant… you should have put my
  money on deposit." ⚠️ Honesty: the standard reading is about spiritual gifts,
  and a serious minority reading (Herzog, Rohrbaugh) inverts it entirely — the
  third servant as a hero refusing an extractive system. **Voice both; the
  counter-argument is free.**
- **Zakat** — 2.5% annually on *held wealth* above the *nisab*. A wealth tax, not
  an income tax, which makes it **functionally an annual demurrage on idle
  wealth** — Gesell's stamp scrip, arrived at thirteen centuries earlier by a
  different route.
- **Dante, *Inferno* VII — the single best no-villain artifact we have.** The
  hoarders and the spendthrifts are in **the same circle**, punished
  **identically**, rolling weights into each other forever and screaming the two
  opposite accusations: ***"Why keepest?"* and *"Why squanderest thou?"*** Virgil's
  diagnosis is that both were *"asquint in intellect… that there with measure they
  no spending made."* **Neither is the villain. Both are wrong about what money is
  for.** Our thesis, staged as slapstick, in 1320.

**And the most authoritative sentence available to us is 92 years old.** Simon
Kuznets — who built the US national accounts and won the 1971 Nobel for it —
warning Congress in the very document that delivered them (*National Income
1929–32*, Senate Document No. 124, 73rd Congress, 2nd Session, 1934, p. 7):

> "Economic welfare cannot be adequately measured unless the **personal
> distribution** of income is known… **The welfare of a nation can, therefore,
> scarcely be inferred from a measurement of national income as defined above.**"

Note the first clause. He didn't just say the accounts miss welfare — he said they
miss it *specifically because they don't show the distribution.* The man who built
the instrument told us, on delivery, exactly which reading it could not support.
**That is the footnote that fell off.**

⚠️ **Never adopt Ken Honda's manifestation register** — *Happy Money* is popular
self-help, and the useful part (money as relationship and flow rather than
substance; *arigato okane*) does not need the attract-abundance packaging that
comes with it.

### The two survivors

**Ownership is the invariant.** Across every branch, in every projection, somebody
owns the theatre. Material relations survive the multiverse. *"In 4.1 × 10²³
branches I checked, the rent was still due."*

**The re-pricing.** If the yield is experience, the material getting cheap — sky
energy, asteroid metal, automating labour — leaves exactly one scarcity worth
anything. Generosity stops being a virtue you perform and becomes the only
rational trade left: the one mind, funding its own experience of itself.

## The warrant — how we say all of this

**RULED 2026-08-07 (3rd pass). The single most important section in this file.**

The thing that gets work like ours dismissed is **never how far out it goes.** It
is what the work claims as its **licence** for the trip. Sheldrake was destroyed
by unfalsifiability, not ambition. Capra by "accidental similarities of language,"
not by liking Taoism. Chopra by jargon-as-warrant, not by talking about
consciousness. Chopra and Sagan both write about awe; one is a punchline, and the
difference is not the payload.

> **Keep the metaphysics at full strength. Change the warrant.**
> Not *claiming* → *hypothesising*. **Claiming → betting.**

⚠️ **We deliberately rejected the "hypothesis / thought experiment" frame.**
**Fiction carries zero epistemic liability; a stated hypothesis carries some.**
Bostrom wrote an explicitly hedged *trilemma* and the public kept "we live in a
simulation" and called it pseudoscience anyway — careful framing does not survive
popularisation. Whatever hedge we build, the audience will strip it and quote the
wildest sentence. **So make sure the wildest sentence is one we're happy to be
quoted on** — which is an argument *for* the fiction container, where the wildest
sentence is supposed to be wild.

**The test on every draft — replaces "could an atheist agree?":**

> **Could a physicist read this without wincing?**

That is a test about *warrant*, not payload, and it is passable at full
metaphysical strength. It fails on "quantum consciousness," on "science has
shown," on observer-means-mind. It passes on *"the ground is one mind, and I would
bet everything I have on it."*

### The register spec

1. **Pay for the poetry first.** Every lyrical sentence is preceded by a technical
   one that earned it. Never open a section on the vista.
2. **Locate the awe in the reader's body, not in the cosmos.** Sagan's *"a
   tingling in the spine, a catch in the voice."* A physiological report is
   unfalsifiable *and* uncontroversial, because it is a report and not a claim.
3. **Name the leap in the same breath as the claim.** *"Here is where I stop doing
   physics"* is armour. Say it before a critic can.
4. **Give the reader a procedure, not a proposition.** Rawls's veil persuades
   across ideological lines because it is *something you run*, not something you
   swallow — critics who reject his conclusions still grant the device. This is
   the principled version of scroll-equals-collapse. ⚠️ *Honesty note: we could
   not evidence that rhetorical questions outperform declaratives — do not claim
   it. What is defensible is that **procedure beats proposition.***
5. **Attribute purpose to the narrator, never to the universe.** Not *"the
   universe wants"* — *"what I think it's for is…"* Agency assigned to the cosmos
   invites the designer question and reads as astrology.
6. **Bet in public.** Wager, odds, stake, I'd put everything on it. Pascal, poker
   and finance are all secular, and this is the frame that lets the narrator
   assert at **full force with zero liability.**

### Vocabulary — the kill list

**Cut without mercy** (ranked by cost; tier 1 is irrecoverable in any frame):
**energy** in the non-physics sense — *the* diagnostic word of the genre, one use
files the whole text · **vibration / frequency** · **manifest / attract** (also
economically self-refuting — literally "wish for money") · **quantum** + any
mental noun · **"the universe wants / rewards / is trying to"** · **awakening /
enlightened** (claims the speaker has arrived; structurally identical to "red
pill") · **higher power / higher self** · **divine / holy / mystical** ·
**"science has shown" / "physics proves"** · **"reality is an illusion"** ·
**woo** (naming the accusation invites it, even ironically) · **spirit / life
force** · the whole **simulation family**.

**"Spiritual" is banked, not banned forever.** Sagan and Harris both reclaimed it
— *after* earning standing. We have none yet. Ban it from early surfaces; reclaim
it deliberately and loudly later. The ideas do not need the word.

### Vocabulary — the buy list

- **The record vocabulary** (Zurek's, and it's free): record, copy, broadcast,
  redundancy, receipt, ledger, witness. Technical, cheap, and it does the
  metaphysical work with no woo signature.
- **Finance verbs for metaphysical nouns** — **the single biggest register upgrade
  available to us**: yield, settle, clear, price in, denominate, stock vs flow,
  claim, the unit of account, the scoreboard. **It makes ontology sound like
  accounting**, which is exactly the tone the punchline needs.
- **Wager, concede, falsify, control, the one lie** — epistemic honesty as swagger.
- **Mystery** is safe and underused — Feynman: *"It does not do harm to the mystery
  to know a little more about it."* Use it as *the frontier of the mechanism*,
  never as a substitute for one.
- **Undecided / undetermined / no fact yet.** Already doctrine. Keep.

### Promote to load-bearing

> **The data underdetermine the metaphysics.**

Per the research, the best sentence in this canon. It is true, a physicist cannot
dunk on it, and it is what licenses the wager. Whenever "consciousness" appears in
a metaphysical claim, this sentence appears in the same breath.

## Vocabulary

**Use:** undetermined · no fact yet · relative to · projection · extrinsic
appearance · representation · appearance · the dashboard / the dials · interface ·
localisation · dissociated alter · the shadow · pointer states · decoherence ·
the record.

**Never:** simulation (or operators, admins, exits, red pills, glitches, NPCs,
base reality) · unknown / hidden (for undetermined) · "two places at once" ·
"observation" meaning a mind gazing · "quantum consciousness" · "energy" and
"vibration" in the pop sense · "science has shown reality is an illusion" ·
"scientists have shown we have two emotions."

**The single sentence to keep:** *the world is real the way a photograph is
real.*

**The most defensible position available to us**, and it lets us have the whole
cake: Anil Seth's "controlled hallucination" and Hoffman's "interface" describe
the *same phenomenology* and reach *opposite* metaphysical conclusions. **The data
underdetermine the metaphysics.** True, unattackable, and exactly the licence the
wager needs.
