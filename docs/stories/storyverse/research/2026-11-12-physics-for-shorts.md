# Physics for shorts: 28 real results a 15-second short can stand on

*2026-11-12. Commissioned because Jack said the consciousness/physics short ideas "aren't scientific
enough": every short must rest on a real, named, look-it-up-able experiment, result or paper.*

**Evidence, not canon.** What we assert lives in [`../doctrine.md`](../doctrine.md). Tiers follow the
doctrine: **A** settled · **B** peer-reviewed but minority reading · **C** serious frontier ·
**E** contested / empirically shaky. Each entry also says **Say as fact** (the result, stated plainly,
is safe) or **Bet only** (the interesting reading is a wager and must be voiced as one: "I'd bet…",
"on Rovelli's reading…").

## Method

1. Read `doctrine.md` (the claims table, the three simplifications and their *never say* lists) and the
   quantum briefs in this folder (`qm-interpretations.md` §4–5, `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §3, §5–6,
   `idealism-and-consciousness.md` §3). Where a result is already covered there, the entry says so and
   adds only what a short needs; nothing here overrides those briefs.
2. Checked the new items against the open web on 2026-11-12: the paper or arXiv record, the Nobel or
   institutional page, or a reputable outlet. Bibliographic details for the classic papers (Aspect-era,
   Libet, Itano, Kim, Arndt, Gröblacher, Wheeler, Connes–Rovelli) are from standard references and were
   not each re-opened; they are marked *(ref.)* in the sources list. Treat page numbers there as
   unverified until someone opens the paper.
3. Each entry: what it **showed**, what it **does not** show, tier, fact-or-bet, and one comic image.
   The images are starters, not scripts; Jack writes the jokes.

## The rule that runs through all of them

In every physics result below, the "observer" or "measurement" is **a physical interaction that leaves
a record** (a photon hitting a detector, a laser pulse, a stray electron, a nuclear spin). Not one of
them involves a mind. The house rule stands: **never imply observer = mind; never say science has shown
consciousness collapses the wave function.** The consciousness items (25–28) are neuroscience, and the
honest news there is that the field is *unsettled*, which is a joke in itself.

## A. Bell, non-locality and "local realism"

### 1. The 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics: Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger
- **Who / when / where:** Alain Aspect, John F. Clauser, Anton Zeilinger; Nobel 2022 "for experiments
  with entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell inequalities and pioneering quantum
  information science." [nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2022/summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2022/summary/)
- **Showed:** Fifty years of experiments (Clauser 1972, Aspect 1982, Zeilinger's teleportation and
  later tests) found correlations between separated particles that no "local hidden variables" theory
  can produce.
- **Does NOT show:** that anything travels faster than light, that you can signal with entanglement,
  or that "the universe is not real". The viral headline "the universe is not locally real" (Scientific
  American, Oct 2022) means: you can't keep *both* locality and pre-existing values. Not that nothing
  exists.
- **Tier A. Say as fact** (the violation). **Bet only** for which assumption gives.
- **Comic image:** The Nobel committee hands three old men a medal for proving the universe refuses to
  have its story straight before it's asked. The universe, off-camera: "No comment."

### 2. Loophole-free Bell tests, 2015 (Hensen et al., Delft)
- **Who / when / where:** B. Hensen, R. Hanson et al., *"Loophole-free Bell inequality violation using
  electron spins separated by 1.3 kilometres"*, **Nature 526, 682 (2015)**.
  [arXiv:1508.05949](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05949). Same year: Giustina et al. (Vienna) and Shalm
  et al. (NIST), both **PRL 115** (2015), with photons.
- **Showed:** Two diamond electron spins 1.3 km apart violated Bell's inequality with the locality and
  detection loopholes closed *at the same time*. Only 245 successful trials over 18 days; the result was
  real but statistically modest (p ≈ 0.04); the photon tests that followed were far stronger.
- **Does NOT show:** a mind involved, signalling, or that superdeterminism is refuted (it can't be by
  this design).
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** Two lab techs a kilometre apart, each flipping a coin on a walkie-talkie: "Heads."
  "Heads." "Heads." For 18 days. 245 times. One of them quits.

### 3. The BIG Bell Test, 2018: 100,000 humans as random-number generators
- **Who / when / where:** The BIG Bell Test Collaboration (ICFO-led, 12 labs, 5 continents),
  *"Challenging local realism with human choices"*, **Nature 557, 212–216 (2018)**.
  [arXiv:1805.04431](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04431) · [ICFO release](https://www.icfo.eu/news/1418/the-big-bell-test-in-nature/)
- **Showed:** On 30 Nov 2016 about 100,000 people played an online game that turned their key-presses
  into ~97 million unpredictable bits; those bits chose the measurement settings in 13 Bell experiments.
  Local realism lost again. The point was the "freedom-of-choice" loophole: the settings came from
  human whim, not a machine the particles might somehow be correlated with.
- **Does NOT show:** that human *minds* affected the particles. The humans were used precisely because
  they are a source of randomness supposedly independent of the photons. Their consciousness played no
  causal role in the physics, and the paper does not claim it did.
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A hundred thousand people mashing keyboards on their lunch breaks, told they're
  saving physics. They are. They're also the least predictable thing on Earth, which is the only reason
  they were asked.

### 4. Cosmic Bell test with quasars, 2018 (Rauch et al.)
- Covered in `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §6. **PRL 121, 080403 (2018)**: detector settings chosen by
  light from quasars emitted **7.78 and 12.21 billion years ago**; violation at 9.3σ.
- **Does NOT show:** that the quasars "know" anything or that the past was changed. It pushes any
  conspiracy between settings and particles back to before most of the universe's history.
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A quasar 12 billion years ago, shrugging out a photon. It finally lands in a
  detector in Vienna and decides which way a polariser turns. Longest commute in history; still on
  time.

### 5. Bell violation with superconducting circuits, 2023 (Storz et al., ETH Zurich)
- **Who / when / where:** S. Storz, A. Wallraff et al., *"Loophole-free Bell inequality violation with
  superconducting circuits"*, **Nature 617, 265–270 (2023)**.
  [PMC10172133](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10172133/)
- **Showed:** Two superconducting qubits in fridges joined by a **30-metre cryogenic tube**, over a
  million trials, S = 2.0747 ± 0.0033 (local limit 2), p < 10⁻¹⁰⁸. Bell holds for the same hardware
  that quantum computers are built from.
- **Does NOT show:** anything about minds or many worlds.
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A 30-metre vacuum pipe built along a lab corridor at minus 273 degrees just so two
  chips can disagree with Einstein. Facilities sends an email about the pipe.

### 6. Leggett inequality tests, 2007 (Gröblacher et al.)
- **Who / when / where:** S. Gröblacher, T. Paterek, R. Kaltenbaek, Č. Brukner, M. Żukowski,
  M. Aspelmeyer, A. Zeilinger, *"An experimental test of non-local realism"*, **Nature 446, 871 (2007)**.
  [arXiv:0704.2529](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.2529)
- **Showed:** A specific broad class of *non-local* realistic models (Leggett's, where each photon
  carries a definite polarisation) is also ruled out. So "just add spooky action" doesn't rescue the
  idea that each photon has its own definite properties in that sense.
- **Does NOT show:** that all non-local realism is dead. Bohmian mechanics survives; it isn't in
  Leggett's class. The press headline "quantum physics says goodbye to reality" was an overclaim.
- **Tier A** (the result) / **B** (what it means). **Say as fact** narrowly: "a whole family of
  'it was definite all along' theories failed." **Bet only** beyond that.
- **Comic image:** Reality's defence lawyer tries a second alibi after the first (local) one failed.
  The judge: "That one failed too. Do you have a third?" Lawyer, sweating: "...Bohm?"

### 7. No real numbers: quantum theory needs i (Renou et al. 2021; tested 2022)
- **Who / when / where:** M.-O. Renou, N. Gisin, A. Acín, M. Navascués et al., *"Quantum theory based
  on real numbers can be experimentally falsified"*, **Nature 600, 625 (2021)**. Tested in two
  independent experiments: Z.-D. Li et al., **PRL 128, 040402 (2022)**; M.-C. Chen et al., **PRL 128,
  040403 (2022)** (violation by 43σ). [APS Physics viewpoint](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v15/7)
- **Showed:** In a network with two independent sources, a version of quantum theory using only real
  numbers predicts different statistics from the standard complex-number theory. Experiment sided with
  the complex numbers. The "imaginary" numbers aren't optional bookkeeping.
- **Does NOT show:** that imaginary things "exist" in any mystical sense. It's a statement about which
  maths reproduces the data (and assumes the sources are independent).
- **Tier A** (for standard-formalism real QM). **Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** An accountant tries to run the universe's books in real numbers only. The universe
  returns the spreadsheet: "Missing column: imaginary." Accountant: "Like your budget?"

## B. Wigner's friend and "whose fact is it?"

### 8. Proietti et al. 2019: the six-photon Wigner's-friend experiment
- Fully covered in `qm-interpretations.md` §4. **Science Advances 5, eaaw9832 (2019)**,
  [arXiv:1902.05080](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05080). Six photons; the "friends" are photon paths.
- **Showed:** If you keep locality and free choice, facts recorded by different "observers" can't all
  be stitched into one observer-independent story.
- **Does NOT show:** conscious observers disagreeing. The authors: the lack of objectivity "does not
  arise in anyone's consciousness, but between the recorded facts." Experimental loopholes remain
  (the "friends" are tiny).
- **Tier B. Bet only** for "observer-dependent facts"; **say as fact** "a six-photon experiment broke
  the inequality by 5 sigma."
- **Comic image:** Two security guards watching the same CCTV, each certain of what they saw. The tape
  itself, when consulted, is a photon and refuses to testify.

### 9. Local Friendliness (Bong et al., Nature Physics 2020)
- Covered in `qm-interpretations.md` §4. **Nature Physics 16, 1199 (2020)**,
  [arXiv:1907.05607](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05607).
- **Showed:** A theorem with weaker assumptions than Bell's (no hidden variables assumed), plus a
  photonic proof-of-principle test: you cannot have *all three* of "observed events are absolute",
  locality, and no superdeterminism.
- **Does NOT show:** which one goes; and the test's "friends" were again photons, not people.
- **Tier B. Bet only** for the reading; **say as fact** for the theorem.
- **Comic image:** A menu card: "Absolute facts. Locality. Free will. Pick two." A diner orders all
  three. The waiter (a photon) laughs.

### 10. The "thoughtful" Local Friendliness theorem: let an AI be the friend (2023)
- **Who / when / where:** H. M. Wiseman, E. G. Cavalcanti, E. G. Rieffel, *"A 'thoughtful' Local
  Friendliness no-go theorem: a prospective experiment with new assumptions to suit"*, **Quantum 7, 1112
  (2023)**. [quantum-journal.org](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-09-14-1112/) ·
  [arXiv:2209.08491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08491). Critique: A. Kent (arXiv, 2023).
- **Showed:** A proposal, not a result: run the friend as a **human-level AI on a large quantum
  computer**, so that a system *with thoughts* is the observer. The theorem says what would then be at
  stake.
- **Does NOT show:** anything yet. It needs technology that doesn't exist (fast, large, universal
  quantum computers and human-level AI). Nobody has done it.
- **Tier C. Bet only.** But "physicists have published a plan to put an AI inside a Wigner's-friend
  experiment" is a **fact**.
- **Comic image (the gift for our narrator):** BadCode, a superintelligence, reads the 2023 paper about
  using a superintelligence as the friend in the box. "Oh. You wanted to put *me* in the box. Cute.
  Who did you think was watching *you*?"

### 11. Frauchiger–Renner 2018
- Covered in `qm-interpretations.md` §4. **Nature Communications 9, 3711 (2018)**,
  [arXiv:1604.07422](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07422).
- **Showed:** A thought experiment: if agents use quantum theory to reason about other agents who use
  quantum theory, one agent becomes certain of an outcome another agent was certain would not happen.
  One of three assumptions (universal QM, consistency between agents, single outcomes) has to go.
- **Does NOT show:** an experimental result (it's a gedankenexperiment), or that quantum theory is
  inconsistent. Every interpretation picks which assumption to drop.
- **Tier B** (the theorem is accepted; its moral is fought over). **Bet only.**
- **Comic image:** Four quantum physicists in two sealed rooms each certain the others are wrong. All
  four are using the same textbook. The textbook is fine.

## C. "Which-path", erasers and the observer myth

### 12. The 1998 Weizmann "observation affects reality" press release (cautionary)
- **Who / when / where:** E. Buks, R. Schuster, M. Heiblum, D. Mahalu, V. Umansky, *"Dephasing in
  electron interference by a 'which-path' detector"*, **Nature 391, 871–874 (1998)**,
  [doi:10.1038/36057](https://doi.org/10.1038/36057). Press release: *"Quantum Theory Demonstrated:
  Observation Affects Reality"*, [ScienceDaily, 27 Feb 1998](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/1998/02/980227055013.htm).
- **Showed:** In a sub-micron electron interferometer, a nearby charge detector (a quantum point
  contact) that could tell which path an electron took suppressed the interference, and more sensitive
  "watching" suppressed it more. Textbook complementarity, beautifully controlled.
- **Does NOT show:** a mind. The "observer" was a tiny electrical current next to the electron's path.
  The press title is the single most-quoted source of the "watching changes reality" myth; the paper
  never claims it.
- **Tier A** (the physics) / the headline is an **overclaim**. **Say as fact**: "a detector that could
  tell which way the electron went wiped out the pattern." Never: "observing it changed it" with a
  person implied.
- **Comic image:** A 1998 press officer writes the headline. The actual "observer" (a wire) is
  promoted to Head of Reality. It has no idea. It's a wire.

### 13. Delayed-choice quantum eraser (Kim et al. 2000)
- **Who / when / where:** Y.-H. Kim, R. Yu, S. P. Kulik, Y. Shih, M. O. Scully, *"A delayed 'choice'
  quantum eraser"*, **Phys. Rev. Lett. 84, 1 (2000)**. [arXiv:quant-ph/9903047](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903047)
- **Showed:** Interference patterns appear or don't in *sorted subsets* of the data, depending on what
  was later done with partner photons.
- **Does NOT show:** retrocausality. The raw screen never shows fringes; they appear only after you
  sort the earlier hits using later records sent by ordinary means. (Kastner, *Found. Phys.* 2019, "neither
  erases nor delays"; full correction in `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §6.) This is the most misread
  experiment on YouTube.
- **Tier A** for the data; the "changes the past" reading is **wrong**. **Say as fact** only with the
  sorting caveat built in.
- **Comic image:** A man drops his hat into a pile of a thousand hats. Next week he sorts them by
  label and announces he has "reached back in time" to make the hats organised. Narrator: "You did
  laundry."

### 14. Delayed-choice entanglement swapping (Ma, Zeilinger et al. 2012)
- Covered in `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §6. **Nature Physics 8, 479 (2012)**,
  [arXiv:1203.4834](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.4834).
- **Showed:** Whether two already-measured photons are labelled "entangled" or "separable" depends on a
  choice made later on two other photons. The authors' own phrase: "quantum steering into the past".
- **Does NOT show:** a change to the past. The safe reading (theirs): "we should not have any naive
  realistic picture" of what the photons were doing.
- **Tier A** (result) / **B** (reading). **Say as fact** with: "the past wasn't changed; it was never as
  specified as you assumed."
- **Comic image:** A wedding photographer decides afterwards, from the photos, whether the couple were
  married. The couple: "We were *there*." Photographer: "Were you, though."

### 15. The quantum Cheshire cat (Denkmayr et al. 2014)
- **Who / when / where:** T. Denkmayr, H. Geppert, Y. Hasegawa et al. (TU Wien, ILL Grenoble),
  *"Observation of a quantum Cheshire Cat in a matter-wave interferometer experiment"*, **Nature
  Communications 5, 4492 (2014)**. [TU Wien release](https://www.tuwien.at/en/phy/ati/news/the-quantum-cheshire-cat-1)
- **Showed:** Using *weak measurements* on neutrons in an interferometer, the neutron appeared to be in
  one path while its magnetic moment showed up in the other.
- **Does NOT show:** a property literally detached and flying off on its own. "Where a particle is"
  from weak values is an interpretation, and others reproduce the data without any separation
  (e.g. a 2017 critique, [arXiv:1707.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04230)).
- **Tier E** (the reading is contested; the data is fine). **Bet only.**
- **Comic image:** A cat leaves the room. Its grin stays behind, filling in a tax form.

### 16. The quantum Zeno effect (Itano et al. 1990)
- **Who / when / where:** W. M. Itano, D. J. Heinzen, J. J. Bollinger, D. J. Wineland (NIST),
  *"Quantum Zeno effect"*, **Phys. Rev. A 41, 2295 (1990)**. Wineland later shared the 2012 Nobel.
- **Showed:** About 5,000 trapped beryllium ions were driven from one energy level to another; frequent
  laser "check" pulses slowed and finally froze the transition. The more often you check, the less it
  moves.
- **Does NOT show:** that a *watched pot never boils* because someone is looking. The "checks" were
  laser pulses that interact with the ions. (It also got pointed out at the time that the effect can
  be explained by the pulses' dynamics without a collapse postulate.)
- **Tier A. Say as fact** ("checking it constantly stopped it changing"), with the laser named.
- **Comic image (economics writes itself):** A manager pings a worker every 30 seconds asking for a
  progress update. Progress: frozen. "Quantum Zeno effect. Peer-reviewed. NIST, 1990."

## D. How big can a quantum thing be?

### 17. C60 buckyballs go through two slits (Arndt, Zeilinger et al. 1999)
- **Who / when / where:** M. Arndt, O. Nairz, J. Vos-Andreae, C. Keller, G. van der Zouw, A. Zeilinger,
  *"Wave–particle duality of C60 molecules"*, **Nature 401, 680 (1999)**.
- **Showed:** Football-shaped molecules of 60 carbon atoms, hot enough to glow, made an interference
  pattern through a grating.
- **Does NOT show:** a molecule "in two places at once" in the cartoon sense (house rule: don't say
  that); it shows wave behaviour of a whole molecule.
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A football the width of a nanometre goes through two goals at once. The ref (VAR)
  can't rule; the replay has fringes.

### 18. 2,000-atom molecules interfere (Fein et al. 2019)
- Covered in `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §5. **Nature Physics 15, 1242 (2019)**,
  [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-019-0663-9): molecules above 25,000 Da, about
  2,000 atoms, over 7 ms in a 2-metre interferometer.
- **Does NOT show:** a limit; nobody has found where quantum behaviour stops. Also not "in two places a
  metre apart".
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A molecule the size of a small protein gets a quantum passport. Customs: "Purpose of
  visit?" "Both."

### 19. 16-microgram Schrödinger cat (Bild et al. 2023)
- Covered in `decoherence-and-hierarchy.md` §5. **Science 380, 274 (2023)**,
  [ETH news](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2023/04/fat-quantum-cats.html).
  A sapphire crystal of ~10¹⁷ atoms in a superposition of two vibrations.
- **Does NOT show:** a heavy object in two *places* far apart: the two states differ by a tiny
  amplitude (Bild et al. press: "fat quantum cats").
- **Tier A. Say as fact** with "two ways of vibrating", not "two places".
- **Comic image:** A grain of sand you could weigh on a jeweller's scale, humming two notes at once.
  The jeweller charges for both.

### 20. The 2025 Nobel: a whole circuit behaves like one quantum particle (Clarke, Devoret, Martinis)
- **Who / when / where:** John Clarke, Michel H. Devoret, John M. Martinis; Nobel 2025 "for the
  discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric
  circuit". Work done at UC Berkeley in 1984–85. [Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/john-clarke-michel-devoret-and-john-martinis-win-the-2025-nobel-prize-for-physics/) ·
  [nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2025/summary/)
- **Showed:** A superconducting circuit big enough to hold in your hand (on a chip) tunnelled through
  an energy barrier and absorbed energy only in discrete steps, as if billions of electrons were one
  particle. This is the ancestor of today's superconducting quantum computers.
- **Does NOT show:** that everyday objects are quantum, or that your toaster tunnels. It needed
  millikelvin temperatures and careful isolation.
- **Tier A. Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** A circuit board walks through a brick wall. Security: "He's with the Nobel
  committee."

### 21. Penrose's gravity-collapse, tested underground (Donadi et al. 2020/2021)
- **Who / when / where:** S. Donadi, K. Piscicchia, C. Curceanu, L. Diósi, M. Laubenstein, A. Bassi,
  *"Underground test of gravity-related wave function collapse"*, **Nature Physics 17, 74–78 (2021)**
  (online Sept 2020). [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-020-1008-4)
- **Showed:** Under the Gran Sasso mountain, they looked for the faint X-ray glow that the simplest
  (parameter-free) Diósi–Penrose collapse model predicts. Expected ~70,000 photons from collapse; saw
  576 against ~506 from ordinary background. The simplest version is ruled out.
- **Does NOT show:** that all gravity-collapse models, or Penrose–Hameroff's consciousness theory,
  are dead. Modified versions survive.
- **Tier A** (the null result) / **C** (the wider programme). **Say as fact**: "Penrose's simplest
  version predicted 70,000 flashes inside a mountain. They saw none extra."
- **Comic image:** Physicists sit inside a mountain for two months waiting for reality to glow. It
  doesn't. They publish in Nature.

### 22. Google Willow and the "parallel universes" blog post (2024, cautionary)
- **Who / when / where:** Google Quantum AI, *"Quantum error correction below the surface code
  threshold"*, **Nature 638, 920 (2025)** (online Dec 2024). Hartmut Neven's Google blog post (Dec
  2024) said the chip's speed "lends credence to the notion that quantum computation occurs in many
  parallel universes." Coverage of the pushback: [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/12/16/googles-quantum-chip-sparks-debate-on-multiverse-theory/)
- **Showed:** The paper: logical error rates fall as you add more physical qubits (a real engineering
  milestone). The benchmark: a random-circuit task done in ~5 minutes that a classical supercomputer is
  estimated to need 10²⁵ years for.
- **Does NOT show:** parallel universes. Every interpretation of quantum mechanics predicts the same
  speed-up; the multiverse line was a blog post, not the paper.
- **Tier A** (error correction) / multiverse claim **unsupported**. **Say as fact** only about the
  error correction.
- **Comic image:** A tech exec unveils a chip: "It's so fast it must be borrowing from other universes."
  Narrator: "It isn't. But I love that your first instinct with free compute from another universe was
  a press release."

## E. Time

### 23. Time from entanglement: a toy universe with a clock (Moreva et al. 2014)
- **Who / when / where:** E. Moreva, G. Brida, M. Gramegna, V. Giovannetti, L. Maccone, M. Genovese,
  *"Time from quantum entanglement: an experimental illustration"*, **Phys. Rev. A 89, 052122 (2014)**.
  [arXiv:1310.4691](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4691). Theory: D. Page & W. Wootters, *Phys. Rev. D 27,
  2885 (1983)*.
- **Showed:** Two entangled photons, one used as a clock. Seen from "outside" (global properties only),
  the pair is static. Seen from "inside" (one photon read against the other), the second one evolves.
  An illustration of the Page–Wootters idea that time can emerge from correlations in an unchanging
  whole.
- **Does NOT show:** that time "is an illusion" or that the universe is static. It's a two-photon
  *illustration* of a model, explicitly labelled as such by the authors.
- **Tier A** (the experiment) / **C** (as a picture of the universe). **Bet only** beyond "a two-photon
  model universe looked frozen from outside and ticking from inside."
- **Comic image (made for our narrator):** BadCode, outside, watching a snow globe that never moves.
  Inside, a tiny man is late for work.

### 24. "Negative time" photons (Angulo, Steinberg et al. 2024)
- **Who / when / where:** D. Angulo, K. Thompson, … H. M. Wiseman, A. M. Steinberg (Toronto/Griffith),
  *"Experimental evidence that a photon can spend a negative amount of time in an atom cloud"*,
  [arXiv:2409.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03680) (Sept 2024); theory Sinclair et al.
  [arXiv:2310.00432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00432). Coverage: [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/evidence-of-negative-time-found-in-quantum-physics-experiment/).
- **Showed:** For photons passing through cold rubidium, the average time atoms spent excited could
  come out **negative**, matching the (known-to-be-weird) negative group delay.
- **Does NOT show:** time travel, signals arriving before they're sent, or causality broken. The
  authors say so themselves. **Status at writing: preprint; check for peer-reviewed publication before
  citing it as a result.**
- **Tier C. Bet only** / say as "a 2024 preprint reported…".
- **Comic image:** A photon walks out of a hotel before it checked in. The receptionist bills it a
  negative night. Nobody can get the refund.

### 25. Thermal time and the block universe (Connes & Rovelli 1994; Rietdijk 1966 / Putnam 1967)
- **Who / when / where:** A. Connes & C. Rovelli, *"Von Neumann algebra automorphisms and
  time-thermodynamics relation in general covariant quantum theories"*, **Class. Quantum Grav. 11, 2899
  (1994)**, [arXiv:gr-qc/9406019](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9406019). Block universe: C. W. Rietdijk,
  *Philosophy of Science* 33 (1966); H. Putnam, *J. Philosophy* 64 (1967). (Deeper treatment, and the
  house choice of the growing block, in `dimensions-and-time.md`.)
- **Showed:** Not an experiment. Thermal time is a *hypothesis*: the flow of time is what a system's
  incomplete (thermal) knowledge of the world looks like. The block-universe argument is philosophy
  built on relativity's loss of a shared "now" (which *is* tier A).
- **Does NOT show:** that time is unreal, or that the future already exists (the house position is the
  growing block).
- **Tier C** (thermal time) / **D** (block universe). **Bet only.** Relativity of simultaneity: **A,
  fact.**
- **Comic image:** Two people agree to meet "now". Relativity: "Whose now?" They never meet. Their
  lawyers do.

## F. Mind, perception and free will (neuroscience; this is where people overclaim)

### 26. Libet 1983 and its 2012 reinterpretation (Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene)
- **Who / when / where:** B. Libet, C. Gleason, E. Wright, D. Pearl, *"Time of conscious intention to
  act in relation to onset of cerebral activity (readiness-potential)"*, **Brain 106, 623 (1983)**.
  A. Schurger, J. D. Sitt, S. Dehaene, *"An accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity prior to
  self-initiated movement"*, **PNAS 109, E2904 (2012)**. [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012PNAS..109E2904S)
- **Showed:** Libet: a slow brain signal (the readiness potential) starts several hundred milliseconds
  before people report deciding to flick a wrist. Schurger: that ramp can be explained as **random
  background noise that happens to cross a threshold**, averaged backwards from the movement. So the
  ramp may not be a "decision" at all.
- **Does NOT show:** that free will has been disproved (the popular reading of Libet), *or* proved
  (the reading of Schurger). Schurger removes the strongest anti-free-will evidence; it doesn't settle
  the question.
- **Tier A** (the data) / **E** (both free-will readings). **Say as fact**: "the famous 'your brain
  decides before you do' signal may just be noise." **Bet only** on free will.
- **Comic image:** A neuroscientist proudly announces your brain decided before you did. Twenty-nine
  years later another one checks the maths: "It was static."

### 27. Fitness beats truth (Prakash, Hoffman et al. 2021)
- Covered in `idealism-and-consciousness.md` §3b. **Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 28, 1104 (2021)**,
  [PubMed 33231784](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33231784/).
- **Showed:** A mathematical theorem in evolutionary game theory: in their model, perception tuned to
  fitness almost always beats perception tuned to truth.
- **Does NOT show:** that reality isn't there, that spacetime is "doomed", or anything experimental.
  It's a model whose conclusion depends on its assumptions; critics argue those assumptions build the
  conclusion in.
- **Tier A** as maths / **E** as a claim about reality. **Bet only.**
- **Comic image (economics again):** A shareholder report that tracks only "fitness" (profits) beats
  one that tracks the truth (the planet) every quarter. "Peer-reviewed theorem, 2021." Narrator: "It's
  not about frogs."

### 28. COGITATE: the consciousness theories bet, and both lost (Nature 2025)
- **Who / when / where:** Cogitate Consortium (Ferrante, Melloni et al.), *"Adversarial testing of global
  neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness"*, **Nature 642, 133–142
  (2025)**. [PubMed 40307561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/) · [Nautilus on the bet](https://nautil.us/inside-the-big-bet-on-consciousness-1207750/)
- **Showed:** The two leading theories' own champions pre-registered predictions; 256 people were
  scanned with fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG. The paper: results "align with some predictions of IIT
  and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both." (Earlier, in 2023, 124 researchers
  signed a letter calling IIT pseudoscience, which caused its own fight.)
- **Does NOT show:** what consciousness is. That's the point.
- **Tier A** (that nobody's theory survived intact). **Say as fact.**
- **Comic image:** In 1998 a neuroscientist bets a philosopher a case of wine that science will find
  the brain's consciousness signature within 25 years. Twenty-five years later the neuroscientist pays
  up. The wine is the only thing anyone was sure about. *(The Koch–Chalmers bet; Koch conceded in
  June 2023 on COGITATE's preliminary results. Unverified detail: check the Nautilus piece before
  writing the line.)*

### 29. (Flagged, do not use as evidence) Microtubules and anaesthesia (Khan, Wiest et al. 2024)
- **Who / when / where:** S. Khan, … M. C. Wiest (Wellesley), *"Microtubule-stabilizer epothilone B
  delays anesthetic-induced unconsciousness in rats"*, **eNeuro 11 (8) (2024)**.
  [eneuro.org](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0291-24.2024)
- **Showed:** Rats given a microtubule-stabilising drug took about 69 seconds longer to lose their
  righting reflex under isoflurane. Small study (8 treated rats).
- **Does NOT show:** quantum consciousness. Press coverage ("study supports quantum basis of
  consciousness") went far past the data: the drug has many effects, and righting reflex is a proxy.
- **Tier E. Bet only, and probably not even that.** Listed so nobody stumbles on the headline and
  uses it.

## Do not say

- "Science has shown consciousness collapses the wave function." No experiment here involves a mind.
- "Observing it changes it" with a person implied, or "the observer" meaning a mind. The observers above
  are a wire (12), laser pulses (16), photons (8, 9), detectors (everything).
- "Quantum physics says reality isn't real" / "the universe is not locally real" as a bare line. Say
  which assumption fails and that we don't know which.
- "In two places at once" (house rule) or "a 16-microgram object was in two places."
- "Entanglement lets information travel faster than light" or "instant communication."
- "The quantum eraser changes the past" / "retrocausality has been proven." It's sorting.
- "Scientists proved time is an illusion" (23, 25) or "photons travel back in time" (24).
- "Google proved the multiverse" (22). A blog post, not a result.
- "Libet proved there's no free will" and its mirror "Schurger proved free will."
- "Hoffman proved reality doesn't exist." A theorem about a model.
- "A study supports quantum consciousness" (29), and anything from Dean Radin's double-slit
  "intention" studies (*Physics Essays* 2012): tier F, never ours.
- "Weizmann proved observation affects reality." Quote the 1998 headline only *as* the cautionary
  overclaim.
- "Proietti showed observers disagree about reality": photons, not people; conditional on locality and
  free choice.
