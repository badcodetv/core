**Epistemic key used throughout:**
- 🟢 **ESTABLISHED** — uncontroversial fact, or a claim the mainstream accepts.

## 0. The one-paragraph orientation

There is a real, live, credentialled minority tradition in analytic philosophy and cognitive science that says consciousness is not produced by matter — matter is how consciousness looks from outside. It is *not* a fringe of cranks: its exponents publish in *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, *Synthese*, *Entropy*, *Philosophical Quarterly*, and have chairs at Durham, UC Irvine, Pavia, and Wisconsin. It is also *not* mainstream: the dominant position in philosophy of mind remains physicalism, and the dominant position in consciousness science is that consciousness is a biological phenomenon to be explained mechanistically. The gap between "serious" and "accepted" is exactly where your fiction should live. The strongest move you can make is to be *more* honest about that gap than the popularisers are.

## 2. Bernardo Kastrup — analytic idealism, the actual argument

🟢 **ESTABLISHED (biography).** Two doctorates: computer engineering (Eindhoven University of Technology, 2001, on AI and reconfigurable computing) and philosophy (Radboud University Nijmegen, 2019, dissertation *Analytic Idealism: A consciousness-only ontology*). Undergrad electronic engineering, Federal University of Rio de Janeiro, 1997. Career at CERN, Philips Research, ASML; co-founder of Silicon Hive (acquired by Intel, 2011). Currently Executive Director of Essentia and CEO of Euclyd. Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Kastrup>. Sixteen books, 2011–2025, mostly Iff Books / Collective Ink.

### The argument, in its load-bearing steps

**Step 1 — the only thing you are ever acquainted with is experience.** 🟡 This is the classic idealist opening and Kastrup treats it as the epistemic baseline: matter is an *inference*, experience is a *given*.

**Step 2 — universal phenomenal consciousness is the sole ontological primitive.** From the dissertation: universal phenomenal consciousness "is all there ultimately is, everything else in nature being reducible to patterns of excitation of this consciousness." Note the vocabulary: **excitations of a field**, not "things made of mind-stuff."

**Step 3 — the world is the *extrinsic appearance* of mental processes.** The canonical formulation, from his own site (<https://analyticidealism.com/home.html>): *"Reality consists of mental processes, and what we call the physical world is the extrinsic appearance of those processes."* This is the single most useful sentence in the whole literature for your purposes — see §8.

Corollary he leans on hard: **brain activity is what a dissociated inner life looks like from across the dissociative boundary.** Brains don't *produce* experience; brains are the *image* of experience, the way a footprint is the image of a foot. He uses this to explain the neural correlates of consciousness without conceding that neurons cause experience.

**Step 4 — dissociation explains individual subjects.** Individual human and animal minds are "localised, dissociated regions" of the one field — "alters," borrowing the clinical term from Dissociative Identity Disorder. 🟡 The empirical hook Kastrup repeatedly cites: DID patients show *measurable physiological differences between alters* (the well-known case literature includes functionally blind alters in a sighted patient), which he offers as proof of concept that one mind can split into subjectively sealed compartments.

**Step 5 — parsimony.** 🟡 His central polemical claim: idealism posits **one** category of thing (experience) where physicalism posits **two** (matter, plus experience somehow arising from it), and physicalism then cannot bridge them — the hard problem. Idealism, he argues, is therefore *more* parsimonious, not less, and is "a realist, naturalist, rationalist, and even reductionist view."

**Step 6 — the hard problem dissolves rather than resolves.** If experience is fundamental, there is nothing to explain about how it arose. What remains to be explained is the *reverse* direction: why does the one mind appear as many, and why does it appear as a world of apparently non-mental objects? Answer: dissociation, plus perception as a **dashboard**.

### The dashboard metaphor (his, and the most quotable thing he has)

Kastrup: our senses present the environment "not as it is in itself, but instead in a coded and truncated form as a **dashboard of dials**" — the physical world *is* the dials. We are pilots flying by instrument with no transparent windshield. The dial showing wind speed is not the wind; it is a *real* reading, *about* something real, in a format that has nothing to do with what it represents. The clouds, lightning and wind outside are real. So is the dial. Neither is fake.

He is explicit that this is **not** "the world is an illusion" and **not** the simulation hypothesis. He rejects naive realism *and* unreality: idealism holds "there is a world out there independent of our individual minds, but such world is — just like ourselves — also mental or experiential."

### The serious criticisms — where the ice is thin

🔴/⚠️ **Where Kastrup gets hit hardest:**

1. **The dissociation mechanism is an analogy, not a model.** Kastrup concedes this himself: "Although the empirical appeal to dissociation is sufficient to substantiate Analytic Idealism, one would like to have a more formal, conceptual, explicit account of the process of dissociation." He listed a formal model of dissociation as an open challenge for himself as recently as 2024 (<https://www.bernardokastrup.com/2024/01/analytic-idealism-uaps-daimon-and-model.html>). **This is the biggest hole. A smart sceptic will go here first.** DID is a contested clinical diagnosis being used as the load-bearing mechanism for the structure of reality.

2. **The metabolism criterion is under-motivated.** Kastrup holds that dissociated alters correspond to *metabolising organisms* — which is how he avoids saying your thermostat is conscious. Richard Carrier's critique (<https://www.richardcarrier.info/archives/32492>) argues this conflates humans with "metabolising organisms" generally (which would include plants and bacteria), and that the inference commits both affirming-the-consequent and denying-the-antecedent. Carrier's overall verdict — "pseudoscientific crackpottery" that passes peer review only because philosophy's standards are weak — is harsh and rhetorically loaded, but the *specific* logical objections are worth knowing.

3. **The parsimony claim can be reversed.** Carrier: "physicalism plus science can thus explain the weirdly complex machinery of the Periodic Table. But Idealism plus science can't." I.e. idealism buys ontological simplicity and pays for it with explanatory epicycles.

4. **The "mind at large" is itself an unobservable posit.** A widely-made objection: mind-at-large and its alters "fall prey to the same criticism he levels against physical materialism: both are unobservable explanatory abstractions derived from patterns in experience."

5. **The quantum mechanics argument is the weakest link.** Sean Carroll on the *Scientific American* piece: "This is a bad article about quantum mechanics, @sciam. What the author labels 'the current paradigm' is something that essentially no working physicist actually believes." (<https://x.com/seanmcarroll/status/988800317717266432>). Carroll's charge: the authors misunderstand the interpretations, misrepresent physicists' actual views, and misread the experiments. **Recommendation for BadCode: do not hang your story on "quantum mechanics proves observers create reality." It's the single most dunkable move available.** Kastrup has also debated Christof Koch, Philip Goff and Sabine Hossenfelder publicly; Rupert Sheldrake has criticised analytic idealism from the *other* side (Kastrup's reply: <https://www.bernardokastrup.com/2024/11/response-to-rupert-sheldrakes.html>).

6. **Journalistic defence exists too:** John Horgan has defended Kastrup's work, comparing it to John Archibald Wheeler's.

## 3. Donald Hoffman

🟢 **ESTABLISHED (biography).** Professor of Cognitive Science, UC Irvine. Genuine standing in vision science before any of this. Faculty page and papers: <https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~ddhoff/>

### 3a. Interface Theory of Perception (ITP)

🟡 **SERIOUS MINORITY VIEW.** Published in *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* (Hoffman, Singh & Prakash, 2015) — <https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-015-0890-8> — with peer commentary, which is the venue's way of saying "this is contentious but worth arguing about."

Claim: perception evolved to guide *adaptive behaviour*, not to report *truth*. What we see is a species-specific **user interface**, not a window. The desktop metaphor: a blue rectangular icon in the corner of your screen tells you nothing true about the file's colour, shape or position — and yet it is not a lie, and dragging it to the trash really does delete the file. **The interface is useful precisely because it hides the truth.** Hoffman also uses the VR-headset framing: every conscious agent wears an evolutionarily-shaped headset.

### 3b. The Fitness-Beats-Truth (FBT) theorem

🟢 **ESTABLISHED as mathematics.** 🔴 **CONTESTED as to what it means.** Prakash, Stephens, Hoffman, Singh & Fields, "Fitness Beats Truth in the Evolution of Perception," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* (2021) — <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33231784/>, PDF <https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~ddhoff/FitnessBeatsTruth_apa_PBR.pdf>

The formal statement (as given in the literature): *for any world whose states form a compact regular Borel space, for any given perceptual map, for all possible fitness functions, and for all a priori probabilities of world states absolutely continuous with respect to the Borel measure, the probability that a strict interface strategy strictly dominates an omniscient realist strategy whose perceptual space X is of equal size |X| is at least (|X| − 3)/(|X| − 1).*

Read that bound: as the perceptual space gets large, the probability tends to 1. That is the punchline — **in a rich world, a truth-tracking perceiver is almost certainly beaten by a fitness-tracking one.** The stated escape clause is that veridical perception survives only if fitness varies *monotonically* with truth.

**Quotable in plain English:** evolution does not select for seeing reality. It selects for having children. Where those come apart, reality loses.

### 3c. Conscious agents and "spacetime is doomed"

🔴 **SPECULATION — formally developed, but speculation.** Hoffman, Prakash & Prentner, "Fusions of Consciousness," *Entropy* 25(1):129, 9 Jan 2023 — open access: <https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/25/1/129> / <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858210/>

A **conscious agent** is formally a 6-tuple *C = ((X,𝒳), (G,𝒢), (W,𝒲), P, D, A)* — a measurable space of potential experiences, of potential actions, of world states, plus Markovian kernels for **P**erception, **D**ecision and **A**ction. Agents combine into networks; the paper proves (Eq. 16) that a network of conscious agents itself satisfies the definition of a single conscious agent — i.e. the formalism is **closed under combination**, which is Hoffman's answer to the combination problem. The possible dynamics of *n* agents form an *n(n−1)*-dimensional **Markov polytope** *Mₙ* with *nⁿ* vertices; total fusions form an *(n−1)*-simplex, the **fusion simplex** *Fₙ*.

The physics hook: Hoffman cites Nima Arkani-Hamed's 2010 Cornell Messenger Lecture — **"the very notion of spacetime is not a fundamental one. Spacetime is doomed."** 🟢 That quote is real and Arkani-Hamed genuinely means it; the amplituhedron programme computes scattering amplitudes without reference to spacetime locality or unitarity as inputs.

Hoffman's move: define a map from Markov chains (via communicating classes) to **decorated permutations** — the combinatorial objects that index cells of the positive Grassmannian and hence faces of the amplituhedron — and propose that *spacetime and its scattering processes are a data structure that codes for interactions of conscious agents.*

**⚠️ Where the ice is thin, precisely:**
- ✅ Proved: the closure result, the polytope/simplex structure, the map to decorated permutations.
- ❌ Conjectured: that particles *are* projections of communicating classes of conscious agents onto amplituhedron faces; that entropic time is an artefact of that projection; that evolution itself is such a projection.
- 🔴 The critical point a physicist will make: Arkani-Hamed's "spacetime is doomed" means *spacetime is emergent from deeper mathematical structure* — it does not mean *consciousness is that structure*. Hoffman is borrowing the authority of a real physics programme for a metaphysics that programme does not entail. Do not conflate them in your prose. (Sharp version of this critique: Paul Austin Murphy, "Donald Hoffman Is Lost in Maths" — <https://medium.com/paul-austin-murphys-essays-on-philosophy/donald-hoffman-is-lost-in-maths-decorated-permutations-markov-chains-and-idealism-945ef72d389d>)

### 3d. The substantive criticisms of Hoffman

1. **Does FBT prove what he says?** The best peer-reviewed attack: "Debunking interface theory: why Hoffman's skepticism (really) is self-defeating," *Synthese* (2022) — <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-022-04021-1>. Core charge: simulations incorporating features of evolution Hoffman himself accepts yield results *inconsistent* with his conclusion, and the inference from theorem to metaphysics relies on an assumption that **begs the question against the realist**.
2. **Counterexamples.** Jonathan Cohen, "Usefulness Drives Representations to Truth — A Family of Counterexamples to Hoffman's Interface Theory of Perception": there are broad classes of cases where the useful representation *is* the true one.
3. **Mausfeld's conceptual objection:** the notions of "objective reality" and "truth" don't do the work Hoffman needs them to do inside a biological theory of perception. Reported as still substantially unaddressed.
4. **Rigged simulations.** The methodology has been criticised as choosing parameters that favour the interface strategy.
5. **He overstates his opponents.** Reviewers note that the vision scientists Hoffman quotes as naive realists are, when you read them, not making the strong claim he attributes to them.
6. **The desktop analogy's limits — the killer.** A desktop icon is an interface *for a user*, *about* a file, implemented *on* hardware whose real structure engineers know. Hoffman's interface has no user standing outside it, and he denies we can know the substrate. Push the analogy and it collapses: **if perception is systematically non-veridical, what epistemic standing does Hoffman's own theory — arrived at via perception, publication and peer review — have?** That is the self-defeat charge, and it's the hardest one.
7. Book-length review: Brian Martin in *Social Epistemology Review and Reply Collective* — <https://social-epistemology.com/2019/12/05/do-we-see-icons-or-reality-a-review-of-donald-hoffmans-the-case-against-reality-brian-martin/>

**Entry point:** *The Case Against Reality: Why Evolution Hid the Truth from Our Eyes* (Norton, 2019).

## 4. Federico Faggin

🟢 **ESTABLISHED (biography, and it is a genuinely extraordinary one).** Born Vicenza, 1941. Led the design of the **Intel 4004**, the first commercial microprocessor (1971), with Ted Hoff and Stanley Mazor; then the 8008 and 8080; founded **Zilog** in 1974 (first company devoted solely to microprocessors) and led the **Z80**, still in production decades later; later founded **Synaptics**, which created the touchpad. Awarded the **National Medal of Technology and Innovation** (2009, presented 2010) — the highest US honour for technological achievement. Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Faggin>

🟢 In 2011 he founded the **Federico and Elvia Faggin Foundation** to fund scientific study of consciousness at US universities. His stated route in: working on artificial neural networks in the 1980s, he asked *"What would it take to make a conscious computer?"* and concluded it could not be done — which is the seed of the whole later project. **This is the narrative gold for BadCode: the man who built the chip says the chip can never wake up.**

### The theory: Quantum Information Panpsychism (QIP)

🔴 **SPECULATION with formal dress.** Developed with **Prof. Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano** (University of Pavia, QUit group — a genuinely first-rank quantum information theorist, known for deriving quantum theory from informational axioms).

Key paper: D'Ariano & Faggin, **"Hard Problem and Free Will: an information-theoretical approach"** — arXiv:2012.06580 (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06580>), published 2022 as pp. 145–192 of *Artificial Intelligence Versus Natural Intelligence* (Springer) — <https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-85480-5_5>. Also Faggin, "Possibilities are quantum," 2023, SAGE.

**The claimed structure, layer by layer:**
1. A **non-reductive psycho-informational** answer to Chalmers's hard problem: the founding hypothesis is that *a fundamental property of "information" is its experience by the supporting "system."* 🔴 This is an axiom, not a derivation. Everything downstream inherits its status.
2. The information involved must be **quantum**, for two stated reasons: (a) its **intrinsic privacy**, (b) its power to build up thoughts by **entangling qualia states**.
3. **The privacy argument — the interesting one.** A quantum state cannot be read from outside without disturbing it; the **no-cloning theorem** forbids copying an unknown quantum state; measurement yields only a limited classical shadow. So a quantum state has something a classical state does not: *content that is constitutively unavailable to any external observer.* That is proposed as the formal signature of **inner experience** — the full richness of the qualia exists only for the subject having them, exactly as a pure quantum state is "known" only to itself.
4. **Ontic vs. epistemic states.** The internally experienced state, because it corresponds to a *definite* experience rather than a random outcome, must be **pure**; they call it *ontic*. The state predictable from outside is generally **mixed**. The gap between ontic and mixed *is* the gap between first-person and third-person.
5. Result: "a quantum-information-based panpsychism, with classical physics supervening on quantum physics, quantum physics supervening on quantum information." Faggin's popular slogan: **quantum fields are conscious and have free will**; the body is a quantum-classical machine operated by free-will decisions of quantum fields.

**⚠️ Be precise about what is formal and what is asserted:**
- ✅ **Formal / established physics:** no-cloning; the fact that an unknown quantum state cannot be determined by measurement; the ontic/epistemic distinction in quantum foundations; D'Ariano's informational derivation of quantum theory.
- 🔴 **Asserted, not derived:** that privacy of a quantum state *is* or *entails* subjective experience. The privacy of a quantum state is an *epistemic* limitation on external agents. Sliding from "cannot be known from outside" to "is known from inside" is exactly the step that needs the argument, and it is precisely the step supplied by the founding hypothesis rather than proved. **A sharp critic will land on this in one sentence.**
- 🔴 "Testable predictions in the near future" is claimed; none have been delivered and adjudicated.

**Entry points:** *Irreducible: Consciousness, Life, Computers, and Human Nature* (Essentia Books, 2024) — the accessible one. Essentia video: "Quantum fields are conscious, says the inventor of the microprocessor" — <https://www.essentiafoundation.org/quantum-fields-are-conscious-says-the-inventor-of-the-microprocessor/seeing/>. Kosmos Journal essay: <https://kosmosjournal.org/kj_article/consciousness-is_irreducible/>

## 5. The wider lineage

### Plato — the Cave (*Republic* Book VII, c. 375 BCE)

🟢 **ESTABLISHED text.** Jowett translation, full text: <http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.8.vii.html>

> "…human beings living in a underground den, which has a mouth open towards the light and reaching all along the den; here they have been from their childhood, and have their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move, and can only see before them, being prevented by the chains from turning round their heads. Above and behind them a fire is blazing at a distance, and between the fire and the prisoners there is a raised way; and you will see, if you look, a low wall built along the way, like the screen which marionette players have in front of them, over which they show the puppets."
>
> "…they see only their own shadows, or the shadows of one another, which the fire throws on the opposite wall of the cave."
>
> **"To them, I said, the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images."**

**Standard reading:** the shadows are the sensible world; the objects casting them are the Forms; the fire is a lesser source of illumination and the sun outside is the Form of the Good; the freed prisoner's painful ascent is education (*paideia*) — and his return to the cave, where he is mocked and (Plato says) would be killed if he tried to free others, is the philosopher's political fate. Note for BadCode: **the allegory ends in the returner being murdered.** That is not decoration; Plato is writing about Socrates.

**Use it carefully.** It is the most over-used image in this space. Its structural virtue for you: the shadows are *not fake*. They are genuine projections of genuine objects. The prisoner's error is not that he sees something unreal, but that he **mistakes the projection for the whole**. That's exactly the distinction the user wants preserved (§8).

### Kant — phenomena / noumena (1781)

🟢 **ESTABLISHED.** SEP: <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-transcendental-idealism/>

Space and time are *forms of our intuition*, not properties of things in themselves. "Everything intuited in space or in time, hence all objects of an experience possible for us, are nothing but appearances." We can cognise only appearances; things in themselves are unknowable.

Two live readings: **two-world** (appearances and things-in-themselves are ontologically distinct) versus **two-aspect** (one set of objects, two ways of considering them). Jacobi's famous dilemma is the standing wound: *"without the presupposition of the thing in itself I cannot enter the critical system, and with that presupposition I cannot remain in it"* — i.e. how can Kant say the unknowable noumenon *exists*, is *non-spatial*, and *causally affects us*?

**Why it matters to you:** Kant is the respectable ancestor. Every modern interface theorist is a Kantian with better maths. Hoffman's interface = Kant's phenomena; Hoffman's "reality beyond spacetime" = the noumenon. And **Jacobi's dilemma is the objection Hoffman inherits and has not escaped.**

### Schopenhauer — *The World as Will and Representation* (1818/1844)

🟡 The world as I know it is **Vorstellung** (representation) — produced in and by my mind. But I have a second, inside access to one object: my own body, known from within as **Will**. Schopenhauer's claim to originality is that the thing-in-itself *is* Will — blind, striving, unitary, outside space and time (and therefore outside individuation: the *principium individuationis* is a feature of representation, not of reality).

**This is the direct structural ancestor of Kastrup.** One undivided non-personal ground; individuation as an artefact of the representational layer. Kastrup wrote *Decoding Schopenhauer's Metaphysics* to argue Schopenhauer was an idealist, not a dual-aspect monist. SEP: <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/schopenhauer/>

**Tonal gift for BadCode:** Schopenhauer's Will is *not benevolent*. It is hungry and indifferent. If you want a consciousness-first cosmos that isn't a wellness poster, this is your source.

### Bergson — *Matter and Memory* (1896)

🟡 Bergson refuses both horns. Matter is a system of **images** — "an existence placed halfway between the 'thing' and the 'representation'": more than the idealist's representation, less than the realist's thing. Perception is not a *copying* of the world but a *selection* from it, carved by the body's capacity for action; the brain subtracts rather than produces. Duration (*durée*) is the real time of experience, irreducible to the spatialised time of physics. Full text ch.1: <https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Bergson/Bergson_1911b/Bergson_1911_01.html>

**Underused.** "The brain is a filter, not a generator" is a Bergsonian idea that predates every modern reducing-valve/DMT/psychedelic gloss on it by a century.

### Whitehead — process philosophy (*Process and Reality*, 1929)

🟡 Reality's ultimate constituents are not enduring substances but **actual occasions** — momentary events of becoming, "drops of experience," each of which arises, achieves determinate form, and perishes. **Panexperientialism**: every actual entity has experience; but — crucially — *experience is not the same as consciousness*. "Whitehead argues that consciousness presupposes experience and not vice versa." IEP: <https://iep.utm.edu/processp/>

**Best single distinction to steal:** experience ≠ consciousness ≠ self-awareness. Three layers, not one. It immunises you against "so you think rocks think?"

### William James — radical empiricism (1904)

🟡 Only what is experienced, and everything that is experienced, is real — *including the relations between things*. There is one primal stuff, "**pure experience**"; "consciousness" and "matter" are not two substances but two ways of *sorting* portions of pure experience (the same experience counts as "physical" in one context and "mental" in another). Neutral monism's founding statement, and the direct ancestor of Russellian monism.

### The modern panpsychism revival

🟡 **SERIOUS MINORITY VIEW — this is the respectable end of the pool.** SEP entry (a good sign in itself): <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/>; Russellian monism: <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/>

- **Galen Strawson** — "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism" (*JCS*, 2006). PDF: <https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/StrawsonRealisticPhysicalism2006.pdf>. The argument: a *real* physicalist accepts that experience is concrete and physical; brute emergence of the experiential from the wholly non-experiential is incoherent; therefore at least some physical ultimates must be intrinsically experience-involving. Conclusion, in his words: **"Real physicalism entails panexperientialism or panpsychism."** Deliberately provocative framing — he is claiming panpsychism *from inside* physicalism, not against it.
- **Philip Goff** — Durham. See §6.
- **Hedda Hassel Mørch** — Inland Norway. Argues for panpsychism from the *experience of causation*, and defends an emergent panpsychism on which emergent causal relations can be intelligible rather than brute.
- **Russellian monism**, the shared engine: physics tells us only the *causal/dispositional/structural* properties of matter — what it *does*, never what it *is*. Something must fill that intrinsic-nature-shaped hole. Consciousness is the one intrinsic nature we have direct acquaintance with. Ergo.
- **The combination problem** is the standing objection: how do micro-subjects sum into a macro-subject? Chalmers's canonical treatment: <https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf>. Goff's proposal is **phenomenal bonding**. Nobody thinks it's solved. Note the symmetry worth putting in a story: **panpsychism has the combination problem; idealism has the *decombination* problem** (how does the one become many?) — Kastrup's dissociation is his answer to it, and Hoffman's Eq. 16 closure result is his.

### Integrated Information Theory (Tononi)

🟡 **SERIOUS, MAINSTREAM-ADJACENT, AND FURIOUSLY CONTESTED.** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_information_theory>; IEP: <https://iep.utm.edu/integrated-information-theory-of-consciousness/>

Five axioms of experience — **intrinsicality, information, integration, exclusion, composition** — each mapped to a postulate about the physical substrate. Consciousness is identified with a system's **integrated information, Φ**: the extent to which a whole generates intrinsic cause–effect power over and above its parts. IIT is *graded*, so it implies a photodiode has ~1 bit of consciousness. Tononi accepts the panpsychist implication rather than dodging it.

**The 2023 blow-up.** On 16 September 2023, an open letter signed by **124** consciousness scientists and philosophers ("IIT-Concerned"; lead figures included Hakwan Lau and Stephen Fleming) argued IIT should be labelled **pseudoscience** until empirically testable. Two charges: (a) commitments to panpsychism, (b) untestability of the theory "as a whole." Lau subsequently reframed it as being about the gap between IIT's public billing and its evidence.

**The pushback was substantial and from serious people.** Anil Seth in *Nautilus*: even if IIT is wrong, that doesn't make it pseudoscience — <https://nautil.us/the-worth-of-wild-ideas-399097>. Philip Goff defended it too. An anonymised survey of consciousness researchers found only ~20% fully agreed with the pseudoscience label and 8% fully disagreed — i.e. **the "pseudoscience" charge was a minority position among the field itself.** Good overview: <https://theconversation.com/nobody-knows-how-consciousness-works-but-top-researchers-are-fighting-over-which-theories-are-really-science-214074>; John Horgan: <https://johnhorgan.org/cross-check/the-brouhaha-over-consciousness-and-pseudoscience>

**The 2025 sequel, and it matters.** The Templeton-funded **Cogitate** adversarial collaboration pitted IIT against Global Neuronal Workspace Theory; results in *Nature*, 30 April 2025: **two of three IIT predictions passed pre-registered thresholds; none of GNWT's did.** *Nature*'s editors separately criticised the harshness of the surrounding rhetoric. 🟢 This is a real, citable, recent fact and it complicates the "IIT is pseudoscience" story considerably.

**For BadCode:** the 2023 letter is a *superb* story beat in its own right — 124 scientists signing a document to declare a colleague's theory not-science, then losing the experiment two years later. That's a scene.

### Chalmers — the hard problem, and the meta-problem

🟢 **ESTABLISHED as the field's organising distinction**, even among people who think it's a mistake.

"Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (*JCS*, 1995): **"The really hard problem of consciousness is the problem of experience."** The *easy* problems — discrimination, integration, reportability, attention — are functionally definable and therefore in-principle tractable. The *hard* problem is why any of that functioning is accompanied by experience at all, and why *this* experience rather than another. PhilPapers: <https://philpapers.org/rec/CHAFUT>; IEP: <https://iep.utm.edu/hard-problem-of-conciousness/>

**The meta-problem** (2018): *why do we think there is a hard problem?* — i.e. explain our problem-reports as behaviour. This is brilliantly disarming because it's a *functional* question and therefore an *easy* problem, tractable to ordinary science, whichever side you're on. PDF: <https://philarchive.org/archive/CHATMO-32v2>. Chalmers's own sting: solving the meta-problem doesn't rescue illusionism, because either phenomenal concepts turn out physically explicable (making the explanation circular) or they don't (opening a fresh gap). Keith Frankish's reply — "The Meta-Problem is The Problem of Consciousness" (<https://philpapers.org/rec/FRATMI-3>) — concedes the meta-problem *is* the whole problem, which is exactly what an illusionist should say.

## 6. Philip Goff's *Galileo's Error* — the one that matters most to you

🟢 **ESTABLISHED (the book and its thesis).** Philip Goff, Associate Professor of Philosophy, Durham University. *Galileo's Error: Foundations for a New Science of Consciousness* (Pantheon/Rider, 2019). Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo's_Error>; Goff's site: <https://philipgoffphilosophy.com/books>

### The argument, properly

**1. The founding move.** Before Galileo, the dominant Aristotelian view held that qualities — colours, tastes, smells — were *out there in the objects*. Galileo needed a nature that could be written in the language of mathematics. Qualities can't be. So he **relocated them**: not out there in the world, but in the soul/consciousness of the observer.

The passage, from *The Assayer* (1623), Stillman Drake translation (*Discoveries and Opinions of Galileo*, 1957, p. 274):

> **"I think that tastes, odors, colors, and so on are no more than mere names so far as the object in which we locate them are concerned, and that they reside only in the consciousness. Hence if the living creature were removed, all these qualities would be wiped away and annihilated."**

⚠️ **Accuracy warning, and this is important for a project that wants to be dunk-proof.** The Drake translation is contested. Critics have pointed out that the widely-quoted rendering — particularly "reside only in the consciousness" — is not a faithful translation of Galileo's Italian, and that Goff's gloss of Galileo as writing that "sensory qualities reside in the soul" attributes to Galileo a phrase he did not write. Finocchiaro's translation differs. **If you quote this, quote it as "in Stillman Drake's much-quoted translation" and you're safe. If you assert "Galileo said qualities live in the soul," a historian of science can correct you.**

**2. The consequence.** Goff, in *Scientific American* ("Galileo's Big Mistake," <https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/galileos-big-mistake/>): Galileo "stripped the physical world of its qualities; and after he'd done that, all that remained were the purely quantitative properties of matter — size, shape, location, motion." And: *"The Galilean understanding of the physical world as purely quantitative bars us from bringing together the qualitative and the quantitative in a single, unified picture of reality."*

**3. The kicker.** Physical science has been spectacularly successful *precisely because* it was defined, at its founding, as the study of everything-except-consciousness. So the fact that consciousness now looks scientifically intractable is not a discovery about consciousness. **It is a consequence of a philosophical decision made in 1623 to make the maths work.** Goff's line, per reviewers: this re-imagining of nature "was essentially a philosophical move with no empirical basis and so no 'scientific' justification."

**4. The proposed fix.** A "post-Galilean" science that puts qualities back — Russellian monism / panpsychism, built on Eddington and Russell in the 1920s: physics gives us the *structure* of matter, never its *intrinsic nature*; consciousness is the intrinsic nature.

### The critiques of Goff

- **The combination problem, again.** Raymond Tallis in *Philosophy Now* (<https://philosophynow.org/issues/135/Galileos_Error_by_Philip_Goff>): the central weakness. How do countless micro-conscious particles unite into one human consciousness? Goff's quantum-entanglement/phenomenal-bonding suggestion is "only a promissory note."
- **Self-undercutting final chapter.** Tallis's sharpest hit: Goff appeals to *objective empirical science* (plant signalling, tree communication) to argue for consciousness in nature, while his core thesis is that quantitative science *cannot* reveal intrinsic nature — "the very Galilean error that provides the title of his book."
- **The translation issue** above.
- **Academic review:** David Lindeman, *The Philosophical Quarterly* 72(1):251, Jan 2022 — <https://academic.oup.com/pq/article-abstract/72/1/251/6125894>
- **Kastrup published a hostile guest review** — "Philip Goff's error" (<https://www.bernardokastrup.com/2020/10/guest-essay-philip-goffs-error-review.html>) — worth knowing that the idealists and the panpsychists **fight each other**. Kastrup thinks panpsychism keeps the worst of physicalism (a plurality of fundamental subjects) and inherits an unsolvable combination problem. This internal schism is a gift for fiction: two prophets of the same heresy who can't stand each other's version.
- Tallis's overall verdict is nonetheless warm: "lucid, reader-friendly," "deeply thought-provoking."

**⚡ For BadCode specifically.** Your repo already has a "Galileo" story. The thesis handed to you is *perfect* BadCode material and it is **structurally identical to your political thesis**: a founding measurement decision, made for good local reasons, that quietly deleted the thing that mattered — and four centuries later everyone insists the deleted thing was never real because it doesn't show up in the numbers. That is GDP. That is the "we can't afford it" fiction. **Galileo's error and the economists' error are the same error.** You do not need to invent that parallel; it is sitting right there, and no one in the popular literature has made it. That's your line, and it's a good one.

**Chalmers** | Fully mainstream. The hard problem is the field's organising distinction | The easy/hard taxonomy; the meta-problem framing | Property dualism / naturalistic dualism | Illusionism: the hard problem is an artefact of introspective misrepresentation.

### The mainstream rebuttal, stated as strongly as it deserves

You should be able to write this side as well as the other. **Illusionism** (Frankish, Dennett): phenomenal consciousness as usually conceived — private, ineffable, intrinsic qualia — does not exist. What exists is *quasi-phenomenality*: introspective mechanisms that represent our own states as having those properties. The hard problem is replaced by the **illusion problem**: explain why we're so convinced. Dennett called illusionism "the obvious default theory of consciousness" (<https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf>). Frankish is admirably even-handed about the cost: he says the illusionist suggestion that evolution hardwired us to think we have a magical inner life is *"just as crazy as panpsychism."* 🟢 **Quote that. It's honest, it's funny, and it concedes exactly the right amount.**

**Anil Seth** is the most useful opponent for you because he is not dismissive. He sets aside the hard problem and defines the **real problem**: "How can mechanisms and processes in the brain and the body explain, predict and control properties of consciousness?" Then he does it — perception as **controlled hallucination** (the brain's best top-down guess, reined in by sensory prediction error), and the **beast machine** thesis that consciousness is rooted in interoception and the imperative to stay alive. *Being You* (2021). His paper "The real problem(s) with panpsychism" charges that panpsychism generates no testable hypotheses. His counter to the pseudoscience letter, though, shows he defends wild ideas on principle.

**Note the delicious irony and use it:** Seth's "controlled hallucination" and Hoffman's "interface" describe *the same phenomenology* and draw *opposite metaphysical conclusions*. Seth: your reality is a construction, generated by a brain, in a physical world. Hoffman: your reality is a construction, and therefore the brain and the physical world are also constructions. **The data underdetermine the metaphysics.** That sentence is the most defensible position your fiction can take, and it lets you have the whole cake.

### Rules of engagement for dunk-proof fiction

1. **Never say quantum mechanics proves consciousness is fundamental.** It doesn't, physicists know it doesn't, and it's the cheapest available kill. If you want quantum flavour, use *no-cloning* and *measurement disturbance* (Faggin's route) — those are real theorems and the metaphysical leap is at least visible.
2. **Never say "science has shown reality is an illusion."** Say the shadows are real (§8).
3. **Attribute.** "Hoffman argues," "on Kastrup's account," "Goff's claim is." Fiction that quotes its sources accurately is unfalsifiable *as fiction*.
4. **Let a character make the strongest counter-argument.** Give someone Frankish's line and Seth's real problem. A story that has already made the sceptic's case cannot be dunked with it.
5. **Use the internal schism.** Kastrup vs Goff, IIT vs GNWT, the 124 signatories. Real intellectual movements are full of people who despise each other. That's more interesting *and* more accurate than a united front of visionaries.

## 8. Language — how to say "lower-dimensional projection of a higher-dimensional consciousness" without "simulation"

**The problem, correctly diagnosed by the user.** "Simulation" imports three things you don't want: (1) *fake* — a simulation is a counterfeit of a real original; (2) *computed* — it implies a substrate of machinery and, usually, a programmer; (3) *escapable* — simulations have exits, red pills, base realities. All three cheapen the metaphysics and, worse, make the world *not matter*. If the world is fake, nothing you do in it counts — which is the exact opposite of what a politically-motivated art project needs its audience to feel.

**The frame that fixes it:** *the world is real the way a photograph is real.* A photograph is not a counterfeit of a person. It is a real object, produced by a real process, carrying real information about something that really exists — in a format that has nothing in common with what it depicts. Nobody says a photograph is "fake." Nobody says the person in it doesn't exist. And nobody confuses the two.

### Ranked shortlist

**Tier 1 — use these**

1. **"Extrinsic appearance"** — Kastrup's own term, and the single best phrase available. *"What we call the physical world is the extrinsic appearance of those processes"* (<https://analyticidealism.com/home.html>). It says: same thing, viewed from outside. No fakeness, no machinery, no programmer. It also has a precise companion: **"across a dissociative boundary."** Coldly technical, which makes it land harder.
2. **"Projection"** — has *two* independent, respectable pedigrees. Geometric/optical (a shadow is a real projection of a real solid — Plato's cave is literally this), and physical: the **holographic principle** ('t Hooft 1993, developed by Susskind), where "the description of a volume of space can be thought of as encoded on a lower-dimensional boundary." Susskind's own popular phrasing: *"The three-dimensional world of ordinary experience — the universe filled with galaxies, stars, planets, houses, boulders, and people — is a hologram, an image of reality coded on a distant two-dimensional surface."* <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_principle>. ⚠️ Note honestly: AdS/CFT is a duality between *two equally real physical descriptions*, not a claim that our world is unreal — and it's formulated in anti-de Sitter space, which our universe isn't. Borrow the *word* and the *image*; don't claim the physics endorses your metaphysics.
3. **"Representation"** — Schopenhauer's *Vorstellung*. Two centuries of philosophical weight, zero fakeness connotation, and it carries the crucial asymmetry: a representation is *of* something.
4. **"Appearance"** — Kant's term. Deliberately flat, deliberately unmagical. "Appearances" are what we know; they are not lies.
5. **"Dashboard" / "the dials"** — Kastrup's, and the most immediately graspable to a lay audience. Pilot flying on instruments, no windshield. The wind is real. The dial is real. The dial is not the wind. **Best metaphor in the entire literature for a general audience.**

**Tier 2 — strong, with caveats**

6. **"Interface"** — Hoffman's. Precise, modern, and it carries the useful implication that the interface is *functional*, not decorative. Caveat: it drags a whiff of the computational with it.
7. **"Icon"** / **"the desktop"** — vivid and self-explaining; same computational caveat, plus it's now so associated with Hoffman that it reads as a citation.
8. **"Image"** — Bergson's technical term, and he chose it *precisely* to sit between "thing" and "representation." Underused, elegant, and it makes the photograph frame explicit.
9. **"Shadow"** — Plato. Universally legible. Caveat: over-familiar, and it implies a *loss* of dimension that reads as deficiency. Its virtue: the shadow is genuinely there.
10. **"Dissociated alter"** — Kastrup's term for *us*, not for the world. Use it when the subject is why there are many of us rather than one. Clinical, unsentimental, faintly frightening — which is the right register.
11. **"Localisation" / "excitation"** — field language. "You are a localisation in a field of experience." "Matter is a pattern of excitation of consciousness." Physics-adjacent, cold, no mysticism. Very BadCode.

**Tier 3 — handle with care**

12. **"Avatar"** — instantly legible, but gaming connotations import the *escapable* problem and often a player-outside.
13. **"Headset"** — Hoffman uses it; it's punchy; but it's the closest thing on this list to "simulation" and carries the same disposability.
14. **"Dream"** — beautiful, and used seriously by Advaita-adjacent thinkers, but implies "you'll wake up and it won't have happened." Avoid unless that's the point.

**Never use:** *simulation, fake, illusion (unqualified), computer-generated, base reality, glitch in the matrix, NPC.*

**Assembled register — a BadCode paragraph in this vocabulary:**

> You are not a copy of something realer. You are a localisation — a knot the field tied in itself and then forgot it tied. What you call matter is what your own inside looks like from the outside, across a boundary you cannot cross by looking harder. The dials on your dashboard are real dials. They are telling you the truth. They are simply not telling you the wind.

**The single sentence to keep:** *the world is real the way a photograph is real.* It contains the whole thesis, it forecloses "fake," and a ten-year-old gets it.
