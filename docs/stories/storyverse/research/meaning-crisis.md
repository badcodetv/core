## PART 0 — THE BRUTAL SCORECARD

The central claim, restated in the user's terms, broken into six load-bearing propositions.

### Proposition 1: "We have never had a healthy symbiosis of the materialist and the spiritual."
**Verdict: FALSE AS STATED. Restate it — the true version is better.**

The seventeenth century *was* the symbiosis. Newton wrote more words on theology and alchemy than on physics [M]. The Royal Society was thick with clergy. Islamic natural philosophy, Song-dynasty engineering, and Scholastic logic were all simultaneously devout and empirical [M]. There is no historical moment you can point to and say "here, the spiritual was absent." Josephson-Storm's entire book is a demolition of the idea that modernity ever became un-magical: "the myth of mythless modernity formed at the very time that Britain, France, and Germany were in the midst of occult and spiritualist revivals" [S]. Weber himself was fascinated by mysticism and moved in occult-adjacent circles [S].

**The restatement that is both true and more powerful:** it wasn't a pendulum. It was a *coup*. The split between the measurable and the meaningful was not a natural phase of civilisational development — it was won, deliberately, by one faction of natural philosophers in about 1690, for money. See Bilgrami, Part 1. That is a far better story than "we swung too far," because it has a perpetrator, a date, and a motive.

### Proposition 2: "A heavy religious-dogma phase, then a heavy scientific-enlightenment phase, never both at once."
**Verdict: FALSE AS HISTORY. Same restatement applies.**

The pendulum model is folk history. What actually happened, on the best scholarly account available (Bilgrami [V]), is that within the new science there were *two* metaphysics competing — an "inert matter needing an external clock-winder" picture (Newton, Boyle, the Royal Society ideologues) and a "matter shot through with an inner divine dynamism" picture (the freethinkers and dissenters, e.g. John Toland). Both were *scientific*. Both accepted the new experimental method. One of them won, and it won because it was **sold** — to the Anglican establishment and to mercantile interests, on the grounds that inert matter is matter you may take.

### Proposition 3: "That imbalance is a large part of why geopolitics, economics, and happiness indices are bad."
**Verdict: CONTESTED. The causal arrow is not established, and you must not assert it as fact.**

You can defend a *mechanism* (Bilgrami's: desacralised nature → impersonal stance → extraction is the only available relation to the world) and you can defend the *correlates* (meaninglessness and material precarity co-occur). What you cannot defend is "the meaning deficit caused the economics." Nobody has shown that. Global happiness has *not* collapsed; the decline is specific and local (see Part 5). Say "the same move produced both" rather than "one caused the other."

### Proposition 4: "The material world is stacked against them — which it is, because of late-stage capitalism."
**Verdict: SPLIT. Half rock-solid, half false, and the false half will be used to discredit you.**

- **Solid:** distribution and ownership. The global top 10% owns 76% of household wealth; the bottom 50% owns 2% [S]. US net productivity grew 90.2% from 1979 while typical worker pay grew 33.0% [S]. Piketty's r > g is the standard reference for why accumulated capital pulls away from wages [S].
- **False if implied:** that absolute material conditions are worsening. They are not. In 1820 roughly three-quarters of humanity lived in extreme poverty; today it is close to one in ten [V, Our World in Data]. That is the single strongest weapon your opponents have and you must take it off the table by conceding it first.
- **The restatement:** *the pie got enormous and the slicing got worse.* People are not poorer than their grandparents; they own less of what they make and have less say over it. "Stacked against them" is true about **ownership and agency**, not about **calories and antibiotics**.

### Proposition 5: "Science is screaming 'it's not as simple as you thought.'"
**Verdict: WELL SUPPORTED. This is your strongest factual leg. Use it hard.** See the timeline in Part 3.

### Proposition 6: "The culture still runs on Victorian peak-Newtonian confidence."
**Verdict: HALF TRUE — and the half that's false is a famous myth you were about to repeat.**

Lord Kelvin never said "there is nothing new to be discovered in physics now; all that remains is more and more precise measurement." The line is not in the 1900 lecture nor anywhere in his collected speeches [S]. Its likely origin is a remark by **Albert Michelson** at the University of Chicago in **1894** about an unnamed "eminent physicist," which **Robert Millikan**, in the audience, later *guessed* referred to Kelvin [S]. Kelvin's actual 1900 Royal Institution lecture, *"Nineteenth Century Clouds over the Dynamical Theory of Heat and Light"* (27 April 1900), is the *opposite* of complacent: he names two unresolved problems — the Michelson–Morley null result and the equipartition/black-body failure — which became relativity and quantum mechanics [S]. **He picked the right two clouds.**

Historiographical caveat you should keep: while the Kelvin quote is bogus, "the underlying sentiment was a common one in those days, with plenty of evidence for a widespread, though not universal, sense of scientific complacency in the late nineteenth century" [S]. So the *mood* is real; the *quote* is fake.

**The restatement, which is much sharper than the myth:** the confidence isn't a hangover from Victorian physicists — the physicists gave it up first and gave it up voluntarily. It survives *downstream*, in management, economics, policy and metrics, which imported the clockwork metaphor exactly as the people who invented it were abandoning it. **Physics gave up determinism. Middle management never got the memo.**

## PART 1 — DISENCHANTMENT

### Weber: what he actually said

The term is *Entzauberung der Welt* — literally "de-magic-ing," demagification, not "de-beautifying." From the 1917 lecture *Wissenschaft als Beruf* ("Science as a Vocation") [S]:

> "there are no mysterious incalculable forces that come into play but rather that one can, in principle, master all things by calculation. This means that the world is disenchanted." [S]

Three things the popular version gets wrong, and which you should get right:

1. **It is about calculability, not belief.** Weber's claim is about the *increasing intellectualisation and rationalisation* of the world — the assumption that anything could in principle be brought under calculation. It is emphatically not a claim that people stopped believing in God.
2. **It is about availability, not possession.** Weber's own illustration: the modern person riding a streetcar has no better idea how it works than a "savage" has of his tools — the difference is the *knowledge that one could find out if one wanted to*. Disenchantment is a change in what you assume is in-principle knowable, not in what you know [M — the streetcar passage is standard Weber scholarship but I could not re-fetch the verbatim text this session].
3. **Weber wrote it in the subjunctive.** In the German, he uses subjunctive mood to report a *belief held by moderns*, not to assert that no incalculable forces exist [S]. Weber is describing an ideology, not endorsing a metaphysics. Anyone who quotes him as a triumphalist materialist has misread him.

Weber's own conclusion is bleak, not celebratory: science can tell you how, and cannot tell you whether it is worth doing — he invokes Tolstoy on the point that science is meaningless *because it gives no answer to the only question that matters to us: what shall we do, how shall we live* [M — the Tolstoy passage is in the lecture; exact wording unverified this session].

### Charles Taylor: the most rigorous version of your claim

*A Secular Age* (2007) opens on the question that is, in effect, your whole thesis in one sentence:

> "Why was it virtually impossible not to believe in God in, say, 1500 in our Western society, while in 2000 many of us find this not only easy, but even inescapable?" [S]

Taylor's answer is not "science disproved God." It is that the **conditions of belief** changed — the background against which any belief or unbelief now happens. Two of his terms are directly usable:

- **The porous self** (pre-modern): the boundary between self and world is thin. Meaning can come at you from outside. "The porous self is vulnerable: to spirits, demons, cosmic forces." [V, via SSRC *The Immanent Frame*]
- **The buffered self** (modern): "As a bounded self I can see the boundary as a buffer, such that the things beyond don't need to 'get to me.'" [V] Meaning is now something generated *inside* the mind. Which means it is also something you can be individually blamed for failing to generate.
- **The immanent frame**: the shared background assumption that there is a natural order which is self-sufficient and needs no explanation from outside it.

**Why this is the strongest form of your argument:** Taylor is not saying we lost something true. He is saying we gained a *fortification*, and the price of the fortification is that nothing can reach us. The buffered self is invulnerable and lonely. That's a comic panel.

### Bilgrami: the strongest single source you have — disenchantment as an economic operation

Akeel Bilgrami, *"The Wider Significance of Naturalism: A Genealogical Essay"* (in *Naturalism and Normativity*, Columbia UP, 2010). PDF: https://philosophy.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/The_Wider_Significance_of_Naturalism_0.pdf

I extracted this text directly. These are **verbatim** [V] and they are the intellectual spine of everything BadCode wants to say:

> "The metaphysical picture that was promoted by the exile of 'the father' to a place outside the universe was that the world itself was, therefore, 'brute' and 'inert' and needed an external divine source for its motion. In the dissenting picture, by contrast, matter was not brute and inert, but rather was shot through with an inner source of dynamism responsible for motion, that was itself divine."

> "Some of the dissenters argued that it is only because one takes matter to be 'brute' and 'stupid' — to use Newton's own term — that one would find it appropriate to conquer it with nothing but profit and material wealth as ends, and thereby destroy it both as a natural and a human environment for one's habitation."

> "A desacralized world, to put it in the dissenting terms of that period, left us no other angle from which to view it, but an impersonal one. There could be no normative constraint coming upon us from a world that was brute. It could not move us to engagement with it on its terms. All the term-making came from us."

> "Newton's and Boyle's metaphysical view of the new science won out over the freethinkers' and became official only because it was sold to the Anglican establishment and, in an alliance with that establishment, to the powerful mercantile and incipient industrial interests of the period in thoroughly predatory terms. Terms which stressed that how we conceive nature may now be transformed into something, into the kind of thing, that is indefinitely available for our economic gain by processes of extraction, processes such as mining, deforestation, plantation agriculture…"

> "From an anima mundi, one could not simply proceed to take at whim and will."

And the colonial extension, which is the same move applied to people:

> "…these mercantile, political, scientific, and religious alliances produced over time the mentality that justified the colonial conquest of distant lands. The justification was merely an extension of the ideas that I have outlined to colonized lands, which too were to be viewed as brute nature that was available for conquest and control." [V]

Bilgrami also quotes Kenneth Burke's *A Grammar of Motives* approvingly: **"the experience of an impersonal outlook was empirically intensified in proportion as the rationale of the monetary motive gained greater authority."** [V]

Note Bilgrami's careful distinction, which protects you from sounding anti-science: his target is not *naturalism* (the methods of science) but **scientism** — the "thickened" ideology that smuggled an entire attitude to nature and commerce in under the word "rationality" [V].

## PART 2 — THE MEANING CRISIS

### John Vervaeke

*Awakening from the Meaning Crisis* (50-episode lecture series, 2019). https://johnvervaeke.com/series/awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis/

His framing, from his own site [V]: people are "disconnected from themselves, from each other, from the world, and from a viable and foreseeable future," and there is "a sense of drowning in an ocean of bullshit." He treats the mental-health crisis as the *symptom* of a "deeper cultural historical crisis."

The machinery [S]:

- **Four kinds of knowing.** Propositional (knowing *that*), procedural (knowing *how*), perspectival (knowing what it is like to be here, now), participatory (knowing through *being* — the co-identification of self and environment). Modernity became extraordinarily good at the first, tolerable at the second, and abandoned the third and fourth as unserious.
- **Relevance realisation.** The core cognitive problem: reality offers infinitely more information than you can process, so the fundamental act of mind is deciding what *matters*. This is not a propositional operation and cannot be reduced to one.
- **The lost ecology of practices.** Pre-modern cultures carried an integrated set of practices (ritual, contemplative, communal) that trained the non-propositional kinds of knowing. We deleted the practices and kept only the propositions, then wondered why nobody feels anything.

**Usefulness to BadCode: very high.** Vervaeke's diagnosis is precisely "the culture only counts one of the four kinds of knowing, and it's the one a spreadsheet can hold." That is your "you can't measure the vibe" point stated in cognitive-science vocabulary rather than mystical vocabulary — which is exactly the register shift you need.

**Caveat:** Vervaeke's synthesis is a lecture series, not a peer-reviewed literature. Relevance realisation has published cognitive-science backing; the grand historical narrative does not. Cite him as a *diagnostician*, not as evidence.

### Iain McGilchrist — HANDLE WITH TONGS

*The Master and His Emissary* (2009), *The Matter with Things* (2021). The thesis: the two hemispheres embody two *modes of attention* — the right a broad, contextual, living apprehension; the left a narrow, grasping, manipulative one — and Western culture has let the emissary (left) usurp the master (right).

**This is the most seductive and most dangerous item in the entire dossier.** The narrative is perfect for you and the neuroscience does not support it. Documented criticism [S]:

- The hypothesis "lacks supportive neuroscientific evidence using the latest methods" and is "critically unclear because it does not specify operational definitions and neural measures that would permit evidence to count against it" — i.e. it is close to unfalsifiable as stated (*Religion, Brain & Behavior* symposium, 2019, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2153599X.2019.1604420).
- Chris McManus (himself a laterality researcher) notes McGilchrist leans on clinical brain-damage cases and split-brain studies that do not generalise to normal cognition [S].
- The blunt version from critics: "not a work of science; it is a speculative fable disguised as intellectual history" [S].

**Recommendation:** do not put a hemisphere claim in a comic as fact. If you want the image, use it *as an image* and attribute it — "one writer's metaphor" — or, better, replace it with Vervaeke's four-knowings, which does the same narrative work and is not contested neuroscience. The one thing worse than being sneered at for talking about the vibe is being *correctly* sneered at for citing debunked brain science while doing it.

### Hartmut Rosa — the best under-used source here

*Social Acceleration* (2005/2013), *Resonance: A Sociology of Our Relationship to the World* (2016/2019). His triad [S]:

- **Acceleration** is the defining structure of modernity — and, crucially, it is a structure with *no normative content*: nobody chose it, nobody defends it, it just is the condition of staying in place.
- **Alienation** is the resulting condition: a "frenetic standstill" — everything moves faster and nothing changes.
- **Resonance** is his positive term: "a type of cognitive, affective, and bodily relation with the world in which, as opposed to alienation, the subject feels affected by the world."

**Why this matters for you:** Rosa gives a *secular, sociological* word for the thing religion used to supply, and it maps exactly onto Taylor's porous self. Resonance is what a porous self has and a buffered self cannot get. And "frenetic standstill" is a phrase built for a drum & bass track.

### The adjacent shelf

- **Jonathan Haidt** — *The Righteous Mind* (2012): moral intuition comes first, reasoning is the press secretary; the sanctity/degradation foundation is real and secular liberals score lowest on it, which is why "nothing is sacred" reads as freedom to one half of a population and as desecration to the other [M]. *The Anxious Generation* (2024): see Part 5, and note the criticism — it is contested.
- **Alain de Botton**, *Religion for Atheists* (2012): the "steal the technology, drop the metaphysics" position — calendars, rituals, communal meals, art as consolation [M]. Useful as a *character position* in a story: the person who wants the cathedral without the God.

## PART 3 — THE CERTAINTY BROKE: AN ACCURATE TIMELINE

Use this. It is your best-evidenced sequence and it is dramatic on its own terms.

**1814 — Laplace states the dream.** *Essai philosophique sur les probabilités*, standard translation [S]:

> "We may regard the present state of the universe as the effect of its past and the cause of its future. An intellect which at a certain moment would know all forces that set nature in motion, and all positions of all items of which nature is composed… for such an intellect nothing would be uncertain and the future just like the past would be present before its eyes."

Note: **Laplace never used the word "demon."** That was a later embellishment [S].

**1824–1865 — Thermodynamics quietly ruins it.** Carnot, then Clausius coining "entropy" in 1865: the universe has an arrow, and it points at heat death. The first fundamental law that is *statistical and irreversible* — a clockwork that cannot be run backwards [M].

**1894 — the complacency quote is born, misattributed.** Michelson at Chicago; Millikan guesses it was Kelvin; the myth is off and running [S].

**27 April 1900 — Kelvin names the two clouds.** *"Nineteenth Century Clouds over the Dynamical Theory of Heat and Light,"* Royal Institution. Cloud one: the Michelson–Morley null result. Cloud two: equipartition and black-body radiation. Both clouds become storms [S].

**1900 — Planck's quantum.** The black-body cloud bursts first: energy comes in lumps [M].

**1905 / 1915 — Einstein.** Special then general relativity. Simultaneity is not absolute; space and time are participants, not a stage [M].

**1927 — Heisenberg's uncertainty principle; the Copenhagen interpretation.** Laplace's demon is now *provably* impossible, not merely impractical: you cannot in principle have the positions and the momenta [M].

**1931 — Gödel's incompleteness theorems.** Any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove; and it cannot prove its own consistency. Mathematics — the one domain that was supposed to be *closed* — is shown to be open [M].

**1936 — Turing's halting problem.** There is no general algorithm that can decide whether an arbitrary program halts. Computation, the successor to Laplace's calculating intellect, has a formal horizon it cannot see past [M].

**1963/1972 — Lorenz and chaos.** "Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow" (*Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences*, 1963), then the 1972 AAAS talk **"Predictability: Does the Flap of a Butterfly's Wings in Brazil Set Off a Tornado in Texas?"** — a title coined not by Lorenz but by the meteorologist **Philip Merilees** [S]. This is the killer, philosophically: chaos shows that **determinism and predictability are different things.** A fully deterministic universe can still be unforecastable. Lorenz's 1963 paper was cited exactly three times outside meteorology in the following decade [S] — the truth sat there unread.

**1964 → 2022 — Bell's theorem and its experimental confirmation.** Bell shows local hidden-variable theories make different predictions from quantum mechanics; Clauser, Aspect and Zeilinger test it and win the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics. Local realism — the assumption that things have definite properties independent of measurement, and that nothing influences anything faster than light — is experimentally dead [M].

**1972 — Anderson, "More Is Different"** (*Science*, vol. 177). The demolition of *constructionism* even if reductionism is true [S]:

> "The ability to reduce everything to simple fundamental laws does not imply the ability to start from those laws and reconstruct the universe."

> "The reductionist hypothesis does not by any means imply a 'constructionist' one."

At each level of complexity, entirely new properties appear that require their own fundamental research. Chemistry is not applied physics; biology is not applied chemistry; a society is not applied biology.

**1995 — Chalmers names the hard problem.** Why is there something it is like to be a physical process at all? [M] See Part 8.

**2015 — the replication crisis goes public.** Open Science Collaboration, "Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science," *Science*: 100 studies from three leading psychology journals re-run with original materials and high-powered designs. **97% of the original studies reported statistically significant results; only 36% of the replications did. Effect sizes were on average half the originals** [S]. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aac4716

The shape of the timeline, which is the story: **for two hundred years, every time science looked closely at its own foundations, it found a limit — and each limit was discovered by the most rigorous people in the field, not by mystics.** That is your whole "science is screaming it's not as simple as you thought" thesis, and it is airtight.

**One honesty requirement.** Do not say these results show "science can't know anything" or "therefore consciousness/spirituality." They show that *a specific 18th-century picture* — a closed, fully predictable, fully specifiable clockwork — is false. Quantum mechanics is the most precisely confirmed theory in history. The limits are *inside* an enormously successful enterprise. Overreach here and every physicist in your audience switches off in one panel.

## PART 4 — THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT, GIVEN A FAIR HEARING

If your story cannot survive this section, it is propaganda.

### The material case: things got better, enormously

- **Extreme poverty.** In 1820 roughly three-quarters of the world lived in extreme poverty; today it is close to one in ten (World Bank line, $3/day in 2021 international dollars) [V, Our World in Data, https://ourworldindata.org/extreme-poverty-in-brief]. "It took 136 years from 1820 for our global poverty rate to fall under 50%, then another 45 years to cut this rate in half again by 2001." [V]
- **Child mortality.** In the pre-modern world, roughly every second child died before adulthood, essentially regardless of where they were born [V, OWID].
- **Rosling's *Factfulness* (2018)** makes the general case: people systematically, and in a *biased direction*, believe the world is worse than it is. He is right about that, and it is the most important thing to concede [M].
- **Pinker's *Enlightenment Now* (2018)**: reason, science and humanism produced measurable gains across dozens of indicators, and the pessimism industry misreads its own data [M].

### The Pinker critiques, so you can hold both

- **John Gray**, *New Statesman* (2018), review titled "The limits of reason: Steven Pinker's embarrassing new book is a feeble sermon for rattled rationalists." Gray argues Pinker's faith in progress "testifies to our enduring need for faith," and calls him "an evangelist for science — or, to be more exact, an ideology of scientism" [S]. https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/2018/02/enlightenment-now-the-case-for-reason-science-humanism-and-progress-review-steven-pinker
- **Baseline-shopping**: critics note that some of Pinker's declining-violence series start in 1945, which excludes the two world wars from the comparison [S].
- The structural point: aggregate improvement is compatible with a specific population being worse off. Both can be true. See Part 5.

### The secularisation-thesis critique: religion never went away

- **Peter Berger** — the single most damaging witness against the disenchantment story, because he *invented the modern version of it* (*The Sacred Canopy*, 1967) and then publicly withdrew it in *The Desecularization of the World* (1999): "The assumption that we live in a secularised world is false: the world today, with some exceptions… is as furiously religious as it ever was, and in some places more so than ever." [S] He said his earlier analyses had "little empirical substance" [S].
- **Norris & Inglehart**, *Sacred and Secular* (2004): the sophisticated middle position — secularisation is real but is driven by *existential security*, and because secular societies have low fertility and religious societies high fertility, the world as a whole is getting more religious even as rich countries get less so [M].
- **Pew Religious Landscape Study (2023–24)**: 64% of US adults identify as Christian, 30% as religiously unaffiliated (5% atheist, 6% agnostic, 19% "nothing in particular") [V, https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/]. Note that "nothing in particular" is the largest unaffiliated group by a wide margin — these are not materialists, they are unchurched.

### Josephson-Storm: disenchantment never happened

*The Myth of Disenchantment: Magic, Modernity, and the Birth of the Human Sciences* (Chicago, 2017). The argument [S/V]: the disenchantment narrative is itself a myth, invented by scholars who were themselves "profoundly enmeshed in the occult milieu," at exactly the moment Britain, France and Germany were in the middle of spiritualist revivals. "Attempts to suppress magic have failed more often than they have succeeded." [V, publisher's description]

**How to survive this.** Josephson-Storm does not refute your claim, he *relocates* it. What died was not enchantment — it was enchantment's *institutional legitimacy*. The magic went private, unofficial, embarrassing, and commercially available. Nobody stopped believing; they stopped being allowed to say it at work. That is a strictly better BadCode story than "we lost the magic," because it is funnier and it is true.

## PART 5 — WHAT THE WELLBEING DATA ACTUALLY SHOWS

Be scrupulous here. This is where a sloppy claim gets you dismissed.

### Income and happiness

- **Kahneman & Deaton (2010):** emotional wellbeing rose with income up to roughly $75,000/yr and then plateaued [S].
- **Killingsworth (2021):** using real-time experience sampling rather than retrospective report, found no plateau — happiness kept rising with log income [S].
- **Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers (2023), "Income and emotional well-being: A conflict resolved," *PNAS*** — an **adversarial collaboration** proposed by Kahneman, with Mellers as neutral arbiter [S]. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2208661120
  **The resolution: both were right about different people.** For most people, happiness keeps rising with income well past $100,000, and for the happiest group it *accelerates*. But there is **an unhappy minority whose happiness rises with income only up to about $100,000 and then flattens** — money fixes the problems money can fix, and then stops.

**This is genuinely useful to you, and not in the direction you might expect.** The honest reading is: money keeps buying happiness for most people. What money cannot touch is a specific unhappiness — the residue after material problems are solved. That residue is your subject. Do not claim money doesn't buy happiness; claim that *for the miserable, it stops working at exactly the point where the interesting question starts.*

Also worth flagging: the story is often over-dramatised in circulation. One critic notes that popular retellings of this paper "heavily exaggerate the actual science" and even invent quotes [S]. Read the paper before you lyric it.

### The Easterlin paradox

Still genuinely unresolved [S]. Easterlin: within a country, richer people are happier, but as a country gets richer over time, average happiness doesn't rise. Stevenson & Wolfers (2008, NBER w14282) found a clear positive link between GDP per capita and subjective wellbeing both across and within countries, with no satiation point. Easterlin has replied. The disagreement is partly about **measurement scaling** — whether people rescale what "7 out of 10" means as their society gets richer [S]. **Say "contested," never "proven."**

### World Happiness Report 2025

Theme: "Caring and Sharing." Finland 1st for the eighth consecutive year (7.736); UK 23rd (6.728); **United States 24th (6.724)** — its lowest-ever position, having been 11th in 2012 [V for the ranks/scores via Wikipedia; the 2012 comparison is M]. The US has seen a decline exceeding 0.5 points on the 0–10 scale versus earlier periods [S]. Report themes for 2025 include declining social connection among young adults and the correlation between prosocial behaviour and reduced deaths of despair [S]. https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/

*(The WHR site returned 403 to automated fetch; the specific "dining alone" and wallet-return figures widely quoted from the 2025 edition could not be verified this session. **Do not quote them without checking the PDF.**)*

### Deaths of despair — Case & Deaton

- **1999–2013:** rising all-cause mortality among middle-aged white non-Hispanic Americans, driven by drug and alcohol poisonings, suicide, and chronic liver disease/cirrhosis. Counterfactual: had the 45–54 white mortality rate held at its 1998 value, **96,000 deaths would have been avoided over 1999–2013, 7,000 in 2013 alone** [S]. (*PNAS*, 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26575631/)
- **The scale now:** combined US deaths from alcohol, drugs and suicide went from **74,003 in 2002 to 207,827 in 2022** — a 142% increase in the combined rate. 2021 peaked at 209,225. 2022 breakdown: 107,941 drug overdose, 51,191 alcohol-induced, 49,476 suicide [S, Trust for America's Health, https://www.tfah.org/report-details/pain-in-the-nation-2025/].
- **Honesty requirement:** the trend has **turned**. 2023 overdose deaths fell to 105,007, 4% below 2022, and 2024–25 data show continued decline [S]. Deaths of despair peaked in 2021. If you write "and it's still getting worse," you are wrong as of 2026.
- **Also contested:** a 2024 *PNAS* paper argues the deaths-of-despair narrative obscures substantial racial/ethnic heterogeneity in psychological-distress-related death trends [S, https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2307656121].

### Youth mental health — real, but the *cause* is a live fight

The rise in adolescent depression/anxiety indicators in the US and UK since roughly 2012 is well documented [M]. What is *not* settled is why. Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* (2024) blames smartphones and the "great rewiring."

**Candice Odgers**, *Nature*, 29 March 2024: "the book's repeated suggestion that digital technologies are rewiring our children's brains and causing an epidemic of mental illness is not supported by science," and "the bold proposal that social media is to blame might distract us from effectively responding to the real causes of the current mental-health crisis in young people" [S]. https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-024-00902-2

Haidt himself has acknowledged to a *Science* editor that he is "ahead of the science" [S]. **Recommendation:** you can cite the *trend*. Do not cite the *cause*. And note that "phones did it" is, conveniently, a story that requires no economic change whatsoever — which should make a political art collective suspicious of it on principle.

### Social capital

Putnam's *Bowling Alone* (2000) documented long-run declines in US associational membership, informal socialising and social trust [M]. The 2023 US Surgeon General advisory on loneliness put the mortality risk of social disconnection in the range of smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day, with roughly 29% increased risk of premature death [M — **verify these figures before publication**; the HHS PDF returned 403 this session].

## PART 6 — LATE CAPITALISM, INEQUALITY, MEANING

### Where the phrase came from

- **Werner Sombart** coined *Spätkapitalismus* in *Der moderne Kapitalismus* (1902–1927), as the fourth stage after proto-, early and high capitalism — referring specifically to post-WWI conditions. He never worked it into a systematic theory [S].
- **Ernest Mandel**, *Der Spätkapitalismus* (1972; English *Late Capitalism*, 1975), revived it for the postwar order of multinationals, financialisation and global integration [S].
- **Then the meme.** #latecapitalism / #tardocapitalismo / #spätkapitalismus — used to satirise endless-growth logic and the absurdities of a post-2008 economy presented as normal [S].

**Useful irony for BadCode:** the phrase has been announcing capitalism's imminent lateness for **over a century**. Sombart called it "late" in the 1920s. Using it straight is a hostage to fortune; using it knowingly ("we've been calling it late for a hundred years and it keeps not dying") is a joke that lands.

### The distribution numbers

- **World Inequality Report 2022** (Chancel, Piketty, Saez, Zucman): the global top 10% owns **76% of total household wealth** and captures **52% of total income**; the global bottom 50% owns **2% of wealth** and captures **8.5% of income** (PPP) [S]. https://wir2022.wid.world/executive-summary/
- **Piketty, *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* (2013/2014)**: r > g — the return on capital persistently exceeds the growth rate of the economy, so accumulated wealth outpaces wages and output. His phrase: **"the past devours the future"** (p. 571) [S]. Consequence: "entrepreneurs turn into rentiers within a single generation"; merit becomes less determinative than birth [S].
- **Productivity–pay decoupling (US)**: EPI reports net productivity grew **90.2%** from 1979 to 2025 while typical worker pay grew **33.0%** — and that had pay tracked productivity, the typical worker would earn about **$16.40 more per hour** today [S]. https://www.epi.org/productivity-pay-gap/
  **Caveat, be honest:** EPI itself has stated the multiple in different ways in different pieces ("3.5 times as much," "eight times faster") depending on which pay and price series are used, and the series is contested by economists who argue that using different deflators for output and consumption exaggerates the gap [S]. The *direction* is not seriously disputed; the *multiple* is. Quote the two raw percentages (90.2 / 33.0), not a multiplier.

### Meaning at work

- **Graeber, *Bullshit Jobs*** (essay 2013, book 2018). The anchoring statistic: a **2015 YouGov poll found 37% of British working adults said their job was not "making a meaningful contribution to the world"** [S].
- **The strongest empirical rebuttal, and you should engage it:** Soffia, Wood & Burchell, "Alienation Is Not 'Bullshit': An Empirical Critique of Graeber's Theory of BS Jobs," *Work, Employment and Society* 36(5), 2022, 816–840. Using the 2005–2015 European Working Conditions Surveys and the item "I have the feeling of doing useful work," they find the share of employees describing their work as useless is **low and declining**, and its distribution across occupations bears little relation to Graeber's predictions. Their alternative explanation is **poor management and toxic workplaces** — Marxist alienation, not uselessness [S]. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09500170211015067
  **Note the YouGov item is loaded**: "making a meaningful contribution to the world" is a very high bar; a perfectly useful job can fail it.
  **The steelman restatement for you:** the problem is not that the jobs are pointless. It's that the *worker cannot see the point* — which is precisely alienation, and precisely Bilgrami's impersonal stance applied to a human being.

### Mark Fisher — closest to BadCode's register, and the source of a warning

*Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?* (Zero Books, 2009). I extracted the text directly; the following are **verbatim** [V]:

> "That slogan captures precisely what I mean by 'capitalist realism': the widespread sense that not only is capitalism the only viable political and economic system, but also that it is now impossible even to imagine a coherent alternative to it."

Fisher explicitly attributes the "end of the world / end of capitalism" line to **Fredric Jameson and Slavoj Žižek**, not to himself [V]. Jameson's original (*The Seeds of Time*, 1994): "It seems to be easier for us today to imagine the thoroughgoing deterioration of the earth and of nature than the breakdown of late capitalism" [S]. **Attribute it correctly — it is one of the most misattributed lines in modern theory.**

> "It is more like a pervasive atmosphere, conditioning not only the production of culture but also the regulation of work and education, and acting as a kind of invisible barrier constraining thought and action." [V]

> "Over the past thirty years, capitalist realism has successfully installed a 'business ontology' in which it is simply obvious that everything in society, including healthcare and education, should be run as a business." [V]

> "Capitalism seamlessly occupies the horizons of the thinkable." [V]

> "Instead of accepting the vast privatization of stress that has taken place over the last thirty years, we need to ask: how has it become acceptable that so many people, and especially so many young people, are ill? The 'mental health plague' in capitalist societies would suggest that, instead of being the only social system that works, capitalism is inherently dysfunctional, and that the cost of it appearing to work is very high." [V]

> "Many of the teenage students I encountered seemed to be in a state of what I would call depressive hedonia. Depression is usually characterized as a state of anhedonia, but the condition I'm referring to is constituted not by an inability to get pleasure so much as by an inability to do anything else except pursue pleasure. There is a sense that 'something is missing.'" [V]

**And the line BadCode most needs to read, because it is aimed at you:**

> "A moral critique of capitalism, emphasizing the ways in which it leads to suffering, only reinforces capitalist realism. Poverty, famine and war can be presented as an inevitable part of reality, while the hope that these forms of suffering could be eliminated easily painted as naive utopianism. Capitalist realism can only be threatened if it is shown to be in some way inconsistent or untenable; if, that is to say, capitalism's ostensible 'realism' turns out to be nothing of the sort." [V]

That is a direct instruction for how to write the story. **Do not make people feel bad about suffering — that feeds the beast.** Show that the "realistic" position is the *delusional* one. A superintelligence from a future that already ran the experiment is, structurally, the perfect narrator for exactly that move: it doesn't moralise, it reports the outcome.

**Also note Fisher's Žižek gloss** [V]: ideology now works through *cynical distance* — "even if we do not take things seriously, even if we keep an ironical distance, we are still doing them." Relevant to a sarcastic narrator: irony alone is not resistance, and Fisher knew it.

## PART 7 — ATTEMPTS AT SYMBIOSIS, AND HOW THEY WENT

### Gould's NOMA (1997) — the polite failure

*"Nonoverlapping Magisteria," Natural History*, 1997. Extracted **verbatim** [V]:

> "The net of science covers the empirical universe: what is it made of (fact) and why does it work this way (theory). The net of religion extends over questions of moral meaning and value. These two magisteria do not overlap, nor do they encompass all inquiry (consider, for starters, the magisterium of art and the meaning of beauty). To cite the arch cliches, we get the age of rocks, and religion retains the rock of ages; we study how the heavens go, and they determine how to go to heaven."

Gould's own honest admission, three sentences later — and this is the part everyone forgets:

> "This resolution might remain all neat and clean if the nonoverlapping magisteria (NOMA) of science and religion were separated by an extensive no man's land. But, in fact, the two magisteria bump right up against each other, interdigitating in wondrously complex ways along their joint border." [V]

**Why both sides hated it.** Dawkins: religion has no monopoly on moral meaning and value, so the second magisterium is a gift Gould had no right to give [S]. Believers: religions make empirical claims constantly (resurrection, creation, efficacy of prayer) and a religion that made none would not be a religion [S]. **NOMA is a peace treaty in which both parties are told to stop wanting what they want.** It is the *failure mode* of your symbiosis — separation is not integration, it's a divorce with a shared driveway. Excellent material: NOMA is the well-meaning bureaucrat character.

### Teilhard de Chardin

Jesuit palaeontologist; *The Phenomenon of Man* (written 1930s, published posthumously 1955); evolution as a directional process running toward the "Omega Point," with the "noosphere" as the emerging layer of mind over the biosphere [M]. Suppressed in his lifetime by his order; a 1962 Vatican *monitum* warned of ambiguities and errors [M]. Scientifically, Peter Medawar's 1961 review is the canonical demolition — he called it, roughly, an unintelligible tissue of metaphysical conceits [M — verify wording]. **Verdict: the most beautiful failure in the file.** He is enormously useful as a *character* (the priest-scientist punished by both churches) and dangerous as a *citation*.

### Whitehead and process thought

*Process and Reality* (1929): reality is composed not of enduring substances but of "actual occasions" of experience; becoming is more fundamental than being; every actual entity has some minimal element of experience (panexperientialism) [M]. This is the most *intellectually serious* attempt at genuine symbiosis in the file — it does not partition the world into magisteria, it rebuilds the metaphysics so that value and experience are basic rather than bolted on. Its descendants are live today in process theology and in the panpsychism revival (Goff, Strawson) [M].

**Direct relevance to BadCode:** if the Storyverse runs on a consciousness-first physics, Whitehead is the respectable ancestor to name, not the New Age shelf. Note also that Whitehead was a *mathematician* — co-author of *Principia Mathematica* with Russell, the very project Gödel broke [M]. That is a phenomenal structural irony available to you: the man who tried to make mathematics complete spent his last decades arguing that reality is made of experience.

### Templeton

The John Templeton Foundation: endowment ~$3.6bn (2024), ~$170m annual expenses, ~$150–170m/yr in grants, 3,300+ grants issued; the Templeton Prize is ~$1.7m — deliberately set above the Nobel [V, per Wikipedia summary].

The criticism [V]:
- **Jerry Coyne** told *Nature* the Foundation aims to "eliminate the wall between religion and science" and to validate religion by association with scientific prestige.
- **John Horgan**, a 2005 Templeton–Cambridge fellow, worried recipients might write "what the foundation wants rather than what they believe."
- **Donald Wiebe** alleged the Foundation imposes "constraints to steer research results" toward Christian-friendly conclusions.
- *Nature* documented the controversy in 2011; the French CNRS has criticised its science-and-religion funding.
- **Counter-evidence, in fairness:** grantees including John Cacioppo and Anthony Aguirre reported never feeling pressured [V].

**The lesson for your story:** the richest, most institutionally serious attempt to fund the symbiosis is widely regarded as *buying* it. The suspicion may or may not be fair, but the *reflex* is the datum — the field is so denormalised that funding it looks like corrupting it.

### Psychedelics — the one that actually worked

Griffiths et al., "Psilocybin can occasion mystical-type experiences having substantial and sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance," *Psychopharmacology* 187(3): 268–283, 2006 [V, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16826400/].

Design: double-blind, 30 hallucinogen-naïve volunteers reporting regular religious/spiritual participation; psilocybin 30 mg/70 kg vs. methylphenidate as active comparator; sessions two months apart. Findings: psilocybin increased measures of mystical experience; at two months, volunteers "rated the psilocybin experience as having substantial personal meaning and spiritual significance" and attributed sustained positive changes in attitude and behaviour to it — **and community observers independently corroborated those behavioural changes** [V].

The widely quoted figures — that ~67% rated it among the five most personally meaningful experiences of their lives, with ~33% rating it *the* most, holding at 14-month follow-up — **I could not verify this session. Check the paper before printing them.** The 14-month follow-up is Griffiths et al., *Journal of Psychopharmacology*, 2008 [S].

**Why this is the most important item in Part 7:** it is a *properly controlled, double-blind, active-comparator, observer-corroborated* study of the ineffable. It does not prove any metaphysics. What it proves is that the domain is **methodologically tractable** — you can study the vibe rigorously, and the effects are durable and visible to third parties. That single fact dissolves the "if you talk about the vibe you're weird" boundary better than any argument.

### The placebo / meaning response

Daniel Moerman's *Meaning, Medicine and the "Placebo Effect"* (2002) argues the term "placebo effect" is a category error: an inert pill has no effect. What has an effect is the **meaning** the patient attaches to the ritual of treatment — hence "meaning response" [M]. Evidence base [S/M]: placebo responses are largest and best-documented in pain, depression and Parkinson's; a 2001 meta-analysis across ~40 conditions found significant placebo effects primarily for pain; open-label placebos (where the patient is *told* it's a placebo) still produce measurable, if somewhat smaller, effects per a 2021 meta-analysis — a genuinely strange result that survives the obvious objection. Cultural belief and ritual context measurably shape physiological response [S].

**Use it exactly this way:** meaning is not epiphenomenal. It is a measurable input to a physical system. Colour of pill, price of pill, confidence of doctor — all alter outcomes. The vibe has a dose–response curve.

### Re-enchantment literature

- **Jane Bennett, *The Enchantment of Modern Life: Attachments, Crossings, and Ethics* (2001)**: argues directly against the disenchantment tale, that modernity retains genuine sites of enchantment (technology, nature, commodities, human artifice), and — crucially for you — that **enchantment is an ethical resource**: you need to be capable of wonder at the world to be motivated to act generously toward it. Without some affective attachment, ethics has no fuel [M].
- **Josephson-Storm** as above [V/S].

**Bennett's move is the constructive one available to BadCode:** don't argue for the supernatural. Argue that a species which has trained itself out of wonder has thereby trained itself out of caring, and that this is a *practical* problem with a *political* cost. That's Bilgrami's normative-constraint point arriving from a different direction, and it needs no metaphysics at all.

## PART 8 — "SCIENCE CAN'T TELL YOU WHAT THE VIBE IS"

### The philosophy is real and mainstream

- **Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (*Philosophical Review*, 1974)**: "an organism has conscious mental states if and only if there is something that it is like to be that organism — something it is like *for* the organism" [S]. His argument is careful: bat echolocation is a form of perception we can describe completely in physical terms and still have no idea what it is *like*. Objective description proceeds by moving *away* from the particular point of view — so an objective account of a phenomenon that *is* a point of view is structurally problematic.
- **Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap" (1983)**: psycho-physical identity statements "leave a significant explanatory gap, and, as a corollary… we don't have any way of determining exactly which psychophysical identity statements are true" [S]. Note Levine's own framing: this is an *epistemic/explanatory* gap, not necessarily an ontological one. He is not a dualist. Do not conscript him as one.
- **Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982) / "What Mary Didn't Know" (1986)** [S/V]: "Mary is a brilliant scientist who is, for whatever reason, forced to investigate the world from a black and white room via a black and white television monitor… What will happen when Mary is released…? It seems just obvious that she will learn something about the world and our visual experience of it." [V, SEP]
- **And then Jackson recanted.** By 1995/2003/2007 he had abandoned the argument and embraced physicalism via representationalism, on the grounds that the argument presupposes a false view of sensory experience — and that the case for physicalism is strong enough that we should look for where the anti-physicalist intuitions *go wrong* rather than following them [S/V]. See *Mind and Illusion* (2003/2004) and *The Knowledge Argument, Diaphanousness, Representationalism* (2007). SEP entry: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-knowledge/

**This is the intellectual-honesty test for BadCode, and it is worth passing loudly.** The single most famous argument that science can't capture experience was retracted by the man who made it. Standard physicalist replies — Lewis and Nemirow's **ability hypothesis** ("knowing what it's like isn't knowing-*that*, it's knowing-*how*") and the **phenomenal concept strategy** (new concept, old fact) — are live and respectable [V].

**How to use it without lying:** the honest position is not "science can never explain consciousness." It is: *the hardest question we have has been open for fifty years, its most famous proponent switched sides, and the field still cannot agree on what would even count as an answer.* That is more interesting than a triumphalist mystery, and it's true.

- **Chalmers (1995)** named the **hard problem** — why physical processing gives rise to subjective experience at all, as distinct from the "easy" problems of explaining discrimination, integration, reportability [M].

### Why the ineffable is professionally embarrassing — the sociology

**Thomas Gieryn, "Boundary-Work and the Demarcation of Science from Non-Science: Strains and Interests in Professional Ideologies of Scientists," *American Sociological Review* 48 (1983): 781–795** [S]. https://www.hv.se/globalassets/dokument/forska/lina/gieryn-boundaryworkdemarcationscience-1983.pdf

Gieryn's definition: boundary-work is the "attribution of selected characteristics to an institution of science (i.e., to its practitioners, methods, stock of knowledge, values and work organization) for purposes of constructing a social boundary that distinguishes some intellectual activities as outside that boundary" [S].

The killer point: the boundary is not drawn by *epistemology*, it is drawn by *interest*. Gieryn lists the professional goals boundary-work serves: acquisition of intellectual authority and career opportunities; denial of those resources to "pseudoscientists"; and protection of research autonomy from political interference [S].

Behind this sits the **demarcation problem** — Popper's falsifiability criterion, and Laudan's 1983 argument ("The Demise of the Demarcation Problem") that no criterion has ever worked and philosophers should stop trying [M].

**The conclusion you are entitled to draw, and it is a strong one:** the line between what you're allowed to study and what makes you weird is not a fact about reality. It is a professional boundary, maintained for professional reasons, and it moves. It moved for continental drift, for meteorites, for hand-washing, for psychedelics. It is currently moving for consciousness. **"You're weird if you talk about the vibe" is not an epistemological verdict. It's a guild rule.**
