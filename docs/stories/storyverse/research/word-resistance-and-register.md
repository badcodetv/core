# Word resistance and register

*Research brief — 2026-08-07. Commissioned to answer one question: **which words and
framings cost us readers, and which buy them?** Specifically, whether to reframe the
Storyverse away from quasi-theology and toward hypothesis / thought experiment /
economic argument.*

> **Sourcing note, read first.** This session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before
> the brief started (200/200 used by earlier agents). The research was done with ~40
> direct WebFetch calls against known canonical URLs, ~30 of which returned substantive
> content. Consequence: several claims below rest on encyclopaedic summaries of the
> primary literature rather than the primary papers, because the publishers (Springer,
> Sage, Wiley, PsycNet) paywall direct access. Every claim is tagged **[evidenced]**,
> **[reported]** (secondary summary, unverified against the paper) or **[reasoning]**
> (my judgement, no evidence). Three things I could **not** verify and which must be
> checked before publication are flagged in §9.

---

## 0. The verdict, up front

**The proposed reframe is half right, and the half it gets wrong is the important half.**

- **Right:** cut the *warrant-borrowing* — every sentence that takes physics' authority
  for a claim physics does not make. This costs nothing and buys the entire sceptical
  gatekeeper class. The doctrine already knows this; it should be enforced harder.
- **Wrong:** making "hypothesis / thought experiment / economic argument" the primary
  container. Three reasons, each evidenced below:
  1. **The economics is the reactance trigger, not the metaphysics.** Solution aversion
     (Campbell & Kay) shows people reject a *problem* to avoid a *solution* they dislike.
     An economic ask about wealth is the single most rejectable thing in the piece. The
     part that feels safe is the dangerous part.
  2. **The thought-experiment frame is a philosophy register, and philosophy registers
     do not survive contact with the public.** Bostrom framed a trilemma with explicit
     disclaimers; the public remembers "we live in a simulation" and Sabine Hossenfelder
     calls it pseudoscience anyway. Careful hedging did not protect him.
  3. **Fiction has zero epistemic liability; a hypothesis has some.** Sanding the
     metaphysics down into a stated hypothesis moves the Storyverse *out* of the safe
     category (a story) and *into* the dangerous one (a nonfiction claim). Nobody accuses
     a novel of pseudoscience. Sapolsky, writing as a scientist, got hammered by
     philosophers for a claim milder than several things *Devs* asserts on screen.

**The real variable is not how far out you go. It is what you claim as your licence.**
Chopra and Sagan both write about awe. One is a punchline. The difference is not the
payload; it is the warrant. **Keep the metaphysics at full strength. Change the warrant.**

And a correction to the brief's own framing: **"could a committed atheist nod along" is
the wrong test.** It optimises for the smallest, most argumentative, already-converted
slice of the audience. The right test is: **could a physicist read it without wincing?**
That is a test about warrant, not payload, and it is passable at full metaphysical
strength.

## 1. Who is actually out there (the audience is not the problem you think it is)

The premise behind "sand off the metaphysics" is that a large secular audience will
bounce off spiritual vocabulary. The survey data does not support that premise as stated.

**United States** — Pew Religious Landscape Study 2023–24 **[evidenced]**:

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Religiously unaffiliated ("nones") | **29%** of US adults |
| Believe in God or a universal spirit | **83%** |
| Believe people have a soul or spirit beyond the physical body | **86%** |
| Believe there is something spiritual beyond the natural world | **79%** |

Pew's 2023 spirituality report **[evidenced]**:

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Describe themselves as spiritual in some way | **70%** |
| "Spiritual but not religious" (asked directly) | **22%** |
| Of SBNRs, share who are religiously unaffiliated | **54%** |
| Say "connected with something bigger than myself" is essential to being spiritual | **74%** of spiritual Americans |

Gallup **[evidenced]**: belief in God fell from 98% (1944–60s) to **81% (2022)**; **68%**
among young adults; **62%** among self-identified liberals. Falling, but from a ceiling.

**United Kingdom** — the more secular market, and the more useful one **[evidenced]**:

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| "No religion", 2021 census (England & Wales) | **37.2%** (up from 25.7% in 2011) |
| "No religion", Scotland 2022 | **51.1%** |
| Non-religious, British Social Attitudes 2018 | ~**52%** |
| Believe in "a god" (YouGov 2020) | **27%** |
| Believe in "a higher spiritual power" but not a god (YouGov 2020) | **16%** |
| Weekly church attendance, 2024–25 | ~**5%** |

**What this means [reasoning].** In the UK, roughly half the population is non-religious,
but only 27% believe in a god and another 16% believe in "a higher spiritual power" —
so *most* of the non-religious are not hard materialists either. In the US, four in five
people believe something spiritual exists beyond the natural world. The population that
will genuinely gag on the word "spiritual" is **small**.

But — and this is the whole point — **it is not proportionally small in the rooms that
decide whether a thing gets called woo in public.** It is concentrated in exactly the
tech-adjacent, online-argumentative, high-status readership BadCode wants, and that
readership has veto power over the label. So the risk is not losing the majority. **The
risk is losing the credibility gatekeepers, who then supply the majority with a
dismissal.** That is a much narrower problem, and it has a much cheaper fix than gutting
the metaphysics.

## 2. Why a single word can lose an argument you would otherwise win

### 2.1 Reactance (Brehm) — controlling language, not content **[evidenced]**

Reactance is "an unpleasant motivational arousal that emerges when people experience a
threat to or loss of their free behaviors." It has four parts: perceived freedom, threat,
reactance response, restoration. Findings that matter here:

- **Controlling language generates reactance independently of the argument.** Miller et
  al. found "more concrete, low-controlling language, and the restoration of freedom
  through the inclusion of a choice-emphasizing postscript" reduces it.
- Silvia (2005) demonstrated a boomerang: censoring or threatening messages *increase*
  the appeal of the forbidden alternative.
- Dillard & Shen: **anger** is a core affective component of reactance.

**Application.** The Storyverse narrator is written with "total authority." Authority is
not the problem; *prescription* is. "You must" triggers reactance. "I'd bet everything
on it" does not — it asserts hard while leaving the reader's freedom formally intact.
The BadCode voice can keep 100% of its swagger by converting every imperative into a
wager. This is a free win.

### 2.2 Identity-protective cognition (Kahan) — the one that should scare us **[evidenced]**

Kahan's cultural cognition: people "selectively credit or dismiss information in a
manner that reinforces beliefs congenial to their values," and are "more persuaded by
policy experts perceived to hold values similar to their own."

The finding that reorganises the whole strategy: in Kahan et al. (2012) on climate,
"ordinary members of the public do not become more concerned about climate change as
their science comprehension increases" — instead "the degree of polarization among
cultural groups with opposing predispositions increases." **More rigour amplifies the
split rather than closing it.**

**Application [reasoning].** Once a piece has been filed under an identity, adding
rigour makes things worse, not better. So the fight is not over the argument. **The
fight is over the filing, and the filing happens in the first thirty seconds.** Every
decision about opening images, first sentences, and which word appears above the fold
is worth more than every decision about the body text.

### 2.3 Solution aversion (Campbell & Kay) — **the most important finding in this brief** **[evidenced]**

Three experiments at Duke. Participants read a statement about a 3.2° temperature rise.

- When the paired solution was a **carbon tax / regulation**: **22%** of Republicans
  accepted the scientific claim.
- When the paired solution was **free-market green technology**: **55%** accepted the
  identical claim.
- Democrats showed no difference across solution type.
- It runs the other way too: **liberals downplayed the frequency of violent home
  break-ins** when the paired solution was loosening gun control.

Campbell: *"The cure can be more immediately threatening than the problem."* Kay: *"The
more threatening a solution is to a person, the more likely that person is to deny the
problem."* Campbell again: *"These divides are not explained by just one party being more
anti-science, but the fact that in general people deny facts that threaten their
ideologies."*

**Application — this inverts the brief's premise.** The Storyverse's punchline is *"if you
hoard wealth you are swimming upstream."* That is a solution-shaped claim about the
reader's money. Solution aversion predicts that **readers who dislike that conclusion
will reject the metaphysics that implies it** — not because the metaphysics is woo, but
because the metaphysics is *load-bearing for an ask they refuse*. Reframing from
metaphysics to economics does not dodge this. **It walks straight into it.**

The existing scope guard — *"NO VILLAINS… the argument is distributional, never
accusatory… if a reader finishes it wanting to blame somebody, it's wrong"* — is exactly
the right countermeasure, and this is the evidence for why it is right. Extend it into a
hard rule: **the diagnosis must never arrive holding a policy.** State the observation;
let the reader supply the prescription.

### 2.4 The backfire effect — do not cite it **[evidenced]**

Nyhan & Reifler's original finding (corrections strengthen false beliefs) has largely
failed to replicate. Wood & Porter's work and Swire-Thompson et al.'s 2020 review found
"widespread failures to replicate their existence." The current position: backfire
effects "are either unlikely to occur on the broader population level, or they only occur
in very specific circumstances, or they do not exist." No demographic group consistently
backfires. Nyhan himself concluded in 2021 that misinformation persistence is "most
likely due to other factors." Corrections generally have positive effects.

**Application.** Good news for `refutation.md`. Publishing the honest concessions
alongside the claim does **not** risk entrenching sceptics. There is no empirical reason
to soften. And never let a BadCode text lean on "you can't just tell people facts" — that
is a debunked crutch.

### 2.5 Framing effects — real, but weaker under scrutiny **[evidenced]**

Tversky & Kahneman's Asian disease problem: 72% chose the certain option under a
lives-saved frame, 78% chose the risky option under an identical deaths frame. Framing
effects are robust but **"diminish substantially when people receive ample credible
information and engage effortful processing."**

**Application [reasoning].** Framing buys you the reader's first reaction, not their
considered one. It gets you in the door; it does not carry the argument. Do not expect
vocabulary surgery alone to do the work.

## 3. The tripwire: who got dismissed, who didn't, and exactly why

This is the section that should change the plan, because the discriminator is **not**
metaphysical boldness.

### 3.1 The dismissed

**Capra, *The Tao of Physics* (1975)** **[evidenced]** — bestseller, 23 languages, still
a byword. Jeremy Bernstein attacked "his use of what seem to me to be accidental
similarities of language" presented as evidence of deep connection, calling the work
"superficial and profoundly misleading." Leon Lederman: Capra "constructs elaborate
extensions, totally bereft of the understanding of how carefully experiment and theory
are woven together." Peter Woit: Capra kept promoting the bootstrap model after it was
"a dead idea."

**What the Bleep Do We Know!? (2004)** **[evidenced]** — João Magueijo: the filmmakers
"deliberately misquote science." David Albert, who appears *in the film*, says he is
"profoundly unsympathetic to attempts at linking quantum mechanics with consciousness"
and was selectively edited. Dawkins: "tosh." All three directors were students of
Ramtha's School of Enlightenment.

**Sheldrake** **[evidenced]** — Maddox's *Nature* editorial "A book for burning?" said the
argument "is in no sense a scientific argument but is an exercise in pseudo-science" and
was not "falsifiable in Popper's sense." Wiseman found the dog-telepathy experiments
suffered "artefacts, bias resulting from experimental design, and post hoc analysis of
unpublished data." Critics: morphic resonance is "so vast and formless that it could
easily… dodge round any opposing argument."

**Chopra** **[evidenced]** — Sam Harris: "spooky physics" merging unrelated concepts "in
a completely unprincipled way." Chad Orzel: "utter gibberish" to experts but convincing
to laypeople through jargon. Robert Park on quantum-jump cancer theories: "cruel
fiction." 1998 Ig Nobel. And the killer: Pennycook et al.'s 2015 "pseudo-profound
bullshit" scale used Chopra's Twitter feed as the canonical stimulus, finding real tweets
**indistinguishable** from randomly generated ones.

**Quantum mysticism generally** **[evidenced]** — the marked rhetorical moves are
enumerated in the literature: equivocation on "observer" (it means *measuring
instrument*, not awareness); "coincidental similarities of language rather than genuine
connections" — Gell-Mann's **"quantum flapdoodle"**; misrepresenting historical
physicists as supporters. Note Wigner *did* suggest a role for consciousness in 1961 and
then **rejected it himself by the late 1970s** — citing early Wigner without that
sentence is a tripwire.

### 3.2 The respected — and note how far out they go

**Hofstadter, *GEB*** **[evidenced]** — 777 pages arguing consciousness is a strange loop.
Pulitzer + National Book Award. Gardner: "a book of such depth, clarity, range, wit,
beauty and originality." Respected because it engages logic and formal systems rigorously
*and* because its playful form — dialogues, a crab canon that reads identically
backwards — advertises itself as a construction.

**Rovelli, *The Order of Time*** **[evidenced]** — a working physicist arguing that time's
direction is not fundamental. Reviewers: "lucid," "jargon-free," "the brevity and
elegance of which belie its depth." One dissent (Anthony Aguirre): parts "occupy a
frustrating ground between too technical and not technical enough."

**Chiang, *Story of Your Life*** **[evidenced]** — a woman remembers her daughter's future
death. Respected because the alien language is anchored to **Fermat's principle of least
time**, a real and checkable piece of physics, and because the metaphysics serves an
emotional payload rather than a truth claim.

**Garland, *Devs*** **[evidenced]** — the machine's true name is **Deus**. Projections
include the Crucifixion. Characters recite Larkin's "Aubade" and Yeats's "The Second
Coming." Nobody called it mysticism. Sean Carroll defended its pace. Garland framed it
in the language of determinism and many-worlds.

**Harari, *Sapiens*** **[evidenced]** — asserts that "gods, nations, money and human
rights" are fictions, intersubjective realities sustained by mutual trust. 182 weeks on
the NYT list, 65 languages. Historians attacked his *facts* (Hallpike, Strawson,
Narayanan) — **nobody attacked him for the ontology.**

**Seth, *Being You*** **[evidenced]** — consciousness as "controlled hallucination."
Multiple book-of-the-year awards. He keeps standing by **dissolving** rather than
solving the hard problem and by holding a chair, editing a journal, and publishing 100+
papers.

**Hoffman** **[evidenced]** — argues perception is a species-specific interface hiding
reality, and that "the objective world consists of conscious agents and their
experiences." *Conscious realism is a stronger idealist claim than anything in the
Storyverse.* He retains a UC Irvine professorship and publishes in *Cognition* and
*Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, because he brings evolutionary game theory and
mathematical models. Controversial, not dismissed.

**Sapolsky, *Determined*** — the cautionary case *inside* the respected set
**[evidenced]**. Praised for accessibility, but Jessica Riskin in the *NYRB*: *"Science
can't prove there's no free will because the question of free will is not a scientific
question but a philosophical one."* Setiya and others faulted him for sidestepping the
philosophical literature. **He was punished for the boundary violation, not the claim.**

### 3.3 The tripwire, stated precisely **[reasoning, from the above]**

Three moves get you dismissed. None of them is "made a big metaphysical claim."

1. **Borrowing physics' authority for a claim physics does not make.** Bernstein's
   "accidental similarities of language." Gell-Mann's "flapdoodle." This is *the* move.
2. **Claiming empirical support that is not there, or building something unfalsifiable
   and then calling it science.** Maddox on Sheldrake.
3. **Selling something.** Chopra sells healing; Bleep's directors ran a school. The
   moment metaphysics attaches to a remedy, a course, or a membership, it is over.

Conspicuously **not** on the list: the word "spiritual" (Sam Harris wrote a book with it
in the subtitle and remains a hero to that audience); the word "consciousness" (Seth,
Chalmers, Hoffman, Goff); bold ontology (Harari, Garland, Chiang, Hofstadter).

**Conclusion: the Storyverse's existing bright lines are already the correct ones** —
*borrow the credible lineage, never the woo* / *converts nobody, no membership*. What
this research adds is that those two lines are **sufficient**. You do not additionally
need to sand the metaphysics.

## 4. Wonder without woo — the register, technique by technique

**Sagan's dragon** **[evidenced]** — *"What's the difference between an invisible,
incorporeal, floating dragon who spits heatless fire and no dragon at all?"* The move:
he applies the falsification test to a claim **he is sympathetic to**, in public, first.
The *Demon-Haunted World* baloney kit is nine rules, of which the load-bearing ones for
us are: recognise that authorities can err; examine alternative hypotheses; test whether
the hypothesis is falsifiable. **[reported]** Reviewers note a tension: one said Sagan was
"rather circumspect" on religion, another that he "splits his universe in two, into
science and irrationality," possibly falling into the untestable-claim trap he critiques.
Useful: even Sagan got accused of this. It is survivable.

**Sagan on wonder** **[evidenced]** — *"Our feeblest contemplations of the Cosmos stir us
— there is a tingling in the spine, a catch in the voice, a faint sensation as if a
distant memory, of falling from a height."* (*Cosmos*, p. 4.) Note the technique: the awe
is described **as a physiological event in the reader's body**, not as a property of the
cosmos. He never says the universe is awesome; he says *here is what happens to your
spine*. That is the entire trick and it is transferable verbatim.

**Feynman on mystery** **[evidenced]** — *"It does not do harm to the mystery to know a
little more about it. For far more marvelous is the truth than any artists of the past
imagined!"* And: *"The imagination of nature is far, far greater than the imagination of
man."* And on epistemics: *"Our freedom to doubt was born out of a struggle against
authority in the early days of science. It was a very deep and strong struggle: permit us
to question — to doubt — to not be sure."* Also **[evidenced]**, and worth pinning above
the writers' desk: *"God was always invented to explain mystery. God is always invented
to explain those things that you do not understand."* That is the sentence a sceptical
reader is silently holding while reading anything the Storyverse publishes.

**Rovelli** **[reported]** — the reception evidence says "lucid," "jargon-free," elegant
and brief. The structural technique: three sections, the physics first ("The Crumbling of
Time"), the human meaning last. **The poetry is paid for by the technical work that
precedes it.**

**Chiang** **[evidenced]** — one real anchor (Fermat's principle) per wild idea. The
metaphysics is in service of grief, not of truth.

**Harari** **[evidenced]** — extreme ontological claim, flat anthropological diction.
"Money is a shared fiction" contains no incense.

**Hofstadter** **[evidenced]** — form as argument. Jokes and dialogues signal *this is a
construction*, which buys latitude that earnest prose cannot.

**Burkeman, *Four Thousand Weeks*** **[evidenced]** — *"the average human lifespan is
absurdly, terrifyingly, insultingly short… Assuming you live to be eighty, you'll have
had about four thousand weeks."* NYT bestseller, *Time* 100 Must-Read 2021. The
technique: **existential content delivered by concrete arithmetic the reader can do in
their head.** Directly transferable to the Storyverse's economic punchline.

**Kurzgesagt** **[evidenced]** — 25M+ subscribers, 350+ videos, flat-design explainers on
"scientific, technological, political, philosophical, and psychological subjects."
Evidence that a young secular audience has enormous appetite for cosmic-scale consolation
delivered with jokes and diagrams. (I could not verify the "Optimistic Nihilism" video's
contents from an encyclopaedic source — see §9.)

**The awe literature (Keltner & Haidt)** **[evidenced]** — awe = **perceived vastness** +
**need for accommodation**. Effects: diminished self-focus (participants "felt small or
insignificant"), greater preference for experiential over material goods, greater
momentary life satisfaction. Two findings that are directly, awkwardly relevant:

- **Awe increases systematic processing** — uniquely among positive emotions, it makes
  people "less susceptible to weak arguments." *Your readers get more critical after the
  vista, not less.* So put your strongest reasoning immediately after the awe passage,
  never your weakest.
- **Awe increased willingness to donate *time*, but not *money*.** For a project whose
  punchline is about money, this is a genuinely deflating result. Awe alone will not
  loosen wallets. **[reasoning]** It suggests the economic turn must be argued, not felt.

**Moral elevation (Haidt)** **[evidenced]** — elicited by witnessing "actual or imagined
virtuous acts of remarkable moral goodness"; produces "warm, pleasurable sensations in
the chest"; directs attention **outward**, motivating people to "help or associate with
others and to cultivate themselves to become better people." Entirely naturalistic —
measurable, physiological, no supernatural apparatus. **This is the emotion the Storyverse
should be engineering for**, and it has a clean secular vocabulary already built.

## 5. The thought-experiment frame — weaker than advertised

**Nozick's experience machine** **[evidenced]** — designed to refute hedonism. Its three
elicited objections: we want to *do* things not have the experience of doing them;
plugging in makes you "an indeterminate blob," "a form of suicide"; the machine gives "no
*actual* contact with any deeper reality." Note: later work (Greene, De Brigard) found
status-quo bias contaminates responses, and **empirical studies have challenged Nozick's
premise that most people would refuse.** The most famous thought experiment in this class
turns out to be partly an artefact of framing.

**Rawls's veil of ignorance** **[evidenced]** — deliberately an imaginary device. It
persuades across ideological lines because "designing rules without knowing which role
you'll occupy seems fundamentally just" — critics who reject Rawls's conclusions still
grant the device. **This is the strongest evidence for the brief's proposal**, and it is
worth noting *why* it works: the veil is a **procedure the reader executes**, not a claim
they must accept. A reader can run it and get their own answer.

**Parfit's teletransporter** **[evidenced]** — presented "as an invitation to reconsider
intuitions about the self rather than a formal proof."

**Bostrom's simulation argument — the cautionary tale** **[evidenced]**. He wrote a
trilemma, hedged explicitly ("does not depend on any very strong version of
functionalism"; "should have no tendency to make us 'go crazy'"), and stated he "sees no
strong argument for which of the three trilemma propositions is the true one."
Result: **the public abandoned the trilemma entirely.** Musk: "better than 50-50 odds."
Tyson: no strong argument against. Hossenfelder calls it "pseudoscience"; Ellis calls it
"totally impracticable."

**The lesson [reasoning].** *Careful framing does not survive popularisation.* Whatever
hedge you build, the audience will strip it and quote the wildest sentence. So the hedge
cannot be your only protection — and if the wildest sentence is the one that travels, you
had better make sure the wildest sentence is one you are happy to be quoted on. This
argues **against** a hypothesis frame as the primary container and **for** a fiction
frame, where the wildest sentence is *supposed* to be wild.

**Self-persuasion** **[evidenced, but modest]** — the mechanism is that "arguments
perceived as self-originated may be more readily accepted than ones perceived as
externally originated," producing "deeper, longer lasting" change. Supported by ELM,
cognitive dissonance, social judgement theory. **[caveat]** I could not source
experimental effect sizes, and I found no accessible evidence on rhetorical questions
specifically (the Petty/Cacioppo/Heesacker work the brief asked for was not reachable).
**Do not claim that interrogatives outperform declaratives — I could not evidence it.**
What is defensible: a procedure the reader executes (Rawls) beats a proposition the
reader must swallow.

## 6. Does economic framing widen the audience?

**Effective Altruism** **[evidenced]** — the clearest test case, and the answer is *yes,
and it cost them*. EA translated a moral obligation into "cost-effectiveness," "expected
value," QALYs — turning "how to help" into an optimisation problem. It recruited
precisely the target demographic: elite Anglo-American institutions, Stanford, the Bay
Area, tech billionaires (Moskovitz, Bankman-Fried). It worked because technical secular
audiences found it "intellectually satisfying" and it "promised to remove emotion and
bias." The costs: critics noted "principles which do not lend themselves to
quantification — justice, fairness, equality — get left in the sidelines"; charges of
"telescopic philanthropy" ignoring structural inequality; and the SBF collapse, where
"the numerical focus had masked ethical blind spots rather than preventing them."

**Lesson [reasoning]: arithmetic buys entry and then constrains what you are allowed to
say.** Once you have argued in numbers, you cannot appeal to justice without looking like
you are changing the rules mid-game. For a project whose actual claim is *the yield is
experience* — an explicitly non-quantifiable good — that is a bad trade. **Adopting the
economic register wholesale would hand your opponents the strongest weapon against your
own thesis.**

**Piketty** **[evidenced]** — r > g, 2.5m+ copies, HUP's biggest ever, NYT #1, Krugman:
"the most important economics book of the year — maybe of the decade." A political
argument (progressive wealth taxes to 2%, income taxes to 80%) delivered as a data book.
But note Clive Crook: the work "invites readers to believe not just that inequality is
important, but that nothing else matters," and the FT's dataset dispute. **The framing
worked and everyone could see it was framing.**

**Doughnut Economics** **[evidenced]** — the model spread to Amsterdam, Scotland, Wales,
South Africa, the Netherlands, India, China. Criticism is severe: Acharya/Geloso/Psurek
found **no empirical support for the central thesis**; Schokkaert says it lacks
specifics; Milanović says the no-growth story is unrealistic. **It spread anyway, because
of a shape.** For a collective that makes comics, this is the single most encouraging
finding in the brief: **the diagram was the payload, and its empirical weakness did not
stop it.**

**Moral reframing (Feinberg & Willer)** — **I must under-sell this, contrary to what the
brief hoped.** The stated finding is that "environmental and other political messages are
more persuasive when framed in terms of an audience's own moral values rather than the
messenger's" **[reported]**. But I could not access the primary papers (Sage and Wiley
both paywalled; the TED transcript page returned only a blurb), **and** the Moral
Foundations literature summary reports a recent (2026) study that "found no evidence that
reframing political positions and issues to moral foundations shifted support among
conservatives and liberals" **[reported]**. Given the brief's own instruction not to cite
a debunked effect: **treat moral reframing as plausible and unproven, not as a proven
method.** It should not be the load-bearing justification for a strategy change. Get the
primary papers before relying on it.

**Lakoff** **[evidenced]** — negating a frame evokes it; "tax relief" presupposes taxes
are an affliction; the "truth sandwich." But Pinker's 2006 review called Lakoff's
prescriptions "a recipe for electoral failure," and there is **no empirical evidence** in
the accessible record that Lakoff-style reframing campaigns measurably work. Use the
diagnosis (don't repeat the opponent's frame); ignore the prescriptions.

## 7. The atheist test, and the maximum metaphysical payload

**What secular audiences demonstrably accept:**

- **Awe and self-transcendence as natural phenomena** — Keltner/Haidt awe, Haidt's
  elevation. Fully naturalised, measurable, respectable **[evidenced]**.
- **Reclaimed "spirituality", from someone who has earned it** — Sam Harris keeps the
  word deliberately, acknowledging its uses are "diverse and sometimes indefensible," and
  defines it functionally: practice aimed at revealing the self as illusory, grounded in
  direct experience rather than doctrine **[evidenced]**. Reception was mixed but he was
  not expelled.
- **Secular ritual and community** — Sunday Assembly, founded 2013 by two comedians who
  "both wanted to do something like church but without God"; 300+ at the first meeting,
  ~48 assemblies by 2019, with published wellbeing gains in *Secularism and Nonreligion*
  **[evidenced]**. Appetite is real but the scale is modest — this is not a mass movement.
- **Borrowing religious *forms*** — de Botton got a NYT bestseller and a kicking. Eagleton
  accused him of trying to "hijack other people's beliefs, empty them of content"; Brooks
  said it made atheism "kind of boring"; Charles Moore: "religion's usefulness is drawn
  from its truth" **[evidenced]**. **Lesson: borrowing the form without the content reads
  as parasitic to both sides.** Do not build a church-shaped thing.

**Maximum payload [reasoning].** Look at what the respected set actually carries:
Hoffman says the objective world consists of conscious agents; Harari says your country
is imaginary; Chiang says a woman remembers the future; Garland names the machine Deus.
**Every one of these is at or beyond the Storyverse's payload.** The ceiling is not the
constraint. The container is.

**Containers, ranked [reasoning]:**

1. **Fiction with a narrator** — highest latitude, zero epistemic liability. A voice only
   has to be *good*; a claim has to be *true*. This is what BadCode already has, and it
   is a stronger asset than the reframe would replace it with.
2. **A picture** — Doughnut proved a shape can travel with no empirical support at all.
   For a comic collective this is the whole game.
3. **A number** — Burkeman's 4,000 weeks. Concrete arithmetic carries existential freight.
4. **An essay / stated hypothesis** — lowest latitude, highest liability. This is what the
   reframe proposes moving toward.

Words worth keeping, with the exact framing that rescues each.

### 8.3 The buy list — safe, vivid, underused

- **The record vocabulary** (Zurek's, and it's free): record, copy, broadcast, redundancy,
  receipt, ledger, witness, the thing everyone agrees on because it was mass-produced.
  Technical, cheap, and it does the metaphysical work without any woo signature.
- **Finance verbs for metaphysical nouns**: yield, settle, clear, price in, denominate,
  stock vs. flow, claim, the unit of account, the scoreboard. **The single biggest
  available register upgrade** — it makes ontology sound like accounting, which is
  exactly the tone the punchline needs.
- **Undecided / undetermined / no fact yet** — already doctrine, keep.
- **Bet, concede, falsify, control, the one lie** — epistemic honesty as swagger.
- **Boredom, appetite, drama, play, dress rehearsal, the bee and the tree.**

### 8.4 The register spec — 13 sentence-level techniques for "hypothesis, not doctrine"

1. **Pay for the poetry first.** (Rovelli) Every lyrical sentence is preceded by a
   technical one that earned it. Never open a section on the vista.
2. **Locate the awe in the reader's body, not in the cosmos.** (Sagan: "a tingling in the
   spine, a catch in the voice") Describe the physiological event. It is unfalsifiable
   *and* uncontroversial, because it is a report, not a claim.
3. **Name the leap in the same breath as the claim.** (Sapolsky's failure mode; Riskin's
   review) "Here is where I stop doing physics" is armour. Say it before a critic can.
4. **Run the falsification test on yourself, first, in public.** (Sagan's dragon) State
   the sceptic's best objection better than they would.
5. **Boring diction, radical content.** (Harari) The more extreme the ontology, the
   flatter the vocabulary. "Money is a shared fiction" — nine words, no incense.
6. **One checkable anchor per wild idea.** (Chiang/Fermat) Exactly one real,
   look-it-uppable fact per metaphysical move — as *illustration*, never as *proof*.
   Two anchors starts to look like evidence, which is the tripwire.
7. **Assert as a wager, never as a fact.** "I'd put everything on it" is both *stronger*
   (it keeps the narrator's swagger) and *safer* (zero epistemic liability) than "it is
   the case that." This alone resolves most of the tension in the brief.
8. **Convert imperatives to wagers.** (Brehm) Controlling language triggers reactance
   independent of content. The voice keeps 100% of its authority; it just stops issuing
   instructions.
9. **Never let the diagnosis arrive holding a policy.** (Campbell & Kay) If you name the
   tax, you lose the physics. State the observation; let the reader write the ask.
10. **Give the reader a procedure, not a proposition.** (Rawls's veil) A thing they can
    execute and get their own answer from beats a thing they must accept.
11. **Form as disclaimer.** (Hofstadter; the fiction frame) Jokes, a narrator with a
    personality, dialogue — these advertise construction and buy latitude. Deadly earnest
    prose has to be true; a voice only has to be good.
12. **Concrete arithmetic as the delivery vehicle.** (Burkeman's 4,000 weeks) Give the
    reader a number they can do in their head. This is how the economic punchline should
    arrive.
13. **Awe, then argument — never awe, then ask.** Awe increases systematic processing and
    makes readers *less* susceptible to weak arguments; and it raises willingness to give
    *time*, not money. Put your strongest reasoning immediately after the vista, and
    never put the request there.

### 8.5 Rules that fall out of this for the existing canon

- **`refutation.md` is empirically vindicated.** The backfire effect does not replicate;
  publishing the concessions alongside the claim carries no entrenchment risk. **Do not
  soften it.**
- **"The data underdetermine the metaphysics"** (currently buried at the end of
  `doctrine.md`'s vocabulary section) is the most valuable sentence in the canon. It
  should be near the top of every surface that touches consciousness.
- **The "no villains" scope guard is the solution-aversion countermeasure** and should be
  restated in those terms so future writers know *why* it is non-negotiable.
- **"Machine for turning sunlight into drama"** is correctly built: "machine" defuses the
  agency exactly as intended. **"The yield of the universe is experience"** is one notch
  riskier — it assigns purpose — but "yield" (a finance word) is doing good defusing
  work. Recommendation: never let that sentence appear without the narrator owning it.
- **The wager frame already in `README.md`** ("it is a wager, never a proof… here is what
  I would put everything on") is the correct primary container and is stronger than the
  proposed hypothesis frame. **Do not replace it. Enforce it everywhere.**

## 10. The verdict, and the counter-case argued honestly

### 10.1 The counter-case, made as strongly as I can

**Sanding off the metaphysics produces a forgettable pamphlet, and the works that changed
minds were the ones that went furthest out.**

The evidence for this is not weak. Nobody quotes r > g at a party; everybody quotes "a
mote of dust suspended in a sunbeam." Doughnut Economics has **no empirical support for
its central thesis** and was adopted by Amsterdam anyway, because it was a shape. *Devs*
named its machine Deus and was praised for intellectual rigour. *Sapiens* sold in 65
languages on the claim that your country is imaginary. Kurzgesagt has 25 million
subscribers doing cosmic consolation for teenagers. **Memorability lives in the
overreach.** And BadCode's entire theory of change is that people remember a story and
absorb a song in a way they never absorb an essay — so converting the story into an
essay-shaped hypothesis destroys the mechanism that justified the project.

Worse: EA is the cautionary tale for the specific reframe being proposed. It successfully
recruited a secular technical elite with arithmetic, and the arithmetic then **crowded out
exactly the values the Storyverse cares about** — "justice, fairness, equality get left in
the sidelines." A project whose thesis is *the yield is experience* — an explicitly
unquantifiable good — cannot win in a register that only credits quantities. **The
economic frame would hand the opposition the best weapon against the thesis.**

### 10.2 Where the counter-case is wrong

It conflates **payload** with **warrant**. The evidence in §3 is unambiguous: the
dismissed works were not dismissed for going far out. They were dismissed for
*borrowing physics' authority*, *claiming absent evidence*, and *selling something*.
Sheldrake was destroyed by unfalsifiability, not by ambition. Capra was destroyed by
"accidental similarities of language," not by liking Taoism. Chopra was destroyed by
jargon-as-warrant, not by talking about consciousness. You can go all the way out and
stay credible, provided you never claim science's licence for the trip.

### 10.3 The ruling

**Do not shift from quasi-theological to hypothetical-economic. Shift from *claiming* to
*betting*, and keep everything else.**

Concretely:

- **Keep the metaphysics at full strength.** The ceiling is far above where you are.
- **Keep the fiction container.** It is the highest-latitude, lowest-liability vehicle
  available, and it is the one thing the reframe would throw away for nothing.
- **Kill the vocabulary in §8.1 without mercy.** That is where the actual reader-loss is,
  and it costs nothing to fix. This is the genuine, actionable half of the brief's
  instinct.
- **Convert every assertion to a wager and every imperative to an observation.** This buys
  the sceptics *and* the reactance-prone, and it makes the narrator sound harder, not
  softer.
- **Move the economics from a demand to an arithmetic.** Burkeman's four thousand weeks,
  not Piketty's 80% top rate. Give the number; withhold the policy. Solution aversion says
  naming the remedy is what loses the reader — and it will take the metaphysics down with
  it.
- **Drop the atheist test. Adopt the physicist test.** "Could a committed atheist nod
  along" optimises for a small, argumentative, already-persuaded slice. "Could a physicist
  read it without wincing" is a test about warrant, passable at full payload, and it
  happens to also satisfy the atheist.

**One line for the decisions log:** *We are not making the Storyverse smaller. We are
making it stop claiming. The bet stays enormous; the licence gets handed back.*
