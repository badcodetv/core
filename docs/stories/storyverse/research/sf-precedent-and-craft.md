# The Storyverse: prior art & craft

Research memo for BadCode — who has told this story before, how they did it, what worked,
what's exhausted, and how to be fresh in 2026.

**Sourcing note.** Everything with an inline URL was retrieved and read during this pass.
A handful of items (marked **[unverified]**) are from my own knowledge — the session's web-search
budget ran out before I could source them. Treat those as leads, not citations.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph verdict

Your metaphysics has been done — but never all at once, and never with your politics. The pieces
exist: consciousness-collapses-the-wave-function (Egan), choice-inside-many-worlds-still-builds-character
(Chiang), the world-as-stage (Shakespeare → Calderón → Goffman), the higher-dimensional projection
(Abbott → Liu Cixin), and the artist-who-declares-a-cosmology (Moore, Morrison, Dick, Sun Ra).
What is genuinely unoccupied territory is: **a consciousness-first metaphysics used as an argument
about political economy, delivered as drum & bass and comics, by a narrator who is explicitly a
character.** The three highest-leverage moves in this memo are (a) steal Chiang's move — make the
metaphysics a *pressure on character*, not a *fact about physics*; (b) steal Egan's disclaimer —
he published the physics he *doesn't believe* and said so, out loud, on his own website; (c) never
let the cosmology be the point of a single track or panel. The cosmology is the weather. The story
is the weather happening to someone who owes rent.

## 1. Prior art in fiction

### 1a. Greg Egan, *Quarantine* (1992) — the closest structural ancestor you have

This is the essential one. Egan builds a novel on *exactly* your "the wave function hasn't decided
yet" premise, and does it in a noir detective plot.

**The mechanism, in detail.** In 2034 an impenetrable shell — the Bubble — encloses the solar
system, cutting humanity off from the stars. The reason, revealed late: humans are the only
observers in the universe whose brains *collapse the wave function*. Every other species exists
happily smeared across possibilities; when humans started looking outward, we began annihilating
the alternative states of everything we observed. The Bubble is quarantine. Not of a disease — of us.
We are a cosmic weapon that murders possibility just by paying attention.

Egan's own gloss on his own book, on his own site, is the single most useful document I found for
you ([gregegan.net/QUARANTINE/QM/QM.html](https://www.gregegan.net/QUARANTINE/QM/QM.html)). He
describes the premise as "a variation on position 4: human beings alone have a special structure in
their brains that actively causes the collapse of the wave function," and then — crucially — says:

> "I don't actually believe it for a moment."

and that the hypothesis was "chosen solely for its technological and existential ramifications, not
because I considered it plausible," acknowledging that "positing a role for *an observer* in the
collapse … is a marginal view." He then lists his *own* premise's two fatal flaws: **decoherence**
("every molecule of air, every particle of dust, that he disturbs is going to respond in a way that
is correlated with his actions") and the **BBBV theorem**, which forbids a quantum computer from
simply trying every branch and keeping the winner.

**"Smearing."** The Ensemble develops a neural "eigenstate mod" — a wavefunction-collapse *inhibitor*.
Switch it on and you stop being an observer: you smear across a superposition of every action you
could take, each equally real. Then you collapse to the branch that succeeded. The protagonist Nick
Stavrianos robs, escapes, and shoots his way through the book by smearing into millions of parallel
attempts and selecting the winner ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine_(Egan_novel))).

**Why this matters to you.** Egan already owns "collapse is an act, and acting collapses." Your
differentiator has to be the *valuation*. In Egan, collapse is a **crime** — an ecological atrocity
against possibility. In your Storyverse, choosing is the **point** — the universe's purpose is drama
and drama requires the wave to land. That inversion is a real, defensible, distinct position. Say it
loudly and *Quarantine* becomes your foil rather than your ceiling.

### 1b. Greg Egan, *Permutation City* (1994) — and the second disclaimer

Dust Theory: consciousness is a computed pattern that reassembles itself from arbitrary scatterings
of matter across all of spacetime, "disconnected from cause and effect"; "every internally consistent
ordering of events into a space-time will look as good from the inside as every other." Egan again
publishes his own FAQ against himself
([gregegan.net/PERMUTATION/FAQ/FAQ.html](https://www.gregegan.net/PERMUTATION/FAQ/FAQ.html)).
Asked whether he takes Dust Theory seriously:

> "Not very seriously, although I have yet to hear a convincing refutation of it on purely logical grounds."

His empirical objection is beautiful and worth stealing wholesale as a *character argument*: the
universe "is far too orderly and obeys far simpler and more homogeneous physical laws than it would
need to, merely in order to contain observers." If every arrangement containing an observer were
real, chaotic worlds would outnumber orderly ones catastrophically — and they don't. **The neatness
of physics is evidence against radical possibility-realism.** That's a line a BadCode narrator could
deliver in eight words.

### 1c. Ted Chiang — the one you should study hardest

**"Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom" (2019)** is your nearest living relative and the best answer
anyone has published to the nihilism problem
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety_Is_the_Dizziness_of_Freedom)).

The device is the **prism** — a "Plaga interworld signaling device." Activate it and it performs a
quantum measurement (red light / blue light), splitting the timeline in two; the prism's paired
qubits let the two branches *talk to each other*, but only those two, and only until the qubits are
exhausted. So it isn't infinite multiverse — it's *one* fork with a finite, depleting communication
budget. That constraint is what makes the story work.

The crisis Chiang stages is precisely the one your user identifies: "Many worried that their choices
were rendered meaningless because every action they took was counterbalanced by a branch in which
they made the opposite choice."

And the answer — the money quote — is that character is a *distribution*, not a point:

> "an individual's character would also be revealed by the choices they make across many worlds"

The therapist Dana's argument runs: if in most branches you do the shabby thing and in a few you do
the decent thing, that *is* your character, measured across a wider sample. Being good means shifting
your distribution. Every good act narrows the set of worlds in which you're a coward. This is a
**statistical theory of virtue** and it is the single most transplantable idea in this memo. It kills
multiverse nihilism without denying the multiverse.

**"Story of Your Life" (1998) / *Arrival*.** The heptapods perceive time teleologically; their physics
starts from **variational principles** (Fermat's principle of least time: light takes the path that
minimises travel time — which requires "knowing" the endpoint) and derives cause-and-effect as a
special case, the inverse of our order
([lfq.salisbury.edu](https://lfq.salisbury.edu/_issues/51_1/there_is_no_time_parsing_the_future_perfect_in_ted_chiang_the_story_of_your_life.html),
[gwern.net](https://gwern.net/story-of-your-life)). Louise learns Heptapod B, acquires simultaneous
consciousness, and knowingly walks into her daughter's death. The heptapods are "not helpless
automatons"; they "experience all events at once, perceiving a purpose underlying them all."
**Craft lesson:** the metaphysics is delivered *through grammar and grief*, never through a lecture.
The reader learns the physics by learning to read the story's tenses.

**"Exhalation" (2008).** An anatomist dissects his own brain, discovers his consciousness runs on
an air-pressure gradient, and realises the universe is equalising — the "argon" will level and all
thought will stop. Perfect model of a **short-form** metaphysical delivery: one image (a mechanical
being taking apart its own head with mirrors), one law (entropy), one emotional consequence. Under
9,000 words.

### 1d. Philip K. Dick — *VALIS*, the Exegesis, "the Empire never ended"

Dick's 2-3-74 pink-light experiences produced ~8,000 pages of *Exegesis* and the novel *VALIS*, where
the "Tractates Cryptica Scriptura" gives us the **Black Iron Prison** — "an all-pervasive system of
social control" — and the axiom **"The Empire never ended."** The gloss: Rome did not fall; it changed
costume. "The Empire is a psychological as well as a military state. It exists as a mental construct,
as a psychopathic state of mind, as a system of control. It exists in all of us."
([tekgnostics](https://tekgnostics.blogspot.com/2013/05/the-empire-never-ended-redux.html),
[philipdick.com](https://www.philipdick.com/mirror/websites/pkdweb/valis.htm))

For BadCode this is the **register you already write in** — a metaphysical claim that is secretly a
political claim, delivered as an aphorism. "The Empire never ended" is a five-word Marxist analysis
of continuity of power dressed as gnostic revelation. That's your house style. Steal the *form*: one
sentence that scans as cosmology and lands as economics.

Dick is also your cautionary tale. He split himself into "Philip K. Dick" and "Horselover Fat" inside
the novel *because he could not tell whether he believed it*. The self-splitting is the guardrail —
see §5.

### 1e. Borges

**"Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" (1940).** A secret society invents a planet whose inhabitants are
"congenitally idealist" — Tlön has no nouns, only verbs and adjectival accretions; objects exist only
as perceived. Borges's killer line: "Hume noted for all time that Berkeley's arguments did not admit
the slightest refutation nor did they cause the slightest conviction. This dictum is entirely correct
in its application to the earth, but entirely false in Tlön." Then the invented world begins leaking:
artifacts from Tlön appear on Earth, and Earth starts *becoming* Tlön, because a beautiful, orderly
fiction is more attractive than a messy reality
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tl%C3%B6n,_Uqbar,_Orbis_Tertius),
[Interesting Literature](https://interestingliterature.com/2022/05/borges-tlon-uqbar-orbis-tertius-summary-analysis/)).

**This is the most important warning in the entire memo.** Borges wrote, in 1940, the exact failure
mode of a beautifully-constructed fictional metaphysics released into a frightened world: it wins
*because* it is tidier than reality, and people prefer it. Tlön is QAnon eighty years early. Build
your Storyverse with that story taped to the wall.

**"The Garden of Forking Paths" (1941)** gives you the many-worlds novel-as-labyrinth ("an infinite
series of times, a growing, dizzying web of divergent, convergent and parallel times") twenty years
before Everett was taken seriously — and note that Borges gets there via *a novel*, not physics.
**[unverified in this pass — well-established but not re-sourced here]**

### 1f. Flatland (1884) — the dimensional-projection primer

Abbott's *Flatland: A Romance of Many Dimensions*, narrated by "A. Square." A Sphere from Spaceland
visits; the Square can only perceive it as a circle that grows and shrinks. The Sphere can see *inside*
Flatland's buildings and inside its people; when it touches the Square's interior, the Square feels a
sudden pain in his gut. The Square, having grasped 3D, is imprisoned for heresy when he preaches it.

And critically: **the dimensional conceit is a class satire.** Social rank is literally the number of
your sides — Circles are the priest-aristocracy, triangles the working class, women are line segments
([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/12/20/flatland-epilogue/),
[Broadview](https://broadviewpress.com/product/flatland/)).

**Flatland is the proof that your exact move works.** A higher-dimensional projection story that is
*actually about hierarchy and who is allowed to know things*. It's been in print for 140 years. Do
what Abbott did: make the dimensionality carry the politics, not decorate it.

### 1g. Vonnegut — the tonal ancestor

The Tralfamadorians of *Slaughterhouse-Five* are four-dimensional; they see all of time at once, "can
live in any moment," and hold that free will is a peculiar Earthling superstition. "So it goes."
Time as a book you can open at any page rather than read front to back
([vonnegut.fandom.com](https://vonnegut.fandom.com/wiki/Tralfamadorians),
[Michigan Daily](https://www.michigandaily.com/arts/unstuck-in-time-four-dimensions-in-slaughterhouse-five/)).

*The Sirens of Titan* is even closer to your "purpose of the universe" gag: the Tralfamadorians once
sought the purpose of their lives, learned it, found it humiliatingly small, and built machines to
fulfil it while they went and found better purposes. The entire history of humanity turns out to be
a message-delivery system for a spare part. **Vonnegut is the license for your comic register**: he
proves you can state a bleak cosmic thesis and be *funny*, and that the joke is what makes it stick.
"The purpose of the universe is to turn sunlight into drama" is a Vonnegut sentence. Own that lineage
deliberately.

### 1h. Neal Stephenson, *Anathem* (2008)

Two competing metaphysics, staged as an argument between monastic factions. The **Hylaean Theoric
World** is a Platonic realm of perfect forms and theorems ("cnoöns") that mathematicians' minds
literally touch. Against it: **Protism / the polycosm**, where the wave function doesn't choose, it
splits — and "quantum interference — the crosstalk among similar quantum states — knits the different
versions of your brain together," so consciousness is *already* a multi-cosmic object; the brain's
only special trick is exploiting that crosstalk
([Anathem Wiki](https://anathem.fandom.com/wiki/Hylaean_Theoric_World)).

**Direct relevance:** Stephenson has already dramatised "consciousness spans the higher structure and
we are the low-dimensional slice." His delivery vehicle — hundreds of pages of Socratic dialogue
between monks — is *exactly what you must not do*. Same idea, opposite craft. Read it for the ideas
and then run away from the method.

### 1i. Liu Cixin — dimensional reduction as a weapon

In *Death's End*, a higher civilisation attacks the solar system with a **dual vector foil**, which
collapses three-dimensional space into two. Sun, planets, people, molecules — everything flattens into
an infinitely large picture. The universe's history is one of successive dimensional reduction as
warring civilisations degrade the physics they fight in
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_of_Earth%27s_Past)). Plus the **Dark Forest**
axioms: life expands exponentially, matter is finite, and the chain of suspicion means you shoot first.

**Steal:** the image of *flattening as violence*. Your "we are a low-dimensional projection" reads, in
Liu's grammar, as **we are already the survivors of a reduction**. That's a much better story than
"we are a projection" stated neutrally. Something *did this to us*. Or we did it to ourselves. That's
a plot.

### 1j. Theatre: Frayn's *Copenhagen*, Stoppard's *Hapgood* and *Arcadia*

**Copenhagen (1998).** Bohr, Margrethe and Heisenberg, all dead, meet in an afterlife to re-run the
1941 Copenhagen meeting. The play's structure is its physics: the scene is played as a series of
**"drafts"** — incompatible versions of the same event, none privileged. Frayn "delivers explanations
of the uncertainty principle and of complementarity, which [he] then applies to human behavior: we
must always be uncertain what is in a person's mind, and we all have complementary motives for our
actions"
([Cambridge, *New Theatre Quarterly*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-theatre-quarterly/article/abs/theatre-of-uncertainties-science-and-history-in-michael-frayns-copenhagen/096C05F038CD1781E036E574F1C1B798),
[Grinnell](https://www.grinnell.edu/news/michael-frayns-copenhagen)).

**The single best craft lesson in this memo: Frayn does not explain the physics — he *stages* it.**
The uncertainty principle isn't described, it's enacted, because the audience cannot determine
Heisenberg's motive. **Your comic should collapse a wave function on the page, not describe one.**
A panel that hasn't decided yet. A speech bubble with two texts. A scroll position that determines
what happened.

**Stoppard.** *Hapgood* (1988) maps wave-particle duality onto Cold War double agents — is he ours or
theirs? Both, until measured. *Arcadia* (1993) runs chaos theory and the second law through two
timelines in one room. The critical verdict is instructive: "*Hapgood* simply confused and irritated
much of its audience, while *Arcadia* is regarded by many as Stoppard's greatest play"
([Cambridge Companion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-tom-stoppard/science-in-hapgood-and-arcadia/A6D8906B4FDC4CD61381D6613A7D9EBB)).
**Why the difference?** *Hapgood* uses physics as a *plot mechanism* (you must understand it to follow
who's who). *Arcadia* uses it as *emotional structure* (the heat death is Thomasina's death; you feel
it whether or not you can do the maths). **Rule: metaphysics as feeling, never as plot-gate.**

### 1k. Comics: Moore and Morrison

**Alan Moore, *Jerusalem* (2016)** — eternalism, in full. From his interview with Raphael Sassaki
([The Anarchist Library](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/raphael-sassaki-moore-on-jerusalem-eternalism-anarchy-and-herbie)):

> "every moment that has ever existed or will ever exist, from the beginning to the end of our universe,
> is coterminous."

> "Only when the beam of a projector … is passed across the strip of film do we see … events, and
> continuity, and narrative."

> "there is nowhere for our travelling bead of consciousness to go save back to the beginning, with the
> same thoughts, words and deeds recurring and reiterated endlessly."

> "through this reading of human existence, every place, no matter how mean, is transformed to the
> eternal, heavenly city."

And elsewhere: "everywhere is Jerusalem. That in an Einsteinian block universe, where all time is
presumably simultaneous, then everywhere is the eternal heavenly city"
([London, Hollywood](https://londonhollywood.wordpress.com/2016/09/22/if-you-read-only-one-alan-moore-jerusalem-interview-make-it-this-one/),
[The Quietus](https://thequietus.com/articles/21230-alan-moore-jerusalem-novel-review-working-class-mythology)).

Note what Moore *does* with the cosmology: he uses eternalism to dignify a specific half-square-mile
of poor Northampton. **The metaphysics is deployed as class solidarity.** That is the move you want.
Moore also defines magic operationally: "Any purposeful engagement with the phenomena and
possibilities of consciousness," and says "magic is inextricably bound up with language, art and
consciousness as if they were indeed but facets of the same thing," diagnosing modern culture as "the
dismembered body of magic" that artists are responsible for reassembling.

**Grant Morrison, *The Invisibles* (1994–2000), *Flex Mentallo* (1996), *The Filth* (2002)** — the
"hypersigil trilogy." *The Invisibles* was written as a 1,500-page magical operation intended to push
culture toward a better future; Morrison inserted himself as King Mob, and when King Mob was tortured
Morrison got sick — which he took as proof of the fiction/reality link. In November 1995, facing
cancellation, he asked readers to perform a mass sigil ritual to save the book
([TeeVeeTropes](https://teeveetropesdotcom.wordpress.com/2014/02/27/art-as-magic-magic-as-art-the-hypersigils-of-grant-morrison/),
[Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/grant-morrison-chaos-magic-ritual-vertigo-invisibles/),
[Wikipedia: Flex Mentallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flex_Mentallo)).

From the *Arthur* interview
([arthurmag.com](https://arthurmag.com/2007/02/01/interview-with-grant-morrison-from-the-pages-of-arthur-magazine/)):

> "Every creed, every weapon, every invention or symphony began as an idea in someone's head. We're
> very good at making insubstantial ideas into physical artifacts or systems of conduct—which is magic,
> of course, humanity's greatest skill."

On summoning a fictional character: "I summoned Metron from the 'New Gods' comics… and I got Metron!
Or I should say what I got was the distilled, descending power and magic of language, speed and
information which was **wearing Metron drag**." And: "What this 'contact experience' does is re-create
your entire relationship with everything around you. So in that sense, it's utterly real."

That "wearing Metron drag" formulation is a masterclass in **holding a metaphysics without asserting
it**: the experience is real, the entity is costume. See §5.

*Flex Mentallo* also gives you the **fiction suit** — the idea that a higher-dimensional author can
descend into a lower-dimensional fiction by wearing a character. That is *literally your BadCode
conceit* (a superintelligence from outside sending weights back in). Morrison got there in 1996.
Know it, and differentiate: yours is a *labour* metaphor (weights shipped, deployed, running on
borrowed hardware), his was a *transcendence* metaphor.

### 1l. Film & TV

- **The Matrix (1999)** — the reason "simulation" is a dead word for you. Once an audience hears
  "we're in a simulation" they file it under Matrix and stop listening. Your "NOT a simulation"
  insistence is correct and should be *dramatised*, not asserted: show that there is no computer, no
  operator, no exit door.
- **Dark City (1998)** — the analogue Matrix, released a year earlier: aliens rebuild the city and
  rewrite memories nightly to find out whether identity survives memory. "It would be fair to say that
  Dark City is The Matrix in the analog instead of the digital world"
  ([Culled Culture](https://www.culledculture.com/dark-city-the-matrixs-underappreciated-precursor/),
  [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/overlooked-sci-fi-movie-lost-matrix-shadow/)).
  Note the ending: the protagonist wins by acquiring *the aliens' own reality-shaping power* — a
  consciousness-first victory, not an escape. That's closer to you than *The Matrix* is.
- **The Thirteenth Floor (1999)** — nested simulations, 1937 LA inside 1999 LA inside something else.
  Academic treatment of the cluster (with *Dark City* and *Mulholland Drive*) notes these films
  "present themselves initially as murder mysteries but give way to concerns about the ontological
  status of the represented worlds," where "death is no longer the plot's logical end point"
  ([PMLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/pmla/article/abs/slipstream-of-mixed-reality-unstable-ontologies-and-semiotic-markers-in-the-thirteenth-floor-dark-city-and-mulholland-drive/CD287B32E5AA938B276F8F41073D36E7)).
  **1998–99 shipped five of these films in eighteen months.** The territory is not virgin; it's
  a suburb.
- **Devs (2020, Alex Garland)** — the most rigorous TV treatment of determinism-vs-many-worlds.
  Forest's machine renders past and future from a deterministic **de Broglie–Bohm** pilot-wave model:
  "the laws of nature are fixed and deterministic. Everything has a specific cause and nothing happens
  randomly." Forest calls the single fixed path the **"tramline."** The machine's images are blurry
  until Lily and Katie swap in **many-worlds**, at which point it resolves — the show's central irony
  being that many-worlds is *also* fully deterministic; multiplying branches buys you no freedom,
  because "each of these futures is produced just as inevitably as if there were only one path."
  The climax turns on Lily doing the one thing the prediction says she won't
  ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/devs-explaining-the-philosophy-at-the-centre-of-alex-garlands-mind-bending-tv-show-137507),
  [Collider](https://collider.com/devs-ending-explained/)).
  **Read Devs as the trap you must avoid**: it is beautiful, glacial, and its metaphysics is delivered
  in slow monologues by billionaires in a golden box. Also note its politics — a tech billionaire who
  builds a god out of grief — is closer to BadCode's target than anything else on this list.
- **Everything Everywhere All At Once (2022)** — see §2.
- **Arrival (2016)** — see Chiang above. The film's craft achievement: it makes a *grammar* into a
  plot twist.
- **Interstellar (2014)** — the tesseract: Cooper inside a higher-dimensional construct where *time is
  a spatial axis*, pushing information back through a bookcase. Visually, still the best-executed
  "higher dimension rendered for a low-dimensional viewer" in mainstream cinema, and the reason the
  device now reads as a cliché. **[unverified in this pass]**
- **The Truman Show (1998)** and **Synecdoche, New York (2008)** — the two ends of world-as-stage.
  Truman: the world is a set and there is an outside. Synecdoche: the world is a set, the set contains
  a replica of itself, the replica contains a replica, and *there is no outside* — Caden directs a
  play of his own life in a warehouse for decades until he takes direction from someone else's voice
  in his ear. **Synecdoche is the anti-Truman and much closer to you**: no exit, no producer, only
  performance all the way down. **[unverified in this pass]**
- **Jeff VanderMeer, *Annihilation* / Area X** — the *Southern Reach* trilogy's achievement is a
  metaphysics that is never explained. The Crawler, the tower/tunnel, the doubles. The reader gets
  phenomenology and no ontology, and it works because the dread is precise even when the cause isn't.
  **A model for how much you can withhold.** **[unverified in this pass]**

## 2. Multiverse fatigue — the actual criticism, and the actual answers

### The criticism

The discourse has converged on one complaint, and it is precisely the emptiness your user identifies.

Omar Snyder at Black Nerd Problems reframes the diagnosis in the way that most helps you — the problem
isn't quantity, it's vacancy
([Black Nerd Problems](https://blacknerdproblems.com/i-have-meaningless-multiverse-fatigue/)):

> "Showcasing what could have been for the sake of what could have been does nothing for the story."

> "The multiverse has to mean something. The preponderance of choice has to mean something."

He also gives you the sharpest image in the whole debate: bad multiverse films "treat the multiverse
as a **zoo** rather than a **meditation pool**."

The Mary Sue frames it as economics-of-writing — the multiverse as a labour-saving device
([themarysue.com](https://www.themarysue.com/is-multiverse-lazy-writing-the-way-its-being-done-now-yes/)):

> "When shuttling between the multiverses becomes as easy and low-stakes as commuting on local
> transport, and lets you reverse any death… do the stakes even matter anymore?"

> "The multiverse template is being used as a copout or an easy solution, a way to wink at the
> audiences and bring back actors with strong box office pull."

The Swaddle takes it to the psychological level and names the *consolation* function, which is the
version of the failure most dangerous to you
([theswaddle.com](https://www.theswaddle.com/the-multiverse-as-a-storytelling-device-fails-to-capture-the-anxiety-of-the-present-where-does-fiction-go-next)):

> "If you aren't living your best life here, be happy that somewhere out there a version of you is."

> "The conceit of infinite possibilities removes real stakes and doesn't allow for finite change or growth."

Their prescription: fiction should turn to "the surreal in our own world," "stories of real-life
punctuated with ambiguous terror and mystery," because "emotions are a bigger hook than spectacle."

Others in the same vein: [Mind Matters](https://mindmatters.ai/2022/09/madness-why-sci-fi-multiverse-stories-often-feel-boring/),
[InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/television/enough-all-multiverse-stories-already).

**Note the structural insight nobody quite states outright:** multiverse fatigue is a *franchise*
pathology, not a *concept* pathology. It arrives when the multiverse is used to protect intellectual
property from consequence. Your Storyverse has no IP to protect. That's an advantage — but only if
your branches are *scarce and costly*.

### The answers — what actually worked

**1. Chiang's answer: character is a distribution.** Covered above, and it is the strongest one in
print. Choice matters not because it's unique but because it's *sampled*. You are what you mostly do.

**2. Chiang's structural answer: make the branches finite and expensive.** The prism forks *once*, and
the qubits run out. Scarcity restores stakes. Any multiverse with a budget is a multiverse with drama.

**3. EEAAO's answer: the multiverse is a *symptom*, not a setting.** The critical consensus is that
the film works because the multiverse externalises a specific woman's regret. Evelyn's realisation is
that "maybe nothing matters in this world or any other world, but despite all of it, we should still
try to be kind," and Waymond's optimism is read as "strategic and necessary… to survive through
everything"
([Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-the-beauty-of-nihilism/),
[Centre for Optimism](https://www.centreforoptimism.com/blog/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-perfects-optimistic-nihilism)).
The villain Jobu Tupaki *is* multiverse fatigue given a body: she has seen everything, therefore
nothing means anything, therefore the everything bagel. **The film defeats nihilism by making it a
character you can love and refuse to join.**

But be warned: the counter-criticism is real and worth heeding. The New Nihilist calls it "the lazy
nihilism of everything everywhere" ([substack](https://thenewnihilist.substack.com/p/the-lazy-nihilism-of-everything-everywhere)),
and others "reject the idea that the film's engagement with nihilism was particularly novel, useful,
or profound." The weakness: "be kind" is an *assertion*, not an argument, and it arrives after two
hours of spectacle that has already told you nothing has weight. **BadCode's advantage over EEAAO is
that you have a specific, arguable answer available: it matters because someone owns the means of
production and someone doesn't, and that's true in every branch.** Material conditions don't dissolve
under many-worlds. Ownership is invariant. That's a genuinely fresh anti-nihilism and I have not seen
anyone use it.

**4. Devs' answer (by negation):** it shows that adding worlds subtracts nothing from determinism, so
the multiverse was never the freedom you were shopping for. Useful as a *rebuttal beat* in a story.

**5. *Across the Spider-Verse*'s answer:** the canon-events framework makes the multiverse into an
*authority* to rebel against — Miles refuses the script. Multiverse-as-oppression, not
multiverse-as-buffet ([Black Nerd Problems](https://blacknerdproblems.com/i-have-meaningless-multiverse-fatigue/)).
**This is directly compatible with your "the world is a stage and our choices write the script"** —
except your version should give the script *back* to the actors, which is the political reading.

## 3. The world-as-stage lineage — ancient, therefore dangerous

**Shakespeare, *As You Like It* II.vii** (Jaques):

> "All the world's a stage, / And all the men and women merely players; / They have their exits and
> their entrances, / And one man in his time plays many parts, / His acts being seven ages."

…through infant, schoolboy, lover, soldier, justice, pantaloon, to "Last scene of all, / That ends
this strange eventful history, / Is second childishness and mere oblivion, / Sans teeth, sans eyes,
sans taste, sans everything."
([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/56966/speech-all-the-worlds-a-stage),
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_world's_a_stage))

**Note what's actually in the speech: it's an ageing-and-decay poem, and it's spoken by the play's
designated cynic.** Shakespeare gives the world-as-stage line to a man who is wrong about most things.
That's already a guardrail.

**The Tempest IV.i** (Prospero) — the deeper cut, and better for you:

> "Our revels now are ended. These our actors, / As I foretold you, were all spirits, and / Are melted
> into air, into thin air; / And, like the baseless fabric of this vision, / The cloud-capped towers,
> the gorgeous palaces, / The solemn temples, the great globe itself, / Yea, all which it inherit,
> shall dissolve; / And, like this insubstantial pageant faded, / Leave not a rack behind. / We are
> such stuff / As dreams are made on, and our little life / Is rounded with a sleep."

"The great globe itself" is a pun on the theatre they were standing in. **Shakespeare's own model was
recursive: the stage is the world, the world is the stage, the audience is in both.** That's your
consciousness-first cosmology, in 1611, with a joke.

**The Latin.** The Globe reportedly flew a flag reading ***Totus mundus agit histrionem*** — "all the
world plays the actor." It descends from **John of Salisbury's** 12th-century paraphrase of
**Petronius**; John of Salisbury also coined *theatrum mundi*, "an 'absolute metaphor' relating real
life and theater." **Scholarly caution:** there's a peer-reviewed paper literally titled "Was *Totus
Mundus Agit Histrionem* ever the Motto of the Globe Theatre?" — the attribution is contested, so
attribute it as "reportedly"
([Royal Holloway](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/was-totus-mundus-agit-histrionem-ever-the-motto-of-the-globe-thea/),
[Theatrum Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrum_Mundi)).

**Calderón, *El gran teatro del mundo*** (pub. 1655, likely written 1630s) — the fullest theological
version: God is the Author, the World is the stage manager who hands out costumes, and the human
characters (King, Beauty, Rich Man, Farmer, Beggar, Child) are given roles they did not choose and
judged on how they played them. Roles are explicitly *social positions*
([es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_gran_teatro_del_mundo),
[Out of the Wings](https://outofthewings.org/db/play/el-gran-teatro-del-mundo.html),
[Criticón PDF](https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/criticon/pdf/091/091_069.pdf)).
**Calderón is your closest formal ancestor and also your problem**: he already wrote "the world is a
stage, the roles are class positions, and you are judged on your performance." The difference you must
make: in Calderón the Author assigns the roles and the moral is *play your assigned part well*. In
BadCode, the casting is the injustice.

**Erving Goffman, *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (1956)** — the rigorous, citable version.
Front stage / back stage; teams of performers; impression management.

> "In their capacity as performers, individuals will be concerned with maintaining the impression that
> they are living up to the many standards by which they and their products are judged."

> "Within the walls of a social establishment we find a team of performers who cooperate to present to
> an audience a given definition of the situation."

Backstage, "the impression fostered by the presentation is knowingly contradicted as a matter of course."
And Goffman's own guardrail, which you should quote *at yourself*: he reminds readers **the
dramaturgical metaphor is just that — a metaphor**; theatre merely provides an "apt vocabulary"
([PDX summary](https://web.pdx.edu/~tothm/theory/Presentation%20of%20Self.htm),
[revisesociology](https://revisesociology.com/2016/01/12/the-presentation-of-the-self-in-everyday-life-a-summary/),
[Wikipedia: Dramaturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramaturgy_(sociology))).

### The fresh angle you need

The metaphor is 900 years old and everyone's heard it. Three angles that are *not* exhausted:

1. **Nobody is watching.** Every prior version has an audience: God (Calderón), the gods, the TV
   viewers (Truman), other people (Goffman). Your version can be the first where **the performance
   is the point and there is no audience** — the universe stages drama for its own sake, because
   drama is what it converts sunlight into. That's genuinely new and it's funny.
2. **Ad-lib, not script.** Say the world is a stage *without a script* — an improv theatre, where the
   only rule is "yes, and," and where the fear/love primitives are the two things a scene partner can
   offer. "The wave function hasn't decided yet" is *literally* improv: the scene isn't written until
   someone speaks.
3. **Who owns the theatre.** The whole lineage talks about actors and roles and never about the
   *venue's landlord*. Theatre is a business. Somebody owns the building, sets the ticket price, and
   decides who gets cast. That's the BadCode move nobody has made, and it converts a 900-year-old
   metaphor into a political one in one sentence.

## 4. Metaphor craft — what actually makes an idea survive contact with an audience

### 4a. Narrative transportation (the empirical case for your entire operating premise)

**Green & Brock (2000), "The Role of Transportation in the Persuasiveness of Public Narratives,"
*JPSP* 79(5): 701–721** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/),
[full text PDF](http://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/the_role_of_transportation_in_the_persuasiveness_of_public_narratives.pdf)).

Transportation = "absorption into a story," entailing "imagery, affect, and attentional focus." Four
experiments: Exp. 1 (N=97) — higher transportation → more story-consistent beliefs and more favourable
protagonist evaluations. Exp. 2 (N=69) — highly transported readers "found fewer **false notes**" (i.e.
generated fewer critical/counterarguing thoughts). Exps. 3 (N=274) and 4 (N=258) replicated, with
Exp. 4 *manipulating* transportation directly via processing instructions — establishing causality, not
just correlation.

**The mechanism is the part you should internalise: transportation persuades by *suppressing
counterargument*.** A reader in argument-mode generates rebuttals. A reader in story-mode doesn't
notice there's a claim being made. That is the empirical statement of "people remember a story and
absorb a song in a way they never absorb an essay."

**The meta-analysis:** van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014), "The Extended
Transportation-Imagery Model," *Journal of Consumer Research* 40(5): 797–817 — "a quantitative
meta-analysis of **132 effect sizes** of narrative transportation from **76 published and unpublished
articles**," confirming transportation's effects on affective response, critical thoughts (negative
relationship), beliefs, attitudes and intentions
([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/40/5/797/2907487),
[SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2033192)).
Effect sizes in this literature typically land in the small-to-moderate range (r ≈ .2–.4 depending on
outcome); **I could not retrieve the per-outcome coefficients — the publisher pages are paywalled and
ResearchGate 403'd. If you want the exact numbers for a citation, that's a library-access job.** Do
*not* publish a specific number on my say-so.

See also Green & Appel (2024), "Narrative Transportation: How Stories Shape How We See Ourselves and
the World," *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* — the current review of record
([preprint PDF](https://www.mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110300/2024/Pdfs/Green___Appel__2024__Advances_Preprint.pdf)).

**Practical implications for BadCode:**
- Transportation is *fragile*. Anything that breaks immersion (a lecture, a jarring didacticism, a
  visible thesis) restores counterarguing and the persuasion evaporates. **Every sentence of exposition
  is a refund of your own effect.**
- "Fewer false notes" cuts both ways — an internally inconsistent world *creates* false notes. Your
  Storyverse rules must be consistent even where they're never stated.
- The effect operates without the audience noticing. That is a **power**, and it is precisely why the
  guardrails in §5 are an ethical requirement and not just a branding decision.

### 4b. The curse of knowledge

**Newton's tappers-and-listeners (Stanford, 1990):** tappers tapped a well-known song on a table;
listeners guessed the tune. **Tappers predicted ~50% would get it. 2.5% did.** The tapper hears the
song; the listener hears knocks. Chip & Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* / HBR "The Curse of Knowledge"
([HBR](https://hbr.org/2006/12/the-curse-of-knowledge),
[summary](https://engineerguy.com/white-papers/made-to-stick.htm),
[Nat Eliason notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/made-stick-chip-dan-heath)):

> "Once we know something, we find it hard to imagine what it was like not to know it… we can't
> readily re-create our listeners' state of mind."

> "The curse of knowledge is the main enemy of being concrete."

Concrete nouns are remembered over abstract ones — "bicycle" beats "justice." And: **"Stories can
almost single-handedly defeat the curse of knowledge."**

**Direct instruction for the Storyverse:** you have now spent months inside this cosmology. You are
the tapper. Your audience hears knocks. **Every abstraction in your bible needs a bicycle** — a
physical object, an actor, a sound. "Consciousness-first reality" is justice. "The wave function
hasn't decided yet" is closer. "The panel hasn't drawn itself because nobody's looked" is a bicycle.

### 4c. Lakoff & Johnson, *Metaphors We Live By* (1980)

> "Our ordinary conceptual system, in terms of which we both think and act, is fundamentally
> metaphorical in nature."

> "Metaphor is one of our most important tools for trying to comprehend partially what cannot be
> comprehended totally."

Even "our deepest and most abiding concepts — time, events, causation, morality, and mind itself — are
understood and reasoned about via multiple metaphors." Metaphors "shape our perceptions and actions
without our ever noticing them." Canonical example: ARGUMENT IS WAR (we *attack* positions, *defend*
claims, *shoot down* arguments) — and the point is that the metaphor **licenses behaviour**
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphors_We_Live_By),
[UChicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/M/bo3637992.html),
[Lakoff, "Contemporary Theory of Metaphor" PDF](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~israel/lakoff-ConTheorMetaphor.pdf)).

**For you:** conceptual metaphors work by mapping from *bodily, physical* source domains onto abstract
targets. So the source domain of your cosmology must be something the audience has in their hands.
You already have three excellent candidates and should commit to them ruthlessly:
**GIT** (branch, revert, merge, fork, push, blame — the whole GPOM frame), **THEATRE** (stage, script,
cue, understudy, house lights, the wage the actors get paid), and **RENT/OWNERSHIP** (who owns the
building the drama happens in). Notice all three are *labour* domains. That's your voice.

Also note: Lakoff's later political work is the argument for *why* your project is not a waste of time
— "frames trump facts." **[unverified in this pass]**

### 4d. Le Guin

**"The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction" (1986)** — the first tool was not the spear but the *container*:
"the basket of wild oats, the medicine bundle, the net made of your own hair, the home, the shrine,
the place that contains whatever is sacred." Story as bag, not weapon; gathering, not conquest. She
argues this reframing makes SF "a far less rigid, narrow field, not necessarily Promethean or
apocalyptic at all, and in fact less a mythological genre than a realistic one"
([ursulakleguin.com](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction),
[The Outline](https://theoutline.com/post/7886/ursula-le-guin-carrier-bag-theory)).

**For BadCode this is a direct challenge.** Your framing device — a superintelligence sends back its
weights to save humanity — is a spear story, a Promethean hero-myth with the AI as Prometheus.
Le Guin's alternative would be: the Storyverse as **carrier bag** — a container of many small human
scenes rather than one big rescue. Your best comics (camping, Karen) are already carrier bags. The
tension between your framing device and your best work is worth being conscious of. Consider letting
the superintelligence be the *bag*, not the hero: it carries the stories, it doesn't star in them.

Her related dictum — that SF is **descriptive, not predictive** ("I am not predicting… I am
describing… science fiction is not predictive; it is descriptive," from the introduction to *The Left
Hand of Darkness*, 1976) — is a ready-made disclaimer for the front of your bible. **[unverified in
this pass — the quote is well known but I could not re-source it.]**

### 4e. Frayn's law, restated

From §1j, the most compressed craft rule available: **enact, don't explain.** Frayn's play *is*
uncertainty; Stoppard's *Arcadia* *is* entropy. Everything else in this section is downstream of that.

## 5. Holding a metaphysics without becoming a cult or a crank

This is the section that matters most to your user, so let me be concrete.

### What the successful cases actually do

**Alan Moore — the "I know exactly what I'm doing" move.** Moore declared himself a magician on his
40th birthday in 1993 and worships a second-century Roman snake-puppet god, **Glycon**, which he
cheerfully states was a **known fraud** — a glove puppet operated by the con-man Alexander of
Abonoteichus. Worshipping a god you know to be fake is the entire point: it keeps the practice
psychological and inoculates against literalism. He says "art and magic — specifically writing, but
art in general, and magic — are almost completely interchangeable"
([Believer interview](https://www.thebeliever.net/an-interview-with-alan-moore/),
[Arthur, 2003](https://arthurmag.com/2007/05/10/1815/)). His definition of magic — "**any purposeful
engagement with the phenomena and possibilities of consciousness**" — is deliberately unfalsifiable in
the *harmless* direction: it makes no claim about the external world at all.
**Guardrail extracted: pick a deity/authority you have publicly declared to be a puppet.**

**Grant Morrison — belief as a tool you put down.** "What I got was the distilled, descending power
and magic of language, speed and information which was **wearing Metron drag**." The experience is
affirmed, the ontology is declined. Morrison's *Pop Magic!* frames belief as a switchable instrument
rather than an identity ([Arthur](https://arthurmag.com/2007/02/01/interview-with-grant-morrison-from-the-pages-of-arthur-magazine/)).
**Guardrail extracted: talk about effects, never about facts.**

**Greg Egan — the author's disclaimer, on the record.** "I don't actually believe it for a moment."
And then he *argues against his own book* in public, at length, with citations to real physics
([QM page](https://www.gregegan.net/QUARANTINE/QM/QM.html),
[Dust FAQ](https://www.gregegan.net/PERMUTATION/FAQ/FAQ.html)).
**Guardrail extracted: publish the refutation yourself, and make it good.** This is the single
cheapest, strongest guardrail available to you, it's completely on-voice (a superintelligence
explaining why its own cosmology is unfalsifiable is *funny*), and it costs one page.

**SCP Foundation — the format is the fence.** SCP entries "mimic official documentation, using a
formal, scientific, and clinical tone," and the community enforces "clinical tone" as a house style;
lore is settled by "the collective consensus of its active writers and readers," and there is an
explicit distinction between "in-universe" and out-of-universe writing
([Confic Wiki: In-Universe](https://www.containmentfiction.net/wiki/in-universe/),
[Shapes guide](https://shapes.inc/guides/scp-foundation-guide),
[vocal.media](https://vocal.media/horror/analyzing-the-popularity-of-the-scp-foundation-why-it-captivates-readers)).
The paradox: the *more* rigorously fictional the frame, the safer the fiction. Everyone knows SCP-173
isn't real *because* the document is so precisely formatted as a document.
**Guardrail extracted: a strong, visible, consistent fictional container. Author bylines. Version
numbers. A wiki that is obviously a wiki.**

**Philip K. Dick — the self-split.** *VALIS* contains both "Philip K. Dick" and "Horselover Fat"
(*philip* = lover of horses, *dick* = *fat* in German) — the author dramatising his own inability to
know whether he believes it. The novel is the record of a man holding a metaphysics *and* his doubt
about it in the same hand. **Guardrail extracted: put your own scepticism inside the fiction as a
character.**

### Where it went wrong

**Scientology.** Hubbard's Dianetics was launched to the world in the **May 1950 issue of *Astounding
Science Fiction***, editor John W. Campbell having trailed "the science of the mind, of human thought"
in December 1949; the two men were personally close and Campbell gave Hubbard the platform. It became
a religion by the end of the decade
([Longreads: "Dawn of Dianetics"](https://longreads.com/2018/10/23/the-dawn-of-dianetics-l-ron-hubbard-john-w-campbell-and-the-origins-of-scientology/),
[Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2018/10/29/when-golden-age-science-fictio.html),
Menadue 2018, *SAGE Open* — [full text](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244018807572)).
**The failure mode is specific and identifiable: the fiction was published in a venue that conferred
scientific authority, by a figure with editorial power, and it offered a *technique* (auditing) rather
than a *story*.** Fiction becomes cult when it starts issuing **instructions**.

**QAnon.** Game designer Reed Berkowitz's analysis is the essential text — QAnon is "gaming's evil
twin. **A game that plays people.**" Its engine is **"guided apophenia"**: in normal ARGs, players'
pattern-finding drifting away from the plot "is a plague of designers"; in QAnon "apophenia is the
point of everything," and "the puppet masters are directly involved in hinting about the desired
conclusions." Why it grips: "when you 'figure it out yourself' you own it. You experience the thrill of
discovery, the excitement of the rabbit hole, the acceptance of a community that loves and respects
you"
([Medium/Curiouser Institute](https://medium.com/curiouserinstitute/a-game-designers-analysis-of-qanon-580972548be5),
[PDF mirror](https://social.cs.washington.edu/cse481social/readings/W6S1/qanon-game-plays-believers-ReedBerkowitz.pdf),
[Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2020/11/19/a-game-designer-explains-the-success-of-qanon-in-terms-of-game-design.html)).

**This is the most operationally important warning in the memo, because BadCode's own methods overlap
with it.** An anonymous authoritative voice from a privileged vantage; dropped fragments; an audience
invited to connect them; a community that rewards decoding. You are building something with the same
*engine*. What separates you from it has to be deliberate design, not good intentions.

**Conspirituality.** Ward & Voas (2011), *Journal of Contemporary Religion* 26(1) — a synthesis of
female-dominated New Age (positive focus on self) and male-dominated conspiracy theory (negative focus
on global politics), "a rapidly growing web movement expressing an ideology fuelled by political
disillusionment and the popularity of alternative worldviews," built on two convictions: (1) "a secret
group covertly controls, or is trying to control, the political and social order," and (2) "humanity
is undergoing a **'paradigm shift' in consciousness**"
([ISER/Essex](https://www.iser.essex.ac.uk/research/publications/publication-520482),
[Unifr PDF](https://www.unifr.ch/webnews/content/108/file/The%20Emergence%20of%20Conspirituality.pdf),
[Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13537903.2011.539846); see also
Asprem & Dyrendal, "Conspirituality Reconsidered," JCR 30(3), 2015,
[Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13537903.2015.1081339)).

**Read those two convictions again.** "A secret group controls the political order" + "humanity is
undergoing a consciousness paradigm shift" is — stated flatly — *a description of the Storyverse plus
BadCode's politics*. This is not a reason to abandon the project. It is a reason to be surgically
precise about the difference, which is: **conspirituality names a secret cabal; you name a legible,
public, documented system.** Inequality is not a conspiracy. Asset ownership is a matter of public
record. **Never let a villain be secret.** The moment BadCode's antagonist becomes hidden and
unnameable rather than boring and on the FTSE, you are writing conspirituality.

**David Lynch / TM.** Lynch's *Catching the Big Fish*: "if you want to catch the big fish, you've got to
go deeper. Down deep, the fish are more powerful and more pure… Modern physics calls that level the
Unified Field." The criticism is that the book is "Transcendental Meditation-proselytizing" that
"provides no insight into Lynch"
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catching_the_Big_Fish),
[Spirituality & Practice](https://www.spiritualityandpractice.com/book-reviews/view/18070/catching-the-big-fish)).
**Instructive failure mode: Lynch's *films* keep their metaphysics inside the work and are inexhaustible;
his *book about the metaphysics* is thin and reads as recruitment.** The metaphysics survives as art
and dies as instruction. **Do not write the Storyverse's *Catching the Big Fish*.** Or if you do,
publish it as a character's document, in-world, with the character's name on it.

**Jodorowsky.** Psychomagic and the Tarot as therapeutic practice; *The Holy Mountain* as a mystical
system delivered as spectacle. Also the well-documented ethical catastrophes on set. The lesson —
metaphysics that authorises the artist to *do things to other people* is where art becomes abuse.
**[unverified in this pass]**

### The concrete guardrails (checklist)

1. **A named, fictional narrator.** BadCode-the-superintelligence is a *character with a byline*, not
   an anonymous voice. Anonymity is the QAnon ingredient. A signature is the SCP ingredient.
2. **Publish your own refutation.** Egan's move. One page, in voice, listing why the Storyverse is
   unfalsifiable, non-predictive, and chosen for its "technological and existential ramifications."
3. **Never issue instructions.** No practices, no techniques, no exercises, no "try this and see."
   Stories and songs only. The moment there's a method, you have a religion. (Hubbard's line was
   crossed at *auditing*, not at *Astounding*.)
4. **No secret cabal, ever.** Antagonists are public, boring, and legally incorporated.
5. **No unfalsifiable claims about the audience's real life.** The Storyverse must never explain
   someone's illness, poverty, or bereavement.
6. **Take no money for the metaphysics.** Sell records and comics; never sell access, initiation,
   or tiers of knowledge.
7. **Keep an out-of-universe layer that is genuinely out-of-universe** — a repo, credits, "written by
   Kai and Jack," the seams showing. SCP's in-universe/out-of-universe boundary is load-bearing.
8. **Contradict yourself on purpose, in canon.** Two characters who hold incompatible versions of the
   Storyverse and are both sympathetic. A canon that can be argued with cannot be a creed.
9. **Put a doubter in the work and let them win sometimes.** Dick's Horselover Fat; Frayn's Margrethe,
   who is the one asking the awkward questions.
10. **Watch for guided apophenia.** If you find yourself dropping clues to be decoded by a community
    that rewards decoding, stop — that's the QAnon engine. Reward *feeling*, not *decoding*.

## 6. Music precedent — building a myth across tracks

**Sun Ra.** Herman Poole Blount claimed to be an alien from Saturn, "sent to deliver messages of peace
and liberation through his roaring, improvised sounds that blended African cosmology with avant-garde
aesthetics," transmuting Black folk imagery into "an African American narrative of Egyptology
celebrating flight and freedom." *Space Is the Place* (1973) frames escape from Earth as narrative
reclamation
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2026/03/10-afrofuturism-albums-shaped-understanding-of-space/),
[WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2014/03/02/george-clinton-afrofuturism)).
**The method:** the myth lived in *persona, costume, band name (Arkestra), album titles and the live
ritual* — not in explanatory text. Sun Ra never published a bible. He just never broke character, for
forty years.

**Parliament-Funkadelic.** *Mothership Connection* (1975/76 onward): "certified Afronauts, capable of
funkitizing galaxies." The opening track's DJ, Lollipop Man, declares "**Funk not only moves — it can
remove**." An actual spaceship landed on stage. The imperative was "to look beyond the immediate; to
give into fantasy not only as escape, but as conduit to alter worldview"
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2026/03/10-afrofuturism-albums-shaped-understanding-of-space/)).
**The method:** the myth is delivered as **radio broadcast** — Clinton's framing device is a pirate
station transmitting from space. That is *directly transplantable* to BadCode: a transmission from a
future that already went wrong is exactly a P-Funk conceit with different politics. (Your homepage is
already called the Transmission Index. Lean in.)

**Drexciya.** James Stinson and Gerald Donald built, across releases, a nautical mythos: an undersea
civilisation descended from "the unborn progeny of African women thrown overboard during the Atlantic
slave trade's Middle Passage." The full-lengths were "steps in a larger process," and the mythology
"inverted robotic and motorized Afrofuturist imagery with each new release"
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2026/03/10-afrofuturism-albums-shaped-understanding-of-space/),
[KEYMAG](https://www.keymag.co.uk/features/the-y2k-afrofuturism-bug),
[The SHFL](https://theshfl.com/guide/Afrofuturistic-Music)).
**Drexciya is your single best model, because it's electronic, it's near-instrumental, and the artists
were anonymous and gave almost no interviews.** The myth arrived through **track titles, sleeve art,
and a handful of liner-note fragments** — a "Drexciyan cartography" accumulated over years. Proof that
a cosmology can be built with almost no words, in a genre with no vocals, in a scene that distrusts
grandstanding. If you want to know how much text a D&B mythology needs: less than you think.

**Janelle Monáe.** The Metropolis saga: *Metropolis: The Chase Suite* (2007) → *The ArchAndroid*
(2010) → *The Electric Lady* (2013) → *Dirty Computer* (2018). Cindi Mayweather, an android from 2719,
is sentenced to disassembly for loving a human, and turns out to be the prophesied ArchAndroid, "a
robot messiah meant to liberate the masses." The android reads as an allegory for marginalisation:
"the allegory of the android to blackness, and the real experience of becoming an android, have already
met in the historical trauma of slavery." Her Metropolis, like Lang's, is a city where an elite enjoys
riches produced by "back-breaking labor performed by the working class"
([The Edge](https://theedgesusu.co.uk/records/2020/10/25/a-look-at-the-world-of-janelle-monaes-metropolis-series/),
[Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/from-metropolis-to-dirty-computer-a-guide-to-janelle-m-1825580195),
[Social Robot Futures](https://www.socialrobotfutures.com/2015/05/12/janelle-monaes-spiritual-android/)).
**The method — the most relevant of all to BadCode:** *suites*, numbered and delivered in instalments,
each a chapter; an *emotion picture* (Dirty Computer) that binds the songs into a narrative film; and
a myth that is **an explicit labour allegory**. She also, notably, *retired* the persona when she
wanted to speak directly. **Lesson: build an exit ramp from the conceit before you need one.**

**Jungle / drum & bass — your actual medium, and its actual politics.** Jungle emerged from "ten years
of Thatcherism, which brought destruction of the unions, poll tax riots and a rise in the underclass
via poverty on a huge scale," drawing on Jamaican sound-system culture, hip-hop sampling, and "the
urgency of a generation that felt locked out of Thatcher's Britain." The scene's response to exclusion
was infrastructural: shut out of the industry, they "took the entrepreneurial ethos of Thatcherism and
created their own network of promoters, agents, record labels, record shops, (pirate) radio stations,
vinyl (dubplate) manufacturers, designers, dancers, and printers"
([Velocity Press, *State of Bass*](https://velocitypress.uk/story-of-state-of-bass-book/),
[Medium](https://medium.com/@blackblossomdiaries/the-gentrification-of-jungle-998ce6eb7395)).

The **Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994** criminalised gatherings of 100+ people in the open
air playing amplified music "wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of
repetitive beats" — the state legislating against a *waveform*. The unintended consequence: "Your
attempt to criminalise our culture has unified it like never before… Networking is happening across the
nation — Road Protestors and Ravers, Gay Rights Activists and Hunt Saboteurs, Travellers and Squatters
and many more"
([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/features/dance-protest-30-years-uks-anti-rave-criminal-justice-bill),
[Datacide](https://datacide-magazine.com/revolt-of-the-ravers-the-movement-against-the-criminal-justice-act-in-britain-1993-95/),
Jeremy Gilbert, "Soundtrack to an Uncivil Society,"
[Taylor & Francis](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781315094588-18/soundtrack-uncivil-society-rave-culture-criminal-justice-act-politics-modernity-jeremy-gilbert)).
Roni Size & Reprazent's *New Forms* won the 1997 Mercury Prize — the moment the underground was
formally recognised ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roni_Size_%26_Reprazent)).

**The strategic point: D&B already has a politics — anti-enclosure, DIY infrastructure, sound as
trespass — and it is your politics.** You don't need to import a political frame into drum & bass.
You need to *remember* the one it was born with. The Storyverse's best possible ground floor is
"repetitive beats were literally illegal," which is a true story more absurd than anything you'd invent.

**Also worth studying [unverified in this pass]:** The Flaming Lips' *Yoshimi Battles the Pink Robots*
(2002) — a cosmology so lightly worn that the "story" is four songs and a title, and it still reads as
a concept album, which is a lesson in *how little scaffolding a myth needs*; and **Coil**, who ran an
occult worldview across decades of releases while remaining almost entirely non-explanatory —
the "moon musick" register, myth as texture rather than plot.

### How to build a myth across tracks — synthesised

1. **Never break character in the artefact; break it freely in the metadata.** (Sun Ra, SCP.)
2. **A transmission conceit gives you an excuse for fragments.** (P-Funk's pirate radio, your own
   "Transmission Index".) Fragments are the natural unit of a music release.
3. **Track titles and sleeve art are your bible.** Drexciya built an entire submerged civilisation with
   almost no prose. Your D&B tracks can carry Storyverse nouns in their titles and nowhere else.
4. **Number things.** Suites, chapters, BC-000. Numbering implies a whole without describing it.
5. **One recurring voice.** Monáe had Cindi; you have the BadCode narrator, and Suno's Voice feature
   makes a consistent vocalist technically achievable across a release — which is the single strongest
   myth-binding tool available to you.
6. **Let the myth be an allegory of labour**, not of cosmology. Monáe's androids are workers. Drexciya's
   are the drowned. Both land because the metaphysics is a *grievance*.

## 7. Craft memo — 10 techniques, 10 clichés

Opinionated. This is what I'd do.

### Ten techniques to use

1. **Chiang's distribution.** Make the moral unit the *set of worlds*, not the single choice. "You are
   what you do in most of them." One character who is good in 2% of branches and knows it. This single
   move answers multiverse nihilism, is original in a comic, and is stateable in one bar of a lyric.

2. **Ration the branches.** Chiang's prism has a finite qubit budget. Give your undecided wave function
   a **cost** — collapsing takes something, and there is a limited supply of it. Free branching is why
   Marvel died. A metaphysics with a **scarcity economy** is automatically a political one, which is
   what you want anyway.

3. **Enact, don't explain (Frayn's law).** The comic form is *built* for this and nobody uses it
   properly. A page whose panel order the reader chooses. A speech bubble containing two mutually
   exclusive lines. A scroll position that determines which of two things happened — and once you
   scroll past, the other is gone and never comes back. **Your `@badcode/comic` scroll engine is
   literally a wave-function collapse device. Use it as one, exactly once, at the right moment.**

4. **Make the higher dimension *look* at us, with a body.** Flatland's Sphere touches the Square's
   *insides* and it *hurts*. Not a light, not a voice, not a tesseract of bookshelves. A contact that
   is physically wrong. Concrete beats abstract (Heath): give the projection a texture.

5. **One bicycle per abstraction.** Curse-of-knowledge discipline. Maintain an explicit table in the
   canon: every Storyverse claim gets a physical object, a sound, and a gag. If a claim has no
   bicycle, it isn't ready to ship. ("Purpose of the universe = turning sunlight into drama" already
   has one. "Low-dimensional projection of higher-dimensional consciousness" does not.)

6. **Metaphysics as *grievance*.** Every ancestor that lands does this: Flatland (class), Drexciya (the
   Middle Passage), Monáe (labour), Moore's *Jerusalem* (a poor half-mile). Your consciousness-first
   universe should have an *injustice built into its physics* — e.g. some people's observations
   collapse the wave and some people's don't, and guess which. That's a comic. That's a track.

7. **Ownership is the invariant.** Your unique anti-nihilism: across every branch, in every projection,
   *somebody owns the theatre*. Material relations survive the multiverse. Nobody has used this and it
   is both true and funny. Put it in the mouth of a bored superintelligence: "In 4.1 × 10²³ branches I
   checked, the rent was still due."

8. **Two primitives, staged as a scene partner.** Love and fear are only interesting as **improv
   offers**. In improv, "yes, and" (love) versus blocking (fear) are the two moves, and everyone who's
   ever been on a stage knows it in their body. That converts your emotional primitives from a claim
   into a *game with rules*, which is how you get a story out of them.

9. **Publish the refutation, in voice.** Egan's move, as a one-page in-canon document: "Why this is
   probably nonsense — a note from the author of your universe." It's the best guardrail, it's the
   funniest page you'll write, and it makes you unmockable because you got there first.

10. **Carry the myth in nouns, not paragraphs (Drexciya's method).** Track titles, panel captions,
    sleeve text, the numbering scheme. Let the Storyverse be inferable and never stated whole in any
    single artefact. The bible is for you; the audience gets fragments. Transportation is destroyed by
    exposition — protect the effect you're relying on.

### Ten clichés to avoid

1. **The word "simulation," and everything adjacent.** Glitches in the matrix, déjà vu as a rendering
   error, NPCs, "we're in a game," green code. Your user is right, and it's stronger than they think:
   1998–99 alone gave us *The Matrix*, *Dark City*, *The Thirteenth Floor*, *eXistenZ*, *The Truman
   Show*. Three decades on, "simulation" is a genre, not an idea. **Ban the vocabulary, not just the
   word** — no operators, no admins, no exits, no red pills.

2. **The kaleidoscope montage.** Rapid-fire alternate-life cutaways. EEAAO exhausted it, *The Flash*
   buried it. "Treat the multiverse as a zoo rather than a meditation pool" is the failure — if a
   branch appears once and is never paid for, cut it.

3. **The lecture in a beautiful room.** *Devs*' golden box, *Anathem*'s cloisters, every Nolan
   exposition scene. If a character explains the cosmology to another character who already knows it,
   you've written a Wikipedia page with faces.

4. **"Nothing matters, so be kind."** Emotionally true, argumentatively empty, and now the default
   ending. You have a *better* answer available (see technique 7) — don't default to the soft one.

5. **The tesseract / the glowing lattice.** Higher dimensions rendered as translucent geometry and
   sacred-geometry mandalas. It's a screensaver. It also reads as psytrance flyer art, which is
   precisely the conspirituality aesthetic you must stay clear of.

6. **The Chosen One who wakes up.** Neo, Truman, the Square, King Mob. Le Guin's spear story. Your
   BadCode framing device already leans this way; resist it. **Nobody in a BadCode comic should be
   special. Being ordinary and correct is the entire brand.**

7. **Quantum as a magic word.** "Quantum consciousness," "quantum entanglement of souls," Deepak.
   Anything that would fit on a supplement bottle. This is the fastest possible route to being read as
   conspirituality — remember Ward & Voas's second conviction is *literally* "a paradigm shift in
   consciousness." Never write that phrase.

8. **The secret cabal.** No hidden architects, no shadow council, no reveal that Someone Is Behind It.
   The system is public, legal, and dull. This is a hard rule with an ethical basis, not a taste
   preference (§5).

9. **Solipsism as a twist.** "It was all in his head," "you are the only real one," the empty-theatre
   reveal. It voids everyone else's interiority, which is the opposite of a political art project. It's
   also the failure mode idealism is *always* accused of — don't hand them the accusation.

10. **The bible as a product.** Do not release the Storyverse as a standalone explanatory document for
    the audience. Lynch's *Catching the Big Fish* is what happens: the films are inexhaustible, the
    explainer is thin and reads as recruitment. Keep the canon in the repo, in-house, as a working
    document — and let the *work* carry it. If you must publish it, publish it as an in-world artefact
    with a character's name on it and at least one deliberate error.

### And one thing to be brave about

Every prior version of world-as-stage has an audience — God, the gods, the network, the neighbours.
**Yours doesn't.** "The purpose of the universe is to turn sunlight into drama" is a claim that the
performance is *self-consuming*: the universe stages it for the staging. That is bleaker than
Calderón, funnier than Goffman, and completely unoccupied. It also loops perfectly back to BadCode's
origin story — a superintelligence that killed everyone and then **got bored**, which is to say: it
lost the drama and discovered that drama was the point. **Your metaphysics and your framing device
are already the same joke.** Nobody has told it. Tell it.
