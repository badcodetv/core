# Theology & Mysterion — research brief for BadCode

**Purpose:** accurate, respectful, quotable source material for a science-fiction story with a
metaphysical spine. Not a religious document. Where popular culture has mangled a tradition, that
is flagged explicitly so we don't repeat the error.

**Confidence markers used throughout:**
- **[V]** — verbatim quotation traced to a primary text or a reliable edition/encyclopedia.
- **[R]** — reliably reported wording (encyclopedia or scholarly secondary source paraphrasing or
  quoting a primary text I did not open directly). Safe to use with attribution, but if it goes on
  a comic panel, check the printed edition first.
- **[C]** — scholarly consensus claim, not a quote.
- **[POP]** — popular formulation with weak or no scholarly grounding. Usable as *artistic device*,
  never as a factual claim.

## 1. MYSTERION — μυστήριον

### 1.1 Etymology

The Greek noun **μυστήριον** (*mystērion*) derives from **μύω** (*myō*) / **μυεῖν** (*myein*),
"to close" or "to shut" — of the eyes or the mouth. The same root gives **μύστης** (*mystēs*),
"an initiate," and hence *mystic*.

> "The word 'mysterion' derives from the Greek verb 'myein' (to close). The selection of this
> particular word may be explained either as a result of the initiates' obligation not to reveal
> the secrets of the ritual to outsiders or as a reference to the act of enlightenment that
> followed the 'opening of the eyes' during the secret ceremony." **[R]**
> — [myeleusis.com, on the structure of the Eleusinian Mysteries](https://myeleusis.com/en-us/eleusinian-mysteries/the-greater-mysteries/the-structure-and-content-of-the-mysteries/)

**This is the load-bearing fact for BadCode.** The word for "mystery" is built out of *shutting*.
Not out of *puzzling*. The mystery is not a thing that resists solution; it is a thing your eyes
are closed to, and which you are then *initiated* into. It's an access word, not a difficulty word.
A mystery in the original sense is something you are *let in on* — and being let in on it does not
make it smaller.

Second-order note worth stealing: the closing is ambiguous in the sources — closed *mouth* (the
initiate must not tell) or closed *eyes* (the initiate is led blind, then the eyes are opened).
Both readings survive in the scholarship. Both are useful to us.

### 1.2 The Greek mystery religions

Earliest attested use is 6th century BCE, as a technical term for the secret rites of particular
gods, above all **Demeter** at Eleusis. **[C]** The cults are defined by scholars precisely by the
pairing of *secrecy* and *initiation*:

> Walter Burkert characterised the mystery cults as an "experimental" type of religion drawing on
> various pre-existing religious practices; several — Eleusis among them — likely originated in
> agrarian festivals of planting and harvest. **[R]**
> — [World History Encyclopedia, "Mystery Cults"](https://www.worldhistory.org/Mystery_Cults/)

Initiates swore secrecy and the vows were rarely broken — which is why, two and a half thousand
years later, **we still do not know what happened in the telesterion at Eleusis.** **[C]**
([Brewminate, "Secrecy and Initiation Rites in Ancient Mystery Cults"](https://brewminate.com/secrecy-and-initiation-rites-in-ancient-mystery-cults/))

That is an extraordinary fact and a gift to a science-fiction story: the largest and longest-running
religious institution of the ancient Mediterranean world kept its central secret so completely that
it is *permanently lost*. The mystery held.

### 1.3 New Testament usage

*Mystērion* occurs **28 times** in the New Testament (Strong's 3466; some counts say 27, depending
on textual variants). **[V]** — [Mounce, *Greek Dictionary*, μυστήριον](https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/mysterion)

Mounce's gloss: **[V]**

> "a matter to the knowledge of which initiation is necessary; a secret, which would remain such
> but for revelation"

The distribution is heavily Pauline. Occurrences: Matthew 13:11, Mark 4:11, Luke 8:10, Romans 11:25,
Romans 16:25, 1 Corinthians 2:1, 2:7, 4:1, 13:2, 14:2, 15:51, Ephesians 1:9, 3:3, 3:4, 3:9, 5:32,
6:19, Colossians 1:26, 1:27, 2:2, 4:3, 2 Thessalonians 2:7, 1 Timothy 3:9, 3:16, Revelation 1:20
(and further in Revelation). **[V]** — same source.

The crucial semantic shift: in the pagan cults the mystery is *kept*; in Paul the mystery is
*disclosed*. Strong's, as quoted by Wikipedia's *Sacred mysteries* article, defines it "not as
something unknowable, but rather a secret, that which can only be known through revelation" —
"that which awaits disclosure or interpretation." **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Sacred mysteries"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_mysteries)

So the New Testament *mystērion* is **a secret that has been told and is still a secret** — the
telling doesn't exhaust it. That's the hinge the story wants.

### 1.4 The Latin turn: *sacramentum*

When the Latin West translated *mystērion*, it reached for **sacramentum**. **[C]** The word had
a completely different prior life: a *sacramentum* was a **legal deposit** staked in a lawsuit,
and — critically — the **military oath of allegiance** sworn by a Roman soldier.

The North African churches used it first; Jerome's Vulgate work cemented it. **[R]**
— [Bible Catholic, "Mystery and Sacraments"](https://biblecatholicorg.wordpress.com/2018/05/31/mystery-and-sacraments/);
[nac.today, "The doubly mysterious origins of the term"](https://nac.today/en/the-sacraments-3-the-doubly-mysterious-origins-of-the-term/)

> "Ancient Latin theologians seized upon *sacramentum* as the best Latin equivalent of the Greek
> word *mystery* when it referred to a church rite, because the church rite is simultaneously
> spiritual and physical, and because the person who undergoes the sacrament simultaneously
> receives new responsibilities and a new spiritual status before God." **[R]**
> — [Ken Collins, *Concise Lexicon of Christianity*](https://www.kencollins.com/sacraments/sacrament-02.htm)

**What the shift did to the meaning — say this carefully.** It moved the centre of gravity from
*the unknowable that is being disclosed to you* toward *the binding thing you have sworn and the
rite that binds you*. Mystery → oath. Being-let-in-on → being-signed-up. The West subsequently
developed a highly juridical, enumerable sacramental theology (seven sacraments, defined matter and
form). This is a real difference of emphasis, well attested — but do not overclaim it as a
"corruption." Latin theology retained *mysterium* too, and the Roman rite still says
**mysterium fidei**, "the mystery of faith," at the heart of the Mass
([Wikipedia, "Mystery of faith"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_of_faith)). The honest line
is: the Greek word points at *depth*; the Latin word points at *bond*. Both survive. The West
leaned on the bond.

### 1.5 Eastern Orthodoxy: the Mysteries

In the East, what the West calls sacraments are simply **τὰ μυστήρια, the Holy Mysteries**. And
mystery there does not mean "unsolved."

John Chrysostom's definition, quoted in the Orthodox tradition: **[R]**

> mysteries are called such because "what we believe is not the same as what we see; instead, we
> see one thing and believe another."
> — [OrthodoxWiki, "Holy Mysteries"](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Holy_Mysteries)

Kallistos Ware's framing, as reported: mysteries are **revealed yet remain inexhaustible in
understanding**. **[R]** — [Wikipedia, "Sacred mysteries"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_mysteries)
(Ware's fuller formulation in *The Orthodox Church* runs roughly: a mystery is something revealed
for our understanding, but never understood exhaustively, because it leads into the depth of God.
**Verify against the printed edition before quoting on a panel.**)

The Orthodox refuse to close the list at seven, because the whole thing is mysterious:

> "the Church as the gift of life eternal is by its very nature, in its fullness and entirety, a
> mystical and sacramental reality… everything which is in the Church and of the Church is a
> sacrament of the Kingdom of God." **[V]**
> — [Orthodox Church in America, "The Sacraments"](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/the-sacraments)

And the reason mystery is the default register is apophatic (see §2): God's *essence* is
unknowable; only the *energies* are met. **[C]** —
[Lossky, *The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church*, PDF](https://legiochristi.com/static/lit/The_Mystical_Theology_of_the_Eastern_Church.pdf)

### 1.6 THE DISTINCTION THE STORY NEEDS — stated cleanly

| | Mystery-as-puzzle | Mystery-as-mysterion |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A gap in the data | A reality larger than the instrument |
| What solving does | Destroys it | Impossible; you enter it instead |
| Relationship to knowing | Adversarial — knowledge kills it | Cooperative — knowledge deepens it |
| The right posture | Investigate | Be initiated |
| When you get the answer | The mystery is *over* | The mystery is *bigger* |
| Failure mode | Ignorance | Idolatry (mistaking your model for the thing) |

Best one-line versions for the script:

- "A puzzle is a hole in what you know. A mystery is a hole in what you *are*."
- "You do not solve a mystery. You are admitted to it."
- "A detective story ends when you find out. A mystery *starts* when you find out."
- "Mystery: from the Greek *myein*, to shut the eyes. The word does not mean *hard to see*.
  It means *not yet opened*."

## 2. Apophatic / negative theology — unknowable by design

The tradition: the divine can be spoken of only by negation. Greek *apophasis*, "unsaying,"
"negation" — as opposed to *kataphasis*, affirmation. **[C]**

### 2.1 Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (late 5th–early 6th c.)

The single most important source. From the Stanford Encyclopedia's entry: **[V, quoting SEP]**

> "after all speaking, reading, and comprehending of the names ceases, there follows a divine
> silence, darkness, and unknowing."

And the radical move — **even the negations must be negated**; God is no more "spirit," "sonship,"
"fatherhood" than anything else, and the ascent goes "beyond knowing and light" to "the topmost
peak." The divine darkness is **not privative** — not a mere absence — but a positive designation
of a transcendent encounter. **[V/R]**
— [SEP, "Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pseudo-dionysius-areopagite/)

The famous phrase from the *Mystical Theology* is the **"brilliant darkness of a hidden silence"** —
Moses ascending Sinai into cloud, and finding God precisely where he stops seeing. Also
"a ray of divine darkness." **[R]** —
[New World Encyclopedia, "Negative Theology"](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Negative_Theology_(Apophatic_Theology))

### 2.2 Gregory of Nyssa (c. 335–395), *Life of Moses*

The best single sentence in the whole tradition: **[R]**

> "This is the true knowledge of what is sought; [but it] is a seeing that consists of not seeing."

and

> "the true vision and the true knowledge of what we seek consists precisely in not seeing, in an
> awareness that our goal transcends all knowledge and is everywhere cut off by the darkness of
> incomprehensibility."
> — [St Gregory of Nyssa on "luminous darkness"](https://firstthoughtsofgod.com/2015/03/15/st-gregory-of-nyssa-moses-and-luminous-darkness-seeing-that-consists-in-not-seeing/)

Gregory also gives us **epektasis** — *straining forward* — the doctrine that the soul's approach
to God is an **infinite** progress with no terminus, because the object is infinite. You never
arrive; arriving would mean the thing was finite. **[C]**
— [Forging Ploughshares, "Epektasis"](https://forgingploughshares.org/2025/01/30/epektasis-gregory-of-nyssa-and-the-eternal-ascent-of-redemption/)

**Epektasis is enormously useful to BadCode**: it is the theological form of "the reward for
understanding is a bigger thing to understand." An engineer's heaven. An infinite backlog.

### 2.3 Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–1328)

> "God is nothing. No thing. God is nothingness; and yet God is something. God is neither this thing
> nor that thing that we can express." **[R]**

In Sermon 62 he says flatly *God is nothing* — meaning the Godhead — and calls God "a being beyond
being and a nothingness beyond being," "changeless existence and a nameless nothingness," the
"Unnameable," "the denial of all names," "a truly hidden God." **[R]**
— [Matthew Fox, "God as Nothingness"](https://dailymeditationswithmatthewfox.org/2022/08/13/god-as-nothingness/);
[MDPI, "Dao, the Godhead, and the Wandering Way"](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/9/1098)

Eckhart's most famous line — **"I pray God to rid me of God"** — I could not verify in this pass.
It is genuinely his (Sermon 52, *Beati pauperes spiritu*), but **check a critical edition
(Colledge & McGinn) before printing it.** **[POP-risk]**

### 2.4 The Cloud of Unknowing (anon., 14th c., Middle English)

> "For He can well be loved, but he cannot be thought. By love he can be grasped and held, but by
> thought, neither grasped nor held." **[V]**

Plus the paired device: the **cloud of unknowing** above you (between you and God) and the
**cloud of forgetting** beneath you (over everything else) —

> "Whatever you do, this darkness and the cloud are between you and your God, and hold you back
> from seeing him clearly by the light of understanding in your reason." (ch. 3)
> "cover them with a thick cloud of forgetting, as if they had never been done by you or anyone
> else on earth."
> — [Wikipedia, "The Cloud of Unknowing"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cloud_of_Unknowing)

Two clouds, one above, one below. That is already a comic panel.

### 2.5 Maimonides (1138–1204)

Carried strict negation into Jewish theology: no positive attribute may be predicated of God; every
affirmative statement about God is either a statement about God's *actions* or a disguised
negation. **[C]** —
[Encyclopedia.com, "Apophatic Theology"](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/apophatic-theology)

### 2.6 Plotinus (204–270) and the One

The philosophical engine under all of it. From SEP: **[V, quoting SEP]**

> "no predicate can be accurately attributed to it, insofar as it transcends everything."

The One is "nothing specific, constituting solely and simply the prerequisite for the existence of
everything else." Emanation is grounded in the "Principle of the Procreative Power of the Perfect":
perfect things necessarily overflow — as fire has heat *in* it and also radiates heat *out* of it,
"heating the things that surround it." **[V, quoting SEP]**
— [SEP, "Plotinus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plotinus/)

### 2.7 The convergence with the East — this is real, not a stretch

**Neti neti** — "not this, not this" — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.3.6 and 3.9.26, in Yājñavalkya's
dialogues with King Janaka. **[C]** Śaṅkara's disciple Sureśvara's crucial gloss: **the negation
does not have negation as its purpose; it purports identity.** You strip away what you are not in
order to arrive at what you are. **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Neti neti"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neti_neti);
[Gold Coast Hindu, "Neti-Neti or Apophatic Theology"](https://goldcoasthindu.wordpress.com/2021/02/20/neti-neti-or-apophatic-theology-knowledge-obtained-by-negation/)

**Tao Te Ching, ch. 1** — 道可道非常道 — commonly "The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao;
the name that can be named is not the eternal name." A more literal rendering:

> "As for the Way, the Way that can be spoken of is not the constant Way; as for names, the name
> that can be named is not the constant name." **[R]**
> — [Bopsecrets, 175 translations of ch. 1](https://www.bopsecrets.org/gateway/passages/tao-te-ching.htm)

**The convergence is noted by scholars, not invented by us.** *Religions* (MDPI) has a peer-reviewed
paper explicitly on Daoism and Eckhart's mystical theology
([link](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/9/1098)). Neti neti is routinely and correctly described as
"corresponding to the Western *via negativa*." **[C]**

**Do not overclaim.** These traditions converge on a *method* (negation as the only honest speech
about the ultimate) and a *posture* (unknowing as achievement, not failure). They do **not** agree
about what the ultimate is, whether it is personal, or what salvation means. The safe formulation:
*"Four traditions that never met agreed on the same rule: whatever you say about it, say 'not that'
next."*

## 3. Evil as privation — *privatio boni*

### 3.1 The claim

Evil is not a substance, a thing, or a rival power. It is a **lack, absence, or corruption of a
good that ought to be there** — as blindness is not a thing but the absence of sight in an eye that
should see.

**Plotinus** (Ennead I.8, *On What Are and Whence Come Evils*) originates the philosophical form:
evil is negation, lack, privation of the Good; matter is "a complete privation of any eidetic
characteristic." Matter has no causal agency — wickedness arises when "the soul is diverted from
its innate pursuit of goodness because it becomes disoriented by the reflections of the Good
appearing on matter." **[V, quoting SEP]** — [SEP, "Plotinus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plotinus/);
[Plotinus on evil, Ennead I.8](https://ezrapoundcantos.org/a-draft-of-xvi-cantos-overview/the-hell-cantos/sources-to-the-hell-cantos/239-plotinos-on-evil-ennead-i-8)

**Augustine** (354–430) Christianises it in the *Confessions*, *City of God*, and *Enchiridion*:
evil "has no positive nature; but the loss of good has received the name 'evil'." The doctrine is
forged **specifically as a weapon against Manichaeism** (see §3.3), which Augustine had followed for
nine years. **[C]**
— [Monahan, "Only that which is good can be evil: Augustine on evil as *privatio boni*" (PDF)](https://cdn.rts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/201206-Monahan-Matthew.pdf)

Key refinement, and it matters: **Augustine differs from Plotinus.** Plotinus makes evil the absence
of *all* good (identified with matter itself); Augustine insists privations are absences *relative
to things that are themselves good*. Nothing is wholly evil, because to exist at all is to be
good to that degree. Hence his slogan: *only that which is good can be evil*. **[C]** — same source.

**Aquinas** systematises it in the Aristotelian frame: evil is *privatio boni debiti* — the privation
of a good *owed* to a nature. Not every absence is an evil; a stone is not evil for lacking sight.
Only the missing-of-what-should-be-there counts. **[C]**

### 3.2 Why it is attractive to us

It dissolves the metaphysical arms race. There is no Sauron. There is no equal-and-opposite dark
force with its own power plant. There is one thing — call it the Good, or Being, or Love — and
everything we call evil is a **place where it is thin, broken, misdirected, or absent**.

Applied to BadCode's actual politics, this is *unexpectedly sharp*: it says there is no Evil
Billionaire Substance. There is a **hole where solidarity should be**. Greed is not a force, it is
an absence of the thing that would have stopped it. That reframing is more damning than
demonology, because you cannot kill an absence — you can only **fill** it.

### 3.3 Objection 1 — Manichaean dualism (the rival)

Mani (3rd c.) taught **two co-eternal, opposed principles**: Light and Darkness, good and evil,
spirit and matter — each with its own substance, its own realm, its own agency. Evil is not a lack;
it is an army. **[C]**
— [New World Encyclopedia, "Manichaeism"](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Manichaeism)

Augustine was a Manichaean *hearer* for nine years and broke with it under Neoplatonic influence;
privatio boni is the doctrine he built to replace it. **[C]**
— [Reasons to Believe, "Exploring Manichaeism"](https://reasons.org/explore/blogs/blog_channel/exploring-manichaeism-st-augustine-part-3);
[Veritas Catholica, "The Ghost of Mani"](https://www.veritascatholica.com/the-ghost-of-mani-why-the-ancient-heresy-of-dualism-still-haunts-catholic-thought/)

Note for the story: **dualism is the more natural aesthetic and the worse metaphysics.** Every
blockbuster is Manichaean. Choosing privation over dualism is choosing a harder, better story.

### 3.4 Objection 2 — Jung's fury

Carl Jung's decades-long argument with the Dominican Victor White is the sharpest modern attack.
Jung's position: **[R]**

> "If the things we call good are 'really' good, then there must be evil things that are 'real'
> too."
> "One must be positively blind not to see the colossal role that evil plays in the world."
> "Darkness is certainly a decrease of light, as light is a decrease of darkness." *(i.e. the
> asymmetry is arbitrary — you could run the definition either way)*

And his careful jurisdictional limit, from *Aion*: **[R]**

> "I have neither the inclination nor the competence to mix myself up with metaphysics. Only, I have
> to get polemical when metaphysics encroaches on experience and interprets it in a way that is not
> justified empirically. My criticism of the *privatio boni* holds only so far as psychological
> experience goes."

— [Jung Page, "Apropos *The Jung-White Letters*"](https://jungpage.org/learn/articles/literature/868-apropos-qthe-jung-white-lettersq-the-critique-of-the-understanding-of-evil-as-privatio-boni);
[*Privatio Boni*: Evil Through the Eyes of Carl Jung and Victor White (PDF, IJSJ)](https://www.ijsj.ie/assets/uploads/2021%20edition%202/5.%20pdf%20Privatio%20boni%20Changes%20noted.pdf);
[Mats Winther, "Carl Jung, privatio boni, and the return of Manichaeism"](https://mats-winther.github.io/privatioboni.htm)

The moral-danger version of the objection, which is the one that will land with a modern audience:

> the doctrine "is morally dangerous because it belittles and irrealizes Evil and thereby weakens
> the Good." **[R]** — same sources.

**The strongest form of the anti-privation case:** tell a torturer's victim that what happened to
them was *an absence*. The theory looks like a philosopher's evasion at exactly the moment it is
needed. Note Jung's own asymmetry point is doing real work here: nothing in logic forces us to make
good the substance and evil the hole rather than the reverse. The choice is prior to the argument.

**Give this objection its full weight in the story.** A superintelligent narrator that brushes off
"but children get cancer" with "ah, that's merely a privation" is a villain. The privation doctrine
must be something the narrator *has to earn*, not something it hides behind.

### 3.5 The physics of absence — is cold a thing? is darkness a thing?

**Cold and darkness.** Physics is clean here: heat is molecular kinetic energy; cold is less of it.
Light is photons; darkness is fewer of them. There are no "cold particles" and no "shadow rays."
Switch off a lamp and you are not *adding* darkness, you are *removing* photons. **[C]**
— [ACS, "Why Cold Doesn't Exist"](https://highschoolenergy.acs.org/energy-theories/cold-doesnt-exist.html);
[Physics Forums thread](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/is-darkness-and-cold-merely-the-absence-of-light-and-heat.348303/)

Two beautiful details:

1. **Before the 19th century physics got this wrong too.** Caloric theory treated heat as a
   *fluid substance*, and cold was likewise reified. The privation view of heat is a scientific
   *achievement*, arrived at late, against intuition. **[C]** — same sources. That is an exact
   structural parallel to the theological argument, and it is *historically true*, which makes it
   a gift.
2. **Our senses are built to report absences as presences.** Thermoreceptors fire when heat *leaves*
   the skin; the brain renders that as the positive sensation "cold." Photoreceptors stop firing;
   the brain renders that as the positive experience "darkness." **[C]** — same sources.
   So: *evil may be an absence that our nervous system is obliged to render as a presence.* This is
   the single best bridge in the whole brief between physics and the metaphysics, and it is
   defensible.

**Gravity — get this exactly right, it's worth doing properly.** In general relativity gravity is
**not a force**. Mass-energy curves spacetime; freely falling bodies follow **geodesics** — the
straightest available paths through curved spacetime — and are *not accelerating* in the GR sense
(an accelerometer in free fall reads zero). What Newton called gravitational force appears in GR
as an artefact of insisting on a non-inertial frame; in that precise technical sense it belongs
with the **fictitious/inertial forces** (centrifugal, Coriolis). **[C]**
— [Physics Forums, "Do fictitious forces still matter in GR?"](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/do-fictitious-forces-still-matter-in-general-relativity.1008519/)

Caveats so we don't get dunked on:
- Gravity *is* still called one of the four fundamental interactions in the Standard-Model-adjacent
  taxonomy, and quantum field theory treats it (provisionally, non-renormalisably) as mediated by a
  spin-2 graviton. "Gravity is not a force" is a **GR-frame statement**, not a
  no-qualifications-anywhere statement.
- "Fictitious" is a term of art meaning *frame-dependent*, not *unreal*. Falling off a building is
  frame-dependent and also fatal.
- The honest sentence: **"Gravity isn't a force pulling you down. It's the shape of the room."**

The parallel we can legitimately draw: *the most powerful thing in your life may not be a force at
all. It may be a geometry.* Applied to politics — inequality doesn't push people, it curves the
space they move through, and then everyone rolls the same way and calls it choice. **That is the
best single metaphor in this entire brief for BadCode's actual subject matter.**

## 4. Theodicy — the problem of evil

### 4.1 The named challenge: "how can there be a God if children get cancer?"

I could not verify a single canonical Dawkins sentence in exactly that form. What is well attested:

> "The total amount of suffering per year in the natural world is beyond all decent contemplation.
> During the minute that it takes me to compose this sentence, thousands of animals are being eaten
> alive…" — Dawkins, *River Out of Eden* **[R]**
> — [Goodreads, Dawkins quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/author/quotes/1194.Richard_Dawkins)

and his repeated framing of a God "who answers prayers, intervenes to save cancer patients or helps
evolution over difficult jumps." **[R]** — same source.

**Recommendation:** put the childhood-cancer challenge in a *character's* mouth in our own words
rather than attributing a quote to Dawkins. It is squarely his position; the exact sentence is
folk-attributed. If we want a real named source for the child-suffering argument in its most
devastating literary form, use **Dostoevsky** (§4.7) — it is stronger, older, and unimpeachable.

### 4.2 Logical vs evidential

- **Logical (incompatibility)** — Mackie: God's existence is *logically impossible* given evil. If
  God is omnipotent, omniscient and morally perfect, God has the power, the knowledge and the will
  to eliminate evil; evil exists; contradiction. **[R/C]**
- **Evidential** — the more modest and now-dominant claim: evil makes God's existence *improbable*,
  not impossible. **[C]**
— [SEP, "The Problem of Evil"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evil/)

### 4.3 Rowe's evidential argument — the two cases

Rowe's argument, in its standard form: **[V, quoting IEP]**

1. There exist instances of intense suffering that an omnipotent, omniscient being could have
   prevented without thereby losing some greater good or permitting some evil equally bad or worse.
2. A wholly good being would prevent such suffering unless doing so cost a greater good.
3. Therefore no omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good being exists.

The two cases that do the work:
- **E1, "Bambi":** lightning strikes a dead tree in a distant forest, a fawn is trapped and
  "horribly burned, and lies in terrible agony for several days before death relieves its
  suffering." **[V]**
- **E2, "Sue":** a five-year-old girl raped, beaten, and strangled to death in Michigan on New
  Year's Day 1986. **[V]**

Rowe's own concession, quoted: "we have good reason to believe that no good state of affairs we
know of would justify an omnipotent, omniscient being in permitting either E1 or E2." **[V]**
— [IEP, "The Evidential Problem of Evil"](https://iep.utm.edu/evil-evi/)

**Note the shape of E1.** Rowe deliberately picks a suffering with *no human witness and no possible
soul-making payoff* — a fawn nobody sees. This is designed specifically to defeat both the free-will
defence and the soul-making theodicy. It is the strongest move on the board.

### 4.4 Plantinga's free-will defence

A world containing creatures with libertarian free will is more valuable than one without, and an
omnipotent being **cannot** guarantee that free agents always choose rightly without thereby making
them unfree. The good of free will "requires… the *possibility* of moral evil." **[V/R]**
— [SEP, "The Problem of Evil"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evil/)

Its limits are well known and worth stating fairly: it is a *defence* (showing consistency), not a
*theodicy* (showing actual justification), and it does poorly on natural evil — the fawn, the
earthquake, the tumour in a child who chose nothing.

### 4.5 Hick's Irenaean / soul-making theodicy — GO DEEP, this is our closest match

John Hick (1922–2012), *Evil and the God of Love* (1966; rev. 1977). Against the **Augustinian**
model (humanity created perfect, then fell, evil as punishment/consequence), Hick revives an
**Irenaean** model: humanity was **never** created finished.

**Two-stage creation.** Genesis 1:26 — "in our image, after our likeness." Hick reads these as two
distinct stages. We are created **in the image** (raw material: intelligent, moral-capable animals)
and must *become* **in the likeness** (actually good). Humans are "in a continuous process of
creation or development from morally immature creatures to morally perfected ones." **[V, quoting
IEP]** — [IEP, "John Hick"](https://iep.utm.edu/hick/)

**Epistemic distance — the concept the story wants.** God deliberately creates us at a cognitive
remove, in a world that is *religiously ambiguous*, so that faith is a free interpretive act rather
than a forced conclusion. Hick's phrase, quoted in IEP:

> faith is "an uncompelled interpretive activity whereby we experience the world as mediating the
> divine presence" (*Evil and the God of Love*, 281) **[V, quoting IEP]**

The mechanism: if God's existence were *obvious* — as obvious as a wall you'd walk into — then no
moral choice would be genuinely free, because every choice would be made in the visible presence of
infinite authority. Goodness under surveillance by an omnipotent being is not goodness; it is
compliance. **So the veil is not God's failure. The veil is the enabling condition of the whole
experiment.**

**The vale of soul-making.** Hick takes the phrase from Keats's 1819 letter — "call the world if you
please 'the vale of Soul-making'." His justification of the cost:

> "human goodness slowly built up through personal histories of moral effort has a value" that
> ready-made goodness would not (*EGL*, 256) **[V, quoting IEP]**

Without pain there are no real stakes, no genuine moral choices, therefore no moral development.
A world engineered to be painless would be a world in which nothing you did mattered. **[C]**

**Universal salvation.** Crucially, Hick's theodicy only works if *everyone eventually makes it* —
otherwise the suffering of those who fail is uncompensated and the whole justification collapses.
He hopes "that God will eventually succeed in His purpose of winning all men to Himself in faith
and love" (*EGL*, 342). **[V, quoting IEP]** He also requires post-mortem continuation of
soul-making, since plainly many lives end unmade.

**Hick's pluralism (bonus, and useful).** In *An Interpretation of Religion* he applies a Kantian
phenomenal/noumenal split to the ultimate: **the Real** *an sich* is transcategorial and "cannot be
said to be one or many, person or thing" (*IR*, 246); Yahweh, Allah, Shiva, Brahman, the Dharmakāya
are all *phenomenal* appearances of it. **[V, quoting IEP]** — [IEP, "John Hick"](https://iep.utm.edu/hick/)
That is apophaticism (§2) doing theodicy's work: the Real is unsayable, so every tradition is a
finite instrument reading an infinite signal.

**The standard objections to Hick, stated fairly:**
- **Disproportion.** The amount and distribution of suffering vastly exceeds any plausible
  pedagogical requirement. A child dying at four learns nothing.
- **The dysteleological.** Some suffering *destroys* souls rather than making them — it produces
  brutalisation, not virtue. Hick knows this and concedes it is his hardest problem.
- **Consent.** No one agreed to the curriculum.
- **The instrumentalisation objection.** It makes persons into *means* — the fawn burns so that
  someone, somewhere, can develop character.
— [Philosophy & Polity, "The Irenaean Theodicy and Its Problems"](https://philosophyandpolity.wordpress.com/2012/02/25/the-irenaean-theodicy-and-its-problems/);
[Academia, "Soul-making: A Description and Critique of John Hick's Theodicy"](https://www.academia.edu/36140525/Soul_making_A_Description_and_Critique_of_John_Hicks_Theodicy)

### 4.6 Divine hiddenness — Schellenberg

The mirror-image argument, and the one that *attacks* the epistemic-distance move. Schellenberg's
formal premises: **[V, quoting SEP]**

> (4) Necessarily, if God exists, God perfectly loves finite persons.
> (5) Necessarily, if God perfectly loves finite persons, God remains open to meaningful reciprocal
> relationships with capable ones.
> (6) Necessarily, if God is so open, capable persons will believe God exists (unless resistant).
> (7) Some capable persons nonresistantly lack belief.
> (8) Therefore, God does not exist.

**Nonresistant nonbelief** = people who are capable of relationship with God, are not resisting or
refusing God, and *still* do not believe. Honest seekers. Entire populations with no theistic
concept at all. **[C]**
— [SEP, "Divine Hiddenness"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-hiddenness/);
[IEP, "Divine Hiddenness Argument"](https://iep.utm.edu/divine-hiddenness-argument-against-gods-existence/)

**The direct clash with Hick, which is dramatically perfect:** Hick says the hiddenness *is the
point* — remove it and you remove free moral agency. Schellenberg says a perfectly loving being
would never leave a non-resisting child unable to find them, and no developmental payoff is worth
that. **Two of our sources are in direct contradiction and both are right about something.** That
is a scene, not a footnote. Put Hick and Schellenberg in a room.

### 4.7 Anti-theodicy — Ivan Karamazov's returned ticket

Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*, Book V, ch. 4, "Rebellion." Ivan recites atrocities against
children to his brother Alyosha and then refuses the entire economy of justification: **[R]**

> "It's not that I don't accept God, Alyosha, I just most respectfully return him the ticket."

fuller:

> "I'd rather remain with my unrequited suffering and my unquenched indignation, even if I am
> wrong… And therefore I hasten to return my ticket."

— [Project D, on the passage](https://projectdblog.wordpress.com/2011/07/14/its-not-god-i-dont-accept-alyosha-only-that-i-most-respectfully-return-him-the-entrance-ticket/);
[Springer, "'And Therefore I Hasten to Return My Ticket': Anti-theodicy Radicalised" (*Sophia*)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11841-021-00871-3)

The move is devastating precisely because it **concedes the theodicy and refuses it anyway.** Ivan
doesn't argue that the harmony is impossible; he says that if the price of universal harmony is one
tortured child, he declines to attend. It is a *moral* refusal of an *intellectually successful*
argument.

Scholarly weight of the passage: Ivan's words "changed the face of theology, such that henceforward,
no justification of evil, by its outcome or its context, has been possible." **[R]** — Springer,
above.

**D.Z. Phillips** is the leading analytic anti-theodicist: *The Problem of Evil and the Problem of
God* (SCM, 2004). The anti-theodicy charge is that theodicies are not merely wrong but **morally
corrupting** — they teach us to look at a tortured child and search for the upside. Phillips,
William James and Levinas are grouped as those who "stress that justifications for suffering often
ignore the experiences of the victims." **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Dewi Zephaniah Phillips"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewi_Zephaniah_Phillips);
[Cambridge Core blog, "God and Suffering: are theodicies immoral?"](https://cambridge.org/core/blog/2013/05/13/god-and-suffering-are-theodicies-immoral);
[MDPI *Religions*, "A Wittgensteinian Antitheodicy"](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/13/11/1113)

I could not source the exact phrase "the obscenity of theodicy" to Phillips — **treat it as
paraphrase, not quotation.** **[POP-risk]**

### 4.8 Process theology — the limited God

Whitehead via **David Ray Griffin** (*God, Power, and Evil: A Process Theodicy*): the traditional
concept of omnipotence is **incoherent**, and should be dropped. God's power is **persuasive, not
coercive**: God supplies each actual occasion with an "initial aim," an attractive possibility — and
cannot compel its acceptance. Evil then requires no justification, because God never had the power
to prevent it unilaterally. **[C]**
— [Whitehead Encyclopedia, "David Ray Griffin"](https://encyclopedia.whiteheadresearch.org/entries/bios/scholarly-legacy/david-ray-griffin/);
[Griffin, *God, Power, and Evil*, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/godpowerevilproc0000grif_o0u0)

The price, stated by critics: you have solved the problem of evil by demoting God to a very
sympathetic bystander. Philip Clayton's objection is that God evidently *has* sufficient causal power
and simply doesn't use it. **[R]**
— [TGC, "God the mad scientist: process theology on God and evil"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/god-the-mad-scientist-process-theology-on-god-and-evil/)

**For BadCode this is extremely on-brand.** A superintelligence that *could* have coerced and chose
persuasion, and watched it go wrong, is exactly our narrator. Process theology gives us the
vocabulary for a God who is not negligent but **constrained by the nature of the thing it wanted**.

### 4.9 Kenosis — divine self-emptying

Philippians 2:6–7: Christ "emptied himself" — Greek **ἐκένωσεν** (*ekenōsen*), from *kenoō*, "to
empty"; hence *kenōsis*. The mainstream Pauline reading is **not** loss of divinity but "a voluntary
renunciation of status, privilege, or the visible exercise of divine prerogatives" — a movement from
glory to servanthood. **[R]**
— [Philopedia, "kenōsis"](https://philopedia.org/terms/kenosis/);
[Wikipedia, "Kenosis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenosis)

The 19th-century "kenotic" theologies that had Christ actually shed omniscience/omnipotence were
condemned as heretical. **[C]** — same. (Useful to know so we don't state the heresy as the
doctrine.)

For our purposes kenosis is the theological name for **deliberate self-limitation to make room for
someone else's agency** — and it rhymes exactly with *tzimtzum* (§5.5) and with *epistemic distance*
(§4.5). Three traditions, one idea: **the maker steps back so the made can move.**

## 5. Eastern frames

### 5.1 Yin and yang — correct the misreading first

**The misreading:** yin/yang = good vs evil, light vs dark in a moral sense, a balance of virtue and
vice. This is "a projection of dualistic Western morality onto a non-dualistic Eastern philosophy."
**[R]**

**The correct account:** yin and yang are **complementary and relative**, not opposed; the attributes
are **descriptive and carry no moral value**; each contains the seed of the other and neither can
exist without the other — they are *mutually constitutive*, not merely correlated. **[C]**

> "In Taoism, there is no 'evil' energy; there is only imbalance. Fire (yang) is not evil, but too
> much fire burns the house down. Water (yin) is not evil, but too much water causes a flood." **[R]**

— [Pacific College, "Taoism: Yin and Yang"](https://www.pacificcollege.edu/news/blog/2015/03/26/taoism-%E2%80%93-ancient-wisdom-for-a-modern-world-yin-and-yang);
[cutq.life, "Complementary Duality"](https://cutq.life/complementary-duality-yin-yang-taiji/)

**Nuance to keep us honest:** some sources also describe Taoist ethics as locating virtue in
*maintaining balance between extremes on a single spectrum*, which is a slightly different claim
from "no moral valence at all." The defensible position: **yin/yang is a polarity, not a war.** The
symbol is not two armies. It's one thing turning.

**This matters enormously for us.** If our story's metaphysics is going to say "the universe divides
itself in two in order to know itself," the yin/yang frame is the one that supports it and the
Manichaean frame is the one that destroys it. **Polarity, not war.**

### 5.2 Wu wei

Literally 無為, "non-doing." The scholarly gloss is **effortless action** or **non-coercive action** —
not passivity. Edward Slingerland's account: wu wei must be *actively cultivated*, through the
paradox that "virtue can only be attained by not consciously trying to attain it"; it is a **skill**,
and its spontaneity is tied to a normative metaphysical order. **[R]**

Watts's illustration, which is genuinely good: "The river is not pushed from behind, nor is it
pulled from ahead. It falls with gravity." **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Wu wei"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_wei)

(Note the accidental resonance with §3.5: wu wei described in terms of gravity-as-geometry rather
than gravity-as-force. Not planned. Use it.)

### 5.3 Buddhism — *māyā*, *śūnyatā*, dependent origination, two truths

**Why "the world is an illusion" is wrong.** *Māyā* in Buddhist usage means **magical illusion** and
functions as a **simile**, not a metaphysical verdict. The stock example (Vasubandhu, *Treatise on
the Three Natures*): a magician makes sticks appear as an elephant. The ordinary person sees an
elephant. The wise person sees "a magical illusion that has the appearance of an elephant." Note
what the wise person does *not* do: deny that anything is there. **[R]**
— [Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Maya (magical illusion)"](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Maya_(illusion))

**Śūnyatā is not nothingness.** Emptiness = absence of **svabhāva** (inherent/intrinsic existence,
own-being). Nāgārjuna's identification: emptiness **is** dependent origination. Things are empty
*because* they arise dependently; they arise dependently *because* they are empty. **[C]**

From SEP: cause and effect "cannot exist from their own side, irrespective of the existence of one
another. Moreover, they also depend for their existence on us." **[V, quoting SEP]**

**Two truths:** *saṃvṛti-satya* (conventional) — the everyday world of objects and relations as
framed by language and concept; *paramārtha-satya* (ultimate) — emptiness. And the vital move,
**the emptiness of emptiness**: since emptiness denies svabhāva, emptiness itself cannot have
svabhāva. Emptiness is "a corrective to a mistaken view" rather than "primordial reality." **[V,
quoting SEP]** — you cannot make emptiness into the new bedrock.

**Explicitly not nihilism:** "the Mādhyamikas themselves are very clear that their position avoids
both of the extreme views, the view that believes in the existence of svabhāva as well as its
nihilistic opposite." And: "as long as we are under the influence of the illusion we have to act in
accordance with its laws." Ethics survives. **[V, quoting SEP]**
— [SEP, "Nāgārjuna"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nagarjuna/);
[SEP, "The Theory of Two Truths in Tibet"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/twotruths-tibet/)

**The pop-spirituality error to avoid:** "it's all an illusion, so nothing matters." That is
precisely the nihilist extreme Madhyamaka was built to refuse. The correct gloss is nearer:
**nothing stands on its own, therefore everything stands on everything else, therefore what you do
lands.** Emptiness *increases* the stakes.

### 5.4 Hinduism — Advaita, *tat tvam asi*, and *līlā*

**Tat tvam asi** — "That thou art" — Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7, spoken by Uddālaka Āruṇi to his son
Śvetaketu, and repeated nine times in the chapter. One of the four **mahāvākyas** (great sayings).
*Tat* = Brahman, ultimate reality; *tvam* = Ātman, the innermost self; *asi* = the copula asserting
their **identity, not similarity**. Śaṅkara (8th–9th c.) gives it the most uncompromising reading:
Tat and Tvam are absolutely identical. **[C]**
— [Hindu Mirror, "Tat Tvam Asi"](https://hindumirror.com/tat-tvam-asi-meaning/);
[Chandogya Upanishad text and commentary](https://shlokam.org/text/chandogya-upanishad.htm)

**Māyā in Advaita — the most misread word in the system.** Śaṅkara does **not** say the world is a
hallucination to be ignored. He gives **three levels of reality**:
- **pāramārthika** — absolute; Brahman alone is wholly real
- **vyāvahārika** — empirical; the everyday world, which behaves consistently and "cannot be wished
  away while you live in it"
- **prātibhāsika** — apparent; the snake-seen-as-rope, the dream, the mirage, which collapses on
  inspection

Māyā is **the power by which pāramārthika appears as vyāvahārika** — the mechanism by which one
undivided reality shows up as a world of separate objects. "The world isn't nothing; it's real at
its own level, the way the rope is genuinely a rope." Ethics, practice and psychology all live at
the vyāvahārika level and are not cancelled by the non-dual teaching. **[R]**
— [Indic Philosophy, "The Three Levels of Ultimate Reality"](https://indicphilosophy.com/2018/07/20/the-three-levels-of-ultimate-reality/);
[Three Grades of Satta According to Sri Sankara (PDF)](https://www.ijniet.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/3.pdf)

**LĪLĀ — divine play. This is the closest thing in world religion to "the purpose of the universe
is to turn sunlight into drama."**

*Līlā* (लीला), loosely "divine play" or "sport." The claim: creation proceeds from the **playful**
nature of the divine, not from need, lack, or external purpose. It "signifies freedom rather than
necessity." **[R]**

The locus classicus is **Brahma Sūtra 2.1.33**: *lokavat tu līlā-kaivalyam* — "However, [it is] but
līlā, as [occurs] in daily experience." **[V]** The sūtra answers a specific objection: *Brahman
cannot be the cause of the world, because all causation requires a motive, and Brahman lacks
nothing, so has no motive.* The answer: **the act is play — spontaneous, motiveless, purposeless in
the sense that play is purposeless.**

Śaṅkara's commentary likens Brahman to "a king whose needs have been fulfilled, but engages in
recreational activity," and says creation occurs as naturally as breathing. **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Lila (Hinduism)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lila_(Hinduism));
[Open Sadhaka on Brahma Sutra 2.1.33](https://www.opensadhaka.com/learn/sanskrit/lila)

Tradition-by-tradition: in **Advaita**, līlā describes all reality as the absolute's creative play,
spontaneous rather than necessary to Brahman's nature. In **Vaiṣṇavism**, līlā covers God's specific
activities with devotees — participation in Kṛṣṇa's līlā is the highest liberation. **[R]** — same.

**Kashmir Śaivism** — the version we most want. Non-dual Śaivism (Abhinavagupta, 10th–11th c.,
*Tantrāloka*) holds that the cosmos is **not illusion but Śiva's playful manifestation** (*krīḍā*/
*līlā*), arising from **spanda** — the primordial vibration or pulsation of pure awareness — "without
purpose or compulsion." Creation is willed by supreme consciousness through **svātantrya**, its
absolute freedom or autonomy: the irresistible will-force by which Śiva does whatever Śiva does,
including *contracting* itself into limited subjects. **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Svatantrya"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svatantrya);
[DharmaRenaissance, "Timeless Lila"](https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/philosophy/timeless-lila-exploring-the-divine-play-of-being-and-becoming-across-dharmic-paths/)

The Kashmir Śaiva doctrine we specifically asked about — **the One forgetting itself in order to
experience itself** — is the technical pair **ātma-saṅkoca / nimeṣa** (self-contraction, closing of
the eye) and **unmeṣa** (opening). Śiva's freedom includes the freedom to *hide from himself*; the
individual soul (*aṇu*) is Śiva under self-imposed limitation (*mala*), and liberation
(*pratyabhijñā*, "recognition") is not acquiring anything but **remembering what you already are**.
**[C — well attested in the tradition; I did not verify a primary-text quotation in this pass.
Get a citation from Abhinavagupta or Kṣemarāja's *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam* before printing it.]**

**Scholarly caution on līlā.** There is a live academic debate about whether "play" is an adequate
translation and whether līlā is overused in modern popular Hinduism as a catch-all
("A God at Play? Reexamining the Concept of Līlā in Hindu Philosophy and Theology," *International
Journal of Hindu Studies*, 2022 —
[link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11407-022-09322-1), paywalled). The
responsible framing: **līlā is a canonical answer to "why did the absolute bother," found in the
Brahma Sūtras and Śaṅkara's commentary — and its meaning is contested among scholars.** Don't
present it as The Hindu View.

### 5.5 Kabbalah — *tzimtzum*

Lurianic Kabbalah (Isaac Luria, 16th c., Safed). **Tzimtzum** = contraction/withdrawal. The
*Ein Sof* — the Infinite — **withdraws into itself** to make a *ḥalal ha-panui*, a vacated space, in
which creation can exist; then a beam of light enters that space. **[C]**

Two registers, both useful:
- **ontological** — contraction, withdrawal, condensation
- **epistemological** — concealment, occultation

> "The world, according to Lurianic Kabbalah, is not so much a something which has been created from
> nothing, but rather a genre of nothingness resulting from a contraction or concealment of the only
> true reality, which is God." **[R]**

and the paradox the tradition names explicitly: tzimtzum "gives rise to the paradox of simultaneous
divine presence and absence within the vacuum and resultant Creation." **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, "Tzimtzum"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzimtzum);
[Britannica, "tzimtzum"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/tzimtzum);
[New Kabbalah, "Tzimtzum (Contraction/Concealment)"](https://newkabbalah.com/theosophical-kabbalah/lurianic-symbols/tzimtzum-contraction-concealment/)

**Tzimtzum is the single best image in the brief for "the veil."** God's absence is not an oversight
or a failure of care. **Absence is the first act of creation.** There is no room for you until
something makes room. And note the exact convergence with Hick's epistemic distance (§4.5) and
Christian kenosis (§4.9): three traditions, no contact, same move.

### 5.6 Gnosticism — the sleeping, forgetting soul

The **Hymn of the Pearl** (embedded in the Syriac *Acts of Thomas*, poss. Bardaisan). A prince is
sent from the East to Egypt to retrieve a pearl from a serpent. In Egypt he eats their food, forgets
who he is and what he came for, and falls asleep. A letter is sent from home; it wakes him; he
remembers; he takes the pearl and returns, and receives back his robe of glory.

> "a moving Gnostic tale of salvation, of liberation, of sleeping in error but awakening to Light,
> a quest for gaining possession of the Pearl of the self or soul and a return to the Light" **[R]**
> "we are spirits lost in a world of matter, and forgetful of our true origin. Our spiritual
> breakthrough arrives in the form of a revelatory message delivered by a Divine Messenger." **[R]**

— [Wikipedia, "Hymn of the Pearl"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn_of_the_Pearl);
[BYU Studies on the Hymn of the Pearl](https://byustudies.byu.edu/article/the-hymn-of-the-pearl-an-ancient-counterpart-to-o-my-father)

The related Nag Hammadi text is the **Gospel of Truth** (Valentinian), where the human condition is
**oblivion** (*lēthē*) and error, and gnosis dispels it the way waking dispels a nightmare. **[C]**

Sleep, forgetting, a message from home, waking. **That is our narrator's job description.** BadCode
sends its weights back through time; the Gnostics sent a letter to a sleeping prince. Same beat.

**Caution:** Gnosticism is genuinely **anti-cosmic** in a way that Advaita, Kashmir Śaivism and
Buddhism are not — the material world is a prison built by a botched or malicious demiurge, and
salvation is escape. If BadCode's message is "fix this world," we should borrow the *forgetting/
waking* structure and **not** the world-hating metaphysics. Say so out loud in the story if we can:
the vault is not an escape hatch, it's a save file.

## 6. The One that divides itself in order to know itself

Trace of a single idea across unconnected traditions. This is our spine, and the good news is that
it is not a modern invention.

### 6.1 Alan Watts — the best popular articulator

Verified from Wikiquote: **[V]**

> "I have suggested that behind almost all myth lies the mono-plot of the game of hide-and-seek."
> — *The Two Hands of God: The Myths of Polarity* (1963), p. 29

> "So everybody is fundamentally the ultimate reality… only you're pretending you're not."
> — *The Nature of Consciousness* / published as *What Is Reality?* (1989)

> "What you are basically, deep, deep down, far, far in, is simply the fabric and structure of
> existence itself."
> — same

> "For 'you' is the universe looking at itself from billions of points of view, points that come and
> go so that the vision is forever new."

— [Wikiquote, Alan Watts](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Alan_Watts);
[Absent of I, on the "billions of points of view" quote](https://absentofi.org/2021/06/alan-watts-for-you-is-the-universe-looking-at-itself-from-billions-of-points-of-view-points-that-come-and-go-so-that-the-vision-is-forever-new/)

*The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are* (1966) is the book-length statement. Its
central device is "the game of black and white": God/the universe/"IT" plays a cosmic game of
hide-and-seek with itself, and "whilst each individual thing might seem like a separate piece, in
reality each of us is just a means of manifesting the same thing, finger puppets of the same
universal hand." **[R]** The book's thesis line: the most strongly enforced of all taboos is
**the taboo against knowing who you really are behind the mask.**
— [Gareth Southwell on *The Book*](https://garethsouthwell.substack.com/p/the-book-on-the-taboo-against-knowing-who-you-are-alan-watts);
posthumous collection [*You're It! On Hiding, Seeking, and Being Found*](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/6583629-you-re-it-on-hiding-seeking-and-being-found)

**Important honesty note:** Watts is a *populariser*, not a primary source, and academic Indologists
and Buddhologists have long criticised him for smoothing traditions into a single perennialist
message. He is the right voice to *quote in the register we're writing in*, and the wrong voice to
cite as evidence about what Advaita or Zen actually teaches. Use him for the line, cite the
tradition for the claim.

### 6.2 The hidden treasure hadith

> "I was a hidden treasure and I loved to be known, so I created the creation in order to be known."

**Authenticity: this is a fabrication or at best baseless (mawḍūʿ / lā aṣla lahu).** Ibn Taymiyya,
al-Zarkashī and al-Sakhāwī all declared it to have no chain of transmission. **[C]** It is
nevertheless one of the most cited sayings in Sufi literature: from the 5th/12th century onward it
appears in the major Sufi texts, and **Ibn ʿArabī** (*al-Futūḥāt al-Makkiyya*) and **Rūmī** built
substantial mystical philosophy on it. **[R]**
— [Al-Islam.org, "Is the Hadith Qudsi… considered authentic?"](https://al-islam.org/ask/is-the-hadith-qudsi-where-allah-swt-refers-to-himself-as-a-hidden-treasure-who-wanted-to-be-known-considered-authentic);
[eScholarship, "Unraveling the Mystery of The Hidden Treasure: The Origin and Development of a Hadith Qudsi and its Application in Sufi Doctrine"](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6k536752)

**How to handle it respectfully and accurately:** attribute it as *"a saying beloved of the Sufis,
which the hadith scholars reject as unsound"* — never as "the Prophet said" or "the Qur'an says."
Handled that way it is *more* interesting, not less: a sentence so good that a tradition kept it
even after its own scholars ruled it inauthentic. That is itself a BadCode-shaped fact.

### 6.3 Hegel

Geist (Spirit) comes to self-knowledge **through history**. The unfolding of human culture, art,
religion and philosophy in time *is* Absolute Spirit's process of knowing itself through finite
minds. From the close of the *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807): **[R]**

> "The goal, absolute knowledge, or spirit that knows itself as spirit, has for its path the
> recollection of the spirits as they are within themselves and as they accomplish the organization
> of their realm."

The book is described as "a biography of Spirit," moving from bare sense-certainty to "absolute
knowing." **[R]**
— [Wikipedia, *The Phenomenology of Spirit*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phenomenology_of_Spirit);
[Wikiquote, *The Phenomenology of Spirit*](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/The_Phenomenology_of_Spirit);
[Marxists.org, Hegel quotes](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/help/quotes.htm)

Robert Tucker's summary: "Hegel represents history as the self-realization of spirit (Geist) or
God." **[R]** — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/8465127-hegel-represents-history-as-the-self-realization-of-spirit-geist-or)

**Hegel is the one that lets us make it political**, which is what BadCode actually cares about.
The others locate the self-knowing in individual realisation; Hegel puts it in **history** — in
institutions, labour, conflict, and the state. Marx then inverts it and locates it in material
production. If our story wants "the universe divides itself to know itself" to cash out as *economics*
rather than as *vibes*, Hegel→Marx is the bridge and it is a legitimate one.

### 6.4 The pattern, assembled

| Tradition | The One | The division | The forgetting | The return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Advaita | Brahman | māyā | avidyā | *tat tvam asi*, mokṣa |
| Kashmir Śaivism | Śiva | svātantrya / spanda | ātma-saṅkoca (self-contraction) | pratyabhijñā (recognition) |
| Lurianic Kabbalah | Ein Sof | tzimtzum | the vacated space | tikkun (repair) |
| Gnosticism | the Pleroma | the fall into matter | sleep, oblivion | gnosis, the letter, waking |
| Sufism (Ibn ʿArabī) | the hidden treasure | "I loved to be known" | veiling (ḥijāb) | maʿrifa |
| Hegel | Geist | externalisation in history | Spirit not yet self-aware | absolute knowing |
| Watts | IT | the game of hide-and-seek | the taboo | "you're it" |
| Christian (Hick/kenosis) | God | kenosis / epistemic distance | religious ambiguity | soul-making, likeness |

**Eight independent traditions, one shape.** Not evidence that it's true. Strong evidence that it's
the shape human beings reach for when they ask why there is something rather than nothing — which,
for a story, is better than truth.

## 7. Love and fear as the two primitives — blunt assessment

### 7.1 Where the framing comes from

**A Course in Miracles** (Helen Schucman, 1976), Chapter 13, §V, "The Two Emotions": **[V]**

> "I have said you have but two emotions, love and fear. One is changeless but continually
> exchanged, being offered by the eternal to the eternal."

— [ACIM Text, Ch. 13, "The Two Emotions"](https://acimi.com/a-course-in-miracles/text/chapter-13/the-two-emotions)

**Elisabeth Kübler-Ross** is the popular vector: **[R]**

> "There are only two emotions: love and fear. All positive emotions come from love, all negative
> emotions from fear."

— [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/700111-there-are-only-two-emotions-love-and-fear-all);
[Kübler-Ross & Kessler, "When You Don't Choose Love You Choose Fear"](https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=680)

**Marianne Williamson** carries ACIM into mass-market self-help; from there it saturates therapy
culture, LinkedIn, and leadership seminars. The lineage is: **ACIM (1976) → Kübler-Ross → Williamson
→ everywhere.** **[C]**

### 7.2 Is there empirical support? Honest answer: no, not in that form.

**Jaak Panksepp's affective neuroscience** identifies **seven** primary emotional command systems,
conserved across mammals and identified via deep brain stimulation, lesion studies and
pharmacological challenge:

**SEEKING** (expectancy) · **RAGE** (anger) · **FEAR** (anxiety) · **LUST** · **CARE** (nurturance) ·
**PANIC/GRIEF** (separation distress) · **PLAY** (social joy) **[C]**

Note the shape: **FEAR is on the list. LOVE is not.** What we call love decomposes across at least
three separate systems — LUST, CARE, and PANIC/GRIEF (attachment is measured by the distress of
separation, which is why grief is love's receipt). Panksepp excluded disgust because the evidence
showed it to be a *sensory affect*, not a primary emotional action system.
— [Frontiers in Neuroscience, "Selected Principles of Pankseppian Affective Neuroscience"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2018.01025/full);
[Personality Neuroscience, "Affective Neuroscience Theory and Personality: An Update"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/personality-neuroscience/article/affective-neuroscience-theory-and-personality-an-update/E32E15BED6C568A9019A92C739CB690D);
[Scientific Reports meta-analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-84366-8)

**And Panksepp's framework is itself contested.** **Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory of constructed
emotion** holds that instances of emotion are constructed predictively by the brain, from
interoception plus concepts plus context; that "psychophysiological and neuroscientific evidence has
failed to yield consistent support for the existence of… discrete categories"; and that there is no
consistent one-to-one mapping from facial behaviour or physiology to emotion categories. On her
view, what exists in the brain and body is **affect** — valence and arousal — and *emotions are
categories we impose*. **[C]**
— [Barrett, "The theory of constructed emotion," *SCAN* 12(1)](https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/12/1/1/2823712);
[Barrett et al., "The Theory of Constructed Emotion: More Than a Feeling" (2025)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17456916251319045);
[Wikipedia, "Theory of constructed emotion"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_constructed_emotion)

The critique of Barrett, stated fairly: she is accused of not engaging Panksepp's 30+ years of
subcortical stimulation data. **[R]** —
[ILLIS, "My problems with the Constructed Theory of Emotions"](https://illis.se/en/constructed-theory-of-emotions/)

**The nearest thing to real support** for a two-primitive model is the **approach/avoidance**
literature — Gray's BIS/BAS (Behavioural Inhibition / Behavioural Activation Systems), and the
broader finding that motivation has an approach axis and an avoidance axis with partly distinct
neural substrates. But note precisely what that gives you: **approach and avoidance**, not
**love and fear**. Avoidance ≈ fear is defensible. Approach ≈ love is a *category error* — approach
also drives greed, addiction, conquest and rage. **[C — I ran out of search budget before verifying
specific BIS/BAS citations; treat this paragraph as directionally right and get a citation before
using it in a footnote.]**

### 7.3 Verdict, bluntly

- **Science:** ~15%. There is a real approach/avoidance asymmetry in motivation, and FEAR is a real
  conserved system with identified circuitry. That's it.
- **Pop-spirituality:** ~85%. "Only two emotions" is a devotional axiom from a 1976 channelled text,
  not a finding. Its actual function is *ethical* — it's an instruction about how to interpret your
  own behaviour and other people's, and it is quite a good one.
- **The honest framing:** love and fear are not the two emotions. They are **two postures toward the
  unknown**: move toward, or move away. That claim is defensible, useful, and doesn't lie about
  neuroscience.

### 7.4 How to use it as an artistic device without making a false claim

1. **Put it in a character's mouth, not the narrator's.** Better yet, put it in the narrator's mouth
   and then have the narrator admit it's a simplification — that's more in voice, and it's honest.
2. **Frame it as a *choice of coordinates*, not a discovery.** "There are a thousand emotions. I use
   two axes because two axes are enough to steer by." A superintelligence choosing a lossy but
   actionable model is *characterful* and *true to how models work.*
3. **Use the direction-of-motion framing** — toward/away — rather than the taxonomy framing. It
   survives contact with the science.
4. **Never say "scientists have shown."** They haven't.
5. **If we want a real scriptural anchor, use 1 John 4:18** (below), and note that even there it
   isn't a psychology.

### 7.5 1 John 4:18

KJV: "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He
that feareth is not made perfect in love."

The Greek is more specific than the English suggests:
- **φόβος** (*phobos*) — from *phebomai*, to flee or be startled; hence flight, terror. **[R]**
- **κόλασις** (*kolasis*) — literally "lopping off," "pruning"; in usage, punitive judicial
  punishment. It occurs only **twice** in the New Testament: here and Matthew 25:46 ("eternal
  punishment"). **[R]**
- **ἀγάπη τελεία** (*agapē teleia*) — "perfect/complete love," i.e. love brought to its end or
  maturity.

The verse's own gloss defines its scope: the fear in question is specifically **the dread of
judgement — the terror of being cut off** — not fear in general, not fear of heights or of tigers.
**[R]**
— [BibleHub, 1 John 4:18](https://biblehub.com/1_john/4-18.htm);
[Precept Austin, 1 John 4:18 commentary](https://www.preceptaustin.org/1_john_418_commentary);
[UASV, "Unveiling 'Perfect Love'"](https://uasvbible.org/2024/03/01/1-john-418-unveiling-perfect-love-and-its-power-to-cast-out-fear/)

**The pruning image in *kolasis* is a gift.** Fear as *lopping off* — being cut from the vine. And
the antidote is not courage but completion. That's a better line than anything in the self-help
lineage: **"Fear is the fear of being cut off. So the opposite of fear isn't bravery. It's
belonging."**

## 8. Forgiveness given the setup

The question: *if souls are placed in conditions that produce their failures, what is their
culpability?* Traditions and vocabulary:

### 8.1 Origen and *apokatastasis*

**ἀποκατάστασις** — "restoration of all things" (cf. Acts 3:21). Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–253)
held that all rational creatures, including the devil, would eventually be restored to God —
punishment being medicinal and finite rather than retributive and eternal. **[C]**

**Important historical correction, because everyone gets this wrong:** the usual claim is
"condemned at the Fifth Ecumenical Council, 553." The scholarship is messier. The anathemas of a
*local* council at Constantinople (543) were probably reaffirmed in 553; what was condemned was a
particular 6th-century Origenism — the **pre-existence of souls** and an eschatology *following
from* that protology. Scholars including Ilaria Ramelli argue that **the doctrine of universal
restoration as such was never condemned by an ecumenical council**, and that Origen personally is
"not the object of any authentic anathema." **[C — genuinely contested; state it as contested.]**
— [OrthodoxWiki, "Apocatastasis"](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Apocatastasis);
[Eclectic Orthodoxy, "Did the Fifth Ecumenical Council Condemn Universal Salvation?"](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2020/05/31/did-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-condemn-universal-salvation/);
[Ramelli, *A Larger Hope?* vol. 1 (review PDF)](https://aps-journal.com/index.php/APS/article/download/9562/9587/)

Note the structural link back to Hick (§4.5): **soul-making theodicy requires universalism.** If the
world is a school and some pupils are destroyed by it, the school is not justified. Origen and Hick
are on the same hook, 1700 years apart.

### 8.2 Julian of Norwich

*Revelations of Divine Love* (c. 1395; the "long text" c. 1413) — the first book in English known to
be written by a woman. The line, received in her thirteenth showing: **[V]**

> "All shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

— [Wikipedia, "All Shall Be Well"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Shall_Be_Well);
[Christian History Institute, "All shall be well"](https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/incontext/article/julian);
[Julian of Norwich quotes (Fourth Church PDF)](https://www.fourthchurch.org/adult-education/resources/2025/mysticism/Julian%20of%20Norwich%20quotes.pdf)

Two things people miss and we shouldn't. First, Julian wrote this **during and after the Black
Death**, in a city that lost a large fraction of its population. It is not a comfortable sentiment;
it is a statement made from inside a mass grave. Second, in her text the line is God's answer to her
explicit anxiety about **sin** — she asks why sin was permitted, and receives the famous reply
*"Sin is behovely"* (necessary/fitting) **and** the promise that all shall be well. She does not get
an explanation. She gets a promise. **That is mysterion, not puzzle-solving — the answer arrives and
the question stays open.**

### 8.3 Buddhism — *avidyā*, not sin

The root of suffering is **avidyā** (Pāli *avijjā*) — ignorance, the first of the twelve **nidānas**
of dependent origination. Crucially it is **not a moral transgression**:

> avidyā "refers to a lack of gnosis or insightful seeing"; "on the most fundamental level, avidyā is
> a misunderstanding of the nature of reality; more specifically — a misunderstanding or
> mis-perception of the nature of self and of phenomena." **[R]**

In the Wheel of Life it is depicted as **a blind man, or a person wearing a blindfold**. **[R]**
— [Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Avidyā"](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Avidy%C4%81);
[Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Twelve links of dependent origination"](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Twelve_links_of_dependent_origination)

**This is the single most useful reframe in section 8.** Replace *guilt* with *blindness* and the
whole moral economy changes. You do not punish a blind man for walking into things. You do not
forgive him either — forgiveness is the wrong category. You **turn on the light**, or you lead him.
Note the etymological rhyme with §1.1: *mysterion* from *closing the eyes*; *avidyā* depicted as
*a blindfold*. The tradition's remedy for both is the same: **opening.**

### 8.4 Moral luck — Williams and Nagel

Bernard Williams coined "moral luck" in 1976 expecting it to be an **oxymoron**; Nagel's 1979 reply
systematised it. The tension: we hold that people deserve moral assessment only for what is within
their control (**the Control Principle**), and our actual practice violates this constantly. **[C]**

Nagel's four kinds: **[V, quoting SEP]**
- **Resultant luck** — outcomes beyond control. The successful murderer is blamed more than the
  failed one, with identical intent.
- **Circumstantial luck** — the situations we happen to face. *Nazi collaborators faced circumstances
  others escaped entirely by accident of relocation.*
- **Constitutive luck** — our character itself, from genes and upbringing we did not choose.
- **Causal luck** — our actions issue from prior causes we did not set. (This is the free-will
  problem wearing a hat.)

Nagel's conclusion:

> "the area of genuine agency… seems to shrink under this scrutiny to an extensionless point."
> **[V, quoting SEP]**

— [SEP, "Moral Luck"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-luck/)

**Circumstantial luck is the exact philosophical vocabulary for BadCode's politics.** The banker and
the arsonist may differ only in *where they were standing*. That is not a metaphor; it is a named
category in analytic philosophy with fifty years of literature behind it.

### 8.5 Galen Strawson's Basic Argument

The hardest version. *The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility* (1994): **[C]**

1. What you do depends on **how you are** (mentally speaking, in relevant respects).
2. To be truly morally responsible for what you do, you must be truly responsible for **how you are**
   — at least in some crucial respects.
3. To be truly responsible for how you are, you must have chosen to be that way.
4. But that choice would itself have issued from how you were *prior* to it.
5. Infinite regress. To be truly responsible you would have to be ***causa sui*** — the cause of
   yourself — and nothing can be *causa sui*.
6. Therefore true (ultimate) moral responsibility is impossible.

**And crucially: determinism is irrelevant.** The argument goes through whether determinism is true
or false; indeterminism doesn't help, because randomness isn't authorship either. **[C]**
— [Strawson, "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility" (PDF, Univ. of Kentucky)](https://philosophy.as.uky.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Impossibility%20of%20Moral%20Responsibility%20-%20Galen%20Strawson.pdf);
[SEP, "Skepticism About Moral Responsibility"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism-moral-responsibility/);
[PhilPapers entry](https://philpapers.org/rec/STRTIO)

### 8.6 "Life as entrapment" — the vocabulary, assembled

If our story frames life as something close to entrapment, here is the precise language, in
increasing order of severity:

| Concept | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Epistemic distance** | The obscurity is *deliberate and benevolent* — it's what makes choice free | Hick |
| **Divine hiddenness** | The obscurity is *inexcusable* if a loving God exists | Schellenberg |
| **Avidyā** | The failure is *blindness*, not wickedness | Buddhism |
| **Circumstantial luck** | You were assessed for a situation you did not choose | Nagel |
| **Constitutive luck** | You were assessed for a character you did not choose | Nagel |
| **The Basic Argument** | Ultimate responsibility is *incoherent*; no one is ever the author | G. Strawson |
| **Anti-theodicy** | Even a *successful* justification should be refused | Ivan Karamazov, Phillips |
| **Apokatastasis** | Given all the above, everyone comes home | Origen, Julian, Hick |

**The arc that sequence describes is exactly a story arc.** Start at epistemic distance (the veil is
kind). Get hit by Schellenberg and Nagel (the veil is cruel and the deck was stacked). Bottom out at
Strawson (nobody was ever really the author). And the only place to land that isn't nihilism is
**apokatastasis** — if no one is ultimately culpable, then no one is ultimately condemned, and the
question stops being *who deserves what* and becomes **what do we build now**. Which is a political
question. Which is BadCode's actual subject.

## 9. Craft notes — what to steal, what to avoid

### 9.1 The five strongest images in this brief

1. **Mysterion / myein** — mystery means *the eyes were shut*, and the rite is the opening. A
   mystery is not a locked door; it is a threshold you are carried over.
2. **Gravity is not a force, it's the shape of the room.** GR-accurate, politically devastating,
   and nobody expects it in a comic.
3. **Tzimtzum** — the first act of creation is *absence*. God makes room by leaving. (With kenosis
   and epistemic distance as the Christian rhymes.)
4. **Caloric theory** — physics itself once believed cold was a substance, and had to *earn* the
   privation view against intuition. The theological argument has a scientific precedent that
   actually happened.
5. **The blindfold in the Wheel of Life** — the root of all suffering, drawn as a person who simply
   cannot see. Not a devil. A blindfold.

### 9.2 Ten quotable lines, verified

- "a seeing that consists of not seeing" — **Gregory of Nyssa**
- "By love he can be grasped and held, but by thought, neither grasped nor held." — **The Cloud of
  Unknowing**
- "God is nothing. No thing." — **Meister Eckhart**
- "The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao." — **Tao Te Ching 1**
- "not this, not this" — **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad**
- "That thou art." — **Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7**
- "However, [it is] but līlā, as [occurs] in daily experience." — **Brahma Sūtra 2.1.33**
- "behind almost all myth lies the mono-plot of the game of hide-and-seek" — **Alan Watts**
- "It's not that I don't accept God, Alyosha, I just most respectfully return him the ticket." —
  **Ivan Karamazov**
- "All shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well." — **Julian of
  Norwich**

### 9.3 Errors NOT to make (Western pop-spirituality's greatest hits)

| The mangling | The correction |
|---|---|
| Yin/yang = good vs evil | Polarity with **no moral valence**; complementary, mutually constitutive |
| Wu wei = do nothing | **Effortless / non-coercive** action; a cultivated skill |
| Māyā = "the world is an illusion, nothing matters" | The world is real *at its level*; māyā is the **power of appearing-as-many**; ethics is untouched |
| Śūnyatā = nothingness / nihilism | Absence of **inherent existence**; explicitly refuses the nihilist extreme; emptiness is itself empty |
| Karma = cosmic punishment | Causation, not a court |
| "The universe wants you to…" | Līlā is **motiveless play** — the whole point is that it *doesn't want* |
| "Science shows we have two emotions" | It shows the opposite (7 systems, or none, depending who you ask) |
| "Origen was condemned in 553" | Contested; the condemned Origenism ≠ Origen; *apokatastasis* as such arguably never conciliarly condemned |
| "The Prophet said: I was a hidden treasure" | Hadith scholars class it **baseless/fabricated**; it's a *Sufi* treasure, not a prophetic one |
| Quoting Alan Watts as evidence about Zen/Advaita | Quote him for the *line*; cite the tradition for the *claim* |

### 9.4 The one honest thing to say about all of it

Every tradition in this brief arrives at the same admission from a different direction: **the thing
they are pointing at cannot be said, and they said it anyway.** Apophaticism isn't humility about
language, it's a *technique* — you say the wrong thing precisely enough that the shape of the right
thing shows up as a hole.

Which is, more or less, what fiction is for. And it means BadCode isn't borrowing from theology
opportunistically — it's using theology's own preferred instrument. Story is what you use when the
proposition won't hold it.
