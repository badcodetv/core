## 2. Kastrup's analytic idealism in plain words

**The core claims (all tier D: argued, not measured):**

1. **There is one thing, and it is experience.** Not matter with minds sprinkled in; one universal field of mind ("mentation"), and that is all there is.
2. **You are a dissociated piece of it.** Each living being is an "alter" of that one mind, sealed off from the rest the way a person with dissociative identity disorder (DID) has alters that cannot reach each other's memories. Kastrup's image: a **whirlpool** in a stream. It is really there and has a boundary, and it is made of nothing but the stream ("Ripples and whirlpools", 2014: <https://www.bernardokastrup.com/2014/07/ripples-and-whirlpools.html>).
3. **The physical world is what mind looks like from outside.** Your brain is what your inner life looks like to someone else. The inanimate universe is the outward look of the universal mind's own processes. Matter is the *appearance*, not the thing.
4. **Perception is a dashboard.** Our senses show the world "in a coded and truncated form as a dashboard of dials." The dial is real and is about something real, but it does not look like what it measures. (Quoted and discussed in the August brief, §2.)
5. **Death is the end of the dissociation**, not the end of the mind (Kastrup's reading; his book *Brief Peeks Beyond* and others). Tier D, and it brushes the afterlife: handle with care.
6. **Computers will not be conscious:** a simulation of a kidney does not urinate on your desk, so a simulation of a brain does not experience. (IAI essay "The lunacy of 'machine consciousness'": <https://iai.tv/articles/bernardo-kastrup-the-lunacy-of-machine-consciousness-auid-2363>; reply to Susan Schneider: <https://bernardokastrup.com/2023/01/ai-wont-be-conscious-and-here-is-why.html>.) The exact wording varies between essay and talks; quote the essay, not the "pee" version.

**What Kastrup himself concedes:** the dissociation step is an analogy without a formal model. He listed a formal model as an open job for himself in 2024 (August brief §2, criticism 1). That is the biggest hole.

## 3. The studies Essentia and Kastrup lean on

Rule for this section: **"Found"** is what the study measured. **"Kastrup's reading"** is what he takes from it. **"Caveat"** is what the rest of the field says. A short may cite the first column freely; the second only as *Kastrup argues*.

### 3a. The DID blindness case

- **Study:** Hans Strasburger & Bruno Waldvogel, "Sight and blindness in the same person: Gating in the visual system," *PsyCh Journal* 4(4): 178–185, 2015. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26468893/>
- **Found:** one patient with DID, diagnosed as cortically blind for about 15 years, regained sight during psychotherapy, at first in only some personality states. Switches took seconds. In blind states, visual evoked potentials (the EEG response to a flash or pattern) were absent; in sighted states they were present.
- **Kastrup's reading:** proof of concept that one mind can split into sealed compartments that are physically different, so the brain is what a dissociated mind *looks like*, not what makes it. Used in the 2018 JCS paper and the *Scientific American* blog.
- **Caveat:** **one patient.** The authors' own explanation is ordinary neuroscience: top-down gating of the visual pathway, possibly at the thalamus. They also note VEPs cannot tell psychogenic blindness from organic damage. DID itself is a contested diagnosis (the long-running trauma vs sociocognitive dispute). Tier: **A** that this happened in this patient; **E** that it says anything about the universe.

### 3b. Psychedelics and "less brain activity, more experience"

- **Study:** Robin Carhart-Harris et al., "Neural correlates of the psychedelic state as determined by fMRI studies with psilocybin," *PNAS* 109(6): 2138–2143, 2012. doi:10.1073/pnas.1119598109
- **Found:** 15 volunteers (arterial spin labelling) and 15 (BOLD fMRI), intravenous psilocybin. **Only decreases** in blood flow and BOLD signal, biggest in hub regions (thalamus, anterior and posterior cingulate, medial prefrontal cortex); the size of the mPFC drop predicted how intense the trip felt.
- **Kastrup's reading:** the richest experiences come with *less* brain activity, which a brain-makes-mind theory should not predict, and which fits a brain that *filters* or *localises* mind (Bergson, Huxley's "reducing valve"). Kastrup and Edward Kelly pressed this in "Misreporting and Confirmation Bias in Psychedelic Research," *Scientific American* blog, 2018: <https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/misreporting-and-confirmation-bias-in-psychedelic-research/>
- **The later and contrary findings:**
  - Carhart-Harris et al., "Neural correlates of the LSD experience revealed by multimodal neuroimaging," *PNAS* 113(17): 4853–4858, 2016 (doi:10.1073/pnas.1518377113): **increased** blood flow in visual cortex under LSD, tracking the visuals.
  - Schartner, Carhart-Harris, Barrett, Seth & Muthukumaraswamy, "Increased spontaneous MEG signal diversity for psychoactive doses of ketamine, LSD and psilocybin," *Scientific Reports* 7: 46421, 2017 (doi:10.1038/srep46421): brain signals become **more varied (higher entropy)**, the opposite direction to sleep and anaesthesia.
  - **The direct reply:** Anil Seth, Michael Schartner, Enzo Tagliazucchi, Suresh Muthukumaraswamy, Robin Carhart-Harris and Adam Barrett, "What Psychedelic Research Can and Cannot Tell Us about Consciousness," *Scientific American* blog, 26 Oct 2018: <https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/what-psychedelic-research-can-and-cannot-tell-us-about-consciousness/>. Their words: the early psilocybin blood-flow decreases "have not been well replicated," and Kastrup and Kelly "fall foul of their own critique by misconstruing and oversimplifying the details of the studies they discuss." Kastrup's answer: <https://bernardokastrup.com/2018/10/setting-record-straight-with-robin.html>
  - The entropy story is shaky too: a 2023 preprint (McCulloch et al., medRxiv 10.1101/2023.07.03.23292164) reports twelve fMRI studies of psychedelic "brain entropy," no finding replicated, and 8 of 13 entropy metrics showing no significant effect in a new cohort (secondary; preprint).
- **Tier: E.** **Never say "psychedelics switch the brain off."** The team that ran the 2012 study says that reading is wrong. The honest line: *the first scans showed less activity; later scans mostly didn't; nobody's measure of "how much brain" maps cleanly onto "how much experience."*

### 3c. Terminal (paradoxical) lucidity

- **Studies:**
  - Michael Nahm & Bruce Greyson coined "terminal lucidity" (2009). Nahm, Greyson, Kelly & Haraldsson, "Terminal lucidity: a review and a case collection," *Archives of Gerontology and Geriatrics* 55(1): 138–142, 2012 (volume and pages unverified): mostly case reports, much of it from 19th-century medical literature.
  - Alexander Batthyány & Bruce Greyson, "Spontaneous remission of dementia before death: Results from a study on paradoxical lucidity," *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice*, online 2020, print 2021: a survey of carers' reports. PDF: <https://med.virginia.edu/perceptual-studies/wp-content/uploads/sites/360/2021/04/terminal-lucidity-PC.pdf>
  - George Mashour et al. (a workgroup convened by the US National Institute on Aging), "Paradoxical lucidity: A potential paradigm shift for the neurobiology and treatment of severe dementias," *Alzheimer's & Dementia*, 2019 (doi:10.1016/j.jalz.2019.04.002). This is the mainstream anchor: the NIA took it seriously enough to fund research.
- **Found:** carers and clinicians report that some people with severe dementia have brief, unexpected episodes of clear recognition and speech, often near death. Prevalence, mechanism and how often it is misremembered are all unknown.
- **Kastrup's reading:** if a badly damaged brain can briefly give back a whole person, the brain looks less like the generator and more like a filter that is loosening.
- **Caveat:** retrospective, anecdotal, no physiological recordings during an episode; definitions still argued (terminal vs paradoxical). Mashour's paper says "if systematically confirmed." The mainstream hypothesis is network-level recovery of function, not mind outside brain. **Tier: E** (that it happens: credible reports, unmeasured; what it means: open).

### 3d. Near-death experiences: AWARE II

- **Study:** Sam Parnia et al. (25 sites, US and UK), "AWAreness during REsuscitation – II: A multi-center study of consciousness and awareness in cardiac arrest," *Resuscitation* 191: 109903, 2023. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.109903. Press release: <https://www.elsevier.com/about/press-releases/new-evidence-indicates-patients-recall-death-experiences-after-cardiac>
- **Found:** 567 in-hospital cardiac arrests → 53 survivors → 28 interviewed. 11 of the 28 had memories or perceptions suggesting consciousness during CPR; 6 had "transcendent recalled experiences of death." In 85 patients with brain monitoring, near-normal EEG patterns reappeared as late as 35–60 minutes into CPR. **The hidden-target test:** a tablet showed one of ten images above the bed; headphones played "apple, pear, banana." **Nobody identified the image.** The abstract says "few" identified the stimuli; one press summary gives 1 of 28 for the spoken words, another source says none (sources disagree; the primary paper settles it, not read this pass).
- **Parnia's own caveat:** research has "neither proved nor disproved the reality or meaning of patients' experiences."
- **The counter-finding:** Gang Xu … Jimo Borjigin, "Surge of neurophysiological coupling and connectivity of gamma oscillations in the dying human brain," *PNAS*, 2023 (doi:10.1073/pnas.2216268120): in 2 of 4 dying patients, a surge of gamma activity after ventilator removal, suggesting the dying brain itself may generate the experience. Also tiny (n=4), and the authors say it may be pathology, not awareness.
- **Tier: A** that people report these experiences and that some EEG returns during long CPR; **E** for what they mean. **The one thing a short can say with full confidence is the punchline:** *they put a picture on a shelf where only a floating soul could see it, and nobody saw it.* Use it with care: it cuts *against* the idealist reading.

### 3e. Hoffman's interface theory

- Hoffman, Singh & Prakash, "The Interface Theory of Perception," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 2015: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-015-0890-8>
- Prakash, Stephens, Hoffman, Singh & Fields, "Fitness Beats Truth in the Evolution of Perception," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 2021: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33231784/>
- Hoffman, Prakash & Prentner, "Fusions of Consciousness," *Entropy* 25(1): 129, 2023: <https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/25/1/129>
- Popular: Amanda Gefter's interview, "The Evolutionary Argument Against Reality," *Quanta*, 21 April 2016: <https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-evolutionary-argument-against-reality-20160421/> (the snakes-and-trains passage: take your perceptions *seriously*, not *literally*).
- **Found:** a mathematical result: in evolutionary game simulations, organisms tuned to payoff outcompete ones tuned to truth. **Tier A as maths, E as metaphysics.** The best attack: "Debunking interface theory: why Hoffman's skepticism (really) is self-defeating," *Synthese*, 2022: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-022-04021-1>. Full critique list in the August brief §3d.
- **A real-world exhibit Hoffman likes to use** (in his 2015 TED talk, "Do we see reality as it is?"; unverified this pass): Australian jewel beetles trying to mate with discarded beer bottles. The beetle study itself is solid and funny: Gwynne & Rentz, "Beetles on the bottle: male buprestids mistake stubbies for females," *J. Australian Entomological Society* 22: 79–80, 1983; **Ig Nobel Prize, 2011**; the brewer changed the bottle. <https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/scicurious-brain/ignobel-prize-winner-the-beetle-and-the-beer-bottle-a-tragic-love-story/>. **Tier A** for the beetles.

### 3f. The quantum results Kastrup cites

| Experiment | What it actually shows | What Kastrup takes from it | How physicists dispute it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gröblacher et al., "An experimental test of non-local realism," *Nature* 446: 871–875, 2007 (arXiv:0704.2529) | Violates a Leggett inequality: rules out **one class** of non-local hidden-variable models. Giving up locality alone is not enough "unless certain intuitive features of realism are abandoned" | Physical properties do not exist before measurement, so the world is not made of observer-independent stuff | It excludes a specific family of models, not realism in general. Bohmian mechanics and many-worlds are untouched. Even the class excluded depends on assumptions (Paterek et al., PRL 2007, widened it; arXiv:0710.3220 disputed the inequality) |
| Proietti et al., "Experimental test of local observer independence," *Science Advances* 5: eaaw9832, 2019 (unverified page id) | A six-photon Wigner's-friend test: two "observers" can record facts that cannot be reconciled, under stated assumptions | Facts are relative to observers, not out there | The "friends" are single photons, not observers in any rich sense. arXiv:1901.10825 argues the experiment does not establish the title's claim or truly instantiate Wigner's friend (the friend's lab is not isolated) |
| Bong et al., "A strong no-go theorem on the Wigner's friend paradox," *Nature Physics* 16: 1199–1205, 2020 | Proves that if quantum theory holds at the scale of observers, no theory can keep all of "Local Friendliness" (freedom of choice, locality, absoluteness of observed events). Their photon test violates LF inequalities | Observed events are not absolute; observers matter | The test used photons as "friends," so it is a proof of principle. Cavalcanti & Wiseman (*Entropy*, 2021: <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8392567/>) note the inequalities might not be violated with genuine observers. "Reassessing the strength of a class of Wigner's friend no-go theorems" (arXiv:2204.12015) argues they constrain reality less than claimed. **Physicists can keep realism by giving up locality (Bohm) or single outcomes (Everett)** |

**Tier for all three as Kastrup uses them: E.** The experiments are A. The step from "no observer-independent definite facts *in this model class*" to "the world is mental" is Kastrup's, and it is the step our own doctrine already forbids: **never imply observer = mind** (doctrine §1). Sean Carroll's line on the 2018 *Scientific American* piece is the one to remember: what it called "the current paradigm" is "something that essentially no working physicist actually believes" (<https://x.com/seanmcarroll/status/988800317717266432>).

## 4. The strongest critics, so we never overclaim

- **Sean Carroll** (physicist). On the quantum argument: above. On consciousness generally: "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics," 2021 (arXiv:2101.07884): any mental ingredient in the ontology must either **change the known laws of physics** (show the equations) or **have no causal effect** on anything physical. The laws of everyday life are known; electrons do not behave differently in brains. Aimed at panpsychism, but it hits any idealism that wants mind to *do* anything in physics.
- **Anil Seth** (neuroscientist, Sussex; *Being You*, 2021). Perception is a "controlled hallucination" generated by a predicting brain: the *same phenomenology* as Hoffman's interface and Kastrup's dashboard, read the opposite way. Co-led the psychedelics rebuttal (3b): "Empirical consciousness science, based firmly on materialistic assumptions, is doing just fine."
- **The psychedelic neuroimagers** (Carhart-Harris, Muthukumaraswamy, Tagliazucchi, Schartner, Barrett): the very people whose data Kastrup cites say he misreads it (3b).
- **Philip Goff** (panpsychist, Durham). Debated Kastrup at HowTheLightGetsIn 2020 (<https://iai.tv/articles/conscious-universe-panpsychism-idealism-goff-kastrup-auid-1584>); "I ultimately don't quite buy Bernardo's idealist view," though he calls it "a really important contribution." Kastrup's replies: <https://bernardokastrup.com/2020/07/the-irony-of-philip-goffs-arguments.html>. The useful symmetry: panpsychism can't explain how small minds **combine**; idealism can't explain how one mind **splits** (dissociation is the patch).
- **Peter Vickers** (philosopher of science): theories like these cannot be settled by scientific evidence at all (<https://iai.tv/articles/idealism-panpsychism-and-science-auid-1700>). Kastrup replied that this is a majority-vote view of truth.
- **Richard Carrier:** the metabolism criterion and the logic of the argument (fallacies named); harsh in tone, specific in substance (<https://www.richardcarrier.info/archives/32492>).
- **Skeptical Inquirer** reviewed the DID essay (Jan/Feb 2019); Kastrup called it "nothing short of pathetic" (<https://www.bernardokastrup.com/2018/12/nothing-short-of-pathetic-skeptical.html>). The fight is real; it is not a fringe fan club versus nobody.
- **Hoffman's critics:** the *Synthese* self-defeat argument; Jonathan Cohen's counterexamples; Paul Austin Murphy on the amplituhedron borrowing (August brief §3d).
- **Roger Penrose**, on Essentia's own channel, thinks quantum theory is incomplete and declines Faggin's and Kastrup's reading of it.

**The summary sentence (already doctrine, and it survives every critic above): *the data underdetermine the metaphysics.*** Every study in section 3 is consistent with mind-from-brain *and* with brain-as-appearance-of-mind. That is the licence for a wager, not evidence for a winner.

## 6. Do not say

Adds to the doctrine kill list; the kill list still applies in full (no *energy*, *vibration*, *quantum* + a mind word, *awakening*, *simulation*, *reality is an illusion*, *science has shown*).

- **"Science has proven consciousness is fundamental"** / **"the Essentia Foundation proves..."** No study in this file shows it. Kastrup himself calls it an inference.
- **"Quantum experiments prove the observer creates reality."** The photon "friends" are photons. Observer ≠ mind (doctrine §1).
- **"Psychedelics switch the brain off" / "less brain, more mind."** The original lab says the reading is wrong and did not replicate.
- **"NDEs prove life after death"** / **"patients saw from the ceiling."** AWARE II's hidden image was seen by nobody.
- **"Dying brains light up = the soul leaving"** (or the reverse, "gamma surge proves it's all brain"). n=4; the authors say it may be pathology.
- **"Terminal lucidity proves the brain is a filter."** Anecdotes, no recordings.
- **"Multiple personalities explain the universe."** It's an analogy; mocking DID patients is out (they are real people with a contested diagnosis).
- **"Hoffman proved reality isn't real."** He proved a theorem about simulated fitness games; the leap is his.
- **"Kastrup, a CERN scientist..."** He worked at CERN as an engineer; his physics standing is not a physicist's.
- **"Penrose agrees."** On Essentia's own channel he disagrees.
- **Quoting the "pee on my desk" version** as Kastrup's words. Quote the essay ("urinate on my desk").
- **Using Essentia as the authority.** Name the person and the paper.
