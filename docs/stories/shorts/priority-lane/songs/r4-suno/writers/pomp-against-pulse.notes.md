# pomp-against-pulse — notes (redo)

Rewritten after the sameness gate flagged three shared phrases in the Style box ("monitor tone then", "accents no singing", "flat monitor tone then dead silence"). Those are gone: the opening now asks for "English-spoken radio-play drama, voices talk rather than sing", and the ending is "a long unbroken ECG whine closes it, followed by total quiet". The exclude box says "sung vocals" instead of "singing". Settings and the experiment's concept are unchanged.

What shaped it:
- Ben Foster describes the Happy Valley score as guitars, solo viola and dirty, dark, fierce synths from vintage instruments. That is why the ambulance has a lone viola and snarling vintage synths, with a taiko pulse added as the heartbeat. I described the sound rather than naming the show in the Style box. https://lbbonline.com/news/composer-ben-foster-announces-soundtrack-release-for-bbcs-happy-valley · https://filmmusicreporter.com/2017/11/21/happy-valley-soundtrack-album-announced/
- Suno takes the language and accent from words in the Style field. Spoken lines need a delivery tag ([Spoken], [Shouted], [Whispered]) or Suno tends to sing them, so every line has its own tag naming its speaker. Keep tags to the key moments. (These are community guides, not Suno's official docs.) https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-4-5-metatag-tutorial · https://www.blakecrosley.com/guides/suno · https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-prompts-guide-2026/
- To keep the two worlds apart, each one is named with its own voice: London/Sheffield crew in the ambulance, an "Etonian toff" (unmistakably upper-class English) in the limousine behind glass. The regal brass and organ are said to be the only grandeur in the track.
