# no-score-fly-on-the-wall — notes (redo)

Redo: the gate flagged "spoken english voices" shared with other style boxes. That phrase is gone; the style box now says "talking and yelling only" and names each accent by city instead. Several other phrases were reworded too ("actuality audio" became "medical actuality, hidden mic", "two-pitch" became "hi-lo nee-naw horn", "total quiet" became "dead air").

What shaped it:
- The Pitt has no score. Its sound team takes the music out and lets clunks, glove snaps and room tone fill the gaps, because a score "pushes viewers back in their chairs" (Bryan Parker). That is why the box lists real-room sounds and allows only one musical element, the sub hum under the minister.

  - https://www.slashfilm.com/1842794/noah-wyle-the-pitt-no-music-explained
  - https://www.asoundeffect.com/?p=806859
- Suno speech: label the delivery in brackets, keep lines short, and say "nobody sings / no tune" so Suno doesn't sing the line or the tag. One user report says a bare "[Spoken word]" tag sometimes gets sung, so the tags here describe the scene instead.
  - https://jackrighteous.com/ja/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-spoken-narration-workflow
  - https://sunowatermark.com/blog/suno-v6-vocal-prompts-2026/
- Accent: no source shows that Suno reliably follows an accent instruction (unverified). So each voice is named by city and dialect name (Scouse/Liverpool, Geordie/Newcastle, Bristol West Country, drawn-out Etonian vowels), and US accents and the US siren are excluded. The toff is made "tinny, behind the sealed glass of a ministerial Jaguar" so he also sounds different on the mix.
