# uk-procedural-piano-motor — what shaped it

- **Reference sound:** the BBC procedural score, Carly Paradis's Line of Duty theme, a pianist's melody described as "circling round and round" that whips up tension, with music acting as "a secret character" under the drama. Ruth Barrett's Bodyguard score (same producer, Jed Mercurio) is the sibling: a piano-trained composer, mostly electronic, briefed on "what does panic sound like". No artist or show names go in the prompt; the style box describes the sound.

- **Suno v6 phrasing:** a third-party guide says `[End]`-style tags are not reliable controls in v6. Instead, describe the ending in plain words, say there is no fade, and don't use "gradually reduce" wording. So the prompt says "every instrument cuts out together on the same beat. Dead stop, silence after", with fade-out and reverb tail in the exclude box. Guides also say to put the tempo first and name the first instrument heard, so it opens "140 BPM, 5/4" and the piano starts "from the very first bar, no intro".

  - https://jackrighteous.com/en-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-v6-ignoring-prompt-how-to-diagnose
- **Leaving room for voices:** the piano sits in the top octaves, the bass is sub synth, and the middle register is "bare", where the shouted dialogue goes.
- **Arc in seconds:** the arc is timed to the cut: the gear drops at the red light (~6 s), a glassy held chord sits under the politician, and everything stops at ~10 s. That leaves 0.5–1 s before the 10.5–11 s flatline to trim in the edit.
- **Settings:** v6, variety normal, weirdness 25, and style influence 75 to hold the meter and tempo. Max mode is off. Duration is 20 s, as the story fixes.
- **Exclude:** the six from the brief plus "reverb tail" (for a hard stop) and "cheerful, comedic" (the story's Never list).
