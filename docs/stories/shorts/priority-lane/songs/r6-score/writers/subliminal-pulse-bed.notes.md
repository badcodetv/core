# subliminal-pulse-bed — writer's note

**Idea:** the score as a pulse you barely hear, under the room sound, not a cue you listen to. A dry sub-bass heartbeat on an analogue sequencer at 124 BPM, small high clicks, and a deliberately empty middle so the shouted dialogue has the whole voice band. Flat, functional wording; no emotional adjectives.

**What shaped it**
- HBO's *The Pitt* runs almost scoreless: Gabriel Brivic's minimal pulse material is mixed under the effects at a level that is hard to hear consciously, and effects are kept out of the dialogue gaps. That is the model for "near-subliminal" and "nothing in the middle". https://zebrahomecinema.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-sound-design-bryan-parker-the-pitt-interview
- Production-library hospital cues are tagged in the same functional vocabulary (heartbeat pulse, bleeps, clock percussion, BPM as a number), which is the register the style box copies. https://sonoton.com/ja/track/SCDV074101
- Community Suno guides: genre first, then mood; BPM as a number, not "fast"; 5-8 tight tags; say instrumental in the box as well. https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/suno-ai-prompt-guide-2026-copy-paste-templates-the-formula-that-actually-works , https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/i-tested-200-suno-prompts-for-detective-music-30-actually-sound-cinematic-here-s-why
  (Single-blog claims, unverified against Suno's own docs.)

**Choices**
- Style ~35 words (a little over the asked ~25, to fit the half-time drop and the hard ending). The half-time drop is described as "midway" rather than a timestamp, since Suno does not reliably follow timestamps (unverified); the editor can cut to the drop at the red light.
- Exclude adds lead synth, strings, pads, riser, build-up, reverb tail, fade out: each would fill the midrange or soften the dead stop.
- Low weirdness (25) and high style influence (80): this needs to obey, not surprise. Variety normal. No vocal gender (instrumental).
