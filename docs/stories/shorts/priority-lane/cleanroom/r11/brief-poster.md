# Shot brief PS11: his campaign poster, seen out of the ambulance's driver's-side window

Write **4 prompts** to `prompts-poster.json` with ids `PS11-a` .. `PS11-d`.

**The face.** A reference photo of the minister is attached in Flow: `/home/jackt/.cache/pl-refs/BM9-b.jpg` (open it to see it). The man on
the poster must be THAT man, recognisably the same face as in the reference, so the audience knows the poster and the
car are one person. The last round drifted: the generator replaced his face with a different, more generic politician.
So:
- Call him only "the man from the reference image". **Never name any real person, never write "looks like", never
  describe his face, hair or body** (that pulls the engine toward a stock face). Instead tell the engine plainly to keep
  his face identical to the reference image: the same nose, eyes, mouth, jaw and hair, reproduced as a printed photo.
- Make the poster big enough in frame that his face is clearly readable (the panel fills a good part of the window).

**The physics (the director's notes, kept from last round).** UK traffic drives on the left; the driver sits on the
right. The ambulance (a tall box-bodied van) drives STRAIGHT down its own lane, never toward a kerb. The camera is at the
high driver's seat, looking straight out through the RIGHT-HAND driver's door window at 90 degrees to travel. Across the
oncoming lane, on the far pavement, a bus shelter stands parallel to the road, square-on, its illuminated ad panel
carrying the poster. Ambulance cab cues in frame: the door's window seal, grab handle, grey door trim, the big wing
mirror at the LEFT (forward) edge of the window. Our own blue roof strobe washes the wet road and the shelter.

**The poster:** a glossy campaign photo of the man from the reference image above the words **SAVE OUR NHS** in huge
heavy capitals, spelled exactly, nothing else written. Vary across the 4: head-and-shoulders beaming at the camera; in a
white hard hat and hi-vis on a hospital visit, thumbs up; shaking a nurse's hand while looking at the camera; pointing
at the viewer.

**Light:** the shelter's backlit panel, a sodium street lamp, our blue strobe, rain on the window. The near road
streaks with motion; the poster stays sharp.
