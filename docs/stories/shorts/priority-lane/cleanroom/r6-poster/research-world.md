# Priority Lane r6: the poster world (web research, 2026-11-14)

Tiers: **A** official/authoritative, **B** several sources agree, **C** single source or my inference (marked).

## 1. The bus shelter and the bus side

- **6-sheet panel = 1200 mm wide x 1800 mm tall, portrait**, just under 2 m², roughly person height. (B) [Alight Media](https://alightmedia.com/advertising/formats/classic-digital-6-sheet-screens), [Camden planning doc](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/10770561/file/document), [Bauer/Adshel](https://www.bauermediaoutdoor.co.uk/advertising/formats/adshel)
- **It sits at the end of the shelter**, a double-sided ad panel as the shelter's end wall, readable from the pavement and from traffic. (B) [Diamond Geezer on new TfL shelters](https://diamondgeezer.blogspot.com/2026/02/new-bus-shelters.html), [Camden](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/7235988/file/document)
- **Backlit**: LED tubes behind the poster in print panels (Camden lists "double sided paper advertising x4 LED tubes"); many London panels are now **digital screens** (Adshel Live, JCDecaux digital 6-sheets) that rotate ads. (B) [media.co.uk](https://media.co.uk/blogs/blog/what-is-street-furniture-advertising-bus-shelters-and-kiosks), [Adshel Live](https://www.bauermediaoutdoor.co.uk/advertising/formats/adshel-live)
- **The shelter (TfL/Foster design)**: two tubular uprights, a slim flat roof, **toughened laminated glass** side/back panes, a perch bench with a curved backrest bar and a central divider, a timetable case. TfL's 2026 trial adds a **red reflective roof** and brighter lighting. (B) [Camden](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/7235988/file/document), [TfL newsroom](https://tfl-newsroom.prgloo.com/news/tfl-trials-new-bus-shelter-designs-at-27-locations-across-london), [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/london/news/first-look-tfl-is-trialling-a-brand-new-bus-stop-design-across-london-020626)
- **At night (C, from looking, not sourced)**: the ad panel is the brightest thing in the shelter, an evenly glowing light box with a thin dark metal frame; rain on the glass picks up its glow; the wet pavement reflects it in a long smear. Digital panels look sharper, with a faint screen sheen.
- **Bus side**: the **T-side** runs along the top deck of a double-decker and drops down to eye level near the front door (T shape); the **superside** is a long landscape strip along the lower deck between the wheels. Sizes in the trade blogs disagree (about 12 m long, ~1-2.7 m high). (B for shape, C for size) [OneDay T-side](https://oneday.agency/bus-advertising/bus-t-side-adverts), [OneDay Superside](https://oneday.agency/bus-advertising/bus-superside-ads), [Global](https://global.com/advertise/audiences/local-advertising/london/outdoor/bus-advertising)
- Precedent: the **Vote Leave bus** ("We send the EU £350 million a week, let's fund our NHS instead") is the best-known political NHS promise ever painted on a bus side, and became shorthand for a broken promise. (A) [Wikipedia: Vote Leave bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_Leave_bus)

## 2. Political poster conventions and NHS slogans

- **Layout (B, from the examples below)**: head-and-shoulders portrait, the face in the upper half, a slight head turn and eyes to camera or just off it, a soft confident smile, open collar or tie with no jacket for "man of the people"; slogan in big bold sans-serif across the lower third or beside the head; party name/logo small in a corner; one strong colour field. A hand gesture is rarer than the face alone. Cameron's 2010 poster is the textbook case: close crop, soft light, slogan beside the face. [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/airbrushed-for-change), [New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/politics/2010/01/160-david-cameron-poster-photo)
- **Real NHS lines** (for flavour only, invent our own wording and colours):
  - "The NHS is safe with us" (Thatcher, 1982) / "safe in our hands" (1983). (B) [PubAffairs](https://www.publicaffairsnetworking.com/news/nhs-70-1980s-safe-in-our-hands-the-beginning-of-the-nhs-as-a-political-football-), [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/history-matters/thatchers-wordsmiths)
  - "24 hours to save the NHS" (Blair, eve of 1997 vote). (B) [HSJ](https://www.hsj.co.uk/home/it-was-a-difficult-journey-but-under-blair-the-nhs-was-saved/58376.article)
  - "I'll cut the deficit, not the NHS" (Cameron, 2010); Labour parodied it in 2012 as "I'm cutting the NHS. Not the deficit." (A/B) [New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/business/economics/2012/10/labour-goes-attack-new-cameron-poster)
  - "Let's fund our NHS instead" (Vote Leave, 2016). (A) above
  - "Stay home, protect the NHS, save lives" (government, 2020). (A) [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8835373/lectern-sign-carrying-the-message-stay-at-home-protect-the-nhs-save-lives-sign)
- **Satire precedent**: the airbrushed 2010 Cameron poster spawned a whole genre (templates at mydavidcameron.com) and was defaced in the street ("F*** off back to Eton" in red paint, per Channel 4). The airbrushing itself was the joke. (B) [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/airbrushed-for-change), [Channel 4](https://www.channel4.com/news/articles/politics/domestic_politics/david%2bcameron%2bposters%2bvandalised/3518047.html)
- Takeaway: the real-world joke is **the NHS promise next to the man who breaks it.** An over-smooth, airbrushed portrait reads as "politician poster" at once.

## 3. Match cut craft

- **Classics** (B): 2001's bone-to-satellite (shape and motion carried across millions of years); Psycho's drain-to-eye (actually a **dissolve**, circle onto circle in the same screen spot); Blade Runner's eye cut. Lawrence of Arabia's famous one is the match blown out → desert sunrise (a sound/action cut, not a face match; not confirmed in my results, so C). [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/match-cuts-creative-transitions-examples/), [Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/blog/what-is-a-match-cut/)
- **How to line it up**: keep the point of interest (here **the eyes**) in the same place in frame across the cut, same size, same head angle; "eye trace" (Murch) means the viewer's eye is already where the next shot needs it. (B) [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/eye-trace-and-rule-of-six-editing/), [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/when-and-where-to-make-the-cut-inspired-by-walter-murchs-in-the-blink-of-an-eye/)
- **For us (C, inference)**: push in on the poster until the face fills the frame, cut (or a 4-8 frame dissolve, Psycho-style) to the car-back close-up with eyes on the same line and the same smile; the smile then drops. Rain streaks or a passing streetlight sweep can hide the seam.
- **Reading time**: Netflix floor is 5/6 s per text event; the BBC rule of thumb is about 0.3 s per word at 160-180 wpm, so a 4-word slogan needs ~1.2 s **once the viewer is looking at it**. (A/B) [Netflix Timed Text](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617), [reading-speed guide](https://subtitle-toolset.pages.dev/guides/subtitle-reading-speed/). Text inside a moving shot, spotted rather than captioned, needs more: **plan 1.5-2 s readable and steady** (C).
- **Shot length in shorts**: practitioner advice is a cut every 2-3 s; no peer-reviewed number found. (C) [ClipFlip](https://clipflip.io/resources/how-to-make-viral-clips)

## 4. Slogan size on a phone

- Canvas 1080x1920; keep text in a central ~900x1400 px box, clear of the bottom ~300-480 px and right edge (platform UI). (B) [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/), [Design Sensory](https://designsensory.com/insights/social-media-safe-zone/)
- Captions: bold **60-75 px** cap-height-ish text is comfortable; 48-55 px is the floor. (C, guides disagree) [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-tiktok-2026)
- So on the poster in shot, the slogan letters should land **at least ~55-75 px tall in the final 1080-wide frame** (C, inference): with a 1.2 m panel the slogan line is roughly 1/6 of panel height, so the panel needs to fill about half the frame height or more while it is read. Short words, heavy weight, high contrast.
