## The film, in plain facts
- A 15-second vertical (9:16) satirical short. Rainy night in London. Inside an ambulance, paramedics shout that they are losing the patient. The ambulance is held at a red light so a government minister's car can cross. He is on the phone in the back, complaining about canapés. The patient dies.
- Problem: viewers can't tell the man in the car is a politician. Fix: while the paramedics shout, we cut quickly to a bus shelter (or a bus) the ambulance passes, carrying the minister's own election-style poster with the slogan **SAVE OUR NHS**, held long enough to read (about 1.5-2 s), then a transition from his face on the poster to him in the back of his car in the same pose, then back to the ambulance.
- The minister is an invented man. Never a real politician, never a real party's name, logo or colours.
- His face comes from an attached reference image: a photo of him in the back seat of a dark car at night, slumped, holding a phone to his right ear with his right hand, head in the upper middle of a vertical frame, eyes rolled up to the roof, mouth in a sulky line. **Do not describe his appearance at all** (no age, hair, face, clothes, build). Write "the man from the reference image". You may describe his pose, hand position, where his eyes look, and muscle-level expression (brows, mouth).
- The look of the whole film: it should feel like a real 35 mm film photo taken at night in the rain, not CGI. Wet tarmac, sodium-orange street lamps, cold teal shadows, film grain, blue emergency lights cutting through. Ordinary London street.
- The slogan text must come out spelled right and readable on a phone. Use the research's advice on text.

## Research (read both)

## Rules for every prompt
- One prompt = one paragraph, written fresh in your own words. Vertical 9:16. Photo-real.
- Name the light sources in the scene.
- The only readable text in the frame is the slogan (and nothing else legible: no plates, no other signs).
- End every prompt with the word "Thanks."
