**The director's note:** "the ambulance needs to be on the english side of the road."
Research it: in the UK traffic drives on the LEFT; a bus stop is on the left-hand kerb for the traffic using it; so the
lane right next to the shelter's kerb carries traffic travelling with the kerb on ITS left. From a seat in the shelter
facing the road, that near-lane traffic moves from the viewer's RIGHT to the viewer's LEFT; looking a little down the
road to the right, it comes TOWARD the camera from the distance in the lane nearest our kerb. The far lane carries the
opposite direction.
- Put the ambulance IN THE FRAME (this is the first frame of the clip): a UK ambulance (tall box van, yellow-green
  chequered Battenburg sides, blue lights flashing, headlights on) in the NEAR lane, hugging our kerb, approaching
  from the distance on the right toward the camera, still some way off (so it can race past in the clip). State the
  lane and direction plainly and physically ("in the lane nearest the shelter's kerb, travelling toward the camera,
  the kerb on its left"). Any other traffic in the far lane goes the other way with red tail lights.
- No readable lettering on the ambulance; no other readable text except the poster.
- Vary across the 3: how far off the ambulance is, how much of the road shows, the strength of its blue light on the
  rain and the shelter glass.
