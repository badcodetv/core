# The junction, lane by lane (UK: traffic keeps LEFT; a driver sits on the RIGHT)

Camera A (the cordon, high on a railway bridge) and camera B (the held shot, inside the ambulance cab) both look along
the SAME main road, in the SAME direction, toward a crossroads with a yellow box junction.

- MAIN ROAD, OUR SIDE (left half of the road as the camera looks along it): traffic travels AWAY from the camera.
  The ambulance is here, at the stop line, its BACK to camera A, its nose toward the junction.
- MAIN ROAD, ONCOMING SIDE (right half of the road as the camera looks along it): traffic travels TOWARD the camera.
  Beyond the junction this side holds a queue (a red double-decker and cars) with HEADLIGHTS toward camera, stopped at
  their stop line on the far side of the junction, held by police motorcycles and an officer standing in front of them.
- POLICE LINE ON OUR SIDE: white police motorcycles and officers in yellow hi-vis across the mouth of the junction just
  ahead of the ambulance, one officer with a gloved hand raised toward the ambulance.
- THE CROSS ROAD runs left-right across the frame through the junction.
- THE CONVOY crosses the junction FROM LEFT TO RIGHT, side-on to the camera: a white police motorcycle leading, then three
  black saloon cars, headlights pointing to the RIGHT of the frame, tail lights to the left. Travelling left to right,
  a UK vehicle keeps to ITS left, which is the FAR side of the cross road (the side away from the camera). So the convoy
  is in the FAR half of the junction box, not the half nearest the police line.
- The convoy is NOT on the main road and NOT coming toward the camera.
- The cross road beyond the junction on the right is empty (cleared for the convoy). On the left side road, behind the
  convoy, nothing yet (it came from there).

Write these as plain physical facts in every prompt: which way each vehicle points and which half of the road it is in.
