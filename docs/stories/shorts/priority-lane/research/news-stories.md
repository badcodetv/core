# Priority Lane: real headlines for the end card

## 2026-11-11, research pass 1

This is for the end card: real headlines flash on screen while the narrator says "A politician blocking the
path of an ambulance. Poetic, really..."

How the research was done: web search, with each article opened where the site allowed it.
- **Verbatim (fetched)** means the article page was opened and the headline copied from it.
- **Verbatim (search listing)** means the headline is the title a search engine showed for that exact URL. It
  was not re-read on the page, because the site returned 403 or a bot check. Re-check these before putting
  them on screen.
- Nothing here is invented. If a headline could not be pinned down, the entry says so.

**The UK gap.** No reputable UK outlet has reported a named UK politician's or royal's convoy holding up an
ambulance. The one UK story found (entry 12) comes from a small agency, the VIP is unidentified, and there was
no police statement. It is *shaky*. So the strongest real material is from Malaysia, India and Russia.

## THE STRONGEST FIVE (use these first)

### 1. ★ Malaysia, 2021: the Prime Minister's convoy, and the police's "safety" excuse
- **Headline (verbatim, fetched):** "After viral video, Selangor police clarify priority given to VIP's convoy over ambulance to avoid accidents"
- **Outlet / date:** Malay Mail, 27 December 2021
- **URL:** https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2021/12/27/after-viral-video-selangor-police-clarify-priority-given-to-vips-convoy-to/2031385
- **Other headlines on the same story (verbatim, search listing):**
  - Free Malaysia Today: "PM's convoy got priority over ambulance as safety measure, say cops". https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2021/12/27/pms-convoy-got-priority-over-ambulance-as-safety-measure-say-cops
  - paultan.org: "Prime Minister's convoy halts ambulance, police clarify it was done on grounds of safety". https://paultan.org/2021/12/27/prime-ministers-convoy-halts-ambulance-police-clarify-it-was-done-on-grounds-of-safety
  - The Star: the URL slug reads "malaysians-not-buying-police-reasons-on-ambulance-being-stopped-to-give-way-to-pm039s-convoy" (28 Dec 2021). *The exact headline is unverified; open the page before quoting it.* https://thestar.com.my/news/nation/2021/12/28/malaysians-not-buying-police-reasons-on-ambulance-being-stopped-to-give-way-to-pm039s-convoy
- **Summary:** Police escorting PM Ismail Sabri Yaakob's convoy through flood-hit Hulu Langat stopped an
  ambulance at a junction. The police said letting it through risked an accident.
- **Verification:** Strong. Malay Mail, FMT, The Star, Malaysiakini and others reported it. The police confirmed

  ambulance, and the police corrected that. It happened again in March 2022 in Johor Bahru, also the PM's
  convoy, and police again said "30 seconds only" (Mothership, Says.com). **This is the closest match to the
  short's scene: police outriders, an ambulance held, the minister glides through.**

### 2. ★ India (Assam), 2026: the Chief Minister apologises and four police are suspended
- **Headline (verbatim, fetched):** "Himanta apologises to patient after ambulance stopped to let his convoy pass; 4 policemen suspended"
- **Outlet / date:** Daily Excelsior, carrying PTI copy. Dateline Guwahati, 30 September 2026; published 1 October 2026.
- **URL:** https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/himanta-apologises-to-patient-after-ambulance-stopped-to-let-his-convoy-pass-4-policemen-suspended/
- **Summary:** Traffic police in Silchar stopped a 108 ambulance carrying a critically ill patient so
  Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma's convoy could pass while he campaigned. The CM said: "No Chief
  Minister's passage should EVER take precedence over a life in need of urgent care."
- **Verification:** Strong. PTI wire copy, also reported by NE Now, The Pioneer and IANS (via MENAFN), and the
  CMO reissued its convoy directive. Parts are disputed:
  - Reports say the ambulance was held about four minutes; Sarma said about 30 seconds.
  - Police first claimed there was no patient on board.
  - The patient (reported as a 42-year-old with liver disease) died the next morning. Sarma said the death was
    not linked to the stop.
  - **Do not claim the stop killed him.**
- **Why it is useful:** It is six weeks old, a politician apologised, and the apology is itself a line.

## Can a UK creator show these? (CDPA 1988 s.30)

**Well established:**
- Fair dealing for **criticism, review or quotation** (s.30(1), (1ZA)) covers *any* kind of work, with a
  sufficient acknowledgement (name the outlet).
- Fair dealing for **reporting current events** (s.30(2)) covers any work **except photographs**.
- The test is how "a fair-minded and honest person" would deal with the work: how much is taken, and whether
  it harms the original's market (gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright).
- **Headlines can themselves be copyright literary works** (NLA v Meltwater [2011] EWCA Civ 890), so you do
  need the exception. A headline's text plus the outlet's name, used briefly to make a critical point, is the
  safest form.

**Uncertain:**
- **Screenshots.** A screenshot also copies the publisher's typographical arrangement and any photo in the
  article. A news photo is excluded from current-events reporting and is only safe if the photo itself is
  being criticised. *Inference: re-set the headline text in our own type, rather than screenshot.*
- **Outlet logos.** Logos are trade marks (and artistic works). Referring to a source is usually fine, but a
  logo risks suggesting endorsement. No case law was checked; this is unverified.
- **Satire.** Whether a satire short counts as "criticism" or "current events" is untested for our use. The
  parody/pastiche exception (s.30A) may also help.

**Recommendation:** Use typed headline text with "Outlet, Year" in small type, keep each on screen briefly,
and don't show the outlets' photos.

Sources: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright · https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2011/890
