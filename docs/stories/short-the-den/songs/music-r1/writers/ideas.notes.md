# Research notes: music r1 ideas

- Search found no published description of the TV pitch show's actual theme sound, only composer credits (e.g. https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Graham_Reilly). We are making a parody, so the ideas lean on the wider genre, not on any one theme.
- The usual TV library sound for this genre is "tension beds": a constant pulse, ticking percussion, synth textures, low strings, all made for game shows and judging rounds. Sources: https://sonoton.com/kr/album/LSYN0105 , https://shockwave-sound.com/stock-music-track/18883/tension-pulse , https://shockwave-sound.com/stock-music-track/11962/tension-bed-i , https://warnerchappellpm.com/playlist/story-tv-tension/MzE4MjM4MS01YmEyZDE
- Suno v6 Style box: treat it as a producer's brief, not a list of tags. One leading genre, then the rhythmic feel, then 2-3 signature instruments, then mood, then production. Give the tempo as a number and say how the beat moves. https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/optimize-suno-v6-style-of-music-prompt
- Exclude: name audible things (instruments, traits) and keep the list under about ten. Words like "loud" or "busy" do nothing there. Don't exclude something the Style box asks for. It's "a helpful boundary, not a promise." https://jackrighteous.com/es-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/negative-prompting-suno-v5-guide , https://github.com/mttkllr/suno-field-guide
- In instrumental mode, "instrumental" usually gets a full band with no vocals. A solo has to be asked for outright. Mixing up instrumental and vocal modes is a common cause of stray "ghost" vocals. https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-v6-solo-instrument-prompts , https://medium.com/@aitooldiscovery/suno-reddit-tips-and-prompts-what-r-sunoai-users-actually-do-65b59d51bcad
- For this short: the bed sits under speech for about 32 s, then a 5 s fact card, then a 5 s rainy-window outro (42.8 s in all). Keep the midrange (roughly where voices sit) thin and the lead sparse. A clear change near 32 s and a soft tail help the edit.
