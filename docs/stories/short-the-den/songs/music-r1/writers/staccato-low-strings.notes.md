# staccato-low-strings: what shaped it

- Scene first, then sound. The style opens with the studio picture (lone founder, silent row of investors), then gives each instrument one job: low cellos and basses carry clipped staccato eighths at 100 BPM, timpani rolls under them, and a single muted horn/trombone swell marks the verdict. v6 guides say the Style box works like a producer's brief rather than a tag cloud. They recommend naming a few signature instruments with roles and the groove, and avoiding contradictions (jackrighteous.com, undetectr.com, the suno-field-guide repo).
- Room for speech. The upper strings are limited to quiet held notes with no melody, and the mix is dry and close, because nine voice lines sit on top. "High violin melody" also goes in Exclude.
- Exclude holds only the unwanted things (synth, electronic, drum kit, choir, vocals). The guides warn that naming an unwanted sound in the Style box tends to bring it in, and that Exclude is a boundary rather than a guarantee (Suno release note; jackrighteous negative-prompting guide).
- Genre reference. Stock "reality tension" cues are built on accented staccato strings, slow builds and timpani or horn swells (Shockwave-Sound "Total Build", 86 BPM staccato strings; AudioJungle "Elimination Round", horns and timpani added toward the end). Composers in this field describe tension in tiers, from subtle to trailer-level, so the cue is kept at the subtle-to-mid tier and creeps up rather than jumping (Slate interview on reality scoring; Berklee TV music primer).
- Settings: v6 rather than v6-wild, for control. Variety is normal, weirdness 25 and styleInfluence 75, so the orchestration follows the brief closely. durationSec is 60, which leaves trim room over the 42.8 s cut. Lyrics are empty because the track is instrumental.
