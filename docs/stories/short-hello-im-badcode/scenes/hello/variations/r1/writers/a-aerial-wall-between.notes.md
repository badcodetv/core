# a-aerial-wall-between — notes

- The idea comes from aerial documentary photography of inequality: a single wall or road separating a rich enclave from an informal settlement, shot from straight above (the "nadir view"), which the photographer behind that work says produces the strongest images, because the border itself becomes the subject. Kibera next to Loresho (Nairobi) and Detroit's Eight Mile wall are the reference cases: a concrete wall with the two worlds pressed against it.
  - https://unequalscenes.com/about
  - https://weburbanist.com/2018/09/18/unequal-scenes-aerials-photos-highlight-stark-lines-between-rich-and-poor/
  - https://www.ignant.com/2018/09/14/global-inequalities-captured-from-above/
  - https://hypebeast.com/2018/8/johnny-miller-unequal-scenes-drone-photography
- Nano Banana tips: name the angle explicitly (top-down / camera pointing straight down), then the subject, setting, light and shot; vague prompts default to a centred subject on an empty background. So the prompt spells out "camera pointing straight down, no horizon, no sky" and lays out the frame zone by zone (above the wall / the wall / below the wall), which suits the tall 9:16 frame.
  - https://storista.io/blog/nano-banana-prompts
  - https://www.curify-ai.com/blog/10-prompting-tips-nano-banana
- Choices: flat overcast light so no shadows break the map-like read; a muted ochre/khaki/ash palette so the pools are the only saturated colour; the empty pools above against smoke, washing and tiny people below carry "greed won" with nothing explained. No people readable, so no likeness issue; an explicit no-text line because the brief forbids lettering. Ends with "Thanks." (the house convention for Flow prompts).
- Settings fixed by the story: Nano Banana 2, 9:16.
