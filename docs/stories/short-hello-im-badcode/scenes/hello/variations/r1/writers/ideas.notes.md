# Research notes — ideas, round 1 (Hello, I'm BadCode)

- Nano Banana 2 responds best to photography vocabulary: named lens (35/50/85mm, macro), angle (low, eye-level, overhead), depth (deep focus vs shallow), named light setups (Rembrandt, rim, direct flash, golden hour) and a colour grade. Build in layers: subject, environment, framing, light, camera, mood. Name WHERE things sit in the frame. No negativePrompt field; a plain "Negative prompt:" clause at the end is followed. The model renders legible text readily, so "no text" has to be stated. — https://runware.ai/docs/models/google-nano-banana-2/guides/prompting , https://fliki.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide , https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide
- Static, centred, symmetrical frames read as deadpan; a fixed camera turns the frame into a diorama and lets stillness carry the joke (Anderson; Keaton/Tati lineage). — https://theconversation.com/the-wes-anderson-recipe-a-detailed-guide-on-how-to-recreate-the-directors-aesthetic-206886 , https://www.tasteray.com/articles/movie-static-camera-comedy
- Roy Andersson: shadowless flat light, pale grey/off-white desaturated palette, static camera, deep focus, built-set tableaux; deadpan absurd melancholy. — https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/where-begin-with-roy-andersson , https://www.redsharknews.com/this-is-cinema-at-its-most-un-cinematic.-or-is-it , https://filmcomment.com/article/review-a-pigeon-sat-on-a-branch-reflecting-on-existence-roy-andersson
- Simon Stålenhag: calm, distant aftermath — "arriving at a crime scene long after it happened"; open vistas, rusting hulks, bystanders bored and looking away. — https://www.cityam.com/simon-stlenhags-haunting-painted-stories-things-flood/
- Edward Burtynsky: aerial, large-format, flat even light that almost abstracts human-altered land; beauty of the damage; recent aerials of the LA fire aftermath. — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/article/edward-burtynsky-photography-exhibit-icp-great-acceleration , https://themodernhouse.com/journal/edward-burtynsky-human-altered-landscapes
- Detroit ruin photography (Marchand & Meffre, Andrew Moore): abandoned wealth (ballrooms, mansions) — but critiqued as "ruin porn" that erases the people still living there. Lesson for us: show the inequality (who is left, who walled themselves off), not just pretty decay. — https://www.inkl.com/news/in-detroit-ruin-porn-ignores-the-voices-of-those-who-still-call-the-city-home , https://coggles.com/life/culture/ruins-of-detroit-yves-marchand-romain-meffre
- Martin Parr: saturated colour, direct on-camera flash, irony about consumption and the wealthy at leisure. Stephen Shore: 8x10, banal scenes, "irreverent and deadpan" colour. — https://petapixel.com/martin-parr/ , https://www.canon.co.uk/pro/stories/martin-parr-style-vision/ , https://theartstory.org/amp/artist/shore-stephen/
