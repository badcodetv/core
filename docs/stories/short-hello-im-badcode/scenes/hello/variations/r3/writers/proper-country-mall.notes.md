# proper-country-mall — notes (r3, redo)

Redo after the sameness gate flagged the shared closing phrase "No text, no ...". The ban list is gone; the
absence of lettering is now said positively and in this prompt's own words (blank single-colour fascia bands,
"Every surface is wordless: bare shop fascias, plain carrier bags, unmarked trays and cups"), so it no longer
echoes the rest of the round. Everything else kept from the first draft.

What shaped it:
- "We used to be a country. A proper country." is ironic nostalgia over photos of ordinary prosperous life,
  started December 2021 on a 1973 7-Eleven photo, then widened to '90s/'00s scenes; malls were a headline example
  ("Remember malls???"). https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/we-used-to-be-a-country-a-proper-country ,
  https://www.bustle.com/life/we-used-to-be-a-country-meme-origins ,
  https://www.elitedaily.com/news/best-we-used-to-be-a-country-proper-country-memes
- Sister template "We had 24 hour Walmarts. We had it all": nostalgia for when everyday things were cheap and
  plentiful, hence "busy, warm, prosperous, ordinary". https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/we-had-24-hour-walmarts-we-had-it-all
- Dead-mall nostalgia as the shared mood (a once-great destination now gone), hence the mall at its peak:
  coin fountain, glass lift, food court, skylight. https://karenattiah.substack.com/p/america-is-giving-dying-mall-vibes ,
  https://sundaylongread.com/2022/09/09/the-american-malls-long-goodbye/
- Mid-2000s point-and-shoot look (on-camera flash on the foreground rail, washed highlights, soft noise, tilted
  horizon) so it reads as a found photo, the way these memes use real snapshots.
