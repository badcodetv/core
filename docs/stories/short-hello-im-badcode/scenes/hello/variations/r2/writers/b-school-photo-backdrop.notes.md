# b-school-photo-backdrop — notes (redo)

Redo after the sameness gate: the closing line shared "or lettering anywhere" with most of the round. Rewrote the no-text instruction in this prompt's own terms ("The print is clean: no writing, digits, name plates or stamps") and lightly reworded a few other phrases; the picture is unchanged.

What shaped it:
- The gently mottled grey-blue canvas is the long-running default school-portrait backdrop (Lifetouch's "tried-and-true classic"), so the backdrop is grey-blue mottle, darker at the edges, not a flat blue: https://www.katebackdrop.com/blogs/about-backdrops/school-picture-day-backdrop
- A 2003 Education Week piece describes the standard product as a pupil "smiling against a generic blue background", which dates the look to the early 2000s: https://www.edweek.org/education/under-pressure-student-photo-industry-upgrades-image/2003/12
- Picture day as a high-volume, assembly-line operation (one stool, fixed lighting, a pose every pupil is put into) explains the flat, forgiving two-light setup and the stock angled-shoulders pose: https://fstoppers.com/business/school-picture-day-strange-economics-largest-portrait-operation-earth-904158
- The laser backdrop is an 80s look, so I left it out to keep the frame in the 2000s: https://blog.treering.com/yearbook-photo-backgrounds/
- Nostalgia comes from the print itself (folder, wallet sizes, minilab colour), the thing these happy-memory compilations linger on: https://www.aol.com/articles/nostalgic-school-picture-day-photos-160035126.html
