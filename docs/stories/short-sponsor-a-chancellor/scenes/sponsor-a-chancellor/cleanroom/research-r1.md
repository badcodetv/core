## 0. Jack's reference (`reference/nan-flat-ref.png`)

Note that it is an **exterior**: the corner of a two- or three-storey brick low-rise block, seen from across the street.

- **Time and light:** late blue hour under a flat overcast sky. There is no sun, no shadows and no sky gradient. The ambient light is very low and even, so the brick and sky sit within about a stop of each other. (GUESS)
- **Colour:** almost monochrome, a cold slate grey shading to aubergine/maroon brick, lifted blacks, low contrast. The only saturation is in two lit windows. The left one glows a flat **orange-amber** through a drawn curtain, with no detail. The right one shows **cream vertical blinds** with a bare bright ceiling bulb behind. The **white uPVC frames pick up a violet-blue edge** from the sky. Warm practical light against a cold ambient is the whole picture. (GUESS)
- **Materials:** stretcher-bond brick, a concrete or brick band at each floor, white uPVC casements with fat frames and top-hung openers, a shallow flat roofline. The upper windows are dark. (GUESS)
- **Framing:** square crop, near eye level, square-on to the building's corner. The verticals are close to straight, as from a tripod with a normal-to-slight-tele lens and no wide-angle distortion. The building fills the frame, with only a sliver of sky. (GUESS)
- **Texture:** soft and slightly smeared, low micro-detail. It reads like mist or drizzle, a long exposure, or a heavy noise-reduced phone night shot. Nothing is crisp. (GUESS)
- **Mood:** quiet, lonely, watched from outside. Someone is home and nobody is visiting. This is the Todd Hido register (see §2). (GUESS)

- Shot grammar is GUESS, from common viewing and not verified by any source found:
  - a single subject, centre-framed, looking just off-lens or straight down the barrel;
  - tight close-ups on eyes and hands;
  - a slow push-in;
  - a subject alone in an empty space (a doorway, a window, the end of a bed);
  - soft window light from one side and lifted blacks;
  - slow motion on a small gesture;
  - white sans-serif caption cards on black ("£3 a month could…", a phone number or text code) and a charity logo end card.

## 3. Prompting Nano Banana / Gemini image for real-looking photos

- Google's guide says to **describe the scene narratively, not as keywords**, with the formula Subject + Action + Location + Composition + Style. It also says to **state what you want, not what you don't** ("empty street", not "no cars"). OFFICIAL: [Google Cloud, ultimate Nano Banana prompting guide (2026-03-05)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana).
- Google's camera and film language:
  - name a camera ("cheap disposable camera" gives a raw flash look; Fujifilm gives its colour science);
  - name a lens and aperture;
  - "as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy";
  - "medium-format analog film, pronounced grain";
  - be specific about materials ("navy blue tweed", not "suit jacket").
  OFFICIAL: same guide, and [Google blog, 7 tips for Nano Banana Pro](https://blog.google/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/).
- Text: put the exact words **in quotes** and name the typeface style ("bold, white, sans-serif"). Google warns that **small text and spelling may not be perfect**. OFFICIAL: same two pages. Reviews say Nano Banana 2 handles **short text well and is weaker than Pro on long strings**. One vendor estimate puts character accuracy at about 90–95% (unverified). REPORTED: [getimg](https://getimg.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-review-whats-new-and-is-it-better), [ponpon](https://ponpon.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-review).
- Prompt length: Google gives no recommended length, and its examples run several sentences. OFFICIAL. Stacked hype words ("8k HDR ultra hyper-realistic") add noise rather than control. NB2 drifts to **clean faces and tidy rooms if the prompt is too polite**. REPORTED: [fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-prompting-guide).
- Realism fixes:
  - ask for skin texture and imperfection;
  - describe light by **direction, hardness, colour temperature, fall-off**;
  - off-centre framing, cropped limbs, awkward headroom;
  - avoid beauty language.
  REPORTED (vendor blogs): [Cloudinary](https://cloudinary.com/guides/image-generation/how-to-make-ai-images-look-real), [Runway](https://runway.com/resources/why-do-ai-images-look-weird), [promptzone](https://www.promptzone.com/cillian_yoon/prompting-deliberate-imperfection-for-realistic-ai-photos-3129.md).
- Elderly faces: nothing NB-specific was found. Smoothing and over-perfect skin are the general tell (REPORTED, above). Name age texture plainly: liver spots, thin skin, deep lines, uneven grey hair. GUESS.

## 4. Slop tells to avoid here

- Glossy, even, shadowless light; a "golden" rim on everyone. REPORTED (Runway).
- Poreless or waxy elderly skin, perfect teeth, salon hair. REPORTED.
- A spotless, tidy, symmetrical room with every object hero-placed. REPORTED (fliki, promptzone).
- Teal-and-orange "cinematic" grade, crushed blacks with neon highlights. GUESS.
- Visible breath clouds and frost to show cold, which overacts the cold. GUESS.
- Over-wide lens distortion in a small room. GUESS.
- A flyer with gibberish small print or too much text. REPORTED (Google's own warning).
- A "sad" face pulled into a pout; a too-handsome chancellor. GUESS.

## What a prompt writer should do

1. Write a short narrative paragraph: subject, action, place, framing, light, medium. No keyword soup, no "8k/ultra/hyperreal".
2. Carry the reference's palette into the interior: **cold grey-blue dusk at the window against one weak warm practical** (a single lamp or the TV glow), lifted blacks, low contrast, soft. Describe the light's direction and colour, not its mood.
3. Name a humble medium: a consumer camera or phone at night, or colour negative film with grain. Avoid "cinematic".
4. Name British materials concretely (unlit gas fire, magnolia woodchip, worn patterned carpet, white uPVC, vertical blinds or nets), and **clutter that is lived in, not styled**.
5. Give the nan real age and real clothes: a buttoned winter coat, a knitted hat, hands round a mug. Ask for texture; never "beautiful".
6. Frame imperfectly: eye-level or slightly high, a normal lens, off-centre, a door frame or chair back cutting in.
7. For the TV appeal: black and white, a lone centre-framed subject, a slow push or slow motion, a white sans-serif card on black. Watch real RSPCA/NSPCC ads first; the shot grammar above is unverified.
8. Keep flyer text to **one to five words in quotes** with a named type style. Expect misspellings: re-roll, or plan to replace the text in post.
9. Phrase positively ("empty hallway", not "no people"), per Google.
10. Run `scripts/variations/fresh.py` on every prompt before sending.
