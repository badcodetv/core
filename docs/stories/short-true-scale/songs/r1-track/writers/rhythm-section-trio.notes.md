# rhythm-section-trio: what shaped it

- **Numbers and named parts over mood words.** Guides agree mood tags barely steer Suno without instrument tags, and recommend ordering the style box tempo, genre, rhythm, instruments, then mood (Layer3 Labs, relaying Suno's own beat-making guidance; documentarytube jazz guide). So the box opens with 112 BPM, swung eighths, 4/4, F major, then each player's exact job (walking quarters, brush swirls, two-note vibraphone stabs, motor off so it doesn't shimmer into a lead).
- **Sparse and quiet by instruction.** The jazz guide's fix for busy output is "sparse", "soft dynamics", "brushes", softer transients; here that becomes "mixed low and distant", "wide gaps in the midrange" (the 300 Hz-3 kHz voice band) and a stab only every two bars.
- **Negatives go in Exclude, not Style.** Negative phrasing fails in the style field (Blake Crosley); Style and Exclude must not contradict (Jack Righteous). Exclude carries the brief's list plus scat/singing/crowd chant (no words in shorts) and drum solo.
- **Timed arc in the box.** No lyrics, so the walk-out (brushes stop, bass walks down alone into rain) and the logo (quick vibraphone arpeggio, one bell, hard stop) are written as timestamps in the style box. BPM and timings are guidance, not a lock (Crosley), so expect to trim in the edit.
- **Settings.** Style Influence 70: Crosley says pair a precise field with strong influence. Weirdness 30: below centre, keep the trio conventional and in the corner. Variety normal, v6, 40 s, instrumental.
