# fruit-machine-electronics: what shaped it

- **The machine is the performer.** Old UK fruit machines are remembered for the drums spinning inside (a "visceral" mechanical sound the later electronic ones lacked) and coins pouring into the chrome hopper; later machines added light shows and music ([Sunday in Old Money, "Two plums and a bell"](https://sundayinoldmoney.blogspot.com/2024/10/two-plums-and-bell.html)). So the groove is built from reel whir/tick, relay clicks and hopper rattle, not drums.
- **Stepper reels and a chip voice.** The Barcrest MPU4 board, the standard British pub machine of the 1980s, drove its reels with stepper motors and had an AY-family three-voice sound chip (the MAME `mpu4` driver lists an AY8913; [git.redump.net mpu4.cpp](https://git.redump.net/hbmame/plain/src/mame/drivers/mpu4.cpp), [MPU4 service manual listing](https://www.emanualonline.com/misc/collectibles/slot-machines/barcrest/barcrest-mpu4-fruit-machine-service-manual-emo-76347)). Hence "stepper-motor reels" and "three-voice square-wave bleeps from an old sound chip". I wrote it generically ("old sound chip") because the chip model is unverified for any one machine and Suno will not know it anyway.
- **Order and length.** v6 guides say lead with what defines the record, keep one leading idea, put positives in Style and unwanted traits in Exclude, keep Exclude short ([Jack Righteous, optimise v6 style prompt](https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/optimize-suno-v6-style-of-music-prompt); [Suno help: exclude](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/3161921)). So the machine noises come first, chiptune only as "glue", and the no-melody need is phrased positively ("quiet, dry and sparse so speech sits on top") rather than as a ban.
- **Sliders.** Style Influence toward Strong suits a precise field, Weirdness stays near centre unless results feel generic ([Blake Crosley on Style Influence](https://blakecrosley.com/fr/blog/suno-style-field-style-influence)): styleInfluence 65, weirdness 40, variety normal. v6 (not wild) because the end needs its structure obeyed (lone reel midway, three clunks, payout chime).
- **Exclude.** Brief's list plus "vocoder" (the most likely route to robot speech in a chip-sound track). Instrumental with empty lyrics, per the brief.
- Unverified: whether Suno renders mechanical reel noises as sound rather than turning them into percussion. That is the experiment.
