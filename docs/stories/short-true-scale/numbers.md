## 1. Households in temporary accommodation
- **134,210 households** on **31 Dec 2025** (primary). Snapshot of the last day of the quarter. Counts HOUSEHOLDS, not people. Also on that page: 85,800 households with children; 176,130 dependent children.
- Released 30 Apr 2026. Up 5.0% on a year earlier, down 0.4% on the previous quarter (read, search summary).
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/statutory-homelessness-in-england-october-to-december-2025/statutory-homelessness-in-england-october-to-december-2025
- Safe wording: "134,210 households in temporary accommodation in England, 31 Dec 2025."
- A newer quarter (Jan-Mar 2026, due ~July 2026) may exist by now; I did not look. Check before publishing.

## 2. People homeless on a given night (Shelter)
- **At least 382,618 people**, "on a given night in 2025", about 1 in 153; 175,025 are children (primary, Shelter press release 11 Dec 2025).
- It is Shelter's ESTIMATE, not an official count. Built from MHCLG data plus FOI replies: 350,480 in council temporary accommodation, about 16,300 in hostels, 7,146 "homeless at home", 4,667 rough sleeping (the 2024 count), 4,031 in social-services accommodation. Sofa-surfing is NOT included.
- No official total of homeless people exists (the gov.uk page says its figures are not suitable for that).
- URL: https://england.shelter.org.uk/media/press_release/at_least_382000_people_are_homeless_in_england_today_
- Safe wording: "Shelter estimates at least 382,618 people homeless in England on a given night, 2025."

## 3. Empty homes (MHCLG council tax data, snapshot 6 Oct 2025)
- **303,185 long-term vacant** dwellings, up from 264,884 (7 Oct 2024). (primary: figures on the gov.uk release below.)
- **754,264 vacant dwellings**, up from 719,470 (primary, same page). This is ALL vacants: "reported as empty (unoccupied and unfurnished) for council tax", and it includes properties with a council tax exemption (the page says so). That means short-term empties are in it, for example between tenants or sales.
- **1,022,158 = 754,264 + 267,894 second homes** (arithmetic checks; primary for the AoEH page, read for the split). It is Action on Empty Homes' total, NOT an MHCLG figure. The ~1 million therefore includes second homes (267,894), which are furnished and used part of the year. Do not call them "empty".
- Long-term definition: the usual one is empty more than 6 months. I could NOT confirm that on the gov.uk pages; the technical notes don't define it. It comes from search summaries and the Table 615 notes, which I did not open (unverified). I also did not open the Table 615 spreadsheet.
- URLs: MHCLG https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/dwelling-stock-estimates-in-england-2025/dwelling-stock-estimates-england-31-march-2025 ; table page https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/live-tables-on-dwelling-stock-including-vacants ; AoEH https://www.actiononemptyhomes.org/the-last-decade
- Safe wording: "303,185 homes in England have been empty long-term (council tax data, Oct 2025)." or "754,264 homes empty in England, Oct 2025."

## 4. Rough sleeping snapshot
- **4,793 people** on a single night in autumn 2025, a record, up 3% (primary). Each council picks one night between 1 Oct and 30 Nov. Mostly evidence-based estimates (83%), not street counts. Excludes hostels, squatters, travellers.
- Published 26 Feb 2026 (read).
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/rough-sleeping-snapshot-in-england-autumn-2025/rough-sleeping-snapshot-in-england-autumn-2025
- Safe wording: "4,793 people estimated sleeping rough on one night, autumn 2025."

## Which comparison is fair for 13 seconds
- **Best: 303,185 long-term empty homes vs 134,210 households in temporary accommodation.** Both are official MHCLG figures, both in homes/households, both late 2025. Long-term empties are more than twice the households in TA. Say "households", and "homes", not people.
- **Also fair, but flag it:** 754,264 empty homes (all kinds, includes short-term empties and exempt ones). It beats 134,210 by 5.6x, but critics will say most are in turnover; the long-term number avoids that.
- **Avoid:** "1,022,158 empty homes", because it includes 267,894 second homes and is a charity's sum. Avoid putting 382,618 people beside homes: people against dwellings, and an NGO estimate against official data. Avoid claiming empties could house the homeless one-for-one. Many are in the wrong place, derelict or in probate. The fair claim is scale only.
- Same-night caution: the empties are 6 Oct 2025 and TA is 31 Dec 2025; say "late 2025".

## Second homes and children (2026-11-16)
Marks: (primary) = I fetched the gov.uk page; (read) = secondary; (unverified) = not seen.

### Second homes, England
- **267,894 dwellings recorded as second homes** for council tax (primary, fetched via the release page). Action on Empty Homes' figure is therefore the MHCLG figure; it is England only (the release covers 296 English billing authorities).
- Source: MHCLG "Local authority Council Taxbase in England 2025", Table 4 "Number of dwellings classed as second homes for the purposes of council tax by level of discount". https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/council-taxbase-2025-in-england/local-authority-council-taxbase-in-england-2025 . Published 6 Nov 2025, tables REVISED 21 Jan 2026 (use the revised number; I did not see the pre-revision one).
- Dates: VOA council tax list snapshot 10 Sep 2025; discount/premium data as at 6 Oct 2025 (first Monday of October). The page itself says "October 2025" in places.
- What it counts: "dwellings which are furnished and are not used by anyone as their main home." Holiday lets/self-catering charged business rates are EXCLUDED. Breakdown: 90,734 no discount/premium; 6,861 with a discount; 170,299 charged the premium. Down 12,000 (4.3%) on 2024.
- Caveat: it counts dwellings, not use. A "second home" may be lived in for weeks a year, and some are job-related or the owner's only other home. "Nobody's main home" is accurate; "empty" is not.
- I did NOT open the Table 4 spreadsheet itself, and Table 615 (the vacants spreadsheet) was not opened either (unverified); the numbers come from the release page text.

### Is 303,185 + 267,894 fair?
- Sum: 303,185 + 267,894 = **571,079**. Arithmetic only; MHCLG does not publish this total.
- Double counting: by council tax definition the classes are separate. Vacant = "unoccupied and unfurnished"; second home = "furnished", not anyone's main home. A dwelling cannot be both on one list (primary definitions). Residual risk (unverified): the two datasets are different tables (Dwelling Stock Table 615 vs Taxbase Table 4) with slightly different snapshot handling; I did not reconcile them line by line.
- Fair wording: "571,079 homes in England that nobody lives in as a main home: 303,185 long-term empty plus 267,894 second homes (council tax, autumn 2025)." Say "nobody's main home", NOT "empty". Present it as two numbers, or the sum with both parts named, and say the sum is ours.
- Still avoid claiming they could house the homeless one-for-one. The 754,264 all-vacants figure is a different, wider measure and must not be added to second homes in a way that mixes it with 303,185 (do not add 754,264 AND 303,185).

- Apr-Jun 2026 may also be out (not checked; normally released ~Nov). Check before publishing.

- Jul-Sep 2025 (30 Sep 2025): 175,990 children (read, Shelter press release, NOT the gov.uk table) and 134,760 households (read, gov.uk via search summary). https://england.shelter.org.uk/media/press_release/rough_sleeping_and_child_homelessness_hit_highest_levels_since_records_began_
- So 175,990 is real but is the Sep 2025 quarter, not the latest. Pick one quarter and use its households AND children together.

## Cost of temporary accommodation (2026-11-16)

**Latest year is 2025-26 (published 17 Sep 2026), not 2024-25. The "~£2.3bn in 2023-24" memory is roughly right for 2023-24 (MHCLG ~£2.3bn; NAO's own measure £2.13bn, unverified, search summary) but is two years old.**

### Primary: MHCLG RO4 outturn table, 2025-26 (opened and summed from the file, £ thousand, England row)
Source file: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab26f188cb3c0580e500fa7/RO4_LA_Data_2025-26_data_by_LA.ods (sheet "LA_drop-down", Table 1c Homelessness, lines 80-86). Release: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/local-authority-revenue-expenditure-and-financing-england-2025-to-2026-first-release/local-authority-revenue-expenditure-and-financing-england-2025-to-2026-first-release
- **Gross spend on temporary accommodation, lines 80-86: £2,934.6m (£2.93bn)**, including £206.0m administration (our sum of the lines; matches Shelter's £2.935bn). Excluding admin: £2,728.7m.
- **Net to councils (after sales/fees and other income such as housing-benefit/DWP-type income): £1,476.1m (£1.48bn)** (our sum of the net column). LGA quotes £1.47bn (read, secondary).
- Lines, gross / net (£m):
  - Nightly paid, privately managed, self-contained: 955.1 / 494.3
  - Bed and breakfast hotels (incl. shared annexes): 822.6 / 467.5
  - Private sector accommodation leased by authority or registered provider: 609.2 / 271.2
  - Hostels: 109.5 / 26.2
  - Local authority or housing association stock: 129.7 / -4.8
  - Any other type (incl. private landlord and not known): 102.5 / 57.9
  - Administration: 206.0 / 157.9
- **Nightly-paid + B&B = £1,777.8m gross, 61% of the £2,934.6m total (65% of the ex-admin £2,728.7m, as Shelter says).** Adding private-sector leased (£609.2m) gives £2,387m, about 81% of gross, in private-market or hotel accommodation. That last sum is ours; "private landlords" is our grouping, the file does not label it so; B&B hotels and nightly-paid units are privately run but the file does not say who owns them.
- Whole homelessness line (all services, not just TA): £4.05bn gross, £2.34bn net. Do not confuse with TA.
- **Caveats (primary, front page of the file):** England totals are ESTIMATED from 377 of 413 councils' returns (the other 36 filled from grant data or last year's proportions). The release also says sub-category splits changed because Lambeth, Newham and Westminster changed how they report, so do not trust year-on-year movement between lines.
- Secondary, same numbers: Shelter, "£8 million a day": £2.935bn, £8.04m a day (our check: 2.935bn/365), nightly paid £955m (35%), B&B £823m (30%), combined 65%, up 88% in five years. https://england.shelter.org.uk/media/press_release/councils_spend_more_than_8_million_a_day_on_temporary_accommodation (read)

### Previous year, 2024-25 (first release, primary; note the release text was part-revised June 2026)
- Net TA incl. admin £1,411m (Table 2a). Gross total about £2.84bn (£2.842bn; read, Shelter/LocalGov, unverified against the table). Nightly paid privately managed net rose to £597m (release, primary) in 2024-25.
- https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/local-authority-revenue-expenditure-and-financing-england-2024-to-2025-first-release/local-authority-revenue-expenditure-and-financing-england-2024-to-2025-first-release
- 2024-25 to 2025-26 gross: £2.84bn to £2.93bn, about +3% in cash (our arithmetic; the "+25%" quoted in 2025 press was 2023-24 to 2024-25).

### Per night / per household
- **No official per-night or per-household cost found.** Shelter gives none; MHCLG publishes none; LSE/London Councils says nightly-paid rates are uncapped (read, snippet only, unverified).
- Our crude figure only: £2.93bn / 135,580 households in TA at 31 Mar 2026 (primary, above) = about £21,600 per household in TA that year. Gross, a year-end snapshot divided into a full-year flow, so it is a rough guide, not a cost per family. Do not put on screen without a footnote.
