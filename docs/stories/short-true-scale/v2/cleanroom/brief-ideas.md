## The source moment
A viral clip of three friends chatting on a podcast (US). One asks: "How many homeless people do you think there are?" The others
guess millions. "600,000." Then: "You know how many unoccupied livable homes there are?" They guess double, triple. "16 million."
Silence, swearing. "We could solve the homeless crisis tomorrow." And: when you ask people why there are homeless people, they say
"Well, who owns those homes?" — "If your response is, 'But who owns those places?' it tells me everything I need to know."
**What makes it land:** casual conversation, people GUESSING the numbers and being wrong, the reveal of the real number, the
reaction. Not a lecture, not a graphic.

## Our numbers (England, official; use these, never the US ones)
- 134,210 homeless households in temporary accommodation (B&Bs, hostels, short lets); 85,800 with children. Only about 4,800 people sleep rough.
- 303,185 homes empty long-term (6+ months); plus 267,894 second homes (furnished, nobody's main home). About 4 for every homeless household.
- Councils in England spent £2.9 billion on temporary accommodation in 2025-26, £1.8 billion of it on B&Bs and nightly lets.

## Our world (characters available, use if they help)
- **Bob** (from our film "Camping"): in 2008 a middle manager, married, a normal nice guy; lost his wife and everything; now lives in a tent in a supermarket car park, ignored. Not lazy: he is the viewer five bad years later. His look is fixed by a reference sheet (don't describe him).
- **The Bank Robbery** (our heist comedy): eleven politicians and media people rob a bank by carrying money INTO it; left and right argue the whole way, holding one end of the same pallet each. Scenes in a greasy-spoon café (a Reservoir Dogs-style breakfast); **Denise** the waitress (by night the bank's cleaner, the only one who notices, because she counts); **Barry**, a café regular who talks with Denise about rent.
- **BadCode** itself: a superintelligence from a future that went wrong, sending warnings back; appears as a figure in a black balaclava with a white ring and a red triangle. Voice: sarcastic, certain, on the viewer's side.

## The audience and the lines we keep
Working-class UK viewer, economy has failed them, drifting right. **The reader is the hero, never the pupil.** Never "you've been
fooled". Name the decision (who keeps homes empty, who profits), never blame homeless people or the viewer. Not a charity advert,
not a left-wing lecture: the point should land for anyone who feels robbed. No real person's likeness, no real show copied (a
format can be echoed, e.g. "two people chatting in a car getting coffee", but no names, branding or recreation of a real show).
