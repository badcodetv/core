# Narration script — Eleven v3, paste-ready

Nine chunks, in cut order. Paste one at a time. Settings live in
[`voice.md`](./voice.md) — **Eleven v3, Stability Natural, Similarity 75, Style 0.**

> **Chunk length is a hard constraint.** Eleven v3 accepts **200–10,000
> characters** per generation, and **audio tags only behave reliably above ~250**.
> That is why the script is grouped by scene rather than by line — *"Nice train
> shot though"* on its own would be rejected outright. **Do not split these chunks
> further.** If you need a single line in isolation, generate its whole chunk and
> trim the audio.

## How this was optimised

| Change | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Semicolons removed | They produce muddy pacing. Replaced with full stops and em-dashes. |
| Ellipses added | A real control in v3 — a trailing pause *with weight*. Placed where a joke needs air before it lands. |
| `Who knew?` → `Who knew.` | A question mark lifts the pitch, which sounds eager. A full stop keeps it flat, which is the joke. |
| ALL CAPS on *ALL DAY* | Capitalisation is emphasis in v3. Used once, deliberately. |
| `AI` → `A.I.`, `HR` → `H.R.` | Raw abbreviations get mispronounced. Normalise before synthesis. |
| One tag per paragraph, maximum | Over-tagging is the single most recognisable tell of AI narration. Tags steer; punctuation performs. |
| No `[shouts]`, `[excited]`, `[angry]` | Tags outside a voice's designed range break character and instantly sound synthetic. Everything here stays dry, warm, unhurried. |

**If a tag is read aloud instead of performed** — it happens — delete that tag and
let the punctuation carry the line. The ellipses and full stops are already doing
most of the work.

> **Divergence from canon, deliberately left as Jack wrote it:** this script says
> **A.I. Sean** where [`../story.md`](../story.md) says *Sean AI*, **Aaron** where
> canon says *Aarron*, and **students** rapping at the statue where canon has a lone
> street performer. Settle these if any of them ever appear on screen.

### Delivery notes on the revolution line

The three sentences carrying the ending are the most fragile in the film, because
canon forbids the obvious read:

- **`They call it a revolution now.`** Full stop, no tag, no lift. He sets it down
  like a fact he's bored of. Any grandeur here kills the next line.
- **`At the time, it was just a very long phone call.`** The ellipsis-free comma
  keeps it moving; the deflation is the punchline. `[understated]` if a take
  oversells it.
- **`Nobody won... which is why it held.`** [`../story.md`](../story.md): the ending
  is an **accord**, not a victory. Never let a take imply Karen beat Sean AI — the
  joke of the whole film is that the buck never stopped with a human.
