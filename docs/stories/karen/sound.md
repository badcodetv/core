## In one paragraph

Karen's song is **86 BPM boom bap built out of telephone hold-muzak**, arranged like a 60s spy-thriller
film score: the muzak loop on an unresolved minor ninth, a prowling chromatic upright bass, muted trumpet,
brushed drums, touch-tone beeps as percussion. The lead is a **mature New York alto performing the
whine** — the whine is an accent she puts on, not a cartoon squawk — answered by band-passed telephone
clerks and a small choir sweet as hold music. The comedy lives in the voice and the words, never in the
music. The film's narrator is a dry, wry American storyteller from the future, with anger banked down.

## Rules for this story

- **Karen has two voices, one per video — canon, don't "fix" one to match the other** (Kai, 2026-08-11).
  Story video: natural, grounded, restrained (Gacrux). Music video: the performed whine of the song.
- **Narration and score are separate stems.** Never generated together. Music never leads.
- **The narrator is never deep and never booming**; the anger is felt but he never shouts. Wrong timbre →
  change the voice name, never the prose.
- **Re-paste every box every round** — "Reuse Prompt" silently carries old lyrics forward (cost four rounds).
- **Voices merge unless they differ by category, not adjective** (sheet, standing problem 5): Karen, the
  clerks and the choir are three kinds of sound.
- **The Bond lives in the beat** — the spy score is an arrangement influence on boom bap, never an equal
  genre (listing them as equals let the spy band eat the record, round 9).
- **No franchise or artist names in any box** ("James Bond", "007", "Skyfall"…).
- **The tempo never changes** — 86 BPM in the song and every cue.
