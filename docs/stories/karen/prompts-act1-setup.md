## §1.1 is deliberately not here

Canon: *"Works as-is in the draft. Done."* The zoom-in from Manhattan to the office
window needs no new plate, and generating one would invite a rebuild of a scene that
is finished. **If it ever is re-shot**, the aerial half overlaps with Act 2's
*"Aerial Manhattan — photoreal, looking down over the city"* and should be made once
and used twice.

## The spine: Act 1 is one outfit and one day

§1.2 through §1.7 are **the same day**, so the wardrobe is a continuity spine:

> **cream blouse · camel cashmere cardigan · tailored trousers · low-heel flats** —
> immaculate in the office, cardigan slipping by the bar, wrecked by the river, slept
> in by the morning.

**Add a lanyard for the office scenes only** (§1.2–§1.4), and lose it from §1.5
onward. Her sheet lists a lanyard among her signals while the Character Look prompt
dresses her in the blouse and cardigan; wearing both, and dropping the lanyard when
she stops being an employee, resolves that quietly and gives the act a visual clock.

## The design principle for §1.2 and §1.3

**The narrator says one thing and the picture shows the opposite.** That is the whole
device — *"Karen listening intently to feedback"* over a shot of her already turning
away. So every plate below is written to be **read against a line it contradicts**,
and the comedy is in the gap.

Which means: **do not let the plates editorialise.** No mugging, no comedy faces. The
funnier the straighter they are played. Her sheet's register is *"controlled
irritation"*, not caricature — the caricature lives in the song, not here.

## One deliberate exception to the style lock

The lock owns capture and optics, and scene blocks do not restate texture — that is
the standing rule from the research pass. **§2i.9 and §2i.10 break it on purpose**,
because they are shot on a *different camera*: the laptop's own webcam. Naming
hardware is the documented way to shift a whole look, so those two blocks override the
lock's optics explicitly and say why. **This is the only case in Act 1 where a scene
block is allowed to touch optics.**

# §1.2 Karen at work — REDO

Two plates, two narrator lines, both ironic.

### 2i.1 The morning motivational speech — plate

Against: *"Karen giving her usual morning motivational speech."*

The joke is entirely in the room. **She is doing it beautifully and nobody is
receiving it** — one person under the table on a phone, one at the window, one with
their eyes shut. Everyone in frame is doing something real and small, which is also
the anti-slop counter: a sterile boardroom of attentive faces is the generic image.

**Cast:** `@Karen`.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman in her fifties standing at the head of a meeting-room table mid-presentation, addressing six seated colleagues who are not listening.

Composition: 16:9, from the back corner of the room at seated eye level, so a colleague's shoulder and the back of a chair sit large and out of focus in the near foreground; she stands well right of centre with the length of the table running away from camera; the frame is not quite level.

Action: caught mid-sentence with one hand raised palm-up and the other flat on the table — while one colleague thumbs a phone held below the table edge, one stares out of the window, one has their eyes closed and their chin dropping, and one is quietly eating.

Location: an ordinary open-plan office meeting room on a weekday morning — a scuffed veneer table, mismatched task chairs, a whiteboard wiped to a grey smear, a dying pot plant on the sill, takeaway cups and a plate of untouched pastries, a tangle of AV cable in the middle of the table, glass partition walls with the open-plan floor visible beyond.

Light: overhead fluorescent panels, flat and slightly green, plus grey daylight from the window wall camera-left; no other source.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film — an ordinary morning meeting nobody wanted. Available light only, unretouched, natural skin texture.

Constraints: cream blouse, camel cashmere cardigan neatly draped over her shoulders, tailored trousers, staff lanyard; she is composed and entirely sincere, not comic; keep every whiteboard mark, cup, screen and badge free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.2 Listening intently to feedback — plate

Against: *"Karen listening intently to feedback."*

One image, one gesture: **she is already leaving.** A colleague is mid-sentence with a
hand still up; Karen has turned away and is halfway out of frame. No expression work
needed at all — the body language is the entire joke, which makes it a cheap plate and
a robust one.

**Cast:** `@Karen`.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman turning away from a younger colleague who is still mid-sentence.

Composition: 16:9, at standing eye level in an open-plan office aisle; the colleague sits left of frame at their desk, Karen crosses the right third already half out of frame with her back three-quarters to them; a monitor and the edge of a partition crop the near foreground out of focus.

Action: mid-stride away, one hand already lifted in a small dismissive acknowledgement without looking back, while the seated colleague still has a hand raised and their mouth open.

Location: an ordinary open-plan office floor on a weekday morning — desks with dual monitors and cable spaghetti, sticky notes fringing a screen, a keyboard with crumbs in it, a jacket over the back of a chair, a shared printer with a paper tray hanging open, a wall of grey daylight beyond.

Light: overhead fluorescent panels, flat and slightly green, with grey window light from deep in the background.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film. Available light only, unretouched, natural skin texture.

Constraints: cream blouse, camel cardigan, tailored trousers, staff lanyard; her expression is pleasant and unbothered rather than sneering; keep every screen, sticky note, badge and printout free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.4 Inside the microwave — plate

**Cast:** `@Karen`. **The signature shot.**

Canon calls for the camera to go inside the microwave, and it is the best image in the
act: a POV nobody expects, through greased glass, with the door mesh as a built-in
foreground occluder. Four anti-slop counters come free with the idea — the odd angle,
the dirty glass, the depth layering, the ugly single light.

Fire this one with a couple of candidates and pick on the **glass**: if it is clean,
it has gone generic.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman's face and upper body seen from inside a microwave, looking out through the greased inner glass and the perforated door screen as she closes the door.

Composition: 16:9, from deep inside the oven cavity at turntable height looking out; the perforated metal screen fills the frame as a fine dark grid in hard foreground, her face beyond it and low-centre; the container edge crosses the bottom of frame very close and out of focus.

Action: mid-movement, pushing the door shut with the heel of one hand, her eyes already going to the keypad rather than to camera.

Location: the inside of a well-used office microwave — a scratched glass turntable, dried splatter and grease smears across the inner glass, a scorch mark on the cavity wall; the kitchenette and the office floor beyond her reduced to soft shapes.

Light: the small internal oven lamp coming on from above and behind camera, warm and dim, against the flat green fluorescent light of the kitchenette beyond the glass.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film — a point of view nobody chose to have. Available light only, unretouched.

Constraints: cream blouse, camel cardigan, staff lanyard swinging forward as she leans in; her expression is ordinary and preoccupied, not comic; keep every dial marking, label and notice free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.5 What it feels like to be on the receiving end — plate

**Cast:** `@Karen`. **This is the switch, and the point of the scene.**

Canon: *"the perspective flips to the cleaner — 'and the cleaner thinks: what a knob.'
We've done enough of 'Karen's a prick'; this shot is what it feels like to be on the
receiving end of Karen."*

So the camera **is** the cleaner: low, close to the floor, looking up. Karen is above,
pointing down at a spot. The angle does the whole job — she is literally talking down
into the lens, and the audience is standing where it lands.

> **Play the cleaner with dignity and keep the frame ordinary.** Do not stack markers
> of hardship; the beat works because the cleaner is a competent person being
> condescended to, not because they are pitiable. This also keeps it clear of the
> policy trigger the art-direction skill flags.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman standing over the camera and pointing down at a spot on the floor, seen from the low viewpoint of the person she is speaking to.

Composition: 16:9, from crouching height very close to the floor looking steeply up at her, so she is large in frame and the ceiling tiles and fluorescent panels run away behind her head; the top of a mop handle and a gloved hand cross the near foreground out of focus; slightly off level.

Action: mid-sentence, weight on one hip, one arm extended down toward a small mark on the vinyl just in front of the lens, the other hand holding a takeaway coffee cup.

Location: an office corridor by the lifts at lunchtime — grey vinyl flooring with a wheeled cleaning cart just in shot, a yellow wet-floor sign folded against the wall, scuffed skirting, a recycling station with its lids open, the open-plan floor beyond.

Light: overhead fluorescent panels directly above her, flat and slightly green, lighting her from above and leaving the underside of her face shadowed.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film — the shot is the other person's point of view, and it should feel like being talked down to. Available light only, unretouched, natural skin texture.

Constraints: cream blouse, camel cardigan, tailored trousers, staff lanyard hanging forward as she leans down; she is polite and helpful in manner rather than angry; keep every sign, label, badge and notice free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.6 Even Karen has a manager — plate

**Cast:** `@Karen`.

Against: *"Even Karen has a manager. And he'd like a word."* Canon's prompt note is
explicit: **Karen has a laptop on her lap.** That detail is the entire mechanism of the
scene and must be visible in the plate.

The manager is not a recurring Character, so he is described rather than cast: ordinary,
decent, and visibly dreading this. He does not know it is about to happen to him too.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman and a man in his forties seated at right angles across the corner of a small meeting-room table, mid-conversation, with a closed laptop resting on her lap.

Composition: 16:9, from across the room at seated eye level, both figures in a loose two-shot with her left of centre and him right; a stack of chairs and the edge of the glass partition crop the near foreground out of focus; not quite level.

Action: he is mid-sentence, leaning forward with his hands clasped and his eyes not quite meeting hers; she sits back with her ankles crossed, listening with polite impatience, one hand resting flat on the closed laptop on her lap.

Location: a small glass-walled meeting room off an open-plan floor, on a weekday morning — a veneer table with a single water jug and two glasses, half-closed vertical blinds on the glass wall, a wall-mounted screen switched off, the open-plan office visible and indifferent beyond the glass, a chair pushed back at an angle.

Light: overhead fluorescent panels, flat and slightly green, with grey daylight through the blinds camera-right laying stripes across the table.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film — a decent man doing something he has been told to do. Available light only, unretouched, natural skin texture.

Constraints: she wears a cream blouse, camel cardigan, tailored trousers and a staff lanyard; he wears an untucked shirt with no tie and a staff lanyard; his manner is apologetic and awkward, hers is composed; keep every screen, badge, notice and document free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.8 Sean AI — the screen plate

**Cast:** none. **No `STYLE LOCK` on this one** — see the note below.

This is a **design artifact**, not a photograph, and it recurs: §4.3 has to reuse it
exactly. Canon: minimal, an **8-bit pixel-art moniker** parodying the Claude Code
symbol, *"Sean AI"* — **iconography, not a face. Never humanize him.**

> **Two departures, both deliberate.**
>
> 1. **The style lock is omitted.** It is a film-capture emulation, and this is a
>    rendered UI seen head-on. Grain and halation would fight it. The photographed
>    version of this screen is §2i.9's job.
> 2. **The message area is generated empty.** The copy goes on in `@badcode/comic`.
>    The one piece of lettering the plate may carry is the moniker wordmark, because
>    it is a logo rather than a message — and if it renders badly, drop it and set
>    that in the comic too.
>
> **Generate this at the highest resolution available and keep the source.** It is
> reused at §4.3 and it must match to the pixel; a second generation will not.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a minimal software interface filling the frame, head-on, as it would appear on a laptop screen.

Composition: 16:9, perfectly square-on and centred — this one frame is deliberately symmetrical, unlike the rest of the sequence; a small 8-bit pixel-art glyph sits alone in the upper third, with a wide empty message area beneath it and generous margins all round.

Action: the interface is at rest, displaying nothing but the glyph and an empty message area.

Location: a flat digital interface — no room, no reflection, no device bezel, no desk.

Style: minimal corporate software UI, flat vector, near-black background with one restrained accent colour; the glyph is chunky low-resolution pixel art in the manner of an early-1980s computer icon — a simple abstract mark, geometric and friendly-looking, with no face, no eyes and nothing humanoid about it. Clean flat rendering with no photographic texture, no grain, no glow and no drop shadows.

Constraints: the message area is completely empty — no body text, no paragraphs, no placeholder lines, no cursor, no buttons, no menu bar, no window chrome, no icons other than the single glyph; nothing anthropomorphic anywhere.
```

### 2i.9 The laptop camera looks back — plate

**Cast:** `@Karen`. **⚠️ This block overrides the lock's optics — see the note above.**

Canon: cut to the laptop-camera POV looking back at Karen, *"shocked/dismayed (avoid
the AI wide-eyes shocked face)."*

The move that makes it work is that it really is **the webcam**: low, close, wide and
distorted, sensor-noisy, lit by the screen. Naming that hardware shifts the entire
look — and it is also, conveniently, the most anti-slop frame in the act, because
webcam capture is everything the generic AI image is not.

**Underplay the reaction.** She has not processed it yet. Stillness reads as dismay;
a face doing *shock* reads as an AI face doing shock.

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: a woman looking down into a laptop camera from close range, having just read something on the screen.

Composition: 16:9, from the laptop's own built-in webcam — very low, very close, angled steeply up at her face and the ceiling behind her, with the wide-angle distortion and the slight barrel curve that lens has; she sits low-centre and slightly right; the top edge of the open screen crosses the very bottom of frame.

Action: gone completely still, eyes tracking down and across the screen rather than at the lens, lips just parted, one hand frozen where it was.

Location: the same small glass-walled meeting room, the ceiling tiles and a fluorescent panel visible above and behind her, the manager a blurred shape at the edge of the frame.

Light: the laptop screen underlighting her face from below, cool and even, against the flat green fluorescent light from the ceiling.

Style: low-resolution built-in webcam capture — soft focus, visible sensor noise in the shadows, slight motion smear, muted washed-out colour and a cool cast. This shot is captured on the laptop's own camera rather than on film, so it does not carry the film texture of the surrounding scene.

Constraints: cream blouse, camel cardigan, staff lanyard; her expression is blank and absorbing rather than wide-eyed or theatrical; keep every badge, ceiling marking and screen free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.11 One or two drinks in — plate

**Cast:** `@Karen`, `@Susan`. Against: *"Karen sensibly decided to have just the one
and get an early night."*

Canon is strict about the length here — *"a couple of seconds, no more"* — so this is
one image, and it has one job: **they are not gone yet.** Susan is consoling her. That
sobriety is what makes the compilation funny half a second later.

> **This is also the first two-Character frame in the act**, and the gentlest test of
> Susan's reference register — warm interior, both women lit by the same practical.
> If she reads as lit differently to Karen here, the divergence flagged in her sheet
> needs re-shooting. See [`characters/susan.md`](./characters/susan.md).

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: two women side by side at a bar counter, one consoling the other, early in the evening.

Composition: 16:9, from along the bar at seated eye level so the counter runs away in a strong diagonal; they sit right of centre with the length of the bar and its bottles falling out of focus behind them; a stranger's shoulder and a glass crop the near foreground; not quite level.

Action: mid-conversation — the younger woman half-turned on her stool with a hand on the older one's forearm, the older one staring straight ahead at the back of the bar with her chin up, holding the stem of an untouched second drink.

Location: an ordinary New York bar early on a weekday evening — a scratched wooden counter with ring marks, a paper coaster gone soft, a bowl of nuts, a folded card machine, a rail of mismatched stools, warm bottle-lit shelves behind, other drinkers as soft shapes further down.

Light: warm practical light from the shelf behind the bar and one low pendant above the counter; no other source.

Style: a still from a 35mm independent film — the beginning of a long night that nobody has admitted to yet. Available light only, unretouched, natural skin texture.

Constraints: the older woman wears a cream blouse and a camel cardigan, still neat, no lanyard; the younger wears an unzipped olive suede bomber over a cream t-shirt; both are sober and subdued, not yet drunk; keep every bottle label, coaster, card and sign free of readable lettering.
```

### 2i.12 The Polaroid compilation — one recipe, ten frames

Canon: *"~10 shots, roughly half a second each, of the two of them across different
bars getting progressively smashed — styled as Polaroids."* Four seconds of screen
time, and the whole premise lands.

**Do not write ten prompts.** Write the recipe once and change one line per frame —
that is how the compilation stays a *set*, and Flow generates four candidates a go, so
ten frames is three generations.

> **The register is different here and it is the point.** These are not film stills,
> they are **flash snapshots**: on-camera flash, blown-out foreground, black nothing
> behind, red-eye, a thumb over the corner. The `STYLE LOCK` already asks for polaroid
> and snapshot energy — **let it carry that, and add only the flash.** The one thing
> to add per frame is the drunkenness, and it should live in posture, wardrobe and
> mess, never in a described expression.

**The recipe** — paste the lock, then this, changing only the bracketed line:

```prompt
SCENE:

Subject: two women photographed together on a night out, caught by a friend's on-camera flash.

Composition: 16:9, hand-held snapshot framing — badly composed on purpose: heads low or crowded to one edge, too much ceiling or too little, the horizon tipped several degrees, one of them half cut off by the frame edge.

Action: [THE FRAME — swap this line per shot, see the list below]

Location: [THE BAR — swap this line per shot]

Light: a single direct on-camera flash from close range — hot blown-out highlights on the nearest faces and glasses, hard black shadows thrown onto the wall behind, the depth of the room falling away into darkness.

Style: an amateur flash snapshot taken by a friend on a night out, of the kind that gets stuck to a fridge. Unposed, badly timed, unretouched.

Constraints: the older woman wears a cream blouse and a camel cardigan; the younger an olive suede bomber over a cream t-shirt; their drunkenness shows only in posture, wardrobe and mess — never in a described expression; keep every bottle label, pump badge, menu, coaster and sign free of readable lettering.
```

**The ten frames — the arc is in the wardrobe and the wreckage, in this order:**

| # | Action line | Bar line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | arms round each other, glasses raised toward the lens, both upright and neat | the same wooden counter as 2i.11, two fresh drinks |
| 2 | mid-laugh with heads tipped together, one hand blurred coming up toward the lens | a crowded standing bar, backs and shoulders pressed close |
| 3 | leaning across a small table shouting at each other over the noise, cardigan now off one shoulder | a booth with a table of empties between them |
| 4 | one pulling the other by the wrist toward the door, both mid-stride and motion-blurred | a doorway with the street dark beyond |
| 5 | squashed cheek to cheek far too close to the lens, the frame mostly faces | a dive bar, a fruit machine glowing behind |
| 6 | both holding shot glasses at the counter, one with her eyes shut mid-swallow | a sticky bar top with a row of empty shot glasses |
| 7 | dancing badly, arms up, entirely out of focus | a small back room with a dark ceiling |
| 8 | sitting on a kerb outside with a takeaway box between them, shoes off | a kerbside under a streetlight, a bar door behind |
| 9 | one asleep upright on the other's shoulder in a booth, the other mugging at the lens | a quiet booth late on, tables cleared around them |

## Fire order, and what to watch

1. **2i.4** — inside the microwave. The best image in the act and the one most likely
   to need rounds. Fire it first while there is patience for it.
2. **2i.8** — the Sean AI screen. Everything in §1.4 and §4.3 depends on it, it is
   reused pixel-for-pixel, and it is unlike anything else in the story. Settle it early.
3. **2i.9 → 2i.10** — the webcam pair, adjacent, with 2i.9 attached to 2i.10.
4. **2i.6 → 2i.7** — the meeting-room setup and the POV.
5. **2i.5** — the cleaner's viewpoint. The scene's actual point.
6. **2i.11**, then the **2i.12 compilation** in three generations of four.
7. **2i.1**, **2i.2** — the office beats. Easy, and nothing depends on them.

**What to watch on 2i.4:** the glass. If it is clean, the model has reverted to type
and the shot has lost its whole texture. Dirt is the subject.

**What to watch on 2i.8:** anything anthropomorphic. If the glyph has acquired eyes, a
smile or a suggestion of a face, reject it — *"iconography, not a face. Never humanize
him"* is canon and this is the one plate that can break it.

**What to watch on 2i.9 / 2i.10:** whether it actually looks like a webcam or just
like a low-angle film shot with noise on it. The distortion is the tell. And **watch
her face for theatre** — the note says avoid the AI wide-eyes, and that failure will
appear if the prompt is fired with any word like *shocked* in it. There isn't one.

**What to watch on 2i.11:** whether Karen and Susan look lit by the same lamp. That is
the Susan-register question and it is answerable at a glance.

**What to watch on the compilation:** whether it reads as a *set*. If frame 7 could
have come from a different film than frame 2, the recipe drifted — re-fire from the
recipe rather than editing individual frames.

**Two regenerations, then rewrite.**
