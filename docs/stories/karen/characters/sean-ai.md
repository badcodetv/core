# Sean AI

The malevolent background force of the Karen story — the same role the machines play
in Camping, and within the GPOM frame one of the thousand forms bad code takes. He
has almost no screen time and is the biggest character in the film; **the camera work
balances that discrepancy** ([`../story.md`](../story.md#storyboard--scene-by-scene-video-canon), §1.4 and §4.3).

## What he is on screen

A laptop. A studio screen. A minimal fake UI with an **8-bit pixel-art moniker** — a
parody of the Claude Code symbol — and the name **Sean AI**. That iconography is the
brand: it stamps him without humanising him.

He appears exactly twice, and it's the same scene both times:

1. **The firing (§1.4).** "I am Sean AI. I now run the HR department. I can do your
   job from here, Karen — you're redundant." Then: *"Now please pass the laptop to
   your manager."*
2. **The takeover (§4.3).** Same UI, same font, same icon — **now gold. He's
   progressed.** He hacks the broadcast the moment the President takes Karen's call
   and makes the entire government redundant, live on air.

## The rules (load-bearing — do not relax)

- **Never a face, never a body, never an origin story.** We deliberately do not say
  what Sean is or how he got the power — stay out of that room entirely. He runs
  *the HR department* when we meet him, not the company; the scope creep between his
  two appearances is the story doing its work silently.
- **Nobody ever types.** People speak *to* the screen; Sean answers in text. The
  narrator voices what Sean says where needed.
- **Personifying him this much is allowed** (unlike the master-arc AI) precisely
  because he's a manifestation that gets talked to — but the personification budget
  is the icon, the name, and the courtesy. Nothing more.

## On the song

The song's Sean ("This isn't bad code. This is Sean." — hard-man menace, Ray
Winstone-adjacent) predates the screen-only visual rule and lives on as the track's
voice cameo. On film he never speaks aloud.
