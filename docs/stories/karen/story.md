# Karen Will Lead the Revolution

> The spine: the single idea, the grounding, the devices. **The beat-by-beat,
> scene-by-scene canon lives in [`storyboard.md`](./storyboard.md)** — locked in the
> Kai + Jack session of 2026-08-05 ([transcript](./master-notes-2026-08-05.md)). This
> file carries the *why*; the storyboard carries the *what and how*.

## Key concept

The "Karen" — entitled, relentless, *I-want-to-speak-to-the-manager* — is exactly the
civic muscle a moment of mass redundancy needs. Her worst trait, complaining, is
reframed as her superpower: **persistence**. And the "ultimate manager" she escalates
to turns out not to be the President — the moment he takes her call, **Sean AI** sacks
the entire government live on air. The buck never stopped with any human.

## Background

Real grounding: automation doesn't stop at the warehouse or the call centre — it
climbs. It takes middle management (Karen), then HR (her manager), then the
government (the President). Sitting under it: how movements actually start now — not
in a manifesto but in a meme. A hook, an upload, a viral clip; culture as the spark
that organises people. That is BadCode's own thesis turned into plot — an *idea*
travels through a song in a way it never would through an essay.

This is the optimistic counterweight to [`../camping/`](../camping/README.md): same
automation-comes-for-everyone engine, but the question here is *agency* — what
ordinary people do about it.

## The shape (one line per act)

Full detail in [`storyboard.md`](./storyboard.md):

1. **Meet Karen** — the archetype we love to hate; Sean AI fires her (and her
   manager) via her own laptop; drunk night in Polaroids; phone in the river; the
   morning-after call that commits her to the phone box.
2. **The Great Escalation** — nine months in one Midtown phone box, climbing the
   ladder rung by rung; the act is the song made visual.
3. **Viral** — Ari's five-second cameo turns her hook into a hit; the train; the
   crowd grows via the drone trick.
4. **The Ultimate Manager** — TV executives escalate her to the President for
   content; two lines of phone call; Sean AI (gold now) hacks the broadcast and
   sacks the government.
5. **One hundred years later** — the lecture hall ("the Karen for President campaign
   of 2032, which ultimately led to the reconciliation between humans and AI"), the
   moral, the bronze statue still on hold.

## How it's told (the devices)

- **The narrator is the engine.** Sarcastic, certain, from the future. Carries the
  character setup, the repeated conversations, the time jumps and the joke-landing —
  the reason the piece won't read as AI slop.
- **The laptop-POV transition.** The screen swallows the frame; Sean AI's UI *is* his
  character. The same UI bookends the film — Karen's firing, then the government's.
- **The zoom-map escalation.** Photoreal aerial Manhattan; zoom down to a building,
  cut to a functionary, "I want to speak to your manager," zoom out — weather and
  seasons turning in the wide shots. Persistence rendered as camera grammar.
- **The song as structure.** Act 2 recreates the song's escalation ladder rung for
  rung; stems of our own track become the film's hold-muzak.
- **The 100-years-later frame.** Received history — the revolution happens entirely
  off-page; the professor's question and the statue close it.

## The twist

Two turns. First, the *character* turn: the trait we're invited to despise
(relentless complaint) is the exact trait that changes things — Karen is not the
problem, she's the method. Second, the *plot* turn: the "ultimate manager" is not the
President. The moment the joke completes — the President on the line — Sean AI takes
the government, and every human in the chain of command turns out to have been as
redundant as Karen was on day one.

## The message

The optimistic inversion of the BadCode beat. Not "humans, don't make this mistake"
so much as "here's the muscle you'll need when you do." Automation comes for
everyone — even the President — but organised, relentless, *persistent* people are
the answer. Up your Karen game.

Sharpened 2026-07-22 (still canon): **it's not complaining, it's resisting.** We're
puppeting the comfortable middle-class person in denial about the AI coming; Karen
was that person, got put through the ringer, and now warns from the outside. People
who hate Karens should recognise the part of her they'll need to adopt. There's
compassion in a character everyone loves to hate — and the statue proves the
persistence worked. The ending is an **accord** — reconciliation between humans and
AI — not a victory.
