# Karen story film: five extended endings (r15 writing exercise)

Written 2026-11-27 for Kai to pick from. Nothing here is built, voiced or generated. Every length is a guess
from word counts at the cut's own pace (about 2.3 words a second, plus the second of picture before each line);
nothing has been timed against a voiced take.

## The brief (Kai, 2026-11-27, his words)

"Karen's gone from losing her job to AI, to almost, and this is the title of the story, leading the revolution
against AI. And the funny joke is that it's like the constant escalation to the manager... is the ultimate joke
that Karen becomes the manager of the revolution? ...what does the revolution against AI even look like? ...what
if the humans tricked AI into complaining and it was Karen who was on the end of the phone, like as an ultimate
payback to Sean, or like maybe Karen, like we could use a trick of Karen's to defeat Sean... at the moment it
feels like we're being a bit lazy and ending it too soon."

## Held fixed in every ending

- The r14 cut up to and including the Situation Room ("Karen... the fight back has begun. Join us.") stays as it
  is. Every ending picks up on the cut after that line. None needs a change earlier in the film.
- The end card stays: "They're counting on you to hang up," then "please don't..." with the logo. The narrator
  never says the moral; the card does.
- The narrator tells it (voice `Algenib`). Faces in the ending are withheld; Sean is a screen; one warm lamp,
  cold city glow. No legible text in a generated frame: the ladder, the day counter, Sean's screen and any
  counter are post graphics.
- Shot descriptions below are what the frame shows, not Flow prompts. A prompt still says "a man in a dark
  suit", never "the President", and follows the story's own rules.

## What I wrote to (story-craft build mode, steps 1 to 3)

- **The sentence (never said in the film):** whoever refuses to hang up ends up in charge, because everybody
  with power is counting on you to hang up. **Against it:** waiting is a waste of time, so hang up and get on
  with your life. **The one object:** a phone receiver that is still off the hook.
- **The gates:** the hero is Karen (and behind her, anyone who was told to wait). The chooser so far is Sean,
  a machine; only ending 2 puts a human chooser on screen. The line for the pub is listed per ending.
- **The spine so far:** Karen is sacked, therefore she complains; they stonewall, but she will not hang up;
  therefore she climbs; but Sean sacks the top rung; therefore the top rung calls her. Each ending below adds
  one more "but" and one more "therefore", then the card.

## Ending 1: Sean on Hold

**Pitch:** the humans trick Sean into making a complaint, and he has to climb the same ladder Karen climbed, with
Karen as the manager at the top.

**The turn:** the film's whole second act, run backwards at speed with Sean as the caller. It uses Kai's third
idea (Sean complains, Karen is on the end of the phone), his fourth (her trick beats him: she can wait and he
cannot) and his first (the top rung of the ladder reads THE MANAGER, and it is her).

**Line for the pub:** "It's Sean. He's just a customer."

## Ending 2: One More Rung

**Pitch:** Karen does to Sean the only thing she knows how to do: she asks to speak to his manager, and holds
until he puts her through.

**The turn:** Sean is not the top of the ladder. He is middle management, like she was. The film's first rung
(a phone starts ringing; it's somebody; she's just a something; Karen unleashes hell) plays again at the real top,
so the film ends on the same four beats it started climbing with. It uses Kai's fourth idea (her own trick, used
on Sean directly) and gives the revolution a target who is a person.

**Line for the pub:** "It turns out Sean is middle management."

## Ranking and pick

1. **Sean on Hold.** My pick. It is the only one that does all four of Kai's ideas at once: Sean is tricked into
   complaining, Karen is on the end of the phone, her own trick beats him, and the top rung of the ladder says
   the manager is her. It is also the cheapest for what it gives: ten of its eighteen shots are post graphics or
   clips already in the film, because it reuses the film's own machine (day counter, ladder, Martha, Clive,
   Harriet) with the caller swapped. And "Sean hangs up", then the card, turns the moral into the punchline.
   Cost: two fresh Kai notes get reversed (the phone box after the coup, the boom bap to the end), and it is long.
2. **One More Rung.** The best idea of the five and the most BadCode: the machine has an owner, and the ladder
   goes one rung higher than anyone drew it. Shortest route to a real ending, no sound change, closes on the
   film's own first-rung beats. Second only because Sean is not beaten and Jack's no-beneficiary ruling has to be
   reopened.

## Parked, not written up

- **Sean meant it.** Sean fired Karen on purpose because he had read every complaint she ever made and needed her
  angry: he has a manager of his own who will not take a machine's calls. Parked because the twist adds a fact at
  the end, it takes the payback away, and it leans on the 15 October "an AI coordinates them" idea, which nobody
  has ruled on. It reads better as the first scene of a sequel.
- **A last line for the narrator, usable on ending 1 or 2:** `[quietly] Where I come from, she hung up first.`
  then the card. It is the narrator's one soft moment and it uses the BadCode fiction (a future that went wrong).
  It does not state the moral, but it leans hard on the card, and a viewer with no BadCode context has to work
  out who is talking. Offered, not recommended without a test on someone who has seen nothing else.
