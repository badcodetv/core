# Trip-hop (Bristol sound)

> Slow, smoky, sample-based downtempo hip-hop beats (60 to 100 BPM) with film-noir strings, dub bass and a cracked, intimate voice, from Bristol in the 1990s.
>

> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Trip-hop was born in Bristol in the early 1990s from the city's sound-system and punk scenes, slowing hip-hop breakbeats to a crawl and layering dub bass, jazz and soundtrack samples, Hammond and Rhodes, scratching and dusty vinyl crackle. Massive Attack's Blue Lines (1991), Portishead's Dummy (1994) and Tricky's Maxinquaye (1995) defined it. The mood is paranoid, melancholic and cinematic, often using spy-film and noir references. The voices are close-mic'd and fragile, and the lyrics are about isolation, class and mistrust.

## Signature traits

- Slow tempo, about 60 to 100 BPM, with heavy, lazy hip-hop breakbeats and crisp rimshots
- Dusty vinyl crackle, low-bit sampler grit and tape saturation
- Deep round dub bass lines moving slowly
- Sampled strings, spy-film guitar twang, theremin, Rhodes, Hammond organ and turntable scratches
- Close-mic'd, breathy, tired or whispered vocals, often female, with little vibrato; or a rasping male whisper
- Minimal, unresolved chord progressions in minor keys with long loops
- Echo and spring reverb in a dub manner; dark and paranoid atmosphere

## References

- *Unfinished Sympathy* — Massive Attack, 1991. Orchestral strings with a slow beat; an emotional, cinematic template.
- *Glory Box* — Portishead, 1994. Noir mood, crackle, a lonely voice and a hip-hop beat sampled from an Isaac Hayes record.
- *Maxinquaye* — Tricky, 1995. Paranoid, murmured, claustrophobic version of the sound.
- *Protection* — Massive Attack, 1994. Title track with a political, social-conscience slant and a dub backbone.

## What it carries

Hushed, suspicious, melancholy, cinematic. It carries paranoia, surveillance, loneliness and a quiet bitter dignity. It fights against high energy, jokes at volume and anything triumphant.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Narrated stories where a calm voice reveals something awful: a slow reveal, an office at night, a sombre aftermath, a personal story of a person ground down. It is a natural bed for spoken voice because the pace leaves room. It would undermine fast or boisterous satire and anything that needs a rousing chorus.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno Style box: 'trip-hop, 80 BPM, slow dusty breakbeat, vinyl crackle, deep dub bass, noir strings, Rhodes, spy-film guitar, breathy intimate female vocal, Bristol 1994'. For a bed, add 'instrumental' or 'spoken word, low calm voice'. Exclude 'EDM, pop chorus, big drums, autotune, trap, upbeat, belting vocal'. Describing the texture (crackle, tape hiss) helps more than genre words, which can drift to chillout lounge music.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno often renders trip-hop as generic 'chillout' or 'lo-fi hip hop beats to study to': clean, pleasant, with no menace. It also tends to give a polished pop-soul singer rather than a cracked intimate voice. Fix by adding 'paranoid', 'noir', 'dusty', 'tape hiss', 'spy film' and excluding 'lo-fi study beats, smooth jazz, lounge'. Real sample-based edits (a loop that is chopped and slightly off) are rarely reproduced.

## Production cost

Easy to hold consistent; slow tempo and loops make a recurring motif simple. A particular singer's timbre drifts, so use instrumental or spoken lanes for continuity. Length is fine and the style tolerates long tracks.
