# Unaccompanied traditional ballad

> A single unaccompanied voice telling a long story-song in a modal tune, with no beat to hide behind.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

The oldest layer of British and Irish song: Child ballads, Scots traveller singing, English pub and kitchen singing, and Irish sean-nos. A lone voice carries a narrative in verses of four lines, often in a Dorian, Mixolydian or other modal scale, with free rhythm that follows the words, and ornament (grace notes, slides) instead of harmony. Field recordists and the 1950s-60s folk revival brought it to record. The stories are murders, betrayals, press-gangs and bad landlords, told flatly.

## Signature traits

- One voice, no instruments, or at most a single drone, fiddle or unison squeezebox
- Free or loosely metred rhythm; phrase endings held and breathed, not quantised
- Modal tune (Dorian or Mixolydian), narrow range, each verse sung to the same melody
- Unvarnished regional vowels, no vibrato or only a slight wobble on held notes
- Ornamentation: grace notes, slides up to a note, a dropped final syllable
- Many verses (8 to 20) with almost no change of delivery, so the story does the work
- Close, dry, room-sized recording; breath audible

## References

- *Blackwaterside* — Anne Briggs, 1966. Bare solo voice in modal tune, the template for the revival sound **(Corrected: Briggs recorded it c.1963 (appears on her 1971 self-titled album). The 1966 recording is Bert Jansch's, on Jack Orion, learned from Briggs. Year 1966 for Briggs is wrong.)**
- *Sweet England* — Shirley Collins, 1959. Early revival album of traditional songs sung with minimal accompaniment
- *Lord Donald (Lord Randal)* — Jeannie Robertson, 1953. Traveller singer recorded by Hamish Henderson, the real unvarnished tradition **(Corrected: No 1953 release found. Earliest found is Collector JFS 4001 (Songs of a Scots Tinker Lady), recorded 1959 and issued 1960, with Hamish Henderson notes. Henderson did record her in the 1950s, but 1953 is unconfirmed.)**
- *Folk Roots, New Routes* — Shirley Collins and Davy Graham, 1964. Shows the tradition met by a single sparse guitar

## What it carries

Chilling, patient, sorrowful and dryly funny. It can carry a murder or a betrayal in the same flat tone as a shopping list, which is dark humour for free. It fights against anything fast, loud or triumphant, and against jokes that need a band to land.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

A single cruel story told with total authority: the landlord, the factory closing, the fire that nobody inspected. It is excellent for a narrator-from-the-future who says what happened with no comfort. It would undermine any piece needing a chorus the audience can join, momentum, or a rousing call to action; it is also slow for short films.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno responds to 'unaccompanied female folk singing, a cappella, solo voice, modal melody, free rhythm, dry close-miked, English regional accent, no vibrato'. Describe the voice (age, region, plainness) instead of naming singers. Put '[a cappella]' and a verse-by-verse lyric with identical line shape, and use 'Exclude: drums, bass, pop production, harmonies, reverb, choir, autotune, backing vocals, guitar'. Suno v5/v6 accepts a cappella but drifts toward added pad or guitar by the third verse unless the exclude box is strict. Keep verses short and rhymed ABCB so the melody is reusable.

## How generators get it wrong

The model hears 'folk' and adds fingerpicked guitar, gentle strings and a breathy indie-folk polish, which is Americana-pop and not tradition. Vocals come out young, pretty and vibrato-heavy with American vowels. It also quantises the rhythm to a grid, losing the free breath. Fix: say 'a cappella' twice (style and a [A cappella] tag), name the accent as English/Scots/Irish regional, ask for 'plain, unornamented, slightly rough voice', exclude guitar/strings/reverb, and keep lines of even syllable count so the tune does not wander. Expect to cull many takes.

## Production cost

Low for cost, high for taste. No instruments to keep consistent, but the voice is exposed so any AI sheen shows immediately; a consistent voice across takes needs the same descriptor each time. Length is a plus (long verses) but Suno often rushes the tempo, and the lack of a pulse makes it hard to cut to picture.
