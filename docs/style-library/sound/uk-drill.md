# UK drill

> Cold, menacing 140 to 145 BPM trap-derived beats with sliding 808s, triplet hi-hats and dark piano or string loops under flat, deadpan rapping.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

UK drill came out of south London (Brixton, Peckham) around 2012 to 2015 by borrowing Chicago drill's sparse menace and giving it a British sound: a bouncing 'sliding' 808 bass, stuttering hi-hat patterns, a snare on beat 3 and eerie minor-key melodies. Producers like 808Melo and Carns Hill built a distinct UK sound, and rappers (67, Headie One, Digga D) wrote in a flat, deadpan tone about road life, money and police. It became the biggest British street genre of the late 2010s, but is also entangled with police surveillance and courts treating lyrics as evidence, which matters ethically when writing in it.

## Signature traits

- Tempo about 140 to 145 BPM, half-time feel with snare on beat 3
- Sliding 808 bass that glides between notes, with a distinct syncopated pattern
- Hi-hats in triplets and stutters with sudden rolls, sparse kicks
- Dark, spare, eerie loop: minor-key piano, strings, flutes, bells, choir pad
- Flat, low-register, deadpan delivery with ad-libs and 'skrrt'-style tags; slang-heavy British lyric
- Producer tag at the start; very short, hook-light song structure
- Dry vocal with a touch of room reverb and doubled ad-libs

## What it carries

Cold, threatening, bleak, deadpan, ruthless, with moments of dark wit. It carries menace and fatalism well. It fights against warmth, optimism and community uplift.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Sinister satire about power: a landlord, a lender, a press baron described with the flat menace of a street threat, where the joke is the borrowed register. It suits the villain's theme or a heist's cold planning. It would undermine any story about hope, care or ordinary tenderness, and carries a real risk: it is strongly associated with violence and real communities, so using it to tell a middle-class-written political joke can read as mockery of the people it claims to serve.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno Style box: 'UK drill, 142 BPM, sliding 808 bass, triplet hi-hats, dark minor piano loop, eerie strings, flat deadpan male London rap, sparse'. Exclude 'melodic singing, autotune pop, EDM, major key, happy, Auto-Tune ballad, American southern accent'. Keep lyrics non-violent and about economics or institutions; tag [Verse] with short lines, and use a short hook. Describing 'sliding 808 with long glides' is the key phrase for the signature bass.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno tends to blend UK drill with US trap and melodic rap, giving smooth auto-tuned singing, and it regularly uses an American or generic accent. The 808 glide pattern is often rendered as a flat bass note. The flat, deadpan delivery becomes emotive or sing-songy. Fix by excluding autotune and melodic rap, naming 'flat monotone delivery', and rerolling for the voice. Lyrics need hand-editing so they do not slip into cliche gang imagery.

## Production cost

Instrumentals are consistent. Vocal accent and delivery drift are the main cost, and the genre is very voice-dependent. A single-minded story could sustain 2 to 3 minute tracks, but the thin musical content gets repetitive over a long film.
