# Baroque concerto and ground bass

> Bright, driving strings and harpsichord in sequences and ostinati, with a rigid mechanical forward motion.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Music of roughly 1600-1750 (Vivaldi, Bach, Handel, Purcell) built on a continuo bass line, terraced dynamics and repeated rhythmic cells that spin forward like clockwork. Concertos alternate a full ensemble with a soloist; a ground bass (passacaglia, chaconne) repeats a short bass pattern while everything above it escalates. It reads as order, wealth and formality, which is exactly why it works as ironic cover for greed and bureaucracy.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 100-140 bpm in fast movements, steady with no rubato, relentless eighth- and sixteenth-note motor rhythm
- Harpsichord or chamber organ continuo plus cello/bass doubling a walking bass line
- Violins in rapid scales and arpeggio sequences that climb by step, repeated a tone lower or higher
- Terraced dynamics: loud tutti then sudden soft solo, no gradual crescendos
- Ground bass of 4 or 8 bars repeating under variations (passacaglia and chaconne)
- Trumpets and timpani for ceremonial pieces; otherwise strings only, no drum kit
- Dry, close acoustic, little reverb in chamber versions; cathedral reverb in organ versions
- Major keys read as courtly and bright; minor keys with falling bass lines (lament bass) read as grief

## References

- *The Four Seasons (Winter, Summer)* — Antonio Vivaldi, c.1723. The archetype of the driving string concerto with programmatic storm and frost effects. **(Corrected: Vivaldi, composed c.1716-17, published 1725 (Op. 8); c.1723 is not a standard date.)**
- *Brandenburg Concerto No. 3* — Johann Sebastian Bach, 1721. Pure motor rhythm; nine string parts in constant interlocking motion.
- *Music for the Royal Fireworks* — George Frideric Handel, 1749. State occasion music written to flatter a king; pomp as propaganda.
- *Dido's Lament (When I am laid in earth)* — Henry Purcell, 1689. The ground-bass lament, five bars repeating while the voice breaks over it.
- *Barry Lyndon (soundtrack using Handel's Sarabande)* — Stanley Kubrick (film), 1975. Shows baroque music as cold aristocratic fate under a story of social climbing.

## What it carries

Can carry brisk comic menace, clockwork bureaucracy, glittering wealth, and, in lament form, genuine grief. It fights against anything that needs to feel spontaneous, intimate or modern.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Satire of the rich, landlords, markets and committees: the elegant music of people who think the order is natural, ideal under a narrator listing how rents rose. A ground-bass lament suits a closing 'it didn't have to happen'. Undermines stories about raw anger, the street, or anything where the audience must feel the speaker is one of them; it can sound posh and distant.

## Asking Suno for it

Terms Suno responds to: 'baroque', 'harpsichord', 'string orchestra', 'basso continuo', 'terraced dynamics', 'chamber organ', 'concerto grosso', 'driving sixteenth notes', 'ground bass'. Say the tempo as a number. Put [Instrumental] in the lyrics box or leave lyrics empty for pure instrumentals. Exclude: 'drums, drum kit, electric guitar, synth pad, bass drop, pop, vocals, trailer, epic'. Describe a vocal instead of naming a singer: 'solo countertenor, ornamented, no vibrato' or 'harpsichord and baritone, recitative'.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno drifts to generic 'classical-ish film score' with sustained legato strings and a piano, losing the motor rhythm; fix by naming the continuo, the tempo and 'detached bowing, no sustained pads'. Harpsichord often comes out plinky and synthetic. Voices default to modern operatic or pop-soul phrasing with heavy vibrato; ask for 'straight tone' and keep the vocal part short. Pieces tend to loop a four-bar idea rather than develop, and Suno may add a modern kick drum at the drop.

## Production cost

Cheap for instrumental beds: the form tolerates looping and 8-second visual cuts. Hard parts: vocals in English that sound baroque rather than musical-theatre, and keeping real ground-bass structure across a 3-minute take. Needs several takes to avoid sample-library sound.
