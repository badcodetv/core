# Shoegaze and dream-gaze

> A wash of heavily effected guitars with buried, hushed vocals; beautiful, blurred and inward.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A UK scene of 1988-94 (My Bloody Valentine, Slowdive, Ride, Lush, Chapterhouse) named for players staring at their effects pedals. Layers of reverb, tremolo-arm bends and delay build a continuous cloud of sound with voice as one more texture. It was dismissed by the press as apolitical, and its mood is dreamy and introverted. It has returned in the 2010s and 2020s in bands like Slowdive's reunion and the 'blackgaze' crossover.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 80-130 bpm, mid or slow, with steady drums often a simple floor-tom pulse
- Multiple guitar layers with heavy reverb, chorus, delay and tremolo-arm pitch bends
- Vocal soft, breathy, low in the mix, with indistinct lyrics, often in harmony
- Drone-like chords with few changes; melody carried by guitar texture
- Drums either loud and tribal (Ride) or soft and distant (Slowdive)
- Dynamic swell and sudden loud 'white noise' sections
- Wide stereo field with a hazy, warm low-fi sheen

## References

- *Loveless* — My Bloody Valentine, 1991. The extreme: glide guitars, sound as texture.
- *Souvlaki* — Slowdive, 1993. The slower, melancholic, ambient pole.
- *Nowhere* — Ride, 1990. Louder, more rhythmic, with sharper melodies.
- *Spooky* — Lush, 1992. Female-led shoegaze with pop hooks.

## What it carries

Wistful, numbed, ecstatic or grieving; a good place for loss, retreat and the feeling of being overwhelmed. It fights against clarity, argument and anger, since lyrics are buried.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

A score or end-credits cue; a scene of consequence (the post-collapse emptiness, the vault, the future seen from a distance); lyrics you do not need the audience to parse. It would undermine anything where the punchline or the political claim must be heard, because the vocals are masked, and it is far from a pub-audience register.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno: shoegaze, 100 bpm, wall of reverb-drenched guitars, tremolo-arm pitch bends, hushed breathy vocals buried in the mix, thick distortion, dreamy, 1991 British, steady floor-tom drums. Ask for the vocal 'buried' or 'low in the mix' if lyrics should be indistinct; ask for 'clear vocal' if they must be heard (rare). Exclude: clean production, EDM, synthpop, loud lead vocal, rap, country, trap hats. Instrumental versions work well for scoring.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno often yields clean dream-pop with synths in place of true guitar walls, or an overly loud, clear vocal that ruins the blur. Adding 'guitar layers, fuzz, tremolo' and 'vocal buried' helps. The guitar noise can collapse into muddy mush at the mix level; shorter sections and fewer instruments help. The slow build and noise-burst dynamics rarely appear without section tags that name them.

## Production cost

Easy for instrumentals and beds, difficult for intelligible words. Visual pairing is soft focus and smeared light, which Veo handles well.
