# Britpop and kitchen-sink indie

> Big-chorus guitar pop with character-sketch lyrics about ordinary British lives, wry rather than furious.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

The 1993-97 UK guitar-pop wave: Suede, Blur, Pulp, Oasis, Elastica, Supergrass. It claimed British pop heritage (the Kinks, the Beatles, Bowie, the Jam) against grunge. Pulp and Blur in particular wrote observational character songs about class, estates, Essex and supermarket life, with a wink. The best of it matched a hook to a social observation, and the most successful bands were later absorbed into mainstream and Cool Britannia politics.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 100-140 bpm, mid-tempo with a lively backbeat
- Chiming and crunchy electric guitars, acoustic strum, sometimes strings or keyboards on choruses
- Melodic bass and a singalong chorus with a 'la-la' or 'whoah' hook
- Vocal sung in a British accent, theatrical, often with spoken asides, narrating a character
- Lyrics full of place names, jobs, brand names and kitchen-sink detail
- Verse-chorus-bridge arrangement with a big final chorus, 3:30 to 5:00
- Warm, slightly compressed 90s studio sound

## References

- *Common People* — Pulp, 1995. The central class-satire pop song: narrative, build and a payoff chorus.
- *Parklife* — Blur, 1994. Character sketches of British life with a spoken-word narrator (Phil Daniels).
- *Definitely Maybe* — Oasis, 1994. The anthemic, working-class, stadium side of the genre.
- *Suede (debut album)* — Suede, 1993. Council-estate romance and glam inflection, opening the movement.

## What it carries

Wry, warm, nostalgic, communal; catchy enough to be sung by a crowd, with the option to turn bitter at the last chorus. It fights against pure rage and against bleakness.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Stories built around a character and a punchline, with an accessible hook, where the audience should laugh and then feel the sting. A good fit for the reader brief: ordinary voices, specifics, and humour rather than sermon. It would undermine total-system critique with no human face and anything needing hard menace.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno defaults to Oasis-style generic anthem rock or modern 'indie' with clean, over-bright vocal. The lyrics tend to be vague and cliched (summer, dreams, rain); push specific nouns and a story. It rarely produces the Pulp-style slow build and storytelling arc unless the lyrics are structured by section with a stated build. Accent drifts to American in sung lines; repeat 'British accent' and exclude American.

## Production cost

Moderate. Full band plus strings is easy for Suno; the hard part is a sardonic vocal consistent across takes, and holding long narrative lyrics without the vocal going generic.
