# Motorik / krautrock

> A relentless, steady 4/4 drive with hypnotic repeating synth and guitar figures, built for forward motion rather than verses and choruses.
>

A German strand of the early 1970s (Neu!, Can, Harmonia, early Kraftwerk) defined by the 'motorik' beat: a constant, metronomic, almost mechanical drum pattern on a driving 4/4 with a hi-hat ride. Songs evolve by slowly adding or removing layers rather than changing chord, and vocals are minimal or absent. It sounds like travelling at a steady speed across a flat landscape.

## Signature traits

- Drum pattern: straight, unvarying kick on the beat, snare on 2 and 4, constant eighth-note hi-hat, tempo around 120-140 BPM
- One or two chords, or a single static harmonic drone, held for minutes
- Repeating arpeggiated synth or clean, echoey guitar figures that shimmer and slowly shift
- Bass locked into a simple pulsing root note pattern
- Long form (5-10 minutes), built by layering: start sparse, add and drop parts, no verse-chorus
- Vocals, if any, are few, flat, spoken or chanted, often in a language other than English
- Dry-ish tape sound with echo/delay and phasing on the guitars and synths

## References

- *Hallogallo* — Neu!, 1972. The defining motorik track: one beat, travelling guitar, no change of pace.
- *Autobahn* — Kraftwerk, 1974. Motorway driving turned into electronic music; shows the form working with synths and a nearly spoken vocal.
- *Vitamin C* — Can, 1972. A tighter, funkier, vocal-driven take on the locked groove.
- *Deluxe* — Harmonia, 1975. Where the motorik pulse meets warm analog synth textures.
- *French Disko* — Stereolab, 1993. A later, English-language revival showing the beat working with a catchy sung hook.

## What it carries

Can carry momentum, inevitability, hypnosis, a cold determined optimism, or mounting dread. It cannot do surprise, big emotional release or chatty comedy. It fights against anything that needs the words to be the centre.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

A narrator-led film with a long single idea: a journey, a process unfolding, a system quietly grinding on. Excellent as a bed under narration because the groove never interrupts the voice. It would undermine a punchline-driven or character-driven song, or any scene that needs a dramatic shift of mood.

## Asking Suno for it

Describe the beat literally: steady four-on-the-floor motorik drum pattern, constant eighth-note hi-hat, 130 BPM, hypnotic repeating analog synth arpeggio, pulsing bass on one note, no chord changes, long evolving layers. Use [Instrumental] and a minimal lyric block, or a lyric of two short repeated lines, for chanted vocals. Ask for 'tape echo' and 'phased guitar' for the period feel. Exclude: drop, build-up and drop, EDM, rock solo, vocal belting, chord changes, bridge, key change.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno tends to make the drums human and loose, adding fills, swing and syncopation, so the metronomic lock is lost, and it will drift into generic indie rock if guitars are mentioned. It also wants to build, drop and change section, which breaks the static trance. The fix is to name the single repeating pattern, exclude fills, drops and key changes, and keep lyrics to almost nothing. Without strong cues it may add a pop vocal hook.

## Production cost

Because the form is repeated and static, takes are easy to loop and extend, which suits narration beds. Long takes can wander in the second half; check the whole piece, not just the opening. Consistency across takes is good if the beat description is fixed.
