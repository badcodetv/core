# Glam rock stomp

> Foot-stomping, handclap-driven pop-rock with sequins, glitter and a crowd chant, working-class spectacle.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

The British glam era of 1971-75: T. Rex, Slade, The Sweet, Mud, early Bowie and Roxy Music. Two strands: the arty androgynous side and the stomping working-class side (Slade from Wolverhampton, The Sweet), which turned the pub singalong into a stadium stomp. Songs were short, hook-heavy singles with big drum beats, handclaps and misspelled titles, made for Top of the Pops. It treats flamboyance as a form of defiance.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 100-140 bpm with a heavy stomping beat, kick on every quarter or a thumping half-time
- Handclaps and foot-stomps layered on the drums, large room reverb
- Chunky riff-driven electric guitar with fuzz, often boogie or three-chord
- Chanted gang chorus and shouted vocal, sometimes a falsetto or sneering half-spoken verse
- Occasional saxophone, piano, or synthesiser squeals
- Short songs, 2:45 to 3:45, built on one repeated hook
- Bright, compressed, early-70s single sound

## References

- *Merry Xmas Everybody* — Slade, 1973. Working-class glam as a communal singalong, with a famous screamed hook.
- *Ballroom Blitz* — The Sweet, 1973. Theatrical stomp and chant with a spoken-shouted intro.
- *Electric Warrior* — T. Rex, 1971. The sparse, swaggering boogie origin of glam.
- *The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars* — David Bowie, 1972. The arty, narrative, apocalyptic pole (a doomed prophet from the future).

## What it carries

Joyful, showy, mischievous, communal; defiance through spectacle. It carries dark content under a party surface (the Ziggy story is an end-of-the-world narrative sung with swagger). It fights against sincerity, quiet and restraint.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Satire where the villain is a grotesque showman, a billionaire spectacle or a ruling class in costume, and for an alien-visitor-from-the-future framing, which glam has done before. Great for a gleeful, ironic anthem. It would undermine plain grief, a sober documentary argument or a respectful treatment of real victims.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno: 1973 British glam rock, 120 bpm, stomping drums with handclaps, chunky fuzz guitar riff, shouted gang chorus, bright saxophone, sneering male vocal with falsetto touches, big room reverb, vintage single sound. Name the handclaps and stomps explicitly. Exclude: modern pop, EDM, trap, autotune, clean polished production, rap, ballad, orchestral. Keep lyrics hook-first with a repeated chant line.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno slides toward generic 70s classic rock or toward modern retro-pop; the stomp rhythm and handclaps need to be named and may be quiet. It rarely makes the sneering, camp vocal; asking for 'theatrical', 'swaggering', and 'camp' can help. Glitter-era lyrics become cliched (rock and roll, tonight); give a specific narrative hook.

## Production cost

Moderate. Takes are easy to keep consistent as the arrangement is simple. The vocal needs a distinct, repeatable character. Visual pairing (costume, makeup, flat studio light) is rich but needs care to avoid cliche.
