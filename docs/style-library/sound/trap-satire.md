# Trap satire

> Dark, minimal 808-and-hi-hat beats with a cold, sing-rapped delivery, used to say something about money and power.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Trap came out of the American South in the 2000s and became the dominant sound of mainstream rap: slow, sub-heavy beats with rapid hi-hat rolls, minor-key synths and an autotuned, melodic delivery. In satire it is used knowingly, as in Childish Gambino's This Is America, where the glossy sound is a trap for the listener. Its connection to money, status and surveillance makes it surprisingly apt for a story about wealth.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 130-150 BPM felt as half-time (65-75), hard snare or clap on beat 3
- Long 808 sub-bass notes with glide
- Hi-hat pattern with triplet rolls and stutters
- Dark minor-key synth, bell or flute loop
- Vocal is melodic rap with autotune, ad-libs ('skrrt', 'yeah') and doubled hooks
- Heavy low-end, wide stereo, sidechained space
- Short verses of 8 or 16 bars with a long repeated hook
- Sudden mood shifts or a drop in the beat for contrast

## References

- *This Is America* — Childish Gambino, 2018. A trap beat that turns to a choir and back, using the sound itself to satirise
- *The Bigger Picture* — Lil Baby, 2020. A trap-sounding political statement on policing and protest
- *Bad and Boujee* — Migos, 2016. The sound of mainstream trap: triplet flow and a minimal beat
- *Mask Off* — Future, 2017. A flute-loop trap hit, dark and hypnotic

## What it carries

Cold, glossy, hypnotic and menacing; it can carry satire of wealth and status when the lyric cuts against the sheen. It fights against warmth and the slow build of an argument, and it carries a US stylistic accent.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Pieces about the rich, status, debt and surveillance, where the glamour of the sound is the joke. It suits a younger reader and a short punchy hook. It would undermine a plain-spoken working-class British story with a documentary tone, and long explanations.

## Asking Suno for it

Style box: 'dark trap, 140 BPM half-time, heavy 808 bass, rolling hi-hats, minor key bell melody, sing-rapped melodic delivery, ad-libs, wide stereo, sparse'. Use [Verse] and [Hook] tags and put ad-libs in brackets in lyrics. Exclude: 'boom bap, acoustic, live drums, choir, pop, EDM drop'. Trap is a genre Suno handles easily; the effort is in making the lyrics specific.

## How generators get it wrong

This is the style Suno does most readily, which is itself the problem: output sounds like stock trap, with a generic hook and filler ad-libs, and lyrics default to boasting. Satire is flattened into the genre's own clichés. Fix: write the lyric's irony yourself, keep the hook concrete and strange, restrict ad-libs, and use a British voice cue or a spoken break so it does not default to a US sound.

## Production cost

Cheap and fast to generate; hardest part is making it not sound stock and keeping the satirical intent audible. Vocal autotune hides small inconsistencies, so voice matching is easier than in other styles.
