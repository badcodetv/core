# Bassline (Sheffield and Yorkshire 4x4)

> Fast, bouncy, 130 to 140 BPM four-on-the-floor club music with a rubbery, wobbling 'bass' line, chopped garage vocals and a party mood, from northern England.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Bassline (also called 'niche' after a Sheffield club) emerged in the early 2000s in Sheffield, Leeds and the Midlands as a rougher, faster, northern descendant of speed garage. It uses a four-on-the-floor kick with bouncing, sub-heavy bass lines and sampled female R&B vocals chopped into hooks. It is deeply a regional, working-class, pre-night-out sound, associated with Yorkshire clubs and with the pop hits that grew out of it, which makes it the most northern and least London-centric entry in this family.

## Signature traits

- Tempo about 130 to 140 BPM, four-on-the-floor kick with a bouncy offbeat bass
- Thick, wobbly, rubbery 'bass' stabs that sound like a plucked or filtered sub
- Chopped and pitched R&B/pop vocal samples as hooks, also MC 'party' chants
- Bright major or modal chords, simple repeated riff, minimal melody
- Snares and claps on beat 2 and 4, with shuffled hi-hats
- Short, loud, club-friendly structure with a clear drop
- Playful, cheeky, flirtatious lyric themes (nights out, money, relationships)

## References

- *Heartbroken* — T2 (feat. Jodie Aysha), 2007. A UK chart hit in the bassline mould that made the sound national.
- *Bassline Junkie* — Dizzee Rascal, 2007. Grime-era nod to the sound and its fixation on low-end bounce (loose genre fit).
- *Wearing My Rolex* — Wiley, 2008. Wiley's crossover into a bouncier, chart-oriented groove adjacent to the bassline sound (loose genre fit). **(Unconfirmed on the web.)**

## What it carries

Cheeky, bouncy, hedonistic, local pride. It carries release, a night out and gallows humour about money. It fights against seriousness and anything that needs gravity.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Short comic pieces: the end-of-week party where the joke is that everyone is dancing while the roof is falling in; a northern town story; a bouncy earworm hook that carries a bitter line. It undermines solemn or sinister stories, and as a genre with few widely agreed canonical records it is easy to mis-specify, so a story should confirm its taste before adopting it.
