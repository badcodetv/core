# Sea shanty and work-song chorus

> Call-and-response labour song for a crowd, led by a soloist with a gang chorus stamping the beat.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Shanties were sung on merchant sailing ships to coordinate hauling and pumping: a shantyman sings the verse and the crew answer with a fixed chorus on the pull. The form passed through the folk revival (Stan Hugill, A.L. Lloyd, Ewan MacColl) and returned in the 2020s via Cornish groups and the online shanty craze. Rhythm is strongly metred (the pull falls on the stressed beat), harmony is minimal, and the lyrics are improvisable, which is why verses are easy to swap for new complaints.

## Signature traits

- Soloist verse then gang chorus in unison or rough open harmony
- Strong stamped or clapped beat, often 6/8 or 2/4 with a pull on the downbeat
- Short repeating chorus refrain (a line or two) the audience can learn in one pass
- Mostly unaccompanied, sometimes with concertina, fiddle or a single drum
- Hearty, roughly blended male voices, some shouting, no polish
- Verses that can be extended with topical complaints against the captain, owner or pay
- Open-air or pub acoustics, with a group sound and audible stomping

- *Blow Boys Blow* — A.L. Lloyd and Ewan MacColl, 1957. Revival album of traditional sea songs and shanties
- *Port Isaac's Fisherman's Friends* — Fisherman's Friends, 2010. Real working men's shanty group, unaccompanied harmony **(Unconfirmed on the web.)**
- *Soon May the Wellerman Come* — Nathan Evans, 2020. ## What it carries

Communal, defiant, cheerful and a little menacing. It can make a grievance feel like a thing a crowd is already chanting, and a class point (the captain's cargo, our backs) is built into the genre. It fights against intimacy, subtlety and any lyric longer than a breath.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Satire about owners and workers, pay, zero-hours, gig labour and debt, where a chorus the audience can shout back is the point; the stolen-from-the-crew idea is native to the form. It would undermine quiet, personal or ambiguous pieces and any argument that needs complex sentences, since the chorus flattens it.

## How generators get it wrong

The engine produces a polished choir or a pop-cheesy 'pirate' with accordion and sound effects, and treats the chorus as a hook with studio harmonies, losing the rough unison. It often gives 4/4 pop swing instead of the 6/8 pull. Vocals are a single doubled voice not a crowd. Fix: ask for 'ragged unison, a dozen men, not harmonised', 'foot stomps', 'no backing track', short chorus of 4 to 6 syllables; use [Gang] tags and consider exclude 'pirate, cinematic, orchestral'.

## Production cost

Cheap and forgiving; the crowd texture hides AI artifacts. The cost is that a convincing multi-voice chorus is the hardest part, and consecutive takes may change the apparent singers. A shanty ends up short (2 minutes) which suits cuts, but lyrics need to be rewritten to fit the pull rhythm.
