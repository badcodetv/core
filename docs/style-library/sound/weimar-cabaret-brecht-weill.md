# Weimar cabaret and Brecht-Weill

> A sneering, half-spoken song with a wheezy small band, built to make an audience laugh and then feel implicated.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Born in Berlin in the 1920s and early 30s, when hyperinflation and mass unemployment made satire a survival tool. Kurt Weill's music mixed jazz, tango, fairground and march with deliberately sour harmony, and Brecht's lyrics told ugly truths about money and hunger in a cheerful voice. The singing is acted rather than beautiful, often Sprechgesang. The 1966 Kander and Ebb Cabaret revived the sound for Broadway and film.

## Signature traits

- Small pit band: upright or slightly out-of-tune piano, muted trumpet, clarinet or saxophone, banjo, accordion, tuba or bass, brushed or clattering snare
- Tempo about 90 to 130 bpm, in a march, foxtrot or tango feel, with oom-pah bass on the beat
- Minor-key melodies that lurch into bright major cadences, with deliberate wrong-note chromatic bends
- Half-spoken, nasal, sneering vocal delivery with consonants spat, little vibrato
- Verse-refrain structure, refrain returning with a harder meaning each time
- Dry, close, room-sized recording with no big reverb and a slightly brittle top end
- Final chorus often sped up or shouted

## References

- *The Threepenny Opera (Mack the Knife, Pirate Jenny, 'What Keeps Mankind Alive?')* — Kurt Weill and Bertolt Brecht, 1928. The template: a cheerful tune delivering a hard class argument.
- *Rise and Fall of the City of Mahagonny* — Kurt Weill and Bertolt Brecht, 1930. A whole opera about a city where the only crime is having no money.
- *Cabaret (stage musical and 1972 film)* — John Kander and Fred Ebb; film directed by Bob Fosse, 1966 / 1972. 'Money Makes the World Go Round' and 'If You Could See Her' show the sound used for modern satire.
- *Happy End (Surabaya-Johnny, 'The Bilbao Song')* — Kurt Weill and Bertolt Brecht (with Elisabeth Hauptmann), 1929. Shows the gentler, bruised side of the same palette.

## What it carries

Wry, knowing, bitterly funny; it can carry real anger and tenderness underneath the smirk. It fights against sincerity played straight and against any big, slick, warm sound.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Stories about money, landlords, bankers and bosses told as a cynical street song; a narrator who sells the audience the horror with a smile. Strong for a villain's number or a bank-robbery caper. It would undermine a story that needs plain earnest uplift or a quiet emotional ending.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno responds well to concrete instrumentation and era words: 'Weimar cabaret, 1920s Berlin, out-of-tune upright piano, muted trumpet, clarinet, banjo, tuba, brushed snare, sprechgesang, sneering theatrical baritone, minor-key march, dry room recording'. Put the voice and instruments early in the Style box. Do not name Weill, Brecht, Bowie or any living singer. Exclude: 'pop, EDM, autotune, choir, big reverb, polished modern production, trap hi-hats, lush strings'. Use short lyric lines and a [Spoken] tag for sprechgesang; Suno tends to sing everything unless told to speak.

## How generators get it wrong

Generators drift to glossy Broadway Cabaret or a jazz-lounge vibe with a clean, warm, trained voice, which loses the sourness. Fix with 'sneering, nasal, half-spoken, thin, unpolished' and an exclude of 'smooth, crooner, musical-theatre belt'. The wrong-note harmony gets smoothed into pleasantness; ask for 'sour chromatic' and 'accordion'. Accents slide to a generic German caricature; keep the accent out of the prompt unless wanted and write plain English lyrics. Long lyrics collapse the spoken delivery into singing.

## Production cost

Cheap to keep consistent across a story because the small-band palette is easy to name. The vocal persona drifts take to take, so lock one voice description and reuse it. Spoken-sung hybrid is the hardest part and often needs several takes. Tracks of 2 to 3 minutes work well.
