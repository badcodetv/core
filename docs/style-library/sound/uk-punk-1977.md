# UK punk, 1976-78

> Fast, loud, three-chord guitar songs shouted by people who cannot or will not sing, aimed at named people in charge.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Born in London and Manchester in 1976-77 out of pub rock, the Ramones and disgust at youth unemployment and the Silver Jubilee. The Sex Pistols, The Clash, The Damned and Buzzcocks made short abrasive songs that dropped virtuosity. The sound is overdriven guitar over a basic rhythm section, with words that name the enemy directly (EMI, the Queen, the dole). The DIY ethos of self-released singles and photocopied sleeves is part of the argument.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 160-200 bpm, songs 1:45 to 2:45
- Down-stroked power chords on doubled overdriven guitar, thick mids, almost no reverb
- Straight eighth-note drums, hard snare backbeat, tom roll into chorus
- Pick-played bass locked to root notes, loud in the mix
- Shouted or sneered vocal in a regional British accent, rough pitch, gang-shouted backing
- Verse-chorus with a chanted hook and an abrupt stop ending
- Dry, close, slightly cheap recording

## References

- *Never Mind the Bollocks, Here's the Sex Pistols* — Sex Pistols, 1977. The template: wall of guitar, named targets, sneering delivery.
- *The Clash (debut album)* — The Clash, 1977. Punk with politics and class specifics (Career Opportunities, White Riot).
- *New Rose* — The Damned, 1976. First UK punk single; speed and fun.
- *Spiral Scratch EP* — Buzzcocks, 1977. Self-released DIY; shows the thin, nervous Manchester variant.

## What it carries

Fury, impatience, comic contempt, and a clear us-versus-them. It can carry righteous anger and gallows humour well. It fights against nuance, tenderness and slow build.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Satires that name a villain and a specific injustice (rents, wages, a landlord, a minister) in under three minutes, and sing-along rage a pub would shout. It would undermine a story that needs patient explanation, sympathy for the person being criticised, or a nurturing tone, because the form reads as shouting at the listener.

## Asking Suno for it

Use short lines and a chorus of one repeated phrase in lyrics. Living band names are blocked, so describe the sound.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno tends to produce 2000s American pop-punk (clean vocal, tight double-tracked polish, skate-park cadence) even when asked for 1977. The fix is to specify year, UK accent, raw and lo-fi, and exclude pop punk and emo. Vocals come out too in tune and too well enunciated; ask for shouted, ragged, off-pitch. Songs run longer than 2 minutes with an extra bridge; keep lyrics to two verses and a chorus and tag [End] to stop.

## Production cost

Cheap to make as audio: short songs, one band sound. Vocal character drifts take to take, so lock one voice description and reuse it. For visuals, the look (photocopy collage, flyers) is an easy match.
