# Spoken word over a sparse bed

> A voice speaking in near-prose over a quiet, slow, ambient or jazz-inflected bed that leaves almost every word clear.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

The oldest line here: Gil Scott-Heron's spoken pieces from the early 70s, the Last Poets, and later narrators like Kae Tempest, who place a performed text over minimal music. The music is a bed (a bass, a keyboard, soft drums, a drone) designed to be listened through. It is the nearest thing to narration with a score, and the form that best carries a long, argued text.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 60 to 90 BPM or free time, often only a pulse
- Voice is close-miked, intimate, mid-register, with natural pauses and breath
- A few instruments only: upright or electric bass, Rhodes or piano, brushed drums, a pad
- Music ducks under the voice and swells in the gaps between stanzas
- Delivery varies from quiet to a rising, clipped, emphatic run, with slow starts
- Stanzas of different lengths with a refrain returning
- Room-sized reverb, few effects
- Bed loops with small changes, so the ear stays on the text

## References

- *The Revolution Will Not Be Televised* — Gil Scott-Heron, 1970. A satirical list-poem over congas, the model of a witty political spoken word
- *Europe Is Lost* — Kae Tempest, 2016. ## What it carries

Intimate, serious, reflective and capable of dark wit; it carries argument and grief. It fights against spectacle and momentum, and it can feel like a lecture if the writing is not alive.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Narration, closing statements, and long argued stretches where the words must land. Suits a story that wants to be listened to, and where an authoritative narrator can play with irony. It would undermine anything that depends on a hook or a beat to hold attention.

## Asking Suno for it

Style box: 'spoken word, narrated poem, close intimate voice, slow 70 BPM, upright bass, soft brushed drums, warm Rhodes, ambient pad, sparse arrangement, music under voice'. Use [Spoken Word] tags, and lyrics as prose broken into short lines. Exclude: 'singing, rap, chorus, drums fill, EDM, epic cinematic, choir'. Keep the Style box short; heavy cues can push the model toward a song.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno wants to turn a text into a song: it adds a melody, a chorus and a climax, or sings the lines. It can also make the music too loud under the voice. Fix: repeat 'spoken, not sung' in Style and Exclude, minimise instruments, and keep lyrics as prose with no repeated lines that look like a chorus. For narration, generating the voice alone and the bed separately is more controllable.

## Production cost

Low for the bed, medium for a voice that stays consistent over a long text. Long takes tend to drift, so splitting by stanza and matching voices is a real task.
