# Post-punk

> Angular, bass-led, cold and analytical rock that treats the mess of capitalism as a puzzle to be pulled apart.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A 1978-82 British movement of bands who took punk's freedom and discarded its three chords: Gang of Four, Joy Division, Wire, The Fall, The Pop Group, Public Image Ltd. They absorbed dub, funk, disco and Krautrock. Lyrics were often second-person, analytic or fragmentary and engaged with consumerism, state power and alienation. A late-2010s UK revival (Idles, Fontaines D.C., Shame) brought it back with a working-class edge.

## Signature traits

- Tempo 110-150 bpm, tight and repetitive, often a motorik or dub-influenced groove
- Dominant melodic bass line carrying the song, sparse thin treble guitar (scratchy or sheet-metal tone)
- Dry drums with tight, flat snare, and little cymbal wash
- Flat, spoken or semi-spoken vocal, often deadpan, sometimes a drone baritone
- Gaps and silence used as rhythm; stop-start structures
- Lyrics: consumer goods, commodity, the office, state control, delivered coolly
- Dry, cold recording, little reverb, or deep dub echo on select elements

## References

- *Entertainment!* — Gang of Four, 1979. The political post-punk blueprint: funk bass, scratchy guitar, commodity analysis (Natural's Not in It).
- *Unknown Pleasures* — Joy Division, 1979. The cold, spacious, despairing pole.
- *Grotesque (After the Gramme)* — The Fall, 1980. Repetitive groove with a ranting Northern voice about work and class.
- *Pink Flag* — Wire, 1977. Terse, short songs; punk reduced to its essentials.
- *Brutalism* — Idles, 2017. A modern revival aimed squarely at austerity Britain.

## What it carries

Dry, alert, ironic, sometimes menacing or bleak. It makes a good home for deadpan satire and for the sense that a system is a machine. It fights against warmth, singalong and catharsis.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Stories about systems, bureaucracy, finance, automation, landlords as abstractions: the cold-narrator voice of a superintelligence fits naturally. It would undermine a story that needs an emotional singalong, or gives the audience a rousing 'we will win' feeling; it can read as arty and distant to a pub audience.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno: post-punk, 130 bpm, prominent melodic bass line, thin scratchy treble guitar, dry tight drums, deadpan spoken baritone vocal, sparse, repetitive groove, angular, British, 1979 recording, little reverb. For narration, a spoken-word [Verse] over a repeated bass groove works well. Exclude: pop, shoegaze, wall of sound, reverb wash, choir, EDM, belting, emo, power ballad, stadium. Mention 'funk bass' or 'dub echo' for the Gang of Four or PiL variants.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno's default for dark indie is a swampy, reverb-heavy 'gothic' or modern synth-wave sound; the fix is dry, thin guitar and an exclude for synthwave and reverb. Vocals tend to melodic singing, so ask for spoken, monotone, flat and deadpan. The bass is often buried under guitar; say bass-led and bass forward. Songs lose repetition and add generic builds; ask for a repeating groove and no drop.

## Production cost

Moderate. Strong spoken voice consistency is achievable and the sparse arrangement gives clean takes, which helps a narrator. Visual match is monochrome, geometric and austere.
