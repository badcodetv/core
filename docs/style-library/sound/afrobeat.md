# Afrobeat

> Long, polyrhythmic Nigerian groove music with call-and-response, big horns and furious political lyrics, with a London chapter.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Afrobeat was created by Fela Kuti and drummer Tony Allen, mixing Nigerian highlife and Yoruba rhythm with James Brown funk and jazz. It was formed partly in London: Fela's London Scene (1971) was recorded there, and Kuti and Allen's later bands and the London scene that grew around them remain an influence on British music. Its songs are 10 minutes or more, with an instrumental groove that builds for a long time before the singing arrives. Lyrics are direct attacks on military rule, corruption and corporate power in Pidgin English. It is a political music as much as a dance one, which makes it an exact match for the BadCode brief.

## Signature traits

- Tempo about 100-125 BPM, interlocking rhythm of drum kit, congas, shekere and claves in a cyclical groove
- Tony-Allen-style drumming: loose, polyrhythmic, hi-hat and kick locked into a deep pocket
- Two interlocking rhythm guitars playing clipped 16th-note patterns, plus an electric piano or organ
- Tight horn section (several saxes, trumpet) playing repeated unison riffs and solos
- Female backing singers answering the lead in call-and-response
- Songs start with 5-10 minutes of instrumental groove before vocals
- Pidgin-English lyrics, direct and narrative, naming institutions and officials
- Long form: a track as a continuous build, with a single vamp and slowly changing parts

## References

- *Zombie* — Fela Kuti and Africa 70, 1976. A satire of soldiers as obedient automatons; a model of political satire in the form.
- *Expensive Shit* — Fela Kuti and Africa 70, 1975. A comic, scatological protest song about a police stool-sample raid.
- *Fela's London Scene* — Fela Ransome-Kuti and the Africa 70, 1971. Recorded in London; the UK root of the style.
- *Jealousy* — Tony Allen and Afrika 70, 1975. The drummer's own record, showing the rhythmic engine of the style.
- *Water No Get Enemy* — Fela Kuti and Africa 70, 1975. A wise, layered, call-and-response example with a folk-proverb lyric.

## What it carries

Relentless, hypnotic, righteous, witty, and physically joyful. It can carry fury, contempt and humour together, and rewards patience. It fights against short formats, fast cuts, and delivering a point in under a minute.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Long-form mockery of institutions: a political class, a bank, a company. The long instrumental build suits a montage, and the horn riff can be a motif that returns. It would undermine very short pieces and anything where the performance would be treated as exotic local colour.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno responds to 'Afrobeat', 'Fela-style' only in descriptive form, so say 'Nigerian Afrobeat, 1970s, polyrhythmic drums, interlocking guitars, unison horn section, female call-and-response chorus, long groove'. Add 'Pidgin English' only if the lyrics are written by someone who can check them. Use [Instrumental intro], [Horn riff], [Verse], [Chorus - call and response]. Exclude: 'Afropop, amapiano, Afrobeats, dancehall, EDM, trap, Auto-Tune, pop'. Ask for 'live band, analog, loose'.

## How generators get it wrong

The model frequently confuses Afrobeat with 'Afrobeats' (modern Nigerian pop) and delivers a polished, programmed, hi-hat-trap groove with Auto-Tuned singing. Real Afrobeat's loose, cyclic live-band feel and long form are hard to get: tracks come out as 3-4 minute pop songs with a verse-chorus shape. Horn sections are often too thin, and the guitar interlock is lost. Fix by asking for 'long instrumental introduction' and 'live 1970s band', and by excluding 'Afrobeats, amapiano, Auto-Tune'.

## Production cost

Length and band density are the cost. Suno clips are shorter than the form wants, so a pass of extension and stitching is needed for any real groove, and keeping the same band feel across extensions is hard. As a short sting or horn riff for a scene, it is cheaper and often sufficient.
