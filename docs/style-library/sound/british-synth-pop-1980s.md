# Early-80s British synth-pop

> Melodic, hooky pop built on polysynths and drum machines, with a deadpan or theatrical vocal and lyrics about money, class and work.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Born in Sheffield, Basildon and Leeds around 1979-84 when cheap synthesisers let working-class bands make pop without a guitar or a studio budget. It is the most lyric-forward style in this family: the verses are story, the chorus is a hook. Its best-known records are quietly political (the dole, Thatcherite greed, the City), which makes it a close fit for BadCode's brief.

## Signature traits

- Tempo around 100-125 BPM, steady four-on-the-floor or LinnDrum-style drum machine with gated-reverb snare and handclaps
- Monophonic sequenced bassline in 8th or 16th notes (octave-jumping), often the main riff
- Bright, slightly thin analog polysynth pads and a plinky lead or string-machine line playing the hook
- Vocal is flat, dry, close-mic'd and baritone or half-spoken; a second, higher voice answers in the chorus
- Verse-chorus-middle-eight form, 3 to 4 minutes, with a big instrumental synth break
- Sparse arrangement: few layers, every part audible, a lot of space between notes
- Lyrics concrete and narrative: jobs, rent, shares, dole queues, with a sardonic twist

## References

- *Everything Counts* — Depeche Mode, 1983. A hooky synth-pop song whose lyric is about greed and the deal-making world; the model for sweet tune and bitter point.
- *Opportunities (Let's Make Lots of Money)* — Pet Shop Boys, 1985. Deadpan Thatcher-era satire of get-rich ambition delivered over a bright electro track.
- *Penthouse and Pavement* — Heaven 17, 1981. Sheffield synth-funk-pop about class and money; politically pointed from a band that came out of the Human League.
- *Dare* — The Human League, 1981. The template album for the form: polysynth hooks, drum machine, plain-spoken Sheffield vocals.
- *Tainted Love* — Soft Cell, 1981. Shows how little is needed: one bassline, one drum machine and a theatrical vocal.

## What it carries

Can carry wry, rueful, cynical, euphoric-but-bitter and bittersweet nostalgia. Hard to make it truly menacing or raw; it fights against sincerity delivered without irony and against anything epic.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Stories where the point is a joke with a sting: salaries, landlords, shareholders, a pay-off line in the chorus. Strong for a sung lyric the audience can repeat. It would undermine a slow, solemn or epic scene, or one needing a landscape rather than a person talking.

## Asking Suno for it

Suno responds to concrete instrument and production words more reliably than to a decade label alone. Describe the parts: tempo (for example 118 BPM), analog polysynth, sequenced octave bass, drum machine with gated reverb snare, handclaps, deadpan baritone vocal, dry close vocal, sparse arrangement. Naming 'synth-pop' and a year range helps, but the engine drifts toward modern glossy pop unless the sound is pinned with period gear words. Put lyric delivery notes in square brackets in the lyrics (for example [Verse - spoken-sung, deadpan]). Living artists' names are blocked or ignored, so describe the voice instead. Exclude box: modern pop, EDM drop, supersaw, autotune, vocal chops, trap hi-hats, guitar, big stadium chorus.

## How generators get it wrong

Suno tends to polish this into contemporary radio pop with fat compressed supersaw pads, a sung-with-feeling female or male belt and a euphoric chorus, which erases the deadpan. The fix is to ask for dry, flat, low vocal and thin analog sounds, and to exclude 'modern pop' and 'powerful vocals'. Lyrics written as generic love songs come out sounding like every AI song; the concrete nouns (rent, shares, bailiff) are what make it sound like the genre. Chorus can arrive too early and repeat too much; shape it with explicit [Verse]/[Chorus]/[Bridge] tags and fewer chorus lines.

## Production cost

Cheap and consistent: the engine is good at this palette and a fixed style box gives takes that sit together. The hard part is the vocal: voice timbre drifts between generations, so keeping one narrator or singer identity across a story needs a fixed vocal description or a saved voice. Duration is easy to hold to about 3 minutes.
