# Miniature model world and tilt-shift

> The real world made to look like a tabletop model through narrow focus, bright colour and tiny people.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Two linked ideas: physically built miniature sets, as in the model work of 1960s British Supermarionation, and the tilt-shift look, where a narrow band of focus and high saturation make real places look like toy models. Both turn scale into the message: a city, a bank or a town reduced to something a hand could rearrange. The viewer sees the powerful as holders of the board. Slightly sped-up, jerky motion sells the toy feel.

## Signature traits

- Very shallow depth of field with a sharp band and heavy blur above and below
- High saturation and contrast, as if a painted model
- Elevated three-quarter viewpoint, 30-60 degrees above the scene
- Tiny figures as dots or toy-like people; vehicles move like model cars
- Model-making details: static grass, painted brick, cotton-wool smoke
- Slightly sped-up time-lapse or 12 fps jerk
- Bright, even daylight with small hard shadows

## References

- *Thunderbirds* — Gerry Anderson (AP Films / ITC), 1965-1966. British Supermarionation: puppets and detailed miniature sets filmed as if full-scale.
- *Sandpit* — Sam O'Hare, 2011. Time-lapse tilt-shift film that turned New York into a toy model. **(Corrected: The film is 'The Sandpit' by Sam O'Hare, made 2009-2010 (SIGGRAPH 2010, Ars Electronica 2010 award), not 2011. Tilt-shift was added in post.)**
- *Isle of Dogs* — Wes Anderson, 2018. Detailed handmade miniature sets and stop-motion; a tabletop world used as political allegory.
- *Bekonscot Model Village* — Roland Callingham (Beaconsfield, England), 1929-present. A real English model village: a ready-made image of toy Britain.

## What it carries

Detached, godlike, dryly funny; the viewer is above the board. It carries melancholy and menace through scale. It fights intimacy and close emotion.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Explaining systems from above: housing markets, supply chains, the city as a board game. Matches the 'superintelligence looking down from the future' framing. Undermines intimate human scenes and anything needing eye contact.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Nano Banana reliably produces tilt-shift if asked: 'tilt-shift miniature photograph, extremely shallow depth of field with a narrow sharp band, high saturation, elevated three-quarter view, toy-sized people and cars'. For model sets: 'handbuilt model village, static grass, painted plaster, cotton-wool smoke'. Veo: 'slightly sped-up miniature motion, 12 fps feel, camera slowly cranes over the model'. Negative: 'wide-angle realism, deep focus, cinematic haze, large people close up'.

## How generators get it wrong

The model blurs the wrong region or applies uniform blur, giving fake bokeh instead of a focus band; state which stripe is sharp. Veo smooths movement and adds realistic crowds, losing the toy motion; ask for sparse, stiff movement. Scale breaks down in close shots, since the engine cannot keep miniature skin and clothing texture. Colour drifts to a teal-orange grade.

## Production cost

Easy for establishing and wide shots, and consistent across clips because the world is a fixed set. Hard for dialogue or close acting. Eight seconds is plenty since motion is slow.
