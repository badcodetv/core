# Comic-book 3D (halftone, offset print, ink lines)

> 3D-rendered scenes made to look printed from a comic: visible halftone dots, misaligned colour plates, hand-inked outlines and on-screen panels and captions.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Into the Spider-Verse (2018) changed the economics of 3D animation by running 3D scenes through an in-house layer of hand-drawn linework, halftone shading and deliberately off-register colour, animated on twos with sudden shifts to ones. Later films (The Bad Guys, The Mitchells vs. the Machines) extended it with drawn ink lines and painted textures. The core idea is that the screen should look like printed paper and a comic's graphic grammar (panels, speed lines, captions, onomatopoeia) is part of the film's language, not a decoration.

## Signature traits

- Visible halftone dot patterns in shadows and gradients, in an offset-print style
- Misregistered colour: a thin offset between the colour fill and the black line, or a chromatic 'print slip' on edges
- Hand-inked contour lines of varying width, with crosshatched hatching in dark areas instead of smooth shading
- Saturated, high-contrast palette with a different colour key for each emotional state (magenta and cyan, acid yellow and violet)
- Comic furniture in frame: caption boxes, thought bubbles, onomatopoeia text, panel borders and split panels
- Animation on twos with abrupt held frames and snappy poses
- Strong perspective and dutch angles, dramatic foreshortening
- Depth of field replaced by blurred halftone patterns or print offset

## References

- *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* — Sony Pictures Animation (Persichetti, Ramsey, Rothman), 2018. The reference point for halftone, offset colour and comic furniture inside 3D animation.
- *Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse* — Sony Pictures Animation, 2023. Pushes style as story: each world has its own print process and medium.
- *The Mitchells vs. the Machines* — Sony Pictures Animation (Mike Rianda), 2021. A technology satire with hand-drawn doodle overlays on 3D, showing the comic layer used for jokes and character.
- *The Bad Guys* — DreamWorks Animation (Pierre Perifel), 2022. 3D with ink-line and painterly treatment; a lighter, less print-heavy version of the idea.

## What it carries

Energetic, loud, young and pop. It carries comedy, outrage and punchy action well, and the comic framing gives a natural place for sarcastic captions. It fights against quiet, restrained or documentary registers, and it can feel like a studio imitation if it is not used for a reason.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Strong for: an audience raised on superhero and comic imagery; fast-cut montage of villains and systems, where captions and onomatopoeia are the narrator's sarcasm; and 'the news as a comic strip'. It would undermine: quiet realist scenes of a person's actual life or any piece where dignified sobriety carries the argument.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Nano Banana responds well to print-process vocabulary: 'comic book illustration look, visible halftone dot shading, offset print misregistration with colour fill shifted from black ink outline, bold varying-width ink contour lines, crosshatch shadows, saturated magenta cyan and yellow palette, caption box in the corner, bold onomatopoeia lettering'. Put the dots in the shadows, not everywhere. For Veo: 'animated on twos, snappy poses, held frames, graphic motion lines' and lock the camera; ask for 'whip pan' or 'zoom punch' as the only camera move. Avoid: 'smooth CGI, photorealistic skin, soft glow, lens flare, Pixar film still'. Text in speech bubbles and captions is rendered legibly by the image model more often than by the video model, so put lettering in the still and keep it static or add it in post. Suno side: describe 'big hip-hop drums, brass stabs, vinyl scratch, orchestral hits' and avoid named artists.

## How generators get it wrong

The default failure is a generic 'comic filter' look: uniform pastel halftone applied evenly over the whole frame like a Photoshop effect, with no ink-line hierarchy and no real offset. Fix by asking for the dots only in shadows and midtones and the black line separately shifted from the colour. Veo tends to smooth halftone dots into noisy shimmer or drop them entirely after a few frames, and the dot pattern 'swims' across moving surfaces instead of sticking to the object. Short clips and still-camera shots hold better. Lettering in bubbles often garbles in video. A second trap: it reads as a copy of one famous film unless a distinct palette and framing are chosen for the story.

## Production cost

Medium to high. Stills are easy; motion is the hard part because halftone and line texture swim and flicker. Character consistency is decent with a reference sheet. Plan short, mostly static shots with strong poses, and consider adding the halftone and offset as a post layer so it stays locked to the frame.
