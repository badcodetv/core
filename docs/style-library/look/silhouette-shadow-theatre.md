# Silhouette and shadow theatre

> Black cut-out figures against a lit translucent screen, with all meaning carried by outline and movement.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Shadow theatre runs from Javanese wayang kulit to the paper-silhouette animation of Lotte Reiniger, who cut jointed black figures and filmed them on a backlit glass table. Every character is pure outline, so gesture and profile carry everything. It lets grim material look elegant, and crowds and scale speak as symbols. In modern art it has been used for pointed historical critique through room-sized cut-paper silhouettes.

## Signature traits

- Solid black figures in strict profile, no interior detail except cut holes (eyes, lace, buttons)
- Backlit translucent screen with a flat gradient of amber, dusk orange or pale teal
- Layered paper planes with slight parallax; foreground trees and bars as black lacework
- Jointed puppet movement: limbs pivot at hinges, stiff stepped 12fps feel
- Horizontal stage-like compositions, characters against sky or lamp
- No facial expression; emotion from posture, scale and head angle
- Crowds as repeated identical shapes

## References

- *The Adventures of Prince Achmed* — Lotte Reiniger, 1926. Oldest surviving feature-length animation; the canonical cut-paper silhouette film.
- *Wayang kulit* — Javanese and Balinese shadow puppet tradition, centuries old. Living theatre form with moral and political storytelling behind a lit screen.
- *Gone, An Historical Romance of a Civil War as It Occurred b'tween the Dusky Thighs of One Young Negress and Her Heart* — Kara Walker, 1994. Room-scale cut-paper silhouette installation showing the form used for unflinching historical critique.
- *Princes and Princesses* — Michel Ocelot, 2000. Modern silhouette animation with coloured backgrounds, readable and polished on screen.

## What it carries

Fable-like, formal, quietly ominous; can be elegiac, cruel or funny with timing. It fights faces, specific detail and realism: no one is identifiable by expression.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Parables about systems and crowds: the factory gate, the queue, the tower looking down on the street. Fits the 'received wisdom from a future' framing well. Undermines dialogue-heavy comedy, named characters whose faces matter, and anything depending on a real-looking location.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Describe the construction: 'cut-paper silhouette animation, black jointed paper puppets in profile, backlit translucent screen, flat amber gradient sky, layered paper cut-outs with slight drop shadow'. Nano Banana is good at silhouettes when told 'pure flat black, no interior shading, no gradients inside figures'. For Veo: 'stiff jointed paper-puppet movement, limbs pivot at joints, parallax between paper layers, camera locked'. Negative: 'gradients on figures, 3D shading, facial detail, realistic lighting, painterly'.

## How generators get it wrong

Models add rim light, inner gradients and faces, turning silhouettes into dark 3D renders; repeat 'flat black, no interior detail'. Veo morphs shapes smoothly instead of pivoting at joints, giving fluid blob animation; ask for stiff hinge movement and short moves. Backgrounds drift toward detailed painterly landscapes; insist on flat colour gradient. Long camera moves break the paper illusion; keep the camera locked.

## Production cost

Consistency is easy because shapes are simple. The hard part is keeping flat black from drifting to lit 3D across many clips, and jointed motion that does not melt. Cheap for crowds and landscapes, poor for acting.
