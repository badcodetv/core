# Cardboard and bin-bag DIY

> Scenes remade by hand from cardboard, foil, tape and household junk, with the effort showing.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A deliberately home-made look in which every prop and set is cardboard, duct tape, tin foil and household clutter, filmed on cheap cameras. Michel Gondry made it a signature in music videos and in Be Kind Rewind, where amateurs remake blockbusters with junk props. It turns spectacle into a joke about cost and effort, and it is generous: anybody could build it. For satire the cheapness mocks the grandeur of institutions and speaks to class directly.

## Signature traits

- Corrugated cardboard with printed brand marks still visible, cut with scissors
- Duct tape, bin bags, foil and bubble wrap as costumes and effects
- Visible hands holding wobbly props
- Harsh on-camera or fluorescent room light, flat and unflattering
- Handheld camera, slightly shaky, in a living room, garage or yard
- Wonky hand-drawn marker lettering on boxes
- Wide shots showing the whole improvised set and its mess

## References

- *Be Kind Rewind* — Michel Gondry, 2008. Feature film about remaking movies with junk props; the clearest statement of the style.
- *Fell in Love with a Girl (music video)* — Michel Gondry for The White Stripes, 2002. Handmade, toy-brick look: DIY construction as spectacle. **(Corrected: Real, Gondry for The White Stripes, 2002, but it is made of animated Lego bricks (stop-motion and digital), not cardboard or junk props.)**
- *Mood Indigo (L'Ecume des jours)* — Michel Gondry, 2013. Gondry's handmade physical effects at feature scale.
- *Brazil* — Terry Gilliam, 1985. British satire with improvised-looking ducting and paperwork machinery depicting a crushing state.

## What it carries

Warm, funny, scrappy, democratic. It carries pathos and solidarity: ordinary people with nothing, making something. It fights polish and menace, since a cardboard villain is never frightening.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Class satire with a scrappy, anti-corporate energy: the landlord's cardboard mansion, the cardboard Bank, community-built replicas of power. The visible cheapness suits a working-class audience and mocks prestige. Undermines serious dystopian dread and any piece that needs to be taken as real.

## How generators get it wrong

The engine builds a polished prop-shop set (closer to a tidy art-house miniature than real mess); push for 'wonky, uneven cuts, tape peeling, crumpled'. Cardboard becomes smooth clean plastic. Veo steadies the camera and makes movement graceful, losing the handheld amateur feel. Marker text is garbled. Cast faces become professional-actor attractive.

## Production cost

Cheap for set consistency if you reuse a reference of the cardboard world. Faces and costumes are the drift risk. Motion tolerates imperfection, which helps Veo.
