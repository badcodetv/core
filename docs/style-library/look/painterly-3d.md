# Painterly 3D (hand-painted textures, brushed light)

> 3D characters and sets with hand-painted textures, visible brush strokes and ink-and-paint lighting, so the film looks like a moving oil or gouache illustration.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A line of recent 3D animation set out to look painted rather than rendered: Arcane (2021) from Fortiche, with its brush-textured faces and hand-painted backgrounds; Puss in Boots: The Last Wish (2022), with its storybook brush strokes and stepped frame rate; Klaus (2019) is hand-drawn 2D but uses lighting to read as volumetric and painted. The principle is the same: keep a form that can be lit and moved in 3D but paint over it so surfaces carry the artist's hand and light feels chosen, not simulated.

## Signature traits

- Visible brush-stroke texture on skin, cloth and walls; strokes follow the form
- Strong key light with a narrow colour palette per scene (amber and teal, magenta and indigo), and rim light picked out in a second colour
- Ink or paint outlines that vary in width and sometimes break
- Backgrounds painted with loose, dabbed brushwork, with atmospheric haze and layered depth
- Faces simplified with painterly planes and hard-edged shadow shapes rather than subsurface realism
- Reduced frame rate or held poses (animation on twos) so motion feels like moving paintings
- Dust, sparks and smoke drawn as painted strokes, not particle simulations
- High-contrast colour scripting that changes mood from scene to scene

## References

- *Arcane* — Fortiche Production / Riot Games (Christian Linke, Alex Yee), 2021. The reference for painted 3D: brushed textures, hand-painted backgrounds, and a class-war story about a rich city above a poor one.
- *Puss in Boots: The Last Wish* — DreamWorks Animation (Joel Crawford), 2022. Mainstream proof that painterly 3D with stepped motion can carry comedy and darkness.
- *Klaus* — The SPA Studios (Sergio Pablos), 2019. A hand-drawn film lit with volumetric depth; the painted-light reference for 2D, useful for comparison.
- *Love, Death & Robots: 'Zima Blue' and other episodes* — Netflix (varied directors), 2019. Shows how many different painted and graphic digital looks one anthology can hold.

## What it carries

Rich, sad, handsome and weighty. It carries tragedy, class anger and moral gravity, and gives people dignity and a body in pain. It fights against jokes that need a flat graphic punch, and it makes anything look 'epic' whether or not the scene wants it.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Strong for: stories where inequality is felt in a face and a street, a rich-city-poor-city divide, the human cost of a decision. Strong for the sober half of a satirical piece when the joke turns serious. It would undermine: fast deadpan satire, where beauty competes with the gag, and anything that might read as a game cinematic if the story is small and domestic.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Use painter's language: 'painterly digital illustration, visible brush strokes following the form, hand-painted texture on skin and fabric, loose dabbed brushwork in the background, narrow palette of amber and teal with a magenta rim light, hard-edged shadow shapes, subtle ink outline, atmospheric haze'. Nano Banana usually delivers this well, as painterly illustration is a very well-represented target. For Veo: 'animated on twos, slow push-in, painterly look preserved, no photorealistic skin, brush texture stays fixed to surfaces'. Avoid: 'ultra-detailed, 8k, photorealistic, subsurface scattering, ray-traced reflections, cinematic bokeh'. Suno side: describe 'low strings, solo cello, detuned piano, heavy sub-bass, sparse choral pad' and similar instruments rather than a named composer.

## How generators get it wrong

This is the style closest to the engines' default, so the risk is genericness: it slides toward the glossy 'AI fantasy poster' with smooth airbrushed skin, over-rendered detail, and teal-and-orange everywhere. A brush texture is easy to prompt but hard to keep: in Veo the strokes often shimmer or dissolve and skin turns smooth and photographic after a second or two of movement. The faces drift between shots, and the painted look gets lost when a character turns. Short shots, slow camera and one fixed palette per story help. Keep the character on reference every shot.

## Production cost

Medium. Stills come easily and look good, which makes it tempting. The costs are keeping the brush texture alive in motion and keeping faces consistent across many shots; plan for tight framing and slow moves and expect to discard a lot of takes.
