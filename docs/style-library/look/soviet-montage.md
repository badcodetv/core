# Soviet montage

> Meaning made by collision between shots: low angles, hard cuts, masses of bodies and machinery.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A 1920s Soviet school (Eisenstein, Vertov, Pudovkin, Kuleshov) holding that cinema's meaning comes from editing, not from the single shot. Shots are chosen for graphic force and clashed against each other to make an idea (the Odessa Steps, the cut from a strike to a slaughtered bull). Cameras go low, high and tilted; faces are chosen as types; machines and crowds are heroes. It was state-sponsored revolutionary propaganda, and we should say plainly that its most famous works are agitprop, which is both what makes it useful for argument and what makes it risky.

## Signature traits

- Rapid cutting with clashing shot sizes and directions of movement; intercut metaphor shots
- Extreme low angles looking up at figures and machinery, Dutch tilts, diagonal composition
- Graphic black-and-white with strong contrast and clean light, 1.33:1 frame
- Faces in tight close-up chosen as types: the worker, the capitalist, the priest, held in profile
- Masses and rhythms: lines of workers, repeated gestures, marching legs, gears and pistons
- Intertitle slogans in bold sans or constructivist lettering, diagonal text
- Double exposure, superimposition and split-screen in Vertov's city films
- Montage of attractions: cutting between unrelated images to produce a feeling or a thesis

## References

- *Battleship Potemkin* — Sergei Eisenstein, 1925. The Odessa Steps sequence is the textbook for emotional argument by editing.
- *Strike (Stachka)* — Sergei Eisenstein, 1925. Cuts a crushed strike to animals in an abattoir; a model of the cut-as-metaphor.
- *Man with a Movie Camera* — Dziga Vertov, 1929. A city film with split screen, superimposition and machine-eye rhythm.
- *Mother* — Vsevolod Pudovkin, 1926. Tightly edited emotional character study of a worker's mother politicised by events.

## What it carries

Urgency, solidarity, righteous anger, mechanical rhythm, mass energy. It is excellent at turning an abstract fact into a felt pulse. It fights against nuance, private feeling, and ambivalence; it can feel like a lecture and its source material has a propaganda history that an audience may bring with them.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Short, punchy arguments about the economy: money moving, banks, factories, queues, a pay packet being divided. It suits a narrator who makes a thesis and cuts on it. Strong for montage sections inside a longer film (the downfall sequence, the figures going up). It would undermine character-driven comedy, and used carelessly it reads as hectoring, which clashes with the 'never say you've been duped' rule for the target reader. Using it with a wink at its own rhetoric works better than using it straight.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Because montage lives in the cut, generate many single images or 8-second clips chosen for graphic opposition, and assemble the argument in the edit. Nano Banana prompts: 'Soviet constructivist film still, 1920s, black and white, extreme low-angle shot looking up at a worker, diagonal composition, hard sunlight, high contrast, 4:3'. Ask for faces in profile and for repeated forms (rows of chimneys, rows of hands). For Veo, give short single-action clips with a strong direction of movement (a gear turning left, a crowd surging right) so cuts can collide. Use 'constructivist lettering' for slogans but add text in post. Exclude: 'smooth cinematic camera, shallow depth of field, soft light, pretty colour grade, lens flare, slow motion'. Name the visual rhyme between shots in your plan, not in the prompt.

## How generators get it wrong

Generators produce a single polished hero shot rather than a set of shots designed to clash, so everything looks too similar when cut together; plan the shot list for opposition (scale, direction, angle) first. Veo defaults to slow, smooth camera moves and shallow focus, which flattens the rhythm; ask for static or abrupt framing and cut short. Low-angle crowds can generate duplicated or melted faces; keep masses at a distance or cropped. Slogans come out garbled. Models also lean toward kitsch 'communist poster' pastiche with stars and red banners, which is a different (and politically loaded) thing than montage; keep it about editing and framing, not hammer-and-sickle iconography.

## Production cost

Needs many shots per idea, which is heavy on generation but each shot can be simple, and consistency across shots matters less because types and objects change every cut. It depends on an editor's rhythm more than on the generator. Very short clips are an advantage: you rarely need the full 8 seconds. Sound design (hits, machine rhythm) matters as much as picture.
