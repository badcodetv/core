# Felt, knit and tapestry textile

> Worlds stitched from wool, felt and woven thread, where soft material makes hard subjects sting.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

A craft look in which everything is built from cloth: felt cut-outs, needle-felted wool, knitted figures, embroidery and woven tapestry. Its power is contradiction: cosy, childlike material wrapped around a bleak subject. The long political tradition of textile includes narrative tapestry such as the Bayeux Tapestry's strip-form history and contemporary British tapestries on class. Visible stitches, fuzz, loose threads and fabric weave carry the look.

## Signature traits

- Visible stitching, running seams and embroidered outlines
- Fuzzy wool fibres catching light, soft diffuse shadow
- Flat felt cut-outs layered with slight depth
- Limited, slightly muted dye-like palette: wool colours, not neon
- Tapestry variant: horizontal strip composition with border bands and stitched captions
- Round simple shapes, button eyes, no fine facial detail
- Handmade imperfections: uneven stitches, lumpy edges

## References

- *The Bayeux Tapestry* — Anonymous (Norman and English workshops), c. 1070s. A strip-form embroidered narrative of conquest, the template for textile as political storytelling.
- *The Vanity of Small Differences* — Grayson Perry, 2012. Six large tapestries tracing British class taste, a direct model for textile class satire.
- *Sesame Street* — Jim Henson and Children's Television Workshop, 1969-present. Mass-audience proof that felt and fabric puppets read warm and expressive.
- *The Wrong Trousers* — Nick Park (Aardman), 1993. British handmade stop-motion with tactile material; a close cousin for texture and tone.

## What it carries

Warm, childlike, ironic; hits hardest when the cosy surface covers cruelty. It fights realism and cold menace unless that contrast is intended.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

Pieces about ordinary people and class, where tenderness disarms resistance and bitter jokes about bailiffs and landlords land harder in soft material. The strip-tapestry form suits a sweeping 'how we got here' history sequence. Undermines technological or cold-power imagery and anything needing sharp precision.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Nano Banana renders textiles convincingly with: 'needle-felted wool figures, visible fibre fuzz, hand-stitched seams, felt cut-out background layers, soft diffuse light, macro lens'. For tapestry: 'embroidered tapestry strip, wool thread on linen, border bands, stitched figures in profile, medieval composition'. Veo: 'slow gentle movement, stop-motion wool texture, 12 fps, camera locked'. Negative: 'smooth plastic, CGI fur, glossy, photoreal human, neon colours'.

## How generators get it wrong

Models make glossy plastic or CGI fur rather than felt; name wool fibre and stitched seams explicitly. Stitching comes out as printed texture without real thread structure. Veo smooths movement and over-animates faces, so the textile deforms like rubber; ask for stiff held poses. Embroidered text is almost always garbled, so add lettering in post.

## Production cost

Consistency is moderate: simple shapes help but wool texture drifts between clips. Motion is cheap if held, hard if limbs must move believably. Macro and close shots are the sweet spot.
