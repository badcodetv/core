# New Topographics (deadpan industrial landscape)

> Flat, neutral, emotionless photographs of warehouses, car parks, housing estates and industrial buildings, as if the picture were an inventory.
>
> Library entry: read only by the `story-style` interview. A story that picks this copies what it
> needs into its own look.md / sound.md; it never links here as its style.

Named for the 1975 exhibition 'New Topographics: Photographs of a Man-Altered Landscape' at the George Eastman House. Robert Adams, Lewis Baltz, Bernd and Hilla Becher and others photographed suburbs, industrial parks and utility structures with detached, clinical neutrality. Stephen Shore's colour and Paul Graham's A1 – The Great North Road (1983) carried it into colour and Britain. The picture makes no appeal: the viewer is left to do the judging.

## Signature traits

- Frontal, level, symmetrical or grid-like composition on a tripod or large format, with no dramatic angle
- Flat, even light: overcast sky or open shade, no hard shadows, no sunset
- Subject placed in the middle distance and fully in view: whole building, whole car park, whole street
- Empty or nearly empty of people; if present, they are small and incidental
- Muted, slightly cool palette in the colour variants; neutral greys in black and white
- Deep focus with every detail sharp, from foreground tarmac to far fence
- Repetition and typology: the same building type shot many times for comparison
- No emotional signalling: no flares, no vignette, no drama

## References

- *New Topographics: Photographs of a Man-Altered Landscape (exhibition)* — George Eastman House, curated by William Jenkins, 1975. The origin of the idea and the name.
- *The New Industrial Parks near Irvine, California* — Lewis Baltz, 1974. A classic case of anonymous commercial architecture photographed as pure fact.
- *The New West* — Robert Adams, 1974. Suburban sprawl and its roadside scars photographed without commentary.
- *A1 – The Great North Road* — Paul Graham, 1983. A British colour version: lay-bys, dole queues and roadside Britain in neutral light.
- *Water Towers* — Bernd and Hilla Becher, 1988. The typology method in its purest form: the same structure type repeated in a grid.

## What it carries

Cool, patient and faintly ominous. It carries dread, institutional indifference and the horror of ordinary buildings very well. It fights warmth, comedy and any character-led emotional beat, because it refuses closeness.

## What it suits, and what it would undermine

The impersonal machinery of the economy: data centres, distribution sheds, Job Centres, business parks, council offices, bank branches, server farms. Perfect for 'who owns this?' sequences, where the narrator fills the silence with sarcasm and the image deadpans. It undermines intimate human drama, and a whole film in it will feel cold and slow to a casual audience. Best used as establishing plates and chapter cards, cut against warmer sequences.

## Asking Flow (Nano Banana stills, Veo clips) for it

Nano Banana is strong at this when asked for neutral, descriptive photography: 'large-format photograph, tripod, perfectly level, frontal, overcast flat light, deep focus, muted colour, no people, 1970s-80s industrial estate, documentary typology'. Ask for the whole subject in the frame with the horizon at a stable height. Negatives: 'no dramatic sky, no lens flare, no vignette, no golden hour, no tilt, no HDR, no cinematic grade'. Veo from a locked still handles near-static shots well: ask for 'locked-off tripod, no camera movement, only small environmental change: wind in grass, a van passing, a light flicking on'. This is among the easiest styles to animate convincingly because stillness is the point.

## How generators get it wrong

Models love drama: they add a moody sunset, volumetric haze, dramatic clouds or neon, which destroys the neutrality. They also tidy the scene into something beautiful or dystopian-cinematic. They break symmetry and verticals subtly, and add invented signage and garbled text. Fix with explicit flat-light language, 'boring', 'plain', 'municipal', and a cleanup pass for text.

## Production cost

Cheapest of the family to keep consistent: no faces, a fixed camera, similar light. The cost is variety, since eight identical-looking warehouses lose the audience, so the story has to supply sharp narration to carry the dead pictures. Veo clips are reliably usable.
