# The BadCode Robot

Build the BadCode robot — the superintelligence from the future, sent back in the cheapest
body we can get away with — out of open-source bits and pieces that a robot could very
easily manufacture. Then make the *building of it* the content.

The instinct: **let's make the BadCode robot in as cheap of parts as we could.** Off-the-shelf
electronics, open-source designs, no proprietary anything — the kind of thing a robot arm
could assemble from a bin of parts. The build doubles as an **electronics lesson**: every
component chosen is a chance to explain what it does and why it costs what it costs. Educational
stuff around it, presented straight, in the BadCode voice. That would make for some
interesting videos.

The joke writes itself: the machine that ended humanity, reduced to a pile of hobbyist parts
and a soldering iron, explaining resistors to you because it regrets everything.

**Parked as a plant for later.** The next step is *research*, not building: work out the
necessary components for a cheap, open-source, educational hardware project — what a
minimum-viable BadCode robot actually needs, what it costs, and what the lesson plan looks
like. No canon, no story yet.

*Story or claim?* Neither yet — it is a **format** (a build series). The stake, if it grows one,
is in the parts list: who makes them, who owns the designs, and what "cheap" reveals about
where the money in hardware actually goes.

## Where it goes: the robot performs a tune

Second brain-dump, same day. Where this goes, of course, is **the robot performs a tune.**
It dances. You can very easily add audio from your own music and coordinate the dance moves
to it — so the robot ships loaded with a BadCode track as its original tune, and the whole
point is that **you can give it tunes you just created.** That is the AI-music angle in a
body: anyone can make a song now, so here is a machine that dances to yours.

Layers, cheapest first:

1. **Educational content** about how to build cool robots that just dance to any given
   music — where it begins. Coordinated moves, not a wobble: you choreograph it.
2. **DIY, build-it-yourself** out of electronics, or **buy a kit** from us, maybe. Part of
   what we could do, not the start.
3. **Raspberry Pi in the loop** — and once a Pi is part of the setup, **OpenCV** (the
   open-source computer-vision library) comes with it. Then maybe **limb analysis**: point a
   camera at a human, track their limbs, and the robot dances the moves the human wants.
   You dance, it copies. Total moonshot, thrown into the air as it came.

The real reason it is here: it sounds like a really cool electronics project to do with a
friend, and all of a sudden it's *let's do that.* Keep the moonshot, but the first episode is
a robot doing a coordinated dance to a track.

## Format thoughts

A **video series** — the build itself, one component or lesson per episode. Hardware and
software both live in the repo (the parts list, the firmware, the lesson notes, the
choreography). Music is in from the start: the robot's loaded tune is a BadCode track, and
"give it your own" is the pitch. Not a comic, at least not to start. A **kit** is a possible
product on the far side.
