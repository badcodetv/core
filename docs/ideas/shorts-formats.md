# Shorts formats (the Pub Quiz recipe, other rituals)

Jack, 2026-11-22: the fact-led pitches were "too boring and too stats heavy … how do we make it exciting, think how we did
with the pub quiz, but a different format", then "remember the rest just in case there are good ones". He picked
The Starting Gate (now `docs/stories/short-starting-gate/`). These are the rest, Claude's drafts; the jokes are Jack's and Kai's.

The recipe: a British ritual everyone already knows; someone wants something (stakes); characters bring their own lives;
BadCode is inside it in the mask (host, judge, commentator); the number is the twist, never the lecture; then the way out.
Each is our own format, never a real show's name, logo or theme. Every number gets fetched from its source before use.

- **The Valuation** (an antiques-valuation day; the coin's idea): a nan brings her 1997 house deeds and her 1997 payslip.
  The valuer (BadCode in the mask): the house, "Oh my word"; the payslip, "Same as it was. Collectors aren't interested."
  Way out: she asks the question nobody asks on that kind of show: "Who decided that?" Fact: ONS house price to earnings,
  England 7.6x in 2025, about double the 1990s (the 1997 figure not yet checked).
- **The Den** (investors hearing pitches; no policy without its test): a smooth minister pitches £2bn: "How will you know
  it works?" "I've got a good feeling." "I'm out." A nervous nurse pitches a one-ward trial with the number that would prove
  her wrong: everyone's in. Fact for one aside: the NAO (2021) found only 8% of major-project spend (£35bn of £432bn, 2019)
  had a robust evaluation plan.
- **Reconstruction** (a crime-appeal reconstruction; test it, stop if it fails): "Teenagers were taken into a prison to be
  scared straight. What happened next shocked researchers": they offended more (Cochrane / Campbell reviews). BadCode as
  presenter: "Test it. And if it fails, stop." A number on screen: "Seen a policy nobody checked?"
- **Black Box** (an air-crash investigation; publish what went wrong without blame): a crash is investigated, nobody is
  blamed, every plane gets the fix; a crashed policy gets a seven-year inquiry, three resignations, no fix. Way out:
  BadCode hands a minister a black box (the NHS safety investigator, HSSIB, already copies aviation and can't blame).
- **The Sofa** (living rooms watching the telly; issue by issue): families like a policy until the party badge comes up,
  "Oh. Them." Way out: the nan turns the badge off with the remote: "Just tell me what it does." Fact (weak until read):
  YouGov polling on names attached to policies.

Also pitched the same day, as fact-led ideas (Jack: too stats heavy as they stood; usable inside a format): "8%",
"So We Stopped", "99 Strangers" (Ireland's citizens' assembly; may fold into You Can Pick and Choose), "Peel the Label",
"£10,000 Each" (Bank of England 2012: about £10,000 a person if QE's wealth boost were spread evenly; the top 5% held about
40% of financial assets; the Bank also found most people better off), "Restraint" (a central banker's 2022 "clear
restraint" on pay, as on-screen words only, no face).
