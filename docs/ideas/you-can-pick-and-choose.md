## The facts (checked 2026-11-21)

**Switzerland: people vote on single national questions, several times a year.**
- About four federal voting days a year; since 1991 close to 10 individual national questions a year (1848-1900:
  about one a year). ([swissinfo: how direct democracy has grown](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/politics/how-direct-democracy-has-grown-over-the-decades/41481992),
  [swissinfo: how it works](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-democracy/how-swiss-direct-democracy-works/89073820))
- How it grew: 1848, a referendum is needed to change the constitution; 1874, people can force a referendum on a
  new law; 1891, people can propose their own change to the constitution (the popular initiative).
  ([Swiss government: the way to modern direct democracy](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/the-way-to-modern-direct-democracy-in-switzerland))
- Most citizens' own proposals fail (about 11% accepted since 1848), so it isn't chaos.
- 🔴 **The honest downside:** the 2009 vote banning new minarets passed (about 58%) after parliament had rejected
  it. Critics say it broke the constitution's guarantee of religious freedom. Anyone attacking the short will raise
  this; Ireland's model (below) is the answer to it.
  ([Migration Policy Institute](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/article/swiss-vote-ban-minarets-sparking-international-criticism),
  [EJIL: Talk!](https://www.ejiltalk.org/the-swiss-referendum-on-the-prohibition-of-minarets/))

**Ireland: ordinary people, picked at random, settled questions politicians wouldn't touch. Twice.**
- **Abortion:** the Citizens' Assembly, 99 people picked at random to reflect the country, met over five weekends
  (Nov 2016 to Apr 2017) and recommended repealing the Eighth Amendment. A parliamentary committee followed it; the
  referendum on 25 May 2018 passed 66.4% to 33.6% on a 64% turnout.
  ([Electoral Reform Society](https://www.electoral-reform.org.uk/?p=1873),
  [RTÉ results](https://www.rte.ie/news/newslens/2018/0526/966234-referendum-results-at-a-glance),
  [Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/web/bioethics/-/ireland-public-debate-concerning-abortion-and-the-repeal-of-the-eighth-amendment-to-the-constitution))
- **Same-sex marriage:** the Constitutional Convention (100 members; Claude's memory says 66 were random citizens,
  🔴 not confirmed) voted 79% in April 2013 to recommend it; the referendum on 22 May 2015 passed with 62% on a 61%
  turnout, the first country to bring it in by popular vote.
  ([Convention reports](https://citizensassembly.ie/overview-previous-assemblies/2013-2014-convention-on-the-constitution/reports-recommendations/),
  [Washington Blade](https://www.washingtonblade.com/?p=64951))

**Taiwan: thousands of people online found what they agreed on, and the government made it law.**
- On Uber, 2015: a four-week online consultation (vTaiwan, on a tool called Pol.is; 3,000 to 4,000+ took part,
  sources differ) started as two camps and found points both sides agreed on. The government agreed in May 2016 to
  adopt them (e.g. app cars can't pick up on the street; Uber pays tax and is regulated).
  ([CompDemocracy case study](https://compdemocracy.org/Case-studies/2014-vTaiwan/),
  [Shareable](https://www.shareable.net/open-data-hactivists-help-taipei-craft-regulatory-response-to-uber/))
- The twist worth using: the tool shows people what they agree on, not what they fight about.
- 🔴 The arguments didn't stop: Uber drivers were still being fined afterwards.

## Corrections to the first draft

- "Switzerland's been doing it since 1848" is only half right: 1848 was constitution changes only. Votes on
  ordinary laws came in 1874; people's own proposals in 1891. Safe line: "since the 1800s".
- Ireland did it twice, not once: same-sex marriage (2015) as well as abortion (2018).

## Why it isn't moany

The way out is real and already working. Three countries, three methods: vote on each question (Switzerland), let a
random jury of ordinary people work it out (Ireland), find what everyone agrees on (Taiwan). Each answers a different
fear: the Swiss one is "will people vote for anything?", the Irish one is "won't it just be the loudest?", the
Taiwanese one is "won't it just be a fight?"
