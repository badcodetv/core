# Nature Abhors a Vacuum

Physics keeps telling us the same thing and we keep not listening: **an imbalance is a
temporary condition.** Nature does not tolerate a gap. It closes it.

Everywhere you look it's the same law wearing a different coat. A vacuum is a place with a
massive lack of particles — so the universe rushes in to fill it. Electricity is the same
trick: a lack of electrons on one side, a surplus on the other, and the imbalance gets
corrected the moment it can. **That correction is lightning.** Not a punishment, not a
judgement — just physics settling an account that got too far out of balance.

The opener: someone nearly gets sucked out of the window of a Ryanair flight. A pinhole
between a pressurised cabin and nothing at all, and instantly the air — and the passenger —
is on its way out. Nobody had to organise that. No one voted for it. The pressure
difference did all the work in under a second.

*(The canonical real-world version of that image is British Airways 5390, 1990 — captain
half out of the windscreen at 17,000 feet, held by the legs. Worth knowing which incident
we're drawing on before it's drawn.)*

Then the turn. **The economy is the imbalance.** The billionaire class hoarding the wealth
is a pressure differential — an enormous surplus on one side of the glass, a vacuum on the
other. Nature abhors it. It always has. The only open question is which form the correction
takes, and whether anyone gets a say in choosing it.

The nod, stated or not: the people at the top are not defying gravity, they're *pending*.
You can equalise the cabin on purpose, or you can wait for the window.
