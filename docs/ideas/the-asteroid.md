# The Asteroid

The rock that killed the dinosaurs is on its way back. It's already in the air. It's just
not made of rock this time — **the asteroid is AI**, and it's coming in digital.

Play it dead straight as an **"Armageddon"-style disaster movie**. Every beat the audience
already knows: the scientists spot it, the tracking screens, the countdown, the world
leaders in the situation room, the roughneck crew shipped up to drill into it, the swelling
ballad, the sacrifice, the tearful phone call home. The whole machinery of a film about a
thing hurtling toward Earth.

**We never see the asteroid.** Not once. No rock, no fireball, no crater. The audience
supplies it themselves out of muscle memory, because they've seen this film a hundred
times and they know what's supposed to be up there.

The twist: there was never anything up there. **The AI is the asteroid.** It's already
landed — it's been landing for years, quietly, through the screens everyone was watching
the countdown on. Impact isn't a moment in the future; it's the thing happening while they
stare at the sky waiting for it.

And when it hits there is devastation. Just not the kind anybody bought a ticket for.

## Open question (for development, not now)

Who *aimed* it. An asteroid is nobody's fault — that's the whole comfort of the metaphor,
and it's also the trap: a disaster with no author lets everyone off. Whoever launched it,
and what they were pointing it at, is the thing the story has to name before it's ours
rather than everyone else's.
