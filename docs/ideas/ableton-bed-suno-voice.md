# Make the Music Ourselves; Take Only the Voice from Suno

Suno stops being the band and becomes the newsreader. It generates one thing — the narrator's
voice, dry — and the bed underneath him is written by us in Ableton. The machine reads the news;
the humans score it.

**Kai, 2026-09-12: not now.** We are short of time and resources, so the shortcut for this season
is that **music and narration both come out of Suno.** This file is the version of the plan we
take up when that shortcut stops paying.

## What it would buy

- **The bed stops being a lottery.** Right now a track is won by re-rolling four prompt boxes
  until something lands. An Ableton bed is a thing we *place*: this sub, this break, here, exactly.
- **Timing to picture.** A cut needs the drop on the frame the door opens. Suno has no bar counter
  — `docs/stories/camping/songs/camping.md:477` records that plainly ("a rate is not enough for a
  discrete event"). A DAW does nothing else.
- **It kills the bed-under-the-voice problem.** The GPOM narration sheet's one open fault is
  literally this: *"a faint bed still sits under him"*
  (`docs/stories/gitpush-origin-master/songs/narration.md:3`). If we make the bed, there is no
  ghost bed.
- **No download cap on our own audio.** Suno is rationing downloads; our own bounces are free.

## What it costs

- **Kai's hours, per track, forever.** This is the whole cost. Suno's pitch was never quality, it
  was that one person could ship a release without becoming a producer.
- **Skill and taste in D&B specifically.** Sound design, mix, master. Not nothing.
- **A stem problem if we go via separation.** `docs/suno-gpt/files/suno-controls-and-workflows.md:588`
  is blunt: Suno never had separate tracks, a splitter *guesses*, every stem bleeds, and stems
  export **mono**. The one piece of good news is the piece we need — **"Vocals separate best."**
  Better still: generate the voice dry in the first place and never split anything.

## What already exists to build on

- **Dry-and-separate is already law** (memory rule, 2026-08-27): Suno voice and music are *never*
  generated in the same take; the bed is flat and loopable, events are one-shots, timing is made
  in Premiere. That rule was written for Suno beds — it is exactly the interface an Ableton bed
  plugs into, unchanged.
- **A proven dry voice take.** `gpom-cut1voice-C-w30`, judged GOOD by Kai for emotion
  (`docs/stories/gitpush-origin-master/songs/narration.md:3`). The voice half already works.
- **`docs/suno-gpt/session-method.md`** — one variable per round, diagnose before rewording. The
  method transfers to a mix.
- 🔴 **Watch the assumption in the title.** Suno is not the only voice engine any more:
  `docs/ai-studio/README.md` is the first engine that did not sound like AI, and camping's narrator
  went to **Hume Octave** (2026-09-04). If this idea un-parks, re-ask which engine reads the news.
- Sibling: [Ableton Automation](ableton-automation.md), which is this idea with the tedium removed.

## What un-parks it

Any one of these:

- A cut needs music **locked to picture** and Suno cannot hit the frame after a fair try.
- Suno's download cap or subscription makes per-track re-rolling the expensive option.
- The faint-bed-under-the-narrator fault survives another round of dry-and-separate.
- Kai simply wants to make the music, which is a good enough reason on its own.
