# The pass — the adversarial review checklist

One ordered pass, roughly forty questions, collapsed from the ~280 diagnostics in the 24 briefs.
It runs top to bottom because the layers nest: a piece that fails a gate is not worth a scene
note, and a scene note is wasted on a story whose theme is still a topic. Each question names the
brief it came from so the reasoning is one click away.

**How to run it** is [§ The procedure](#the-procedure) at the bottom. Read that first if you are
reviewing, not drafting.

---

## 0 · Gates — the reader's rules (fail any → stop)

| # | Question | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | Does the reader's avatar appear as a **hero wronged by the system**, never as a pupil being taught? | the-reader 1 |
| G2 | Could an unsympathetic reader summarise this piece as **"you've been fooled"**? | the-reader 2 · 08 |
| G3 | Wherever automation/economic fear is raised, is a **chooser and a beneficiary on-panel in the same piece** — shown doing something, not gestured at as "the system"? | the-reader 5–6 · 13, 24 |
| G4 | Is the **re-aim** ("here is who") played straight, in the care register — and is it *quieter* than the sarcasm, not just warmer words? | the-reader 9 · 09 |
| G5 | Is there **one line quotable into the argument in the pub**? | the-reader 8 |

## 1 · The idea

| # | Question | From |

| 1.1 | Can the theme be stated as **one causal sentence** — and is that sentence never spoken aloud in the piece? | 02, 08, 18 |

## 2 · Structure

| # | Question | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Read the beat list aloud: does every seam join with **"but" or "therefore"** — where does only "and then" fit? | 03, 12 |
| 2.2 | Does every scene **turn a value** from one charge to its opposite? If a scene were removed, would the story lose a turn or just a fact? | 03 |
| 2.3 | Does each scene end on **disaster or complication** ("no, and…"), not clean resolution — and does its ending generate the next scene's goal? | 03 |
| 2.4 | Is there a **midpoint that reverses direction** — a false victory that curdles or a false defeat that cracks open — not just a louder version of the same threat? | 01, 04 |
| 2.5 | Is the **lowest point** distinct from the character's **grief about it** (two beats, not one)? | 01 |
| 2.6 | State the **promise** from the opening in one sentence: does the ending pay off *that* sentence, surprising *and* inevitable? | 04 |
| 2.7 | Does every **planted object or claim fire** inside this release — or is the payoff already in canon? Anything dangling without a deliberate flag? (A plant the text itself discharges — "the argument was not about that" — is not a gun.) | 04, 13 |
| 2.8 | Is the shape a **compound arc** (a reversal nested in a reversal) rather than one clean rise or fall? | 07 |
| 2.9 | Which thread opened first — does it **close last**? Any thread left open by accident? (MICE) | 18 |
| 2.10 | Is there a run of scenes that **does not shift the value** — could six become one? | 18 |

## 3 · Engagement

| # | Question | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Is the **bomb stated before it goes off** — does the audience know more than the character where we want them worried *for* the character? | 04 |
| 3.2 | Where something is withheld, is it **mystery** (the audience can reconstruct it) or **confusion** (they can't)? Do *we* already know what is in the box? | 13, 04 |
| 3.3 | If there is a twist, does it **reassemble facts already shown** — and does the piece make *more* sense on a second pass? | 06, 20 |
| 3.4 | Is there a **high-arousal beat** (awe, anger, anxiety) — and is every anger beat **paired with awe/scale** in the same piece? Does the piece sit anywhere in resignation? | 07 |
| 3.5 | Does **each panel / shot / verse carry weight alone** — a strong image, a number, a legible mechanism — without relying on cumulative immersion? | 08 |
| 3.6 | Would a stranger shown only the **first 10%** say correctly what kind of story this is and who it is about? Does the opener make a **promise** the rest honours, without an unsignalled register swing? | 05 |
| 3.7 | Does the **closing image rhyme with and transform the opening image**? Does the last line **compress** rather than explain? | 05, 06 |
| 3.8 | Does the foreknowledge **build irony** — or does pre-announcing the ending leave the middle slack? Would the scene still be tense for someone who knows the outcome? | 04, 09 |

## 4 · Persuasion

| # | Question | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | Is the political point ever stated as a **general claim**, or does it exist only as **consequence**? Delete the most on-the-nose line: does the point survive? | 08 |
| 4.2 | Is the **beneficiary named once, plainly, after the feeling has landed** — not instead of it, and not repeatedly? | 19, 24 |
| 4.3 | Is the manipulation **demonstrated on a third party** before — or instead of — being pinned on the reader's own belief? | 08 |
| 4.4 | **The Lee/Coe test:** if a viewer who believes the opposite saw only this beat, **whose contempt does it confirm**? | 24 |
| 4.5 | Is every **ironic line paired with an undeniable on-screen consequence** in the same beat, or does the joke depend on the viewer already knowing it's ironic? | 24 |
| 4.6 | Does every "reasonable voice" moment correspond to a **real, checkable policy or mechanism** (the receipt rule), or an invented absurdity? Is exactly **one** mechanism pushed one notch, not several stacked? | 15, 16 |
| 4.7 | Is the argument framed in the **reader's moral vocabulary** (loyalty, desert, a broken promise), or only ours (fairness, harm)? | 24 |
| 4.8 | Is there **one exchanged first-person account** — received, not argued — rather than a debate? | 24 |
| 4.9 | Does the ending **accuse the viewer** or tell them what to do? Is direct address spent **once, last, aimed at the chooser**? | 06 |
| 4.10 | Does the piece **hold the comic register through the diagnosis**, or drop into sincere tragedy in the last third? Is the hope beat **present as a line**, not assumed from tone? | 08, 12 |
| 4.11 | Would someone who **disagrees with our politics still feel this character's specific loss**? | 08 |

## 5 · Voice and line

| # | Question | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | For every scene, **Mamet's three**: who wants what, what happens if they don't get it, why now? | 03 |
| 5.2 | **Silent-movie test:** strip the words — does the scene still show who wants what and what's at stake? | 03 |
| 5.3 | Any line where **two characters discuss a third**, or "as you know"? Any **thought verb** (knows / realises / wants) that could be a physical tell? | 03 |
| 5.4 | **McKee's VO test:** could this narration line be cut with the picture left intact and lose nothing? Keep only lines that carry foreknowledge, causality or the beneficiary. | 09, 22 |
| 5.5 | Does every joke and caption **resolve on one read**? | 10 |
| 5.6 | Is there a **real build before each bathos drop**, or is the flat line flat with nothing to fall from? Is bathos budgeted, or spent every beat? | 10 |
| 5.7 | Is the narrator's joke about its own power in the **authority-vs-control gap** — composure intact, plot lost — never in it being dumb? Are the humans non-heroes (struggling, not stupid)? | 10 |
| 5.8 | Is there **exactly one crack** in the flat register per episode — not zero, not several? *(Satellites. The flagship's one change arc is exempt — principle 24; count only genuine register breaks, not heavy content delivered flat.)* | 23 |
| 5.9 | Is there a **recurring refrain** carrying the worldview, or is it only ever argued? | 09 |
| 5.10 | Read every narration line aloud: does the **rhythm vary with the moment** (short dry sentences, one long cosmic one), or is it uniform? With names removed, can you tell who is speaking? | 03, 18 |
| 5.11 | Does confident narration ever get **complicated by what's on screen** — or does the image always agree with the voice? | 09 |

## The procedure

1. **Read it once as a reader.** No notes. Then write the **coverage line** before anything else:
   logline · what it's about · what it's *really* about · one-line verdict (Pass / Consider /
   Recommend, with the one reason). **Read what was actually made, not only the canon file** — the
   scene ledgers, the recorded narration, the cut-order table, the banked alternates. In the first
   GPOM run three of twelve findings died on material the canon file didn't show
   ([`critique-pass-3.md`](../stories/gitpush-origin-master/critique-pass-3.md)).
2. **Gates first.** Any failure in §0 is the review. Stop, report it, don't proceed to scene notes.
3. **One layer per pass.** Do not run §1–§7 in one undifferentiated read. A theme pass, a structure
   pass, a line pass — separately (Jericho Writers; brief 18).
4. **Notes, not fixes.** Each finding is a diagnosis ("this beat isn't landing, and here is the
   mechanism") plus, at most, one *example* of a fix, clearly labelled as one option. The writer finds
   the solution. The reviewer has no authority to mandate anything — that is what makes candour
   possible. Cite principles, rulings and binding rules **by number with the file open** — a
   mis-cited ruling is the first thing a refuter finds.
5. **Grade each finding.** 🔴 fails a gate or breaks the promise · 🟠 a missed trick with a named
   mechanism · 🟡 polish. Lead with the 🔴s; cap the list — a review with forty findings is a review
   with none.
6. **Adversarial verification.** Before a finding ships, try to refute it: is it already handled
   somewhere in the canon? Would the fix break a binding rule? Is the evidence behind it **A** or
   **p**? Findings that survive get the grade; findings that don't get cut. For a flagship piece,
   run one *refuter* and one *strengthener* agent per finding and rule on the pair. **Expect a
   third of the draft to die and half to downgrade** — that is the method working (GPOM pass 3:
   2 cut, 7 downgraded, 3 held of 12). Record the cuts; a checklist's false positives are how it
   improves.
7. **Scope discipline.** The reviewer names what they were asked to hold fixed (the arc, the
   backbone, the rulings) and does not relitigate it. A finding that needs the arc to change is
   reported as *out of scope* with one line, not argued.
8. **Write back.** Anything the review taught that the toolkit didn't already know goes back into
   [`principles.md`](./principles.md) or the relevant file, dated — the same discipline as the Suno
   session method. The checklist is live.

### Coverage sheet (the output shape)

```
COVERAGE — <piece>, <date>, reviewer
Logline:
About / really about:
Verdict: Pass | Consider | Recommend — because <one reason>
Held fixed (out of scope):
Gates: G1 ✅ G2 ✅ G3 🔴 … (one line each where it fails)
Findings (graded, ≤ 12):
  🔴 <layer.q> <diagnosis> — mechanism: <principle/brief> — e.g. <one option>
  🟠 …
  🟡 …
Kept (things that work and must not be "fixed"):
Write-back: <what the toolkit learned>
```
