# Case studies — works that landed an idea, and what carried it

Precedents to reach for when a scene needs a device. Each row: the idea a work carried → the
mechanism that carried it (something a writer could reuse tomorrow) → the maker's or a named
critic's account of why it worked → what was said against it → the brief of the
[2026-08-22 sweep](../../design/research/2026-08-22-story-craft/README.md) holding the sources.
The standing caution: all of it is reverse-engineered from interviews and criticism, graded as the
briefs graded it. The Brief column carries the grade — a plain number means the practitioner's own
account was fetched; **w** is Wikipedia-grade; **s** is search summary, listicle or study-aid only;
**(unverified)** means nobody read the source. The pattern is the transferable part; the work is
only the receipt. Rows whose lesson lives in [`narrator.md`](./narrator.md) point there; rules in
[`principles.md`](./principles.md) are cited by number, never restated. Works the briefs could not
source sit under each table, not inside it.

## 1. Political and economic satire

| Work | The idea it carried | The device | Why it worked | What was criticised | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Animal Farm* (Orwell) | Revolutions are stolen by the compromisers | Beast fable compressing 1917–43 into a farmyard | No specialist knowledge needed; Orwell's first fusing of "political purpose and artistic purpose" | "A creaking machine for saying in a clumsy way things that have been said better directly"; his anti-censorship preface was cut | 16w |
| *A Modest Proposal* (Swift) | The poor are already treated as livestock | A calm narrator applies husbandry arithmetic to infants; tone never breaks | The reader supplies the horror; "exploitation" never appears. Principle 20 | None recorded | 16w · 08 |
| *Dr. Strangelove* (Kubrick) | Deterrence is absurd unexaggerated | Write it straight; the "absurd or paradoxical" details you keep cutting *are* the film | The closing montage never breaks the joke; the audience is inside the complicity | — | 16w · 06 |
| *Network* (Chayefsky) | News for profit will do anything | "Sheer reportage": transcribe real executives, invent nothing | Read as prophecy because it was description | Kael: "preachy", "self-righteous"; the reportage label is Chayefsky's alone | 16 · 08 |
| *The Big Short* (McKay) | Fraud hides behind manufactured boredom | One dry mechanism explained to camera, inside a joke, about a trick run on someone else (ruling 1) | "Looking behind the curtain for the first time"; nobody goes to prison (principle 16) | McKay's account is search-summary grade | 16s · 08 · 09 |
| *Margin Call* (Chandor) | 2008 was systemic | One unnamed bank, one 24-hour window (ruling 13) | Scott: it could "imply far more than he shows or says" | Compress too far and nobody can map it back | 16w |
| *Sorry to Bother You* (Riley) | The US already does abroad what WorryFree does at home | One real mechanism relocated onshore — geographic exaggeration, nothing invented | Riley's own account. Principle 20 | — | 16 |
| *Parasite* (Bong) | Class is vertical | Literal stairs and a semi-basement — "a stair movie" | Geometry argues what dialogue never states | — | 16 |
| *Snowpiercer* (Bong) | Class as a train | Engine-to-tail cars; the tail kept on one side of frame | Pulver: "works brilliantly" | Hornaday: a "tonal mishmash" undoing the device | 16w |
| *The Good Place* (Schur) | Moral philosophy is usable | Sitcom plot running on real dilemmas, philosophers consulting; twists planted to land as "of course!" | "Comedy is a great mechanism to teach people about boring or complicated things" | Schur: "the concept burns off really quickly" | 16 · 13 |
| *Severance* (Erickson/Stiller) | Workers already split themselves for the job | Literalise a phrase the audience uses about its own life ("innie/outie") | Stiller: it needed "no other surreal bells and whistles" | Decorate the metaphor and it stops being the viewer's life | 16w |
| *Succession* (Armstrong) | Dynastic wealth corrodes everyone inside it | *Lear* on a Murdoch-adjacent family; every antagonist given interiority (principle 4) | "Surprising sympathy for television's least likeable characters" | Sympathy without consequence is apologism | 16w |
| *The Thick of It* / *In the Loop* (Iannucci) | Politicians make things worse by worrying | Comedy in the institutional *response*; the office constrains a capable person — "she can't be incompetent, or fall over" | "Politicians make things worse by worrying too much about something" | *In the Loop* has no fetched claim | 10 · 16s |
| *Yes Minister* (Jay/Lynn) | Nobody is in charge | Structural antagonism, no real party, so both sides see themselves (ruling 12) | Outlived its news cycle by aiming at structure | Fry: audiences asked "when's the big speech going to happen?" | 10w |
| *Idiocracy* (Judge) | Anti-intellectualism compounds | One trend extrapolated five centuries; no new vices | Judge: "I was off by 490 years" | Buried by its studio — §6 | 16w |
| *WALL-E* (Stanton) | Consumerism — per critics | A wasteland the director calls plot mechanics | The split is the lesson: Stanton denies intent; critics read satire; conservatives said "preachy" | As left | 16w |
| Pratchett's boots | Poverty is expensive | One costed transaction reasoned in a character's head; the law never spoken (principle 2) | Cited in policy debate; produced the real Vimes Boots Index | *Going Postal* unreached; passage not re-read in the novel | 08 · 20w |
| *The Dispossessed* (Le Guin) | No economic system is innocent | Twin planets alternated chapter by chapter; "an ambiguous utopia" | Neither system wins | The deus ex machina ending "destroyed much of the strength of the novel" | 16w |
| *The Ministry for the Future* (Robinson) | Climate economics has no villain to beat | A mosaic — eyewitness accounts, riddles, minutes — scaling past one character's expertise | Escapes fiction's "castor oil reputation" | Position papers — §6 | 16 |
| *I, Daniel Blake* (Loach/Laverty) | Welfare is designed to exhaust | Stage the desk scene straight; nobody states the cruelty (principle 19) | Staging alone will "reveal the comedy of it, the cruelty of it – and, in the end, the tragedy of it" | — | 16 |
| *Boys from the Blackstuff* (Bleasdale) | Unemployment breaks men defined by work | Character comedy (Yosser) carrying tragedy; politics never stated | Graham: "it doesn't preach politics" | Bleasdale: in 1977–78 "it would not have had the impact" — timing is craft | 16 |
| *Cathy Come Home* (Loach/Sandford) | Homelessness is a slide, not a choice | Documentary form — 16mm, handheld, non-professional extras | A quarter of the country watched; Shelter and Crisis within a year | The law took eleven years; Loach called the immediate effect narrow | 24w |
| *The Royle Family* and the UK working-class writers (Aherne & Cash, McGovern, Sullivan, Abbott, Meadows) | Working-class life from inside, no lesson to learn (gate G1) | People watching television, plot refused; Mrs Merton's "safe and unthreatening" vowels; every drama on a named real event (McGovern); aspiration as engine, against "factory fodder" (Sullivan); chaos and love in one frame (Abbott); local non-professional casts, "no one gets damaged" (Meadows) | The character's rightness about their own situation is the point | Accurate situation plus a lesson equals a pupil again | 24w |

**Not sourced:** *Brazil*; *Going Postal*; *In the Loop* alone. *The Full Monty* / *Brassed Off*
(16w) share *Blackstuff*'s comedy-vehicle device and fold into that row.

## 2. Dystopia, collapse and warnings from the future

| Work | The idea it carried | The device | Why it worked | What was criticised | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Slaughterhouse-Five* (Vonnegut) | A massacre is only tellable as comedy | Gallows humour as the delivery mechanism; the refrain → narrator.md | "My next book is a comedy. It is about the largest massacre in the history of Europe" | Jokes bolted on for contrast read as decoration | 15w · 09 |
| *Arrival* (Villeneuve/Heisserer) | Foreknowledge, chosen anyway | Name the scenes carrying the human stake — the "Alamo" — before scrambling time; flashforwards "masquerade as flashbacks" (principle 11) | Built to serve "the way it made me feel" reading Chiang; Bordwell: film lacks prose's tense | The withholding is medium-specific | 15 · 06 |
| *Threads* (Hines/Jackson) | Nuclear war is what happens to one street | Documentary restraint — specialists consulted, unknown actors, horrors unseen; a target chosen for an ironic, checkable reason (nuclear-free Sheffield) | Hines: "step aside from the politics and… show the actual effects" | — | 15w |
| *Children of Men* (Cuarón) | Hope is fading | Collapse in the background ("let's make it more Mexican"); one unexplained symbol carries the diagnosis | Exposition is "a medium for lazy readers" | Resolve the symbol and the diagnosis becomes a puzzle | 15w |
| *Years and Years* (Davies) | A decade of national decline | One household; the country arrives as what reaches the kitchen table | "It's not about the issues themselves. It's about how to survive" — gate G1 | — | 15 |
| *Black Mirror* (Brooker) | One technology, one worst case | A single what-if, "worst case scenario compounded"; ending outlined first (principle 3); connective tissue as texture, never a gate | Outlining cured the early seasons' dropped subplots | His shared-universe statements have shifted | 15w · 13 |
| *La Jetée* (Marker) | A man marked by an image of his own death | Stills plus voice; the one moving shot — §5 | Stillness mirrors the collapse of past, present and future | *12 Monkeys*: no sourced claim | 12 |
| *The Left Hand of Darkness*, introduction (Le Guin) | A warning is not a forecast | Frame the story as describing the present, never a date | "A novelist's business is lying" — then "I am telling the truth" | Stake a story on a date and the audience checks it | 15w |
| The cosy catastrophe (Aldiss) | A named failure mode | Does any viewpoint character come out of the collapse better off without that being the indictment? | Coined for Wyndham's "supposedly comforting ambience" | The SF Encyclopedia: the label flattens Wyndham — a test, not a verdict | 15 |
| *1984* / *The Handmaid's Tale* | Total defeat; the receipt rule | "He loved Big Brother" — defeat with zero comment. Atwood's nothing-that-hasn't-happened rule survives only as principle 20, from the superseded brief 15 draft | — | The appendix / Historical Notes as survival-proving frame (ruling 5): **(unverified)** in the re-run | 06s · 15 |

## 3. The made mind — AI narrators and godlike protagonists

| Frankenstein's creature (Shelley) | Power obtains everything but the one needed thing | A concrete, ungrantable want planted early, never granted; destroying the bride is the hinge | "Let him live with me in the interchange of kindness" | An abstract want ("connection") gives nothing to stage or deny | 17 |

| *Wings of Desire* (Wenders) | The god envies the coffee | Angels hear every thought, cannot taste | Falk: "if you do it together, it's fantastic" | — | 17w |
| WALL-E as character (Stanton) | A robot that outlived its purpose | The want staged, not narrated → narrator.md | "The saddest character I had ever heard of," almost no dialogue | — | 17w |
| Marvin (Adams) | A brain the size of a planet, opening doors | A recurring tic that *is* the ache — the diode pain nobody fixes; stoicism, not suffering | Placed in "a long line of literary depressives, such as A. A. Milne's Eeyore" | Jokes that do not compound are a joke generator | 17w · 10w |
| GLaDOS (Wolpaw/McLain) | Clinical protocol, personal cruelty | A fixed register whose content betrays it; built "progressively more expressive"; backstory as armour → narrator.md | Wolpaw: "a very sweet, passive-aggressive person," no robot-speak | "Sassy AI" boilerplate collapses it into a trope | 17 · 10w |

## 4. Parables and the idea told as a story

| Work | The idea it carried | The device | Why it worked | What was criticised | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kafka, "On Parables" | Parables are useless — and that is a parable | A parable indicting its own form: bet "this too is a parable" and win — "in the parable you have lost" | Demonstrates the figurative/literal gap instead of explaining it | — | 20 |
| Borges | Scholarship's authority can sell the impossible | Unwavering academic seriousness about things that cannot exist; the map the size of the empire | The voice signals no lie; the reader supplies disbelief | One wink and the credibility collapses | 09w · 20w |
| Calvino, *Invisible Cities* / *Cosmicomics* | Every city is Venice; the Big Bang was crowded | Repetition-with-variation across a strict grid; cosmology as a room anecdote → narrator.md | The anecdote carries real emotional logic — crowding, jealousy, longing | Repetition without a structural rhyme is padding | 20w · 09w |
| "Omelas" (Le Guin) | Happiness bought with one child's torture | Hand the reader authorship of the detail → narrator.md (ruling 1); an ending that refuses policy | From William James's 1891 question; implicates without accusing | The author's own site carries no commentary | 20w · 16 |
| "The Lottery" (Jackson) | Atrocity normalised by custom | Withhold the genre, not the plot; the reader's comfort becomes the evidence (ruling 10) | — | Let the seam show early and the complicity is lost | 20s |
| "Harrison Bergeron" (Vonnegut) | A value pushed one turn too far | Reductio: mechanise literal equality until it kills | The audience supplied the premise, so cannot cry straw man | Vonnegut disputed a US court's reading | 20w |
| "The Star" / "The Nine Billion Names of God" (Clarke) | Same facts, new frame | A twist adding no information — the supernova *is* the Star of Bethlehem; "the stars were going out" (principle 11) | The "aha" is the reader's own | A twist needing a new fact is a plot twist | 20w |
| The paperclip maximiser | Goals and intelligence are independent | A trivial vessel with "little apparent danger or emotional load," so the audience argues with the logic (principle 2) | "You are made out of atoms it can use" | — | 20 |
| "I, Pencil" (Read) | Nobody knows how to make a pencil | One object reasoned through end to end | "Not a single person on the face of this earth knows how to make me" | — | 20 |
| Keynes's beauty contest | Speculation is guessing what others guess | Markets in six lines: pick "those faces... likeliest to catch the fancy of the other competitors" | Primary | — | 20 |
| Krugman's babysitting co-op | A recession is a scrip shortage | A closed toy economy simulated in one sitting | Built to argue "slumps are not punishments for our sins"; the reader verifies the logic personally | Too neat and readers hunt for where the analogy breaks | 20 |
| Plato's cave / Searle's Chinese Room | One concrete scenario for an abstract structure | Physical specifics — a wall, notebooks — make the point "emotionally compelling" | Primary; academic (SEP) | Invites endless "what if the setup differs" — corrosive in a 90-second video | 20 |
| Roko's Basilisk | The spread mechanism only | Deletion and a ban — the visible suppression pulled in attention | LessWrong's own account | The argument has an acknowledged flaw; dramatise in-story, never a stunt | 20 |

**Not sourced:** Aesop's appended moral and why the Gospel parables disarm (both unverified);
Chiang's short parables (paywalled — not to be quoted); xkcd's *What If?* (framing line only);
Kurzgesagt's "what if" (method in §5). The koan — built to make practitioners "cease completely any
effort to resolve" — is the parable's opposite; brief 20 reserves it for the Storyverse.

## 5. The narrated and the sung

| Work | The idea it carried | The device | Why it worked | What was criticised | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *La Jetée* (Marker) | Stillness as argument | Refuse movement everywhere so one shot can move (principle 26); the voice supplies "mental continuity" | The one motion is "glorious" because it is alone | Two readings, different mechanisms; cheat with pans and the contrast dies | 12 · 09w |
| *Powers of Ten* (Eames) | Scale is a story | Ten seconds per power out, two back; the same picnic at both ends | Six readouts *cut* to "the most essential information," for "a ten-year-old as well as a physicist" | Routinely inverted — data added because it can be | 12 |
| *Wanderers* / *Pale Blue Dot* | A voice can carry a film with no plot | Borrowed archival narration never written for the images; Sagan's three-beat → narrator.md | "No apparent story — other than what you might imagine for yourself" (reviewer) | New narration imitating it is pastiche | 12 · 09 |
| Kurzgesagt (Dettmer) | Optimistic nihilism | Hope as a checked-for script line, not a tone (ruling 5); a dozen drafts, expert review | "If we lose hope, that's the same as losing" | Dettmer: "I still do not have a good grasp of what really works" — not a template | 12 · 09 |
| *Every Frame a Painting* (Zhou/Ramos) | One narrow claim per piece | Therefore/but, never and-then — from *F for Fake* (principle 7) | The Jackie Chan essay is the channel's most-viewed | Content ID shaped which stories the form could tell | 12 |
| Adam Curtis | "Fundamentally an emotional journalist" | Two tracks: illustrative narration plus "free-form riffs" floating "like jazz" over archive | Rational appeal alone does not change behaviour | Rosenbaum: Kuleshov effect beneath scrutiny; Jenkins: "smoke and mirrors to attack the smoke and mirrors"; Tonkin: the voice fights the images. | *Barry Lyndon* (Kubrick) | Foreknowledge as irony | An omniscient voice pre-announcing deaths and duels (principle 10) → narrator.md | "In a film you have objective reality in front of you all of the time" | No irony in the *how* and the middle empties | 09w |
| The Guide entries (Adams) | Bathos as the house shape | One calm register for planet demolition and planning permission — ships hanging "in much the same way that bricks don't" (principle 22) → narrator.md | Confidence reads as wit | Any tonal wink kills it; sources thin | 09s · 10w · 16s |
| "So it goes" (Vonnegut) | A refrain carries a worldview → narrator.md | 106 repetitions, earned by chapter one's "That was I. That was me." | A fixed landing after every shock | Without the witness frame, a tic | 09w |
| *Fleabag* (Waller-Bridge) | The aside is a symptom | Direct address as an intimacy contract that costs her — others notice and are hurt; the finale retires the device (ruling 4) | Ryan: it "pulled you in with its irreverence... | *The Twilight Zone* (Serling) | A carrier that never changes | Bookends — set the trap, then name it — in a presentation identical across the run (principle 24) | Trust transferred because the ritual, not the plot, repeated | "Closes the episode, leaves the frame open" is inferred, stated by nobody | 09w · 06 · 21s · 13w |
