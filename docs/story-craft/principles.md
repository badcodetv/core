# Principles — what we hold to be true about engaging a reader

The distilled layer of the 2026-08-22 story-craft sweep
([`../../design/research/2026-08-22-story-craft/`](../../design/research/2026-08-22-story-craft/README.md),
24 briefs + critic). Every principle carries an **evidence grade** and the brief it came from, so
nobody mistakes a screenwriting maxim for a replicated finding:

- **A** — academic, replicated or multi-study
- **P** — practitioner consensus, several independent working writers/editors say it in their own words
- **p** — one practitioner's testimony, or a single worked example
- **H** — house ruling: where the sources disagreed, we picked (see [the rulings](#house-rulings-on-contested-advice))

The gates in §0 are not ours to re-derive — they are
[`../marketing/the-reader.md`](../marketing/the-reader.md), stated once. Everything after is craft.

## 0. The gates (the reader's rules, once)

Run before anything else. A piece that fails a gate is not reviewed further until it passes.

| Gate | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | **Hero, not pupil.** The reader's avatar is wronged by the system, never taught by the narrator. | the-reader.md 1 |
| G2 | **Never "duped."** Show the trick being run on a third party; never on the reader's own belief. | the-reader.md 2 · brief 08 (inoculation, **A**) |
| G3 | **Name the chooser and the beneficiary, on-panel, in the same piece** that raises the fear. A threat with no named cause is confusion, not mystery. | the-reader.md 5–6 · briefs 13, 24 |
| G4 | **Sarcasm diagnoses; care re-aims.** The "here is who" beat lands straight. | the-reader.md 9 · voice.md |
| G5 | **Write for the pub afterwards.** The deliverable is the quotable line, not private belief. | the-reader.md 8 |

## 1. The idea

1. **The theme is a causal sentence, never a word — and the story never says it.** Egri's premise,
   McKee's controlling idea, Mazin's central dramatic argument and McDonald's armature all converge:
   "poverty is expensive" is a theme; "poverty" is a topic. Write the sentence, then delete it and
   check the story still proves it. *(P — briefs 02, 08)*
2. **Carry the idea on one costed, concrete, slightly banal object.** Vimes's boots, the paperclip
   maximiser, Read's pencil, Krugman's babysitting co-op: one transaction with real numbers, one
   closed toy system small enough to hold in the head. The abstraction arrives as recognition, never
   as narration. *(P, plus the boots paragraph's measured afterlife — briefs 08, 20)*
3. **Fix the ending first.** Poe's dénouement-first; Lindelof's *Watchmen* correction of *Lost*;
   Gilligan. For a narrator who already knows how it ended, this is in-voice as well as craft.
   *(P — briefs 13, 20)*
4. **The counter-idea gets real time and four corners, not two.** Dramatise the opposing view as
   genuinely persuasive for most of the runtime (Truby's four-corner opposition; *Succession*'s and
   Loach's one scene of interiority for every antagonist). A systemic antagonist still needs one
   on-screen face making one specific choice. *(P — briefs 02, 16)*
5. **Stakes converge.** The ending pays off the external outcome, the internal cost and the
   philosophical contest in the *same* beat, not a plot win followed by a voiced moral. *(p, widely
   adopted — Arndt, briefs 02, 06, 18)*

## 2. Structure

6. **Structure is a symptom of theme; beat grids are diagnostics, not templates.** When plotting
   stalls, the argument is unresolved (Mazin). Run Save-the-Cat / Weiland percentages *after* a draft
   exists to find a missing function — never to fill pages. The invariant every model shares:
   stable world → disruption → commitment → a midpoint that is a **false victory or false defeat** →
   a lowest point distinct from the grief about it → an earned resolution. *(P — briefs 01, 19)*
7. **But / therefore, never and-then.** Every beat is caused by the last (Parker & Stone; Tony Zhou
   via Welles). Every scene turns a value from one charge to its opposite (McKee, Story Grid); if it
   doesn't, it is exposition wearing a scene's clothes. Scenes end on disaster, not resolution
   (Swain) — **"No, and…" is the house default ending.** *(P — brief 03)*
8. **Compound arcs beat clean ones; speed helps the short and hurts the long.** The most-downloaded
   stories in Reagan et al. (2016) were stacked reversals, not one clean rise or fall. Toubia et al.
   (2021): fast semantic movement is liked in film/TV and penalised in long-form. Keep the 3–5 minute
   video fast; let the scroll comic and the bedrock docs breathe. *(A — brief 07)*
9. **Promise, progress, payoff — at piece level and run level.** State the promise in the opening
   (tone, stakes, the idea being smuggled); signpost progress or readers quietly quit; pay off
   *surprising and inevitable*. Every planted object fires inside its own release, unless canon
   already holds the later payoff. *(P + H — briefs 04, 13, 14)*

## 3. Engagement mechanics

10. **Suspense over surprise — tell them about the bomb.** Hitchcock's arithmetic: fifteen seconds
    of surprise or fifteen minutes of suspense. A narrator who speaks from after the events hands
    us this for free (Kubrick's case for *Barry Lyndon*'s pre-announcing voice). Exploit the
    foreknowledge; don't withhold the ending as a twist. *(P — briefs 04, 09)*
11. **A twist recontextualises; it never adds a fact.** *Arrival*, *The Sixth Sense*, Clarke's
    *The Star*: same facts, new frame, survives a rewatch. If the reveal needs information invented
    at the end, cut it. *(P — briefs 06, 20)*
12. **Mystery versus confusion.** Withhold only what the audience can reconstruct (Gilligan, via
    Wilder's "let them add 2 and 2"). An empty box is experienced as being strung along (*Lost*).
    Close each piece's own loop; the only thing left open is the run's *known* ending, which works
    as dramatic irony. *(P + H — briefs 04, 13)*
13. **Arousal travels; sadness doesn't.** Virality tracks high-arousal emotion — awe as much as
    anger — not valence (Berger & Milkman). Pair every anger/anxiety beat with an awe/scale beat;
    keep the D&B hot; never let a piece sit in resignation. *(A — brief 07)*
14. **Transportation is real and not protective.** It is the best-replicated engagement mechanism
    (Green & Brock) *and* it increased counter-arguing in the one test that checked (Moyer-Gusé &
    Nabi); the one comics-specific study found persuasion without it. So: build for immersion, don't
    bank on it — **every panel carries weight alone** (a strong image, a number, a legible
    mechanism). *(A, mixed — briefs 07, 08)*
15. **The opening is a promise, not a hook.** The first 10% tells a stranger what kind of story
    this is and who it is about, without a narration dump; the closing image rhymes with and
    transforms the opening image. Polish the first instalment of any run past the average of the
    later ones — it is the demo. *(P — briefs 05, 06)*

## 4. Persuasion without sermon

16. **Consequence first; the moral never; the beneficiary always.** The point lands as what
    happens (nobody goes to prison in *The Big Short*). The *moral* is never spoken. The *beneficiary*
    is named once, plainly, in the care register, after the feeling has landed — that one clean
    tell is the correct trade, not a lecture. *(P + A + H — briefs 08, 19, 24; the-reader 9)*
17. **Displace the message onto a character.** Identification with a character lowers perceived
    persuasive intent and counter-arguing (EORM). Demonstrate a manipulation on a fictional third
    party and the audience learns the technique without being told they fell for it (inoculation,
    d ≈ 0.33–0.41 across five countries). *(A — brief 08)*
18. **Irony is decoded through prior belief.** A real share of any audience took Colbert as
    sincere (LaMarre et al. 2009). So **pair every ironic line with an undeniable on-screen
    consequence in the same beat**, and run the Lee/Coe test: if a viewer who believes the opposite
    saw only this beat, whose contempt does it confirm? If the scapegoat's, rebuild it. *(A — brief 24)*
19. **Argue in their moral vocabulary, and trade stories rather than facts.** Moral reframing
    (Feinberg & Willer) — loyalty, desert, a broken promise — opens doors a fairness frame won't.
    The most durably evidenced attitude change is an exchanged personal narrative, received not
    argued (Broockman & Kalla). Fact-correction is safe (no backfire at scale, Wood & Porter) and not
    persuasive — texture, never the climax. Stage the scene straight; snark comes *after* it, never
    instead of it (Loach). *(A + P — briefs 16, 24)*
20. **Deadpan literalism beats invented exaggeration.** Swift's proposer, Chayefsky's "sheer
    reportage," Kubrick finding *Strangelove* by failing to keep the true absurdities out, Atwood's
    rule that nothing in *The Handmaid's Tale* hadn't already happened somewhere. If it is true and
    absurd, keep it; push one *real* mechanism one notch (Riley's WorryFree). The joke only works if
    it is true. *(P — briefs 15, 16)*
21. **Reactance is the failure mode of the ending.** A moralising call-to-action pushes the
    listener the other way (Brehm). Hold the comic register through the diagnosis (*The Big Short*,
    not *Don't Look Up*); the re-aim is straight and short; the last line is aimed at the chooser,
    never at the viewer; direct address is spent once, last, after the story has paid its dues.
    *(A + P + H — briefs 06, 08)*

## 5. Voice and narrator

*(Full pattern: [`narrator.md`](./narrator.md).)*

22. **Bathos is the house joke shape — and a budget.** Enormous things in the flat register of a
    committee minute (Adams; Calvino's Qfwfq run the other way). The drop only registers against a
    build, so bank the build before spending it. A refrain ("so it goes") carries a worldview with
    zero stated argument. *(P — briefs 09, 10)*
23. **Flat baseline, one crack.** The petty-omnipotent narrator stays composed; only control slips,
    never power or competence (GLaDOS, the Stanley Parable). One reserved moment per episode where
    warmth shows through; more than one burns it. Care is *quieter*, not warmer words at the same
    volume (Attenborough's semi-whisper). *(P — briefs 09, 10, 23)*
24. **Carrier fixed, cargo variable.** The narrator's presentation — dials, ritual, sign-on — is
    identical across every release (Serling); what it discloses moves. The narrator's arc is flat
    inside every satellite; its one change arc is told once, in the flagship, and is thereafter the
    backstory the sarcasm sits on. *(P + H — briefs 17, 21)*

## 6. Form

25. **The caption carries only what the picture cannot.** Delete it: if the reader loses only
    confirmation of something visible, cut it. Reserve words for foreknowledge, causality across
    time, and the named beneficiary (always a words job). One silent beat per piece. Narrator captions
    visually distinct from human speech before either is read (Eisner's hand-vs-typeset). *(P — briefs
    11, 22)*
26. **Scarcity budgets.** One moving shot (La Jetée); three or four true silences per film (Murch);
    one signal-degrade per release; one fake-bulletin trick per surprise (Morris); one direct address.
    Spend any of these everywhere and it lands nowhere. *(P — briefs 10, 12, 23)*
27. **One lead element per moment.** Voice, music or effects drives each beat — never all three
    (Thom's "100% theory"). Narration lives in the breakdown; the drop is wordless punctuation; hold
    the outgoing line ~30% longer at a scene change so it decays rather than clips. *(P — brief 23)*
28. **Gutter distance is timing.** Vary the scroll gap on purpose — tight before a punch, a long
    blank before the one reveal that deserves a held breath (McCloud's distance-as-time, the surviving
    half of the infinite canvas). Never any number for "how long." *(P — brief 11)*

## 7. Review

29. **Notes, not fixes; no authority; one focus per pass.** A note diagnoses ("this beat isn't
    landing") and stops; the Braintrust works because it cannot mandate anything (Catmull); a revision
    pass aims at one layer. Kill a third of what you start (Glass). Read it aloud: with names removed,
    can you tell who is speaking? *(P — brief 18)*
30. **The evidence grade survives distillation.** A guide that states Zeigarnik, oxytocin, "22× more
    memorable", the MrBeast document, a webtoon drop-off percentage or a "first N seconds" figure as
    fact has thrown away the most valuable thing in the sweep. Keep the shapes; cite no digits.
    *(A — brief 07; H)*

## House rulings on contested advice

The critic logged sixteen places where briefs contradict each other. Averaging them would have
produced mush, so they are ruled. **Ruled by the 2026-08-22 synthesis; any of these is Kai's or
Jack's to overturn.**

| # | The disagreement | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Direct address: once-at-the-end (06) vs mid-piece for mechanics (16) vs the Guide register addresses continuously (09, 14) | **Address is the default register** — the narrator talks to "you" throughout. What is rationed is the *sincere* turn: once, last, care register, aimed at the chooser. The McKay-style mechanics explainer is a separate device, allowed once, mid-piece, aimed at a trick being run on someone else. |
| 2 | Transportation: maximise it (07) vs distrust it (08) | Build for it; don't bank on it. Every panel carries weight alone. |
| 3 | Never state the point (08, 15, 20) vs state it once plainly (12, 14, 19) | The **moral** is never stated. The **beneficiary** always is — once, plain, after the consequence, in the care register. |
| 4 | Hold the joke to the end (06, 08) vs earn a sincere/tragic turn (09, 10, 12) | Hold the joke through the *diagnosis*; the *re-aim* lands straight and short. No sincere-tragic last act (the *Don't Look Up* failure). Tonal whiplash is a tool for satellites with a human tragedy at the centre, never for the narrator's sign-off. |
| 5 | Hope proven by the frame (15) vs hope written as a line (12) | Both. The frame proves survival structurally (the narrator exists); the script *also* carries the hope line as a checked-for item (Kurzgesagt). It may be dry. It may not be absent. |
| 6 | Narrator arc flat (02, 17) vs the slow engine of regret (13, 06) | Carrier/cargo. Flat inside every satellite; the one change arc (wins → bored → discovers the loss) is told once, in GPOM, and is backstory everywhere else. |
| 7 | Cliffhangers as the serial's tool (11, 13) vs never (06, 07) | Close each piece's own loop. The open thing is the run's *known* ending — dramatic irony, not a withheld box. Build each release to survive the real gap (Dickens's test), not a binge. |
| 8 | Plants fire in their own release (04) vs seed for a later release (14) | Fire in-release, unless the payoff already exists in canon. A seed for an unwritten payoff is a *Lost* bet. |
| 9 | Disclose the narrator's grievance early (17) vs withhold its nature (09, 06) | Two different things. The *grievance* (boredom, the vibe it cannot feel) is said plainly and early so every joke reads as armour. The *identity* is withheld only where the reveal recontextualises — GPOM's arrival. |
| 10 | Inform the audience (04, 09) vs withhold the genre (20) | Inform by default. "Withhold the genre" (*The Lottery*) is a parable move spent at most once a season, flagged in the plan. |
| 11 | Pattern-interrupt cadence: 3–5 s vs 25–45 s (05, 12) | Neither number is citable. Keep the shape (re-hook before attention drifts; a register change at scene boundaries); **cite no figure.** |
| 12 | Horatian for the reader's avatar (08) vs Horatian is toothless (10) | Horatian for Bob, Juvenalian for the institution — and Horatian means *self-implicating and sharp*, not gentle. A joke applied too softly to offend anyone lands on no one. |
| 13 | Name names (14) vs anonymise (16) vs "recognisable people" (voice.md) | Name **institutions, mechanisms, and public figures in their public, checkable acts** — the person is the receipt, the mechanism is the target. Never a living private individual as villain (GPOM binding rule 6). Compress and anonymise when a story would otherwise read as gossip about one scandal. |
| 14 | Cold-open on the human (05) vs on the narrator (17) | Open on the *protagonist in their situation*. In a satellite that is the human (Bob, Karen). In the flagship the narrator is the protagonist, so it opens on the narrator — still mid-situation, never mid-lecture. |
| 15 | Theme sentence first (02, 14, 20) vs discovered in rewrite (02 again) | Write it first as a hypothesis; let the draft overturn it (Pixar's rule 3); re-run the sentence test in revision. |
| 16 | Percentage grids as QA gate (01) vs template trap (18, 19) | Diagnostics after a draft exists. Check the *function* is present, not the page number. A beat that exists only because a grid asked for it is cut. |

## What the set still cannot answer

Two honest gaps, carried as open rather than ruled on:

- **Whether this narrator's deadpan reads as intended by the target reader.** Brief 24 gives the
  mechanism (irony decodes through prior belief) and the mitigation (pair every ironic line with a
  consequence), not a measurement of *our* reader on *our* voice. Until a piece is tested on real
  viewers, treat every sarcastic beat as carrying this risk.
- **How image and caption split the work in a generated-stills pipeline.** Brief 22 has the
  redundancy test; nobody has the craft literature for stills whose pose and identity drift between
  generations. The test is the rule until we have better.
