# Evidence — what the science supports, what it doesn't, and what we never cite

A craft guide that launders a working writer's rule of thumb into "research shows" has thrown away
the most valuable thing in the 2026-08-22 sweep: the line between a measured finding, a
practitioner's testimony and a number invented for a listicle. This file is the ledger. Every
claim carries the grade it had in its brief — **A** (academic, replicated or multi-study),
**A (mixed)** (peer-reviewed but contested or single-study), **P** (practitioner consensus), **p**
(one practitioner or one case), **myth** — plus *(unverified in sweep)* where the brief could not
reach the primary source. It underwrites the grades in [`principles.md`](./principles.md) and
principle 30 in particular. Check it before any mechanism or number appears in a brief, a review
note, a canon file or public copy: find the row, take the grade with you; no row, no citation.

| **Narrative transportation** predicts belief change | **A** | Green & Brock 2000 (N=97); validated scale, condensed (Appel), video version (Williams), revisited Green & Appel 2024. | **Inoculation** — run the trick on a third party | **A** | Roozenbeek & van der Linden, *Bad News*: 15,000+ players, five countries, *d* = 0.33–0.41, negligible demographic moderation. | Gate G2; principle 17. Fails the moment the dupe resembles the reader. | 08 |
| The **sleeper effect** | **A (mixed)** | Hovland: more shift at nine weeks than five days. Cook et al.: narrow conditions only. "Notoriously difficult to replicate." | A tailwind, never a design lever. | 08 |
| **Neural coupling** | **A** (descriptive) | Hasson: coupling tracks comprehension, only with meaningful speech; one framing sentence split an audience into two neural clusters. | Correlates with understanding; says nothing about which craft choice causes it. Never "this syncs brains." | 07 |

| Narrative **speed** predicts success — direction flips by domain | **A** | Toubia, Berger & Eliashberg 2021 PNAS, ~50,000 texts: faster film/TV liked more; papers reversed (slower, wider, indirect cited more). | Principle 8 — fast video, slow comic. | **Predictive processing** — pleasure is the *rate* of resolving uncertainty | **A** (theory) | Friston, Seth, Hohwy, Clark; thinly tested on narrative. | **Peak–end rule** | **A** (short) / weak (long) | Kahneman & Fredrickson; Diener's "Jen." Explanatory share shrinks as episodes lengthen. | The sign-off does outsized work in a 3–5 minute video; trust coherence in the long scroll. | 07 |
| **Information-gap** curiosity | **A** *(unverified in sweep)* | Loewenstein 1994, *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1). Primary PDF unreadable; secondary summaries only. | A gap must be noticed and closable. Close it by naming the beneficiary (G3). | 04 |

| **Ovsiankina** — the urge to resume | **A** (the robust sibling) | Same meta-analysis: holds up better than Zeigarnik. | If a cliffhanger mechanism must be named, this one. | 04, 07 |

| **Moral reframing** | **A** | Feinberg & Willer 2013, 2015, 2019: purity-framed appeals moved conservatives where harm/fairness did not. Effect sizes not fetched. | Principle 19 — loyalty, desert, a broken promise, before fairness. | 24 |
| **Deep canvassing** — exchanged narrative durably shifts attitudes | **A** (one field experiment) | Broockman & Kalla 2016, *Science*: N=501, ten-minute conversations, held at three months. Preceded by LaCour & Green 2014, retracted for fabricated data. | Principle 19 — one received, not argued, first-person scene per story. | 24 |
| The **backfire effect** | **A** (null) | Wood & Porter 2019: five experiments, 10,100+ subjects, no backfire. Not evidence that facts persuade. | Fact-correction is safe texture, never the climax. | 24 |
| **Parasocial contact** | **A** (meta, correlational) | Schiappa, Gregg & Hewes 2005; Banas et al. 2020, 79 cases: positive *r* = −.23, negative *r* = +.31. | A recurring, textured character from a feared group; a walk-on does harm. | 24 |
| **Exemplification** — one vivid case beats the base rate | **A** (multi-study) | Zillmann 1994–2012: the exemplar's influence decays slower than the statistic's. | Why principle 2 works — and why a scapegoat anecdote works. G3, no exceptions. | 24 |

(01, 19) | A scaffold some stories fit. GPOM isn't one. Never "universal." |
| **Show, don't tell** | Workshop orthodoxy | Prose ("bad advice"); Le Guin ("the telling that moves"); Yorke (exposition survives if disguised by conflict); Chekhov's real 1889 letter is about false promises; Nguyen's caveat *(unverified; via critic notes)* | Practitioners both sides; nothing measured. | **Save the Cat's page counts** | Snyder; Weiland's ±5% "story physics" | Suderman (*Slate*): became "a formula"; pre-Snyder films hit the beats less rigidly; Dalton on the sexism | True by being believed. (01) | Ruling 16 — diagnostics after a draft. |

## 3. The never-cite list

None of these appears as fact in a brief, a note, a canon file or public copy.

- **"22× more memorable."** Untraceable to Bruner; the real ~7× and ~12–13× are one word-list test and one classroom exercise. (07)
- **Oxytocin / cortisol / "the neurochemistry of story."** Failed two powered replications. (07)
- **Zeigarnik as memory.** Lacks universal validity. Say Ovsiankina or "audiences want closure." (04, 07)
- **Any "first N seconds" percentage** — "18% higher retention," "71% decide within 3 seconds," the **"Stanford 8–15s study"** that vanished on a careful fetch. SEO content and one blog's self-reported accounts. (05, 12)
- **The MrBeast document's figures** ("lost 21 million in the first minute," the 3–6 minute zone). Provenance unconfirmed. Shape only. (05, 12)
- **Webtoon pixel ranges, the 60-panel episode, the drop-off at panel 20, 30–40% → 5–15%.** Two craft blogs, one reading as SaaS marketing. (11)
- **Pattern-interrupt cadences** — 3–5 s vs 25–45 s. An order of magnitude apart; both briefs disown their numbers (ruling 11). (05, 12)
- **"Retention Triangle," seven-part hook formulas, percentage thresholds.** Unsourced marketing blogs. (12)
- **Royal Road / Wattpad retention claims.** Forum anecdote and a marketing guide. (13)
- **The Feige "homework" quote.** WSJ via blogs; no primary transcript. The phenomenon is attested; the attribution isn't. (13)
- **The external/internal/philosophical stakes triad as research.** Arndt's practitioner rule — **P**, never **A**. (02, 04, 06)
- **Dent's four-part formula, Vonnegut's eight rules, Hemingway's iceberg** as sourced rules. All *(unverified)*; described from memory. (20)
- **Harmon's "nearly every turn on *Community*"; the *Fight Club* case for Yorke.** Secondary sourcing only. (01)
- **Hasson's coupling as proof a craft choice works.** Descriptive. (07)
- **The sleeper effect as a plan.** Real, fragile, narrow. (08)
- **"Fiction builds empathy" as settled.** A programme. (07)
- **Adam Curtis's dual-track method as validated.** Craft or manipulation is an open dispute (Rosenbaum, Jenkins). (12)
- **Any comics-persuasion mechanism stated as known.** One thesis, either direction. (08)

## 4. How to cite a mechanism in BadCode copy

1. **The grade travels with the claim.** *(unverified)* or **p** in the brief stays *(unverified)* or **p** in the note, the canon and the caption.
2. **Prefer "a working writer's rule" to "research shows"** unless the row says **A** — and then name the study, not "science."
3. **If a mechanism must be named in public, name one of two:** transportation (Green & Brock) or arousal over valence (Berger & Milkman). Nothing else here is sturdy enough to be armour.
4. **Effect sizes are small; the bet is cumulative presence.** the-reader.md's honest-limits line — *d* ≈ 0.04–0.08 durable, at best — is the number to hold whenever a brief starts sounding like a conversion plan.
5. **When in doubt, name the practitioner, not the science.** "Hitchcock's bomb," "Vimes's boots," "Gilligan's test" are checkable and in-voice; "studies show" is neither.
