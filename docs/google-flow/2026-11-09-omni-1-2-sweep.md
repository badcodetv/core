# Omni 1.2 Flash sweep, and what the 5 Dec retirement leaves us (2026-11-09)

**Why this exists:** on **5 Dec 2026** Flow retires Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 Quality and Omni 1.1 Flash. Most of
our clips were made on those three: Veo 3.1 Fast for camera-locked start+end frame shots, and Omni 1.1 Flash
for start-frame shots, cast characters and talking clips. What remains is **Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash** and
**Veo 3.1 Lite**. This file records what is known about 1.2 four days after launch, and what we must test.

**Researched:** 2026-11-09, web only (17 searches, 7 YouTube transcripts tried and 4 read). **Nothing here is
`[confirmed]`**: we have not run 1.2 ourselves yet. **New items only**: anything already in
[`omni-flash.md`](./omni-flash.md) or [`../flow/platform-controls.md`](../flow/platform-controls.md) is not
repeated, only corrected where it is now wrong.

**Tiers used:** `[vendor]` Google's own pages · `[press]` news write-ups (all of them repeat Google's claims;
none ran a test) · `[runware]` a model host's docs · `[community]` practitioner blogs · `[yt]` a creator
demonstrating it on screen · `[leaderboard]` public arena boards. Per the README, nothing below earns a rule
until we run it.

**What the evidence is like:** the launch was 5 Nov, so there is almost no independent testing yet. One creator
ran 18 matched takes of 1.2 against 1.1 inside Flow (Adam Campbell Cinema). One blogger made a 49-second avatar
promo in Flow (Pragma-Code). Everything else is Google's claims, repeated. No arena has a 1.2 entry yet. Reddit
could not be reached by the search tool.

## 1. Official: what Flow actually offers after 5 Dec

### Dates `[vendor]`

- **5 Nov 2026: Veo Extend was retired in Flow.** Extending a clip now needs a Plus, Pro or Ultra plan **and**
  Omni 1.2. (Flow's models page and Flow's credits page, both.)
- **5 Dec 2026: Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 Quality and Omni 1.1 Flash are retired in Flow.** Google recommends Omni 1.2
  or Veo 3.1 Lite. Until then the retiring models run at their usual rates. (Our flow-mcp tests already see the
  UI label `Omni 1.1 Flash Leaving 5 Dec`.)
- **3 Dec 2026: the API alias `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` starts routing to 1.2** (AI Studio docs). This is the API,
  not Flow. The two dates differ by two days and are separate surfaces. Don't mix them up.
- 1.2 was released 5 Nov as **generally available**, the first GA Omni (blog.google). Model ID
  `gemini-omni-1.2-flash`.

### Flow's feature matrix for Omni 1.2 `[vendor]`, Flow help page 16352836

| Feature | Free | Plus / Pro / Ultra |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text → video | 3–10 s | **3–15 s** |
| Frames → video (first frame) | 3–10 s, web only | 3–15 s, web only |
| Ingredients / references | 3–10 s | 3–15 s |
| Video → video edit | 3–10 s | 3–15 s |
| Extend (Omni Extend) | no | 3–10 s per extend, up to 3 images, web only |
| Upscale | no | to 1080p (4K Ultra only) |

- Both aspect ratios everywhere (16:9, 9:16 only, as before).
- **Duration is a slider, not tabs.** 3–15 s in one-second steps. `[vendor]` Our uncommitted flow-mcp change
  (`packages/flow-mcp/src/compose.ts`) already notes a "Length SLIDER, 3–15 seconds" seen live.
- 🔴 **First + last frame is NOT in Flow's matrix for 1.2.** The page lists it for Veo 3.1 Lite and the
  retiring models only. **Conflict:** the API supports two-frame interpolation on 1.2, capped at 10 s
  (AI Studio docs `[vendor]`; Runware `[runware]`), and blog.google mentions interpolation. One creator ran
  "a first frame … and a last frame" on 1.2 **inside Flow**, 10 s, and said "10 is the cap when you use both
  frames" `[yt]`. So the UI may well have it and the help page may be behind. **Test it** (see §4). It decides
  where our start+end shots go.
- **Voices:** Omni models offer "custom voices": you pick a preset voice and describe how to style it. Some voice
  features are region-restricted. `[vendor]` The help page does not mention character references by name.
- **Resolution:** generation is at 360p or 720p, then upscaled. 360p costs a third as much as 720p. `[vendor]`
- **Veo 3.1 Lite after 5 Dec** `[vendor]`: text, first frame **and first+last frame** at 4/6/8 s; ingredients
  8 s only; no edit; no extend (Veo Extend is gone). 720p. Google calls it "a dependable, low-cost baseline".
  **It is the only Veo left, and the only model Flow's own page says does first+last frame after 5 Dec.**

- 🔴 **In Flow, 1.2 at 720p costs about twice what 1.1 did** (8 s: 24 against 12). The API price fell 30 %
  ($0.07/s at 720p `[vendor]`). Flow credits went the other way. Two sources measured the same thing in Flow:
  Adam Campbell `[yt]` ("10 seconds is 15 credits on 1.1 and 30 on 1.2") and Pragma-Code `[community]`.
- **1.2 edit is billed per second** (720p 3/s, so 24 for 8 s) instead of 1.1's flat 40. **Extend** costs 3/s at
  720p, 1/s at 360p.
- **Upscale to 1080p is free up to 10 s**, then 1 credit per second. 4K is 50 flat (Ultra) plus 5/s over 10 s.
- With a **video reference** attached, Flow locks the output to the reference clip's length (3 s). That makes 1.2
  the cheap option there: "Nine credits against 20" for 1.1. `[yt]`, one creator.
- Monthly allowances: Plus 200, Pro 1,000, Ultra $100 10,000, Ultra $200 25,000, plus 50 a day. No rollover.
  `[vendor]`
- ⚠️ **`platform-controls.md` §2 is now stale.** Its Omni row ("generation 15/20/25/30 for 4/6/8/10s", edit 40
  flat) is the pre-split Omni 1.1 table. That file was not edited by this sweep.

### API limits that bound what Flow can do `[vendor]`, AI Studio Omni docs

- Single-frame or text: 3–15 s, **default 10 s**. Two frames: 3–10 s, and over 10 s is an invalid-argument error.
- Extend: uses the **last 10 s** of the input, adds 3–10 s, up to **30 s total**, **append-only** (no prepend,
  no extending the middle).
- References: **up to 6 images, up to 3 per character, up to 2 characters**. Video refs ≤3 s: three of them
  for outputs up to 10 s, one for 11–15 s. Audio in a reference video is ignored. Video refs only on new
  generations, not on edit or extend.
- Runware `[runware]`: on the API, **frames cannot be combined with reference images or videos**; the request is
  rejected. (That matches our Flow tab rule: Frames holds staging, Ingredients holds identity.)
- **Audio upload / voice references: still unsupported in the API.** "Voice editing is not supported." An
  allowlist for audio references exists on Google Cloud (MyClaw `[community]`, unverified). Google's blog shows a
  one-second voice reference routing voices to four characters. **Conflict, as with 1.1:** the blog demo vs the
  API docs. Flow's own UI offers preset voices plus a description, not uploads.
- 🔴 **UK:** editing **or extending uploaded** videos is not available in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK.
  Editing or extending **model-generated** video is. Uploading images containing minors is also blocked in those
  regions. `[vendor]` So Omni Extend should work on clips Flow made, but not on footage we upload.
- English is "fully supported"; other languages "may work".

### Prompting changes Google documents for 1.2 `[vendor]`, AI Studio Omni docs

- 🔴 **`<FIRST_FRAME>` / control-string tags are "deprecated and unsupported in future models".** Our
  [`omni-flash.md` § The `<FIRST_FRAME>` tag](./omni-flash.md#the-first_frame-tag--a-documented-binding-mechanism-vendor)
  presents them as a binding mechanism. Several Bank Robbery clean-room briefs and `docs/stories/karen/prompts.md`
  still mention them. **Drop them from 1.2 prompts.**
- **Timecode syntax works:** `[0-5s] … [5-10s] …`. Runware and Segmind `[runware]` also say 1.2 follows explicitly
  ordered beats well.
- **"A single unbroken scene" / "No scene cuts"** is still the advice. This matches our `[confirmed 2026-10-29]`
  finding in the clean-room sweep.
- For edits: "Simple prompts work best", and you can add "Keep everything else the same."
- ⚠️ **Conflict on negatives.** Google's docs now say: write negatives into the prompt ("Do not do X").
  [`omni-flash.md` § Negatives do not work](./omni-flash.md#️-negatives-do-not-work-and-they-actively-backfire)
  has our 1.1-era finding that they backfire. Neither has been tested on 1.2. Keep the house practice of positive
  phrasing until an A/B says otherwise.

### Google's quality claims for 1.2 vs 1.1 `[vendor]`, blog.google 5 Nov

- Better reference following, held "from start to finish". Faces, garments, packaging type and brand colours stay
  stable across camera angles. "Less visual drift or morphing."
- "Clearer lip-sync" and "distinct multi-speaker conditioning".
- Complex moves (FPV, zoom-out, orbit) get "significantly fewer unwanted jump cuts, steadier scale".
- **Not improved, in Google's own words:** "fast sports action, rapid script changes and complex physics".
- The model card for 1.2 lists **no evals and no limitations of its own**. It points back to the original Omni
  Flash card. The press `[press]` (The Decoder, MarkTechPost) confirms that no benchmark numbers were published.
- ⚠️ A creator `[yt]` quoted Google as saying raters preferred 1.2 over 1.1 "about 62 % of the time for consistency
  and 63 % for keeping characters looking like themselves". **We could not find that figure in any Google page
  we read.** Treat it as unverified.

## 2. Opinion: what people who ran it say

**Leaderboard `[leaderboard]`:** there is no 1.2 entry on Artificial Analysis yet (searched 2026-11-09). The
latest Omni row we found is **Omni Flash 1.1 at Elo 1132**, 4th on AA image-to-video with audio (v1.1 board, Oct
2026). Our own 2026-08 A/B already showed that arena rank doesn't carry over to our dark single-source look
(`platform-controls.md` §1), so a 1.2 rank wouldn't settle anything for us either.

### Adam Campbell Cinema: 18 takes in Flow, 1.2 against 1.1, same prompt and start image, 720p `[yt]`

Single takes per shot, so these are anecdotes, but it is the only matched test in Flow:

- **Faces and emotion: both excellent.** A laugh turning to tears, with the tear landing at 6.5 s. 1.2 looks
  "a lot more like a real person: the skin, the way the light sits". 1.1 added an unprompted neck flex that he
  liked. He'd use either.
- **Start + end frame (10 s, in Flow): 1.2 won.** 1.2 walked the man from the driveway to the workbench. The room
  "rearranges itself" at the door because the two frames didn't agree, but it lands exactly on the last frame.
  **1.1 teleported him** ("one frame he's at the door, the next he's at the workbench").
- **Physics: neither usable.** A pickup's back "became the front" between frames. Sparks looked like fireworks.
  On 1.2 a marble column **appeared from nothing** so a thrown god had something to hit; 1.1 had it in frame
  already. Both switched car headlights on unasked. He gave car action to 1.1 "by a hair".
- **Camera orbit: neither moved the camera.** Asked for a half-circle round a woman, **the subject turned and the
  world stayed put**, on both models. This contradicts Google's camera claim for this kind of move, n=1.
- **Props swap hands:** a wrench went left to right, or became two wrenches, in 3 of 4 styles. **Fix that held:**
  the start image shows one prop in one named hand, and the prompt names that hand in every beat.
- **Two speakers, one clip:** "both voices on the right mouths". Styles (live action, anime, 3D, clay) each held
  their look for the full 10 s.
- His verdict: use 1.2 for "dialogue, close-ups, emotion, a character walking through a space, and stylised
  scenes"; keep it away from car chases and effects fights. 464 credits for 18 takes.

### Pragma-Code: a 49 s avatar promo made in Flow, 7 Nov `[community]`

- Face, image quality and voice similarity were "very good, better than with Omni 1.1". **Four takes from one start
  frame kept the room, the clothes and the light consistent.**
- 🔴 **Long takes break the script.** At 15 s one take added a sentence nobody wrote, and another dropped ten words
  and repeated the last sentence. The author's guess, from two tests: the model plans about 11 s of speech.
- Tips: keep a talking take at **≤10 s with 22–26 words** (measured in German, 2.7–3.1 words per second). Replace
  "speak until the last frame" with "spoken **exactly once**, then look calmly into the camera". Write numbers
  out in words.
- Flow output was 24 fps. Avatar takes downloaded at 1080p, cutaways only at 720p. (Odd; unexplained.)

### Others

- **Extrude Studio `[yt]`, video edit in Flow on 1.2** (their own uploaded 5 s clip, presumably outside the UK):
  a background swap worked, and the hands, rail and head turn were kept. But a butterfly's **timing** drifted, and
  an amber eye detail became an **unrequested glow**. Their advice: compare the same timestamps side by side, and
  save each edit turn as its own version.
- **MindStudio `[community]`**, one AI Studio dialogue test: lip-sync "held up across the clip", voices on the
  right characters. Their drone and try-on examples are Google's demos repeated.
- **Screenweaver `[community]`**: in stitched long scenes, drift shows at the end; compare the last 3 s of each clip
  with the first 3 s.
- **FlobiteAi `[yt]`** (Flow tutorial): a character reference plus 15 s worked; quotes 60 credits at 720p and 20 at
  360p for 15 s. Results "can vary".
- **Older evidence that may no longer hold:** the 1.1-era "lip-sync degrades after 6–7 s" (invideo, MindStudio
  `[community]`, already in `omni-flash.md`). Nobody has re-measured it on 1.2. Pragma's failure was *script*
  drift at 15 s, not mouth drift.

### What nobody has covered (gaps)

- **Dark, moody, single-source lighting:** nothing found for 1.2, either way.
- **Start-frame faithfulness on an illustrated or stylised plate:** only the four-style test above (styles held).
- **Camera-locked shots specifically:** nobody tested the "camera does not move" case, which is our default.
- **Veo 3.1 Lite vs Fast on start+end frames:** no practitioner comparison found. The only number is Google's
  model-card win rate for Lite over Fast on image-to-video, about 47 % (secondary source; already cited in
  `../flow/README.md` rule 5).
- Reddit threads: not reachable by our search tool.

## 3. Do 1.1 / Veo prompts need to change for 1.2?

Nobody has published a 1.1→1.2 prompt migration. MyClaw `[community]`, working from the docs only, says retest
locked pipelines rather than rewrite. What we can say:

| Change | Grade | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `<FIRST_FRAME>` / `<IMAGE_REF_N>` tags deprecated | `[vendor]` | Remove them |
| Duration is now 3–15 s, any second | `[vendor]` | flow-mcp's 4/6/8/10 assumptions need the slider (already in progress) |
| Two-frame prompts cap at 10 s | `[vendor]` | A start+end shot is never 15 s |
| Talking takes over ~10 s invent or drop lines | `[community]`, n=2 | Keep talking clips ≤10 s, script ~25 words, "exactly once" |
| Name a prop's hand in the image and in every beat | `[yt]`, n=1 | Cheap to adopt |
| Orbit / "camera circles" may turn the subject instead | `[yt]`, n=1 on both models | Keep camera moves in Premiere (our hybrid method already does this) |
| Negatives: Google now says "Do not do X" | `[vendor]` vs our 1.1 finding | Untested on 1.2; keep positive phrasing for now |
| Veo prompts moving to Omni | — | Veo-style long constraint blocks were already wrong for Omni (`omni-flash.md`: 3–4 sentences). A Veo Fast start+end prompt moved to **Veo 3.1 Lite** should run unchanged |
| The 1.1 speech-punctuation trap (colon → speech, quote marks → subtitle) | untested on 1.2 | Assume it still applies |

## 4. Test in Flow ourselves (the internet cannot settle these)

1. **Does Flow expose first+last frame on Omni 1.2?** The help page says no; a creator used it. Then: one of our
   own Veo Fast camera-locked start+end shots on **Veo 3.1 Lite** against **Omni 1.2** (if offered), same frames.
2. **Camera lock on 1.2:** do "locked-off camera, the camera does not move" shots hold on 1.2 as they did on 1.1?
   (Our default; nobody tested it.)
3. **The calibration protocol** (README): about 8 accepted Omni 1.1 start-frame prompts rerun unchanged on 1.2,
   including at least one dark, single-source plate and one illustrated plate.
4. **Lip-sync length:** one talking clip at 6, 10 and 15 s, measured with the `lip-sync-dub` skill. Does the 1.1
   6–7 s falloff still hold? Do 15 s takes invent lines?
5. **Cast Characters on 1.2:** does an attached Flow Character still hold identity, and do the preset voices plus
   a styling description replace what we did on 1.1?
6. **360p drafts:** at a third of the credits, is a 360p draft a fair predictor of the 720p take of the same prompt?
7. **Omni Extend on a generated clip, from the UK:** is it there, and does it hold identity over the seam?
   (Uploaded footage is region-blocked.)
8. **Negatives A/B on 1.2:** one shot, "Do not X" against the positive rewrite.
9. **Read the credit figure off the compose bar** for our plan before a batch. The tables above are Google's
   help page, and Google says the in-app number is the truth.
10. **Policy blocks:** do the Bank Robbery clean-room rewrites still pass on 1.2? A new model can mean new filters.

## Our own first test — 2026-11-09 `[observed, n=1 per shot]`

Four accepted shots re-run on **Omni 1.2 Flash** with the same start still, prompt and length, count 1, one fresh
Flow project each (`scripts/flow-tests/model-ab.mts` + `omni12-jobs.json`; clips in `D:\badcode-videos\model-tests\omni-1-2\`;
side-by-side page https://claude.ai/artifact/LAVzPiNXaC76hB1TGmgRaH). Judged from 2 fps contact sheets, not listened to.

| Shot | Old model | Verdict | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GPOM van past the bus shelter, 4 s | Veo 3.1 Fast | 1.2 better | Veo morphed the van into a different vehicle mid-pass; 1.2 kept it, then **cut on its own to a close-up** (an unrequested second shot) |
| Karen dial, illustrated, 8 s | Omni 1.1 | tie | Flat style, palette and light held on both |
| GPOM Derek apology, talking, 6 s | Omni 1.1 | tie on picture | Same beat; audio and lip-sync not yet judged |
| GPOM vault crowd + horn, 6 s | Omni 1.1 | 1.2 better | 1.1 swung the horn up (the very fault Kai noted); 1.2 kept it hanging and swaying, crowd more alive, no clones seen |

- 🔴 **Omni 1.2 can insert its own cut.** If a shot must be one continuous take, say so ("one continuous shot, no cuts") and check for it.
- **Mechanics:** 1.2's length is a **slider, 3–15 s** (`input[type=range][aria-label=Length]`, value = seconds − 3); a scripted
  value change does not reach Angular, the keyboard does (Home, then ArrowRight). The popover also offers **360p**. flow-mcp
  learned the slider on 2026-11-09 (`setLengthSlider`); a running MCP server needs a restart to pick it up.
- Each clip rendered in ~70 s.
