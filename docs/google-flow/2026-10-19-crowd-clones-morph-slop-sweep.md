came back with **clone faces** (the same young white lad in a beanie, the same bald older man, repeated), and an
Omni Frames clip where a man holding a phone was asked to "take a swig of tea" and **the phone morphed into the
mug**. It reports only what the [10-08](./2026-10-08-anti-slop-sweep.md), [10-09 dialogue and morph](./2026-10-09-dialogue-and-morph-sweep.md),
[10-09 crowd](./2026-10-09-crowd-vault-accent-sweep.md), [10-12](./2026-10-12-shouting-faces-sweep.md) and

crowd-individuation section, and `docs/flow/failure-modes.md` do not already hold.

**Nothing here was tested by us.** Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so re-open a page before
ruling on exact wording. Reddit returned no thread on either fault.

**Headline:** Google says nothing about crowds, clone faces or prop permanence on any page checked today. The one
genuinely new idea is from a September 2026 casting write-up: **repeated faces are a casting default summoned by
archetype words, and a shared reference makes them certain.** For the morph, the new material is a clean
statement that the start frame is fixed and the prompt only describes change. The rest is my inference.

## 1. Crowd clones

- **[practitioner]** Seedance blog, Emma Chen, 2026-09-17 (about MiniMax H3, not Nano Banana):
  <https://www.seedance.tv/blog/minimax-h3-keeps-generating-the-same-face>
  - The failure is a repeated **casting pattern**, not an exact copy. "Broad character prompts can repeatedly
    land on a familiar-looking face even when the seed changes." Prompts that "describe a role and aesthetic, not
    an individual" cause it; "Extra quality words do not add identity information."
  - Negative wording can strip the traits that tell people apart (wrinkles, asymmetry, unusual hairlines).
  - Fix: a compact identity line per person of **structural** traits (face shape, brow, nose bridge, jaw, age
    cues), not adjectives. Hair and wardrobe are weaker separators than bone structure.
  - "If every candidate uses the same portrait reference, repeated faces are expected." Lock a reference only
    after a face is chosen.
  - "must not resemble prior candidates" is an intention the model cannot act on; concrete traits do the work.
  - "Small faces in wide shots hide distinguishing features."
- **[practitioner]** Declining Academic (Substack, Midjourney protest images): generated protests skew young and
  racially uniform; "older people do not protest". <https://decliningacademic.substack.com/p/protest-is-in-the-ai-of-the-beholder>
  Adobe community users report the same default. **[inference]** "protester" is an archetype word; the lad in
  the beanie is likely what that word looks like to the model.
- **[practitioner]** Pruna docs: draw variety from deliberate lists (ages, ethnicities, jobs), warns against
  quotas. <https://docs.pruna.ai/en/stable/docs_pruna_endpoints/general/bias_and_diversity.html>
- **[practitioner]** Morphic, Omni 1.1 guide (updated August 2026): "Four or more tracked subjects merge or
  drift. Hold it to three." Video, not stills; agrees with the README's four-or-more row.
  <https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/gemini-omni-flash-1-1-guide>
- **[vendor]** Google Cloud, Nano Banana prompting guide, 2026-03-06: multi-reference formula is
  "[Reference images] + [Relationship instruction] + [New scenario]"; references serve "maintaining character
  consistency". Nothing on crowds or distinct faces.
  <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana>
- **[vendor]** Omni API meta-prompt: "Apply costume design principles to characters." <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni>

**Will the old still as a composition reference re-import the clones?** No source tests it. **[inference]** Very
likely yes: Nano Banana is built to hold up to four or five characters from references, the clone faces are the
largest human features in that still, and Seedance's "same reference, repeated faces" points the same way. Safer
options, in order: (a) no reference, the composition in words (camera position, barrier line, depth bands);
(b) a reference with the faces unreadable (a heavy blur or a crop to the barrier and street only) plus a
relationship line, "Use the reference only for the camera position and the barrier; every person is new". (b) is
untested and Google's "relationship instruction" has never been shown to exclude faces.

**[inference] Wording for a 15 to 25 person barrier still** (none tested):
1. Cast **five to seven near and mid-ground people individually**, one short clause each, each with a different
   head (bare grey hair, a hood up, a flat cap, wet braids, a bald crown, a woollen headscarf), a different coat,
   one thing in the hands, and one structural trait (long face, heavy jaw, broken nose, glasses). Lead with what
   they wear and carry, as `failure-modes.md` advises, and add age and ethnicity as plain description of a mixed
   English town crowd, not as a list of categories. Keep "protester", "activist", "young people" out.
2. Everyone behind them: a density word, backs and shoulders, rain-blurred, hoods and umbrellas covering faces.
   **Fewer readable faces is fewer chances to clone.**
3. One variety line: "No two people alike in age, height, build, hair or coat."
4. 2K. Make four and pick; check faces at 100 per cent before animating, since Omni carries the clones on.
5. No negative trait wording ("no wrinkles", "flawless skin") anywhere in the prompt or the style lock.

## 2. Prop morphing in Frames (the phone that became a mug)

- **[practitioner]** Runware, image-to-video primer (2026): "The image is not a reference the model consults for
  inspiration, it is the first frame of the result"; "Describing them differently does not override them"; "A
  prompt here is a description of change, not of content." Its stillness wording: "The cup, the counter and the
  cloth stay completely still, and the camera is locked off". <https://runware.ai/docs/learn/image-to-video>
- **[practitioner]** CapCut, AI video physics, 2026-08-16: "An object may move before the hand makes contact";
  "Use one main subject, one primary object, and simple staging"; "If a cutaway can communicate the idea, do not
  force a detailed hand-object interaction"; never approve a held-object clip "from a single attractive frame".
  <https://www.capcut.com/create/ai-video-physics-realism-whats-fixed-and-whats-broken>
- **[practitioner]** HackerNoon, Frank Houbre, 2026-07-22: "A hand holding an object generates far more reliably
  than an open hand mid-gesture"; props drift because the model has no memory of earlier frames; shorter
  segments hold better. <https://hackernoon.com/how-to-fix-warped-hands-and-faces-in-ai-generated-video>
- **[vendor]** Omni API meta-prompt: "Be very specific about the people, items and objects in the scene." Its
  edit example "Make the phone invisible. Keep everything else the same." shows Omni can remove a prop in an edit
  (an edit is 40 credits, per the 10-09 crowd sweep). <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni>

**[inference] Why the phone became the mug:** the mug was not in the hand, so "take a swig of tea" had no mug to
lift. The hand already held a phone-sized object at mouth-reachable height, and the cheapest path to "a drink in
the hand" was to repaint the object it had. The action asked for a prop that was not in the start frame.

**[inference] Rules (untested, built on the above and the README row):**
1. **Every prop the action uses is in the start frame, in the hand that uses it.** For a swig, the still has the
   mug in his hand, already near the chest. Never ask a clip to fetch a prop.
2. **One object per hand, and a hand that holds something does not start a new action.** The phone hand stays a
   phone hand; the drink comes from the other hand, or the phone is on the table in the still.
3. **Name every held object once with its owner and place**: "The phone stays in his left hand, screen down, by
   his hip. He lifts the white mug in his right hand and drinks." Not "his drink", not "it".
4. **No hand-offs** (phone to table, mug to lips) in one clip. Split it: a still-and-clip for each, or a cutaway.
5. **Make the clip's main action something other than the prop** where the prop only has to survive (README; 10-09).
6. If one shot needs both, consider an Omni edit removing the phone, or cut on the settled tail.

## 3. Slop avoidance: what is new

Most of the list in the brief is already in the repo (§0). Only these are new:

- **[practitioner]** Negative trait wording ("no blemishes", "perfect skin") removes the asymmetries that make
  faces individual (Seedance, above). Check style locks for it.
- **[practitioner]** Archetype and quality words summon a default face; describe a person by structure and a job.
  (Seedance; matches CapCut in the 10-12 sweep.)
- **[practitioner]** "Over-smooth, floaty, physics-defying" motion and frozen or mirrored expressions are named
  slop traits; a single still "can now be flawless", so judge clips in motion. <https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-slop-aesthetic-12-tells>
- **[practitioner]** Inspect full-resolution exports frame by frame; hand and prop errors last one or two frames
  (HackerNoon). Matches README rule 4 (four frames a second or more).
- **[vendor]** Omni: "Simple prompts work best for video editing. Overly descriptive prompts can lead to unintended
  changes." Also "Keep everything else the same" when editing one aspect.

## 7. What happened when we used it (observed 2026-10-19, The Bank Robbery cut 7; n=1 each, Nano Banana 2 + Omni 1.1 Flash)

- **Crowd clones: fixed first time, twice.** Five or six front-row people written one by one (clothes, what they carry, one
  face feature, plain age and ethnicity), "everyone further back is hooded, under umbrellas or turned away", no reference
  image. Both stills came back with every front-row face distinct, and no policy block on the demographic words. The faces
  stayed distinct through both Omni clips (Frames mode). Prompts: `scripts/bank-robbery/brs7-stills.json`.
- **🔴 A long cast list pushes the camera out of the prompt.** With the camera clause after the cast, the reply still went
  eye-level, straight-on and symmetrical, a line-up. Moving the camera clause to the very start (front-load, rule 3) got
  the high diagonal angle back, but the light then went flat afternoon. Saying "after dark" and naming one hard floodlight
  fixed it on round three. Write the camera first and the light concretely when the cast is long.
- **The swig: staging the prop fixed it (test 3 above, the first variant).** Mug already in both hands and phone already on the
  table in the start frame, and no drinking in the prompt: 8 s with no morph, read at 4 fps. Not tested: whether a drink
  would have survived with that start frame.
