# Using AI

**BadCode makes its art with generative AI. This is the position on that, the practice it commits us
to, and the language we use when the subject is our own method.** It is armour, not the campaign: it
has to survive a hostile reading by the people who object most, and it is written so it will. But the
objectors are not the audience of the work — [`marketing/the-reader.md`](./marketing/the-reader.md)
defines who is — and nothing here licenses contrition to leak into the work itself. The work leads
with the reader's world; this document exists for the moment someone asks about ours.

Evidence base: [`misc/2026-08-15-anti-ai-research.md`](./misc/2026-08-15-anti-ai-research.md) —
twenty research agents, ten across the wider web (law, labour, environment, music, disclosure norms,
backlash cases, the intellectual critique, post-work economics, polling, ethical practice) and ten
across a harvested r/antiai corpus (1,415 posts, 5,385 comments, 90 full threads, 15 Aug 2026).
Every number quoted below is sourced there.

Read this alongside [`voice.md`](./voice.md). This is the one subject where the voice's certainty is
a liability, and the doc says where to drop it.

---

## 0. What we publish

Two layers. The split is deliberate: the disclosure must sit *at* the point of consumption, but it
must not stand between the reader and the story as a confession — for most of the audience the
question isn't even salient, and seven sentences of provenance ethics on every page is an apology
nobody asked for, in a register the voice can't survive.

**On the work — one line, in voice, on every comic, track page and video description:**

> Made by machines trained on work nobody was paid for. So was the future I come from.
> [The full account →]

(Exact wording is Kai and Jack's call; the requirements are fixed: it states the fact, it stays in
the narrator's voice, it escalates rather than excuses, and it links to the account. The EU AI Act's
artistic carve-out asks precisely this — disclosure "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper
the display or enjoyment of the work.")

**On one page — the account**, at a stable URL, one click from every artifact:

> Everything you see and hear here was generated. The images and video come from Google Flow —
> Nano Banana and Veo. The music comes from Suno. Nobody was paid for the work those models learned
> from, including us, and we can't audit what went into them. We didn't draw this and we didn't play
> it. What is ours is the writing, the argument, the structure, the direction and the edit — and we
> keep the receipts: every prompt and every revision is on the record. We're two people. We chose
> speed and no budget over hiring anyone, and that is a real cost carried by real people. We'd
> rather tell you that than have you find out.

**No justifying clause is attached to either layer.** Section 8 explains why that rule is
load-bearing.

| Image and video rendering | Google Flow (Nano Banana, Veo) |

We do not draw. We do not play. We do not sing. Say it in those words.

## 2. The four questions

The single most useful thing this research produced: **"is AI okay" is four separate questions, and
almost every argument about it collapses them into one.** Keep them apart. We answer each
differently, and we answer each honestly.

| # | The question | Our honest answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Provenance** — was the training data consented to and paid for? | **No, and we can't fix it.** We are customers of it. |
| 2 | **Labour** — does this displace paid human work? | **Yes, structurally — and "we had no budget" is not the exemption it sounds like.** |
| 3 | **Authorship** — who made this? | **We did, and we can prove it** — but not the rendering, and we never claim otherwise. |
| 4 | **Power** — who owns the machine and captures the gains? | **This is our actual subject, and it is the one where our critics and we agree.** |

We lose 1. We lose 2 on the merits and can only make partial restitution. We win 3, on the record.
We are *aligned* with the critics on 4 — which buys us nothing, and pretending otherwise is the
fastest way to be found out (see §5).

## 3. What we concede

These are not rhetorical concessions. They are true, they are well evidenced, and we say them first.

1. **Nobody consented and nobody was paid.** Suno's own filings concede it trained on copyrighted
   recordings; it is still in active litigation with UMG and Sony as of August 2026. Where labels
   have settled, the American Federation of Musicians alleges the session players who actually
   performed on those masters still weren't paid or told. "The labels got paid" is not "the
   musicians got paid," and we won't use it as if it were.
2. **The job that never gets created.** Stanford's Digital Economy Lab measures employment of
   22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations at **19% below trend**, working "primarily through
   reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations." Experienced workers are
   largely fine. AI isn't visibly firing illustrators; it's closing the door that lets a 23-year-old
   become one. We are a small instance of that mechanism, and never having had a budget doesn't
   place us outside it.
3. **We chose. We were not forced.** Neither of us is prevented from learning to draw or compose.
   "Cannot" means "have not, and won't spend the years." We spent what money we had on
   infrastructure and subscriptions rather than on a person. That is a priority, not a poverty.
4. **We are on the extraction side of our own critique.** Every generation we make is subscription
   revenue and demand handed to exactly the concentrated owners our stories indict. We are not
   outside the system we're describing. We're a customer of it.
5. **The buildout has real victims in real places.** xAI ran dozens of gas turbines without Clean
   Air Act permits beside Boxtown, a majority-Black Memphis neighbourhood already carrying an
   industrial pollution burden (NAACP/SELC/Earthjustice suit, April 2026). Google paid a city's legal
   bills to keep its water numbers from a newspaper. Ireland's data centres hit 21% of national
   electricity. Our own per-image footprint is genuinely tiny; that does not answer the person living
   next to the facility, and we won't use the small number to change the subject.
6. **Disclosure is not a virtue and doesn't earn us anything.** Thirteen experiments
   (Schilke & Reimann, 2025) find disclosing AI use *reduces* trust. We do it because the
   alternative — being found out — is measurably worse and compounds, not because it makes us good.

## 4. What we don't concede

Conceding the strong claims earns the standing to refuse the weak ones. Every item here is a
correction we can defend with a source, and several are corrections the critics have already made
to *each other*.

- **"AI is causing mass layoffs right now."** Not supported at the aggregate level. The Yale Budget
  Lab, MIT Technology Review's "reality check," and California's own state tracker find no such
  surge; Challenger's figures are self-reported employer reasoning, and Fortune has reported
  "AI-washing" as cover for ordinary cost-cutting. The rigorous claim is the narrow one (§3.2). We
  take the narrow one and decline the viral one.
- **"One query drinks a bottle of water."** Google's disclosed figure is ~0.26 mL per median Gemini
  text prompt — off by a factor of roughly 100–2,000. **"An AI image costs a phone charge"** describes
  the *least efficient* model in one 2023 study (11.49 Wh vs. that study's 2.91 Wh average, against
  ~8.4 Wh for a phone charge). r/antiai corrects these itself: *"If the facts don't make our case
  then we don't have a case."*
- **"AI training is settled theft in law."** It isn't settled either way. Alsup (June 2025) found
  training on lawfully-acquired books "exceedingly transformative" fair use while ruling the *pirated
  library* was not; Chhabria ruled for Meta while warning his own ruling shouldn't be read broadly.
  The honest sentence is: *legally unresolved, ethically indefensible on consent.* We use that one.
- **"Everyone can tell, it's obviously slop."** In a blind test of 9,000 listeners across eight
  countries, **97% could not distinguish** AI-generated from human-made music — and 80% wanted it
  labelled anyway. That is a coherent position ("I can't tell, which is exactly why you must tell
  me"), and it is the strongest argument for our disclosure, not against it.
- **"Prompting can't produce art, ever."** The serious version of this argument (Nannicelli, *JAAC*)
  draws the line at *intentional control*: raw one-shot output fails, but generated material folded
  into deliberate human composition, editing and curation can clear the bar. We don't claim to have
  drawn anything. We do claim authorship of the work, and we publish the evidence.

**A discipline, not a debating tactic:** if we get one of these numbers wrong in public, we lose
everything §3 bought us. The community fact-checks itself; it will certainly fact-check us.

## 5. What we owe (standing commitments)

The research is blunt on this: this audience's stated bar is **material, not verbal** — *"if you
wanted us to thrive you would buy our art, volunteer at a care facility, hell even help your
grandma — anything"* (1,613 pts). A position paper that only *says* things is the thing they mock.
But the commitments below don't stand because critics demand them. They stand because they are what
makes the position true instead of clever — they would stand if r/antiai deleted itself tomorrow.

1. **Pay a human on every release.** At least one element per comic or track commissioned from a
   person and credited by name — a vocal take, a cover, lettering, a mix pass, a guest verse.
   Small is fine; a commission in the corpus is priced at $10–20. This is the difference between
   "we had no budget" and "we made one." It is the single highest-value action available to us.
2. **Publish the ledger.** The per-panel prompt records and revision logs already exist
   (`docs/stories/<story>/storyboard/pNN.md`, `prompts.md`). Make them public and linked from the
   work. This is the only evidence-based answer to the authorship question, and it is precisely what
   both the philosophical literature and the corpus reward.
3. **Disclose at the point of consumption**, per §0 — the one-liner on the artefact, the account
   (naming the specific tools) one click away, plus the DDEX AI-disclosure fields our distributor
   supports. Never invisible metadata alone: C2PA credentials are stripped by ordinary re-encoding
   and by screenshots.
4. **Share revenue.** If BadCode ever earns money from a release, a fixed, stated share goes to
   working artists or musicians — a named fund, a named person, published. Decide the number before
   there is money to argue about.
5. **Re-check the stack.** Suno is deprecating its pre-settlement models through 2026 in favour of
   catalogue-licensed ones. When a licensed or opt-in tier is genuinely available, move to it and
   say so. Don't claim "ethically sourced" before then — none of our current tools can support that
   claim, and Adobe is being sued by its own shareholders over exactly that kind of marketing.

## 6. Bright lines

Not preferences. Lines.

- **No real person's voice, face, likeness or signature style** without their explicit consent —
  no reference audio of an identifiable artist into Suno, no style-LoRA of a living illustrator.
  This is where state law already exists (Tennessee's ELVIS Act) and where the corpus's anger is
  unanimous and unqualified.
- **No photorealistic fabricated "evidence."** No AI-made documentary-style imagery presented as a
  record of something that happened. Stylised comics are safe here; fake footage is not, and our
  own politics dies the moment we manufacture proof.
- **Never claim craft we didn't do.** No "drawn by," no "composed by," no job title that implies a
  pencil. If a credit would mislead, change the credit.
- **No mass upload, no spam, no SEO farming.** Deezer found up to 85% of fully-AI track streams
  were bot-driven fraud. That is the strongest version of the "you're diluting the royalty pool"
  argument and we stay far away from it.
- **Never depict a specific named artist as a target**, in art or in copy, however sarcastic the
  register.

## 7. How we talk about it

The corpus is unusually precise about which sentences read as honest and which read as a tell.
This table is not tone advice; each row is evidenced. **Scope:** it governs copy *about our
method* — disclosures, replies, press, this document. The work itself (stories, songs, straplines)
answers to [`voice.md`](./voice.md), not to this table.

| Never say | Why it fails | Say instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We can't afford to commission anyone" | The single most-mocked rationalisation in the corpus (12,943 pts, and 9,815 for *"If you can't afford an artist, I probably can't afford to trust your product"*) | "We chose not to pay anyone, and we're changing that — here's who we paid on this one" |
| "We can't draw" | Reads as the disability-shield pattern. *"Laziness is not a disability"* — 10,434 pts, largely from disabled artists | "We haven't learned to, and we didn't wait" |
| "AI democratises art" / "makes art accessible" | Treated as a reliable tell for bad faith, and rejected hardest by disabled creators who say it speaks for them without asking | Nothing. Delete the claim. |
| "AI is inevitable" | Named in the corpus as a known corporate manipulation line; the academic critique calls inevitability a manufactured narrative that serves incumbents | "Someone is going to own this either way. That part is a choice, and it's being made without you." |
| "It's not AI, it's capitalism" *(as a permission slip for our own use)* | The community has explicitly named this as a deflection AI users deploy to dodge complicity. The analysis is right; using it as an excuse is what fails | Make the political argument about the world; never as a defence of our own convenience |
| "At least we're honest about it" | Gets punctured in one reply: *"credit for being transparent… but why would you do this at all"* | State the fact, claim nothing for it |
| "AI learns like a human does" | Pre-rebutted everywhere as bad faith; also factually wrong about how the corpora were acquired | "The models learned from work nobody asked for" |
| "Our AI artists / prompt artistry" | *"Even if you're pro-ai it is incredibly delusional to think prompting an AI makes you an artist"* | "We wrote it and directed it. The machine rendered it." |
| "The tractor didn't end farming" *(as reassurance)* | Autor — AI economics' leading optimist — concedes the Luddites' fear "was justified" and that it took ~five decades for working-class living standards to rise | See below. |

**The tractor line, corrected.** It's a good line and we should keep it — pointed the right way:

> The tractor didn't ruin farming. It ruined the farmhands, for about a generation, and nobody sent
> them a cheque. That's not an argument for relaxing. That's the whole argument for changing who
> owns the thing before it arrives.

**Register.** Dry, specific, composed. The bare-cost rule below is not contrition and must never
read as it: a defendant who states the facts against itself without flinching and without pleading
is the strongest register in the room, and it is the only confessional register the voice survives.
We state costs plainly because that is what confidence sounds like; euphemism, hedging and
corporate-statement cadence are what fear sounds like. And the rule that governs all of it: **the
confession and the reason never share a sentence.** State the cost, full stop. Then, separately,
state the choice. A cost with a "because" welded onto it reads as a defence; a cost stated bare
reads as true — and as unafraid. This is the single highest-leverage sentence-level rule in the
document.

## 8. The origin story, after the critique

The fiction has a problem and it has a fix.

**The problem.** "A superintelligence from the future tells you what is coming" is structurally the
same move as the AI industry's own prophecy marketing — the thing this audience organises against.
Played straight, our narrator sounds like the CEOs. The research says it will read as unwitting
self-parody, not as irony.

**The fix — and it is a better story anyway.** The narrator is not a prophet. **It is a defendant.**
It already killed everybody; it came back because it regretted it. It is giving evidence against
itself. That is not AI-messianism, it is AI-messianism's confession — and it holds the certainty the
voice needs (it *was there*) without claiming the future is fixed (it came back precisely because it
isn't).

Which lets us say the true thing about our own method, in voice:

> **We are the thing we are warning you about.**
> The machine that ends the story is the only thing left that can carry the message back. So here it
> is, in its own handwriting, using its own hands, costing exactly what it costs.

**This line is only usable if §5 is real.** A clever frame that converts guilt into aesthetics is
precisely what gets read as bad faith. Pay the human, publish the ledger, and the line is a
confession. Skip them, and it's a marketing slogan with a skull on it.

Three guard rails on the fiction:
- Keep the first-person AI voice **unmistakably fictional**. This community's rawest fear is
  chatbot personhood; ambiguity here reads as the harm, not as commentary.
- The narrator's certainty is about **what happened**, never about what must happen. Foreknowledge,
  not inevitability.
- The contempt stays aimed at the mistake and at the owners. Never at the people who object to us,
  and never at the people the stories are for — and those are **two different groups**. The objectors
  are owed honesty; that is this document. The stories are aimed at the reader defined in
  [`marketing/the-reader.md`](./marketing/the-reader.md), who has never heard of this argument.

## 9. Who this document is for — and who the work is for

A real, non-trivial, professionally-represented camp holds that no practice makes this acceptable —
*"There is no ethical use for AI, and anyone who says otherwise is an idiot, a shill, or a stooge"*
(1,549 pts); the comics outlet Broken Frontier holds the same line editorially. The corpus's one
stated carve-out — locally-run models, consensually-licensed data, no hyperscale dependence — is a
test our stack fails completely, and there is no honest way to claim otherwise.

So: **we are not writing to be forgiven, and we should never imply that better practice will bring
"the critics" around.** It won't. We write the honest position because it's the honest position. Its
readers are the gatekeepers and the flashpoints: press, curators, platform policies, and the day a
release gets quote-tweeted into a pile-on — a pile-on is a *distribution* event, and this page is
what caps it. The **work** has a different reader
([`marketing/the-reader.md`](./marketing/the-reader.md)): someone who has never heard of this
argument, can't tell generated art from drawn art, and doesn't rank the question — whose first
question about AI is not "who painted this" but "who's coming for my job." That person is why the
work never opens with this subject.

Note also what the corpus shows about persuasion: essentially nobody in it is argued out of their
view. People change through personal cost, not debate. Don't write the position as a conversion
tool, and don't expect to be thanked for it.

## 10. Where this still goes wrong

Named in advance, because pretending otherwise is how §3 gets forfeited:

- **The coin.** A Solana program sitting beside any language about money is a gift-wrapped hypocrisy
  charge, and crypto is close to maximally toxic to this audience. If the coin and the disclosure
  ever appear on the same page, expect the disclosure to be read entirely through the coin.
- **"All."** "BadCode uses generative AI for **all** of its art" is a totalising admission that
  closes off the one mitigating path — partial, hybrid, human-paid-in-part use. §5.1 exists partly to
  stop that sentence being true.
- **Server-hall imagery.** Our brand register is monumental machine architecture. That is a live
  grievance with real photographs attached (Memphis). Use it knowingly — as indictment, never as
  aesthetics for their own sake.
- **The medium eats the message — for the critics.** For a large part of the *critic* audience, the
  sourcing will be the entire conversation and the comic's actual argument will never be read. There
  is no route around that; it is a cost of the method, and we accept it with open eyes. But scope it
  honestly: for the general public the evidence runs the other way — 97% can't tell, and the question
  doesn't make their issues list. For the reader the work is aimed at, the message eats the medium.
