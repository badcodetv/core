## Position

Your mum's hip is one of 7.4 million waits for planned treatment in England, up from 7.1 million in March, the month the target was hit. Hitting a date is not fixing a queue, so we answer to the median wait, published monthly: 12.2 weeks in August.

NHS England has told the health service to adopt AI note-takers that listen to your appointment. Nobody has published whether they buy you more of the doctor, or the hospital fewer doctors.

Tried on a ward first. Most note-takers fall outside today's checks on medical software. We make a ward trial compulsory before the whole NHS buys anything. Hospitals volunteer, the order is drawn by lot, and nobody gets less care meanwhile. Fails if, after a year, it adds under a twentieth to time with patients, safety slips, or a group named in advance does worse. Then it comes out. A named clinical lead signs each tool.

The time goes to the bed. Every hour a machine saves is counted, ward by ward, each quarter. If under half turns up as patient time or shorter waits, the contract isn't renewed.

A name on every decision. If software helped decide your triage, diagnosis, referral or discharge, you are told, a named clinician answers for it, and you reach a person the same working day. Today the law only covers decisions no human touched. This right never stops: if fewer than 9 in 10 reach their person on time, the Health Secretary explains why to MPs within 30 days.

A sensor can tell you she fell. It can't pick her up. No council swaps her visit for a screen without her say-so. Volunteer councils buy care by the hour, not the task; it stops if hospital admissions don't drop by one in ten. Full care funding: Not ready, until the Casey Commission reports next summer.

If a company profits from NHS records, the NHS gets a cut. No sale.

Each trial stops on its date unless Parliament votes to keep it, on the published review. Passes, and it goes national.

Left out, and counted apart: GP practices too stretched to volunteer, people paying for their own care, five million unpaid carers.

Bed 4 first, then the press release. Your hospital board chooses that order this year.
