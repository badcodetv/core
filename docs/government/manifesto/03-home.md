## Position

A village in North Yorkshire has learned the Home Office may put up to 1,200 asylum seekers in the old RAF base down the road. Nobody asked it. Who decided, who pays, and where's the extra GP?

Migration has a cost, and it lands at the bottom. The government's own advisers found the lowest-paid lost a few per cent of their pay to EU migration over 25 years, while the best-paid gained about the same. We print the size with every official release, beside the homes built.

So the roof comes with the arrival. Work visas and their families don't grow faster than the homes, GP and school places funded for them, paid for first from what the closing asylum hotels cost. Where the homes aren't built, the number falls. Refugees aren't capped; their arrival brings the roof money.

The boats. Nobody can promise the Channel's weather. We promise every asylum claim a first decision within six months. Fails if more claims are waiting for a decision or an appeal a year from now. Leaving the human rights convention isn't our fix; it's a judgement call. If our review finds the convention, not the queue, stops returns, we'll publish that.

Nobody gets hired to undercut you. We point the Fair Work Agency at the worst trades. The boss pays the back wages, then the fine.

A scam is a crime with a bill. Banks refund most victims; the platform that sold the advert and the network that carried the call pay nothing. Under us they share it, fake investment adverts first. Fails if those losses don't fall against trend within 18 months. Passes, and it covers every scam.

Police cameras. On 4 November the Home Office promised a regulator, bias tests and £11 million to triple the live facial recognition vans. Right rules, wrong order. The new vans go first to volunteer forces drawn by lot, against forces like them. Fails if, in a year, they solve no more crime, or more than one stop in twenty is the wrong person. A match is a lead, not a fact about you: an officer decides, and says so, at the stop. No machine uses force on a person here. Ever.

The test. The roof money goes first to volunteer councils, drawn by lot. Fails if their GP and housing waits aren't a tenth shorter than councils like them within three years, or the money isn't there first by eighteen months. Each trial stops on its date unless Parliament votes to keep it, on the published review. Passes, and it goes national.

Who it leaves out: people already here on a route that changes, counted on their own line. Nothing is retrospective.

Yes, it's a cost. Whitehall sets it alone today. Under us, MPs vote on it alone, once a year, in public.
