## Position

A data centre wants as much power as a town, and ministers have decided it should pay less than you do: up to £24 off every megawatt-hour in the AI zones, from 2027. Brilliant machine. Wrong queue.

Your cap rose 4% this autumn, to £1,723, and about £300 of that is standing charge, paid before the kettle boils.

Households first. In a shortage, homes and the factories already here keep their power; big new loads switch down first and pay for their own wires. Ministers say your bill doesn't fund that discount; we make them prove it, yearly. One region first, drawn by lot. Fails if, in three years, no big project there signs up, or household network charges rise faster than the national average.

The hosepipe ban applies to the data centre. The host town writes the deal, not the company: local ownership of the new power, water use published, the campus cut first in a drought. Fails if developers quit deal towns at twice the usual rate.

Cut the standing charge, prepayment first. On 4 November ministers said they'd scrap the daily gas standing charge through the Energy Independence Bill; about six gas customers in ten save, on average £45 a year, they say. Good. The electricity one stays. A levy on the windfall wind, nuclear and biomass stations make while gas sets the price already exists, ending March 2028. The Chancellor's Windfall Act renews it, raises it while war keeps gas dear, and puts every pound on the electricity standing charge. Fails if prepayment bills don't show the cut within six months.

Own what can't compete. One set of wires reaches your door; with no rival, the market is a toll booth. One network region goes public first, priced by an independent valuer. Fails if, after five years, its bills or power cuts are worse than regions like it. A bust water company stays public: our judgement call, reviewed in five years.

Each stops on its date unless Parliament votes to keep it, on the published review. Passes, and it goes national.

Net zero stays, security first: home-grown power is the one kind nobody can blockade. Bus fare caps get the same treatment: fails if journeys aren't 5% above trend by the end of 2027.

Who it leaves out: homes on heating oil and prepayment meters, who pay most and get counted least, and the four gas customers in ten the government doesn't expect to save. Counted separately, and first.

Ministers chose that discount this year. Your MPs choose whether it stays.
