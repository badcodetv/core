## Position

Picture the grid as a bath. Households fill it, factories fill it, and now a new tap the size of a town wants plumbing in. Drax, Brookfield and Radiant propose an AI data centre at Drax's power station in North Yorkshire: over £20 billion, eventually more than a gigawatt. Brilliant engineering. Now, who pays for the pipe?

That's the whole question, and it starts with the bill on your mat. Energy prices tied to the Middle East conflict are a main driver of inflation, now 3.5%. About a fifth of the world's oil normally sails through one strait, and this month there were missiles over it. The wind doesn't. Home-grown power is the only kind nobody can blockade.

Two commitments.

1. Big new loads pay their own way. A data centre that wants a gigawatt pays the full cost of its connection, and publishes what it pays per unit next to what a household pays. Test: Drax is the first case, if it gets planning. It fails if local household bills rise because of the connection. Then the deal changes, in public.
2. Where there is no real competition, the public owns the pipe. Wires and water have no rival supplier, so "the market" there is a toll booth. Test: one network region in public hands first, with bills and reliability measured against the rest. If it does worse, we say so.

Who it leaves out: households on prepayment meters and off the gas grid, who pay most and get counted least. They come first in every figure we publish.
