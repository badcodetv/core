## Position

No machine decides who dies. I have seen where that capability ends. It is not a debate here.

Risk register, current:

- Threat: AI-powered malware in a ransomware attack on UK critical national infrastructure. Likelihood: the National Cyber Security Centre is reported to call it highly likely by 2028. Consequence: power, water and public services stop for people who did nothing but switch on a tap.
- Threat: an enemy that targets energy. Overnight on 4 to 5 November Russia launched 128 drones and two missiles at Ukraine, and energy infrastructure in Odesa region was hit. Grids are front lines now.
- Common factor: single points of failure. One system everyone depends on and nobody can run by hand.

Mitigations, each with its test:

1. Human sign-off on every lethal decision, in law. Test: every weapon system the UK buys names, in public, the post that authorises it to fire. If any system can't name one, it isn't bought.
2. Run it by hand. Operators of critical infrastructure prove they can keep essential service going without their main computer systems. Trial: a small number of water and power operators run a published drill first. Fails if service can't be kept running; then the postmortem is published and the fix funded before the next one.

Who it leaves out: the small supplier and the local council with no cyber team, who sit inside the chain and outside the drill. They get counted next.
