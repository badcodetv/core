## Position

No machine decides who dies. It is not a debate here.

Threat: in 2024 a cyberattack sent London hospitals' blood tests back to paper. The head of the National Cyber Security Centre calls it highly likely that by 2028 attackers will use AI to break into the old systems running your power and water. In October, France says, Iran's Revolutionary Guard hacked the fridges in more than 700 French shops: food spoiled, fires started. US agencies warn the attacks are live; UK supermarket units were reportedly hit. Budget: 3% of national income by 2030, spent on kit, not on keeping the lights on.

The rule. No UK weapon selects a person to attack. A named officer answers for every shot, the way a soldier always has. Incoming missiles and drones are fair game for a fast machine. People are not. Today the UK's rule is a phrase in a policy paper. We make it law, a right with no stop date. A yearly audit publishes who authorised each shot and how long they had. One shot approved too fast to be a decision, and the minister answers to Parliament within 30 days.

Run it by hand. Water, power, hospital labs, payments, ports and the cold stores that keep the food prove in a published drill that the essentials hold with the main systems off. Whitehall's own 2027 exercise, Albiston Shadow, the largest home defence drill in decades, tests the War Book. Ours tests the pump room. Volunteers first, drawn by lot. Fails if, by year two, under three drills in four hold, or a failure isn't fixed before the next. It stops on its date, five years in, unless Parliament votes to keep it. Passes, and it goes national. Paid a dividend and ran no fallback? The dividend pays, not your bill. Our share comes from NATO's resilience pledge, already made.

Fixable here. Nothing bought that we can't stop, mend or restart without a foreign supplier's permission; the repairs go to defence towns. Fails if it adds 10% to the price without faster repairs.

Who got the contract. Every big contract, its rivals and its stop point go in the public Ledger. A pound lost to a late contract is a pound not spent on the people who serve. We print both.

Who it leaves out: the small supplier, the council with no cyber team, the veteran still waiting for help, anyone with no cash when the card machines go down. Each counted separately.

Who decides who answers for a shot today: a policy paper. Who will: Parliament, and an officer with a name.
