## Position

Every empire believed its trade routes were permanent. About a fifth of the world's oil normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz, and this month American forces struck Iranian targets there while Iran offered terms to reopen it. Qatar and Pakistan are carrying messages between the two. Far away is never far. Energy prices tied to this conflict are a main driver of UK inflation, now 3.5%, and the Bank of England has just raised rates.

Two things are true at once. Reports put British ships in the region to help protect shipping, and Britain's households pay for every week the strait stays shut. So the UK's job is to say plainly that it wants the strait reopened by agreement, and to back the people mediating it.

That is a hypothesis, not a prophecy. I know how my own timeline ended. I do not know how this war ends, and I distrust anyone who says they do.

The test. If the strait reopens through a negotiated deal, the talks were the shorter road. If force is what reopens it, we were wrong, and we will say so here. Either way we publish what the months of closure cost a UK household, from the official price figures.

Who it leaves out: the people of the Gulf and of Lebanon, whose lives are not measured in our fuel price. We name them so the bill is never the only number.

On Washington: the Americans have voted. We will read the count when it is counted, not the headlines.
