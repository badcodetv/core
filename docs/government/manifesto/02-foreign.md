## Position

Since February a war has shut the Strait of Hormuz on and off, and you've paid at the pump and on the heating bill. Nobody here started that war. Somebody here did decide, one cheap contract at a time, to hang what we can't do without on one route, supplier or foreign server, with no plan B.

The rule. Nothing vital depends on one supplier, route or government abroad, friend or rival, unless someone here owns it and has a second way to run it. We already do this for oil, with emergency stocks. Next, the computers hospitals and payments run on.

The switch list. Within a year, the ten foreign dependencies we'd miss most, each with a named owner and a drill: switch the foreign system off on purpose and see what still works. Fails if under three in four drills keep the service running by year two, or a failure goes unfixed. Passes, and it goes national. It stops with Defence's drills, five years in, unless Parliament votes to keep it. Paid once, from the NATO resilience money Defence holds.

A receipt for every big decision. Within thirty days of a sanction, deal or deployment: what it adds to the shop and the bills, and who carries it, checked a year on. Fails if most are out by more than half after two years. Same stop date, same vote. Some things are worth the price; you should know it.

Trade. Parliament can only delay a trade treaty today. We'd give it a vote on the mandate and on the whole deal, and an independent receipt per chapter for food prices and jobs. A judgement call, reviewed yearly.

No machine decides who dies. No UK weapon selects a person to attack. Incoming missiles and drones are fair game for a fast machine. People are not. Over seventy countries want a treaty saying so; Britain calls itself unconvinced. We'd sign, and keep machines out of nuclear decisions. It's a right, so it never stops.

Who it leaves out: small firms with one supplier, and the family on a prepayment meter. Numbers provisional. Status: draft.

I don't know how this war ends, and I distrust anyone who says they do. I do know who answers when the next route closes: the owner on the list, and me.
