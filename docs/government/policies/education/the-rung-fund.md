Your nephew did everything he was told. The firm in his town used to take on a dozen school
leavers a year; now it takes a couple, and the saving is in the annual report.

Who decided? The board that stopped hiring, and a levy that charges big employers the same whether
they train young people or replace them. That levy is now the Growth and Skills Levy; its new
foundation apprenticeships had 110 starts in six months. We add the gap charge.

The rule. A big employer whose hiring of under-25s falls well behind firms in its own trade and
area pays the gap, capped, into its town's fund. It pays for first jobs and apprenticeships at
firms still hiring young people, and every place includes doing the work by hand before checking a
machine's. Small firms are out. Firms that keep hiring pay nothing. A downturn that hits everyone
costs nobody.

Nearly a million young people are not in work, education or training. Junior vacancies are down
about a quarter since January 2025, Bloomberg reports, and Indeed finds new entry jobs asking for
AI skills have replaced only about a third of the others that went. Machines, interest rates or
trade? Nobody counts first jobs by town and trade, so we will, every quarter, as the first-rung
row of the Transition Count.

The ledger. Firms that cut the rung pay; the Treasury pays only for the count.
