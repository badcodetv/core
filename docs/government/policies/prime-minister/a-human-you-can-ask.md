Since February 2026 the law lets a machine's decision stand if you're told and can ask for a human.
If a person rubber-stamped it, you get nothing.

Who decided. Parliament, in the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, which replaced Article 22 of UK
GDPR with Articles 22A to 22D. Their safeguards cover only decisions made *solely* by a machine; a
decision a machine helped with, with a person involved, carries none of them.

The rule. We close that: you're told, a named official signs, a person hears your appeal by a
deadline. One framework statute builds on Articles 22A to 22D and covers any decision about a
person a machine helped make. Each sector's deadline sits in a schedule: benefits 4 weeks, clinical
the same working day, tenancy 10 working days, policing at the stop. Special category data, health
included, keeps the stricter rule it has today.

Rights don't expire; we measure them.

The ledger. The departments that use machines on people pay for the people who answer the appeals;
the person on the other end of the decision gains a name and a deadline.
