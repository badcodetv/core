The rules that will set your wage, your bill and your benefit for a generation are being written
now, by ministers and boards with a machine's help. We'd make every one publish, before the vote, the
number that would prove it wrong. Even where the state spends, it seldom plans to check: a 2019
review, reported by the National Audit Office in 2021, found only 8% of £432bn of major-project
spending had robust evaluation plans. That's projects, not laws, and we use it for nothing more.

Who decided. Governments of every colour, by passing laws with no test, no stop date and no one
counting.

The rule. Nobody's benefit is lowered before the review into it comes back. Every new bill
publishes its test, the number that would prove it wrong and a stop date before MPs debate it.
Trials go where towns volunteer; nobody gets less than today. It stops on its date unless
Parliament votes to keep it. Passes, and it goes national.

Every number here is provisional until its trial starts. We speak for the UK on money, defence,
foreign policy, work and testing laws; for England on what Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run
themselves, and we ask them to run the same tests.

The ledger. Small: the cost of writing the test down before the vote, against the cost of finding
out after.

Agree with one chapter and not the rest? Fine. Each can be voted on alone. Other parties are welcome to print the number that would prove them wrong. We'll wait.
