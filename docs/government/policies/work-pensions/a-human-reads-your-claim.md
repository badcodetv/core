judgement.principle: Any benefit decision that stops, lowers or sanctions you names who signed it, says if a machine helped, and gets a human review within four weeks.
judgement.review: every quarter, on the published share of reviews done within four weeks
judgement.change_mind: The four-week review turns out slower, or less likely to put a wrong award right, than today's mandatory reconsideration (a median 45 days, 26% changing the award, July 2026).
stop: The right never stops. The four-week deadline stops on its date unless Parliament votes to keep it, on the published review.
leaves_out: New claimants refused at the start; anyone whose award is too low but not lowered

The letter comes from a system too. Of the PIP decisions taken to a tribunal hearing, the
department loses two in three, about ten months later. That's a long wait to be told you were
right.

The fraud check stays. Nobody's money stops until a person signs.

This is the benefits deadline in the Prime Minister's right to a human you can ask.
