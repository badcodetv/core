Since February a war has shut the Strait of Hormuz on and off, and you've paid at the pump and on
the heating bill. Nobody here started that war.

Who decided. Somebody here did decide, one cheap contract at a time, to hang what we can't do
without on one route, supplier or foreign server, with no plan B.

The rule. Nothing vital depends on one supplier, route or government abroad, friend or rival,
unless someone here owns it and has a second way to run it. We already do this for oil, with
emergency stocks. Next, the computers hospitals and payments run on.

The switch list. Within a year, the ten foreign dependencies we'd miss most, each with a named
owner and a drill: switch the foreign system off on purpose and see what still works.

The ledger. Paid once, from the NATO resilience money Defence holds.
