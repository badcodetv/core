# Principles — what we hold to be true about a frame

Twenty-six principles distilled from the 2026-08-26 cinematography sweep (20 briefs, two
adversarial critic passes — [`briefs.md`](./briefs.md)). Each carries an evidence grade:

**A** academic/empirical or hard optical fact · **A (mixed)** peer-reviewed but contested or
single-study · **P** practitioner consensus, multiple independent named humans · **p** one
practitioner or one case · **H** house ruling, ours, from our own work

🔴 **The sweep's own critic caught wave one inflating grades** — building **P** out of three
content-mill sites agreeing. Every grade below has been re-checked against
[`evidence.md`](./evidence.md). Where a brief's grade outran what it actually verified, the
grade here is lower than the grade there. Trust this file over the briefs.

---

## 0. The gates

Three questions before any shot is designed. A shot that fails one is not designed further.

1. **What is this shot FOR?** Not what it shows — what it does to the viewer that the shot
   before it did not. If the answer is "it looks good", there is no shot yet.
2. **Is there a visible cost in frame?** For any monumental shot — scale, symmetry, a machine,
   an institution — something or someone must be visibly paying a price. *(principle 22; this
   is the visual twin of `the-reader.md`'s standing rule: never raise a fear without naming the
   beneficiary.)*
3. **Where does the light come from?** Name the source in the world. If the answer is "it's
   just lit", the frame will read as generic before anything else about it registers.

## 1. The frame

**1 — A flat frame is the single most common tell of amateur work.** Foreground, midground and
background must be visually separated. A subject on one plane against a backdrop reads as a
photograph of a stage no matter how sharp or detailed it is. This is the first thing to check
when someone says a shot is "flat", and detail or resolution never fixes it. **P**

**2 — Every frame needs one focal point.** Without a single place for the eye to rest, the
viewer scans and settles nowhere, and reports the frame as cluttered or confusing even when it
is technically perfect. Negative space is the cheapest way to manufacture one. **P**

**3 — Composition competes with hard-wired attention; it does not override it.** The eye goes
to motion first, then faces and high-contrast regions, and only then follows the composition's
lines and depth cues. Design with that order, not against it. **A (mixed)**

**4 — No geometric device has a fixed emotional meaning.** Kubrick's one-point symmetry produces
dread; Wes Anderson's produces safety and comedy. Same device, opposite effect, decided entirely
by everything else in the frame. Any claim that symmetry, centring or an angle "means" something
on its own is a myth. **P/A**

**5 — Pretty and tense use the same vocabulary in opposite directions.** Balance, symmetry and
centred weight resolve a frame. Imbalance, converging lines onto an off-centre subject, and
violated headroom leave it unresolved. Decide which you want before you compose; "nice" is not a
target. **P**

**6 — A frame-within-a-frame does narration's job for free.** A doorway, window, arch or
structural division around the subject both aims the eye and silently states contained, trapped,
or observed — without a caption. **P**

**7 — A frame that implies no world past its edge is a diorama, not a moment.** Bazin's
distinction: a painting's frame contains itself, a film frame implies the world continuing
beyond it. Withholding — a partial view, an obstruction, a gaze aimed off-frame — is what makes
a picture feel like it was cut out of something larger. **A (mixed)**

**8 — The rule of thirds is a beginner's calibration tool, not a law.** Treating it as mandatory
is itself one of the causes of the generic stock-photo read. Off-centre placement creates
tension; that is the useful half. The grid is not the finding. **P** *(and the "eye naturally
goes to the intersections" claim is **myth** — see [`evidence.md`](./evidence.md))*

## 2. Light

**9 — Light is a decision about the story, not decoration on it.** Where it comes from and what
it hides *is* the meaning. **P**

**10 — Motivated light is what makes a stylised frame still feel true.** The eye must be able to
trace the light to something plausibly in the scene — a window, a lamp, a screen, a fire.
Ungrounded light reads as "lit", and a modern eye trained on realism breaks with the image
immediately. This is already the BadCode house rule; the sweep independently confirms it from
four directions. **P**

**11 — 🔴 Darkness reads as depth only when one small region inside it is deliberately
brighter.** Uniform blackness with nothing lit reads as a technical failure — a crushed file,
a dead screen — not as mood. There is now a perceptual mechanism for this, not just craft lore:
the visual system computes how dark a thing is relative to the brightest region it can group
into the same scene (anchoring theory of lightness perception, Gilchrist). Remove the anchor and
there is nothing to normalise against. **This is the most load-bearing single principle in the
file for our register.** **A** (mechanism) / **P** (craft rule)

**12 — Hard versus soft is optical fact, not taste.** A small or distant source gives
sharp-edged shadow and reads as harsh, exposing, unforgiving. A large or close source gives
gradual falloff and reads as gentle and safe. **A**

**13 — The lighting ratio is the actual mood knob.** Roughly 2:1 (key barely brighter than fill)
reads calm and natural; 8:1 and beyond reads dramatic and morally ambiguous. Three-point
lighting is a teaching diagram, not a rule — working DPs routinely drop the fill entirely.
**P** *(the specific genre-to-ratio lookup tables are **myth**)*

**14 — Rim light and silhouette are how a dark subject stays readable against a dark
background.** Separate by edge, never by lifting the whole frame. **P**

## 3. Colour

**15 — Plan colour for the whole piece before making a single frame.** We cannot fix drift the
way a DP fixes it across a shooting day — every frame is made separately and will wander unless
the palette was decided first. A restricted palette is also a net technical advantage for us:
fewer places for a generative model to drift off-brief between shots. **P**

**16 — A colour code is arbitrary and learned, like a flag.** Give one colour to one idea,
character or era, let it appear only when that thing is present, and its return becomes a
signal. The power comes from rarity and consistency, not from what the colour "means". Fixed
colour-to-emotion codes are **myth**. **P**

**17 — Warm-against-cool inside one frame is most of what viewers mean by "production
value."** A warm source against a cold ambient reads instantly as *someone lit this on purpose*.
**P** *(teal-and-orange is the same idea worn out into a cliché — banned in our register)*

## 4. Lens, height and angle

**18 — The distortion blamed on a wide lens is caused by standing close, not by the lens.** Same
distance, same subject size, any focal length gives identical geometry; changing lenses without
moving changes only field of view. Get this right — most sources have it wrong. **A**

**19 — Camera height and camera angle are separate variables.** The lens can sit low and stay
level, or sit high and tilt down; each does something different. Nearly every listicle conflates
them. Treat height as a slider, not three boxes — and note that the one study to test it found
only ~30° differences produced clear effects, so a barely-off eye-line does nothing. **P/A (mixed)**

**20 — "Low angle equals power" is real but weak and context-dependent, not a law.** One
narrative study found it; one direct-address trust study found eye-level was trusted *more* than
either low or high angle, and a third found the effect running backwards. Know whether you are
optimising for dramatic dominance or for credibility — they pull in opposite directions.
**A (mixed)**

**21 — The overhead is the position of a narrator who already knows how it ends.** It produces
detachment, irony or dread rather than contempt — which is exactly our narrator's gaze. But an
overhead buys omniscience for free and must earn *scale* separately: from above, an empty space
collapses into geometry and pattern rather than absence. **P** (omniscience) / **A (mixed)** (the
scale caveat)

## 5. Register — the monumental problem

**22 — 🔴 The monumental grammar is neutral; the reading is built by what surrounds it.**
Soviet monumentalism, Nazi rally architecture and Deakins' *Blade Runner 2049* interiors are
functionally identical as grammar. Scale, symmetry and a single hard light say nothing on their
own about whether the power depicted is good or bad. **"Monumental equals fascist" and
"monumental is safe because we mean it ironically" are equally false.** **P**

**23 — 🔴 The most reliable marker separating indictment from celebration is a visible cost.**
Across every propaganda case in the sweep — the Nuremberg cathedral of light, *Triumph of the
Will*, Stalinist monumental photography — none shows exhaustion, wreckage, a body that failed,
or anyone the system is failing. The frame is clean because a cost would break the spell.
Conversely the counter-tradition (*Metropolis*, *Brazil*) adds cost, agency-asymmetry and a
visible outside, and never softens the scale. **This is a mandatory pre-publish check on any
monumental shot.** **p** (as film criticism) / **H** (as our gate)

**24 — 🔴 Satire that borrows power's grammar gets read as celebration by part of any
audience.** This is documented and repeated, not hypothetical. Verhoeven built *Starship
Troopers* quoting *Triumph of the Will* deliberately and was called neo-fascist on release; Mary
Harron has said on the record she is mystified that traders adopted Patrick Bateman as
aspirational. The same pattern recurs across *Fight Club* and *The Wolf of Wall Street*. **Doing
it on purpose does not protect you.** Pair with our existing ruling that irony is decoded
through prior belief (`story-craft` principle 18). **A (mixed)**

**25 — Awe is vastness plus a need for accommodation, not size.** Keltner & Haidt's model: the
experience must fail to fit existing categories, so the concept of the world has to update. A
frame that explains itself instantly has nothing left to accommodate. What kills awe follows
directly — fast cutting, showing the whole object at once, over-explaining in narration or
score, and clutter competing with the vastness cue. Threat-based awe (roughly 1 in 5 naturally
reported awe experiences) is the variant our narrator embodies: awed by its own catastrophe.
**A (mixed)**

**26 — Scale for a non-human subject must be manufactured in frame.** There is no default sense
of how big a machine or building is without a known-size reference — a doorway, a stair, a
vehicle, a figure. One human-scale figure visibly small against architecture is the single most
load-bearing awe device available to us. **P** (application) / **A** (the size-perception
mechanism)

## The house rulings — where the sources disagree and we had to choose

| # | The disagreement | Our ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | Is our near-black monumental register right for our reader? | **🔴 OPEN — a human must rule.** See below. |
| **R2** | Awe wants a tiny figure against vastness; Andrea Arnold's Academy frame refuses to let a landscape make a person small, because that is what respect looks like. Both are deliberate; they cannot coexist in one shot. | **Pair, don't choose.** Monumental is the default awe engine. The tight human-scale frame is a deliberate exception for character beats inside the machine-scale story — spent, not defaulted to. |
| **R3** | Continuity grammar (180-degree rule, eyeline match, the triangle) versus our actual process. | **Mostly not our problem.** Those rules govern cutting between multiple angles of one continuous action, which we do not shoot. What survives: consistent light direction, consistent scale, object permanence. |
| **R4** | Kuleshov — foundational finding or teaching legend? | **The narrow version only.** A hard cut between two images makes the viewer infer a relationship; that much is real and now has fMRI support. The famous "one face reads as three emotions" story failed a controlled replication (Prince & Hensley 1992) and is **myth in its popular form**. |
| **R5** | Coverage versus a designed shot list. | **Designed, always.** We cannot afford coverage and do not need it. Each shot has a job decided before it is made. |
| **R6** | Is documentary/vérité grammar (grain, wobble, imperfect framing) available to us? | **With a named limit.** The visual codes transfer to a generated clip. Borrowing them for *invented* footage of a *real* subject manufactures the signal "this really happened" for something that did not. Our money film is a true subject — flag every use. |
| **R7** | Handheld as authenticity. | **Rejected as a default.** Handheld is a style, not truth. Ozu and Haneke get more unease from a locked-off frame than most handheld gets urgency. |
| **R8** | On-screen text — necessary layer or confession of failure? | **A cost, paid deliberately.** Feed platforms are watched sound-off so a caption often carries the whole beat. But a caption explaining what the image should have shown means the image failed. Both are true; decide per beat. |

## 🔴 R1 — the open ruling

**The corpus contains material arguing against our own house register, and it does not resolve
itself.** Stated honestly, because a human has to choose:

- **Brief 15** documents that the visual grammar closest to our target reader's own vocabulary
  (`docs/marketing/the-reader.md` — a working-class UK reader) is British social realism:
  democratic eye-level, available light, handheld, refusing exactly the symmetry, engineered
  light and scale-dwarfing our register is built from. The brief's own words: our register is
  *"close to the visual opposite of this whole tradition on every axis that matters."*
- **Brief 17** documents that our grammar, absent a visible cost, reads as celebration of power
  to a documented fraction of any audience — including the audiences of directors who built the
  critique on purpose.

**Neither brief proves the house look is wrong. Neither proves social realism is the fix.**
Nobody has evidence that either register works on this specific reader. What is available now:

1. **A partial reconciliation** — R2 above (pair, don't choose) plus principle 23 made mandatory.
2. **An empirical step nobody has taken** — brief 17's checklist has never been run against an
   actual BadCode frame. Its own §7 recommends testing it against three or four shipped panels
   before it becomes doctrine.

Until ruled: keep the register, apply gate 2 (visible cost) to every monumental shot, and do not
claim the question is settled in any public copy or any canon file.
