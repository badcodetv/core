# The static frame — making stillness grip

Our dominant form. A held image under narration is not film coverage and not a photograph — it
is closer to a comic panel with a caption box. The viewer scans it actively *while* listening,
so it needs a clear entry point and somewhere for the eye to land when the sentence ends.

---

## 1. What makes a still hold

**🔴 A still only holds attention if it contains an unresolved question.** A gesture halfway
through, a gaze aimed at something, a body about to fall or about to speak. **Resting poses die
fast**, and this is the most common reason a technically good generated still is boring.

**An unstable arrangement reads as "before" or "during".** An off-balance body, an object about
to tip, two figures not looking at each other — this is what makes a frozen frame feel like it is
still moving.

**🔴 The decisive moment is not peak action.** Cartier-Bresson meant the moment when the
*composition itself* resolves into meaning — geometry and event locking at once. A still with
strong composition and no story beat is furniture regardless of what is happening in it. This is
the single most misquoted idea in photography and it matters to us: we are choosing between
generated candidates, and "which one is most dramatic" is the wrong question. **Which one has the
geometry and the event arriving together** is the right one.

**A frame with an identical first and last frame reads as a photo, not a shot.** Even a held
still wants one thing to change — light drift, haze, a slow breath of camera movement.

## 2. Word and picture

**🔴 Voice-over and image should not agree on timing.** If the narration describes exactly what
is on screen exactly when it appears, the still has nothing left to do. Let the words arrive
early, late, or sideways of the image — **the reader does the join, and the join is where the
feeling lives.**

**A caption carries only what the eye cannot get** — motive, backstory, causality across time,
irony, the named beneficiary. Redundant narration is the fastest way to kill a still's tension.
This is the McKee test our `story-craft` toolkit already runs on every narration line: keep the
line only if a silent cut would lose real information.

**Anchorage.** Changing the words under an unchanged image changes what the image means, because
viewers resolve visual ambiguity by reading it against the adjacent text (Barthes). We rely on
this constantly — it is how narration-plus-still functions as a form at all. **A**

**And the argument against text**, held in tension: a caption explaining what the image should
have shown is a confession the image did not work. Treat every caption as a cost.

## 3. The gutter — what happens between two stills

**The gap between two images is where the reader constructs cause, effect and elapsed time** —
not the panels themselves (McCloud). **What you cut out between two stills does more storytelling
work than what you put in either one.**

Practically: two stills with a large implied gap make the viewer work and reward them for it. Two
stills with a small gap read as redundancy — one of them is padding.

## 4. Duration

**There is no correct length.** The shot dies the instant it finishes delivering information or
feeling, and content sets that moment, not a stopwatch.

- A frame with new information arriving every second earns 6–8s.
- A wide establishing beat with nothing left to read past 2s should cut at 2s.
- The failure mode is uniform duration — every still held the same length reads as a slideshow.

## 5. Motion in a still form

**🔴 One real moment of motion after a long stretch of stillness is worth ten scattered motion
beats.** *La Jetée* spends roughly 26 minutes as photographs and cashes its one moving shot
exactly once. It converts because it is rare, not because it is flashy.

**Pan-and-zoom on a still does not manufacture drama that is not already in the composition.**
The Ken Burns move only *reveals* drama that was staged into the frame in advance. Used to
disguise a boring photo it reads as exactly that — a boring photo, moving.

Which gives the working rule: **decide whether the still earns a move by looking at the still.**
If the composition has a second thing worth arriving at, a slow push finds it. If it does not,
the move advertises the absence.

## 6. What this means for choosing between candidates

We generate several options and pick one. The questions, in order:

1. **Is there an unresolved question in it?** If not, reject regardless of quality.
2. **Do geometry and event arrive together?** The decisive-moment test.
3. **Are there separated depth layers?** *(→ [`frame.md`](./frame.md) §1)*
4. **Is there one bright anchor in the darkness?** *(→ principle 11)*
5. **Is there a visible cost?** For anything monumental. *(→ [`registers.md`](./registers.md))*
6. **What does the caption have left to say?** If the answer is "describe this", the frame is
   doing the caption's job and one of them is redundant.
