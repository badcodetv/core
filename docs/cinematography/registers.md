# Registers — the monumental, the documentary, and the one we did not choose

A register is a set of learned conventions an audience reads as "this kind of thing". It is not
a natural law: a viewer reads noir or horror because they have seen the codes before, not because
the codes are inherently menacing.

Three registers matter to us. One is the house look. One we use for the money film. One is the
grammar our reader already owns, which we did not adopt — and that is an open question.

---

## 1. 🔴 The monumental register — ours, and its problem

**What produces it.** Symmetry plus a single hard motivated source in a mostly-dark frame reads
as designed-to-dominate rather than lived-in. Deep converging perspective — a corridor, a
colonnade, a line of columns receding into shadow — is the fastest single-frame signal of
institutional power. Straight verticals read as permanent and institutional; converging verticals
(camera tilted up, building leaning inward) read as unstable and dynamic. The geometry is optical
fact; the convention riding on it is craft.

**The scale rule.** Big is not oppressive. **A human figure at a visibly, uncomfortably small
scale against the structure** is what converts "large building" into "building that could crush
you". Without a known-size reference — a doorway, a stair, a vehicle, a figure — size collapses
into abstraction.

**Material carries mood.** Raw concrete holds shadow in its texture and reads as mass and
permanence. Polished steel and glass reflect and fragment the source, multiplying it — cold,
inhuman, corporate. Dust in the air scatters and softens — neglect, age, abandonment.

**Four registers for the same machine**, chosen by distance, scale-crowding and colour:

| Register | Named body of work | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indifferent | The Bechers / Düsseldorf School | Frontal, shadowless, dead-centre, identical distance, **all rhetoric removed** |
| Sublime | Burtynsky | Scale beyond assimilation, colour retained |
| Menacing | *Alien*'s Nostromo | Crowding, occlusion, hard source, no exit visible |
| Exhausted | Salgado | Bodies at the limit, crowding, monochrome |

Note the first one: the leading tradition achieves menace by *subtracting* drama, not adding it.
That is available to us and we have never used it.

### 🔴 The problem with this register

**The grammar is neutral. Soviet monumentalism, Nazi rally architecture and Deakins'
*Blade Runner 2049* interiors are functionally identical as grammar.** Nothing in scale, symmetry
or one hard light tells the eye whether the power depicted is good or bad.

**Sontag's markers of fascist aesthetics** (*Fascinating Fascism*, 1975) are a usable checklist
precisely because they are formal: massing people into ornamental patterns; turning people into
things; grouping them around an all-powerful figure; alternating ceaseless motion with congealed,
static posing; and glorifying surrender, exalting mindlessness, glamorising death. **p** — one
named critic, the canonical text in the field.

**Benjamin's frame is about function, not appearance.** Fascism aestheticises politics so the
public can *feel* agency — spectacle, self-expression — while property relations stay untouched.
His prescribed opposite is not "look different", it is make the image argue about who owns what.
That is a sharper description of BadCode's actual job than anything else in the sweep.

**🔴 The single most reliable formal marker is the absence of a visible cost.** The Nuremberg
cathedral of light, *Triumph of the Will*, Stalinist monumental photography — none shows
exhaustion, wreckage, a body that failed, or anyone the system is failing. The frame is clean
because a cost would break the spell. The counter-tradition (*Metropolis*, *Brazil*) adds cost,
agency-asymmetry and a visible outside, and never softens the scale.

### The gate

> **Before any monumental shot ships: is there a visible cost in this frame?**
> Wreckage, exhaustion, a body that failed, someone the system is failing.
> If not, it is drifting toward celebration.

This is the one test from brief 17 that the critic judged genuinely usable on a single still. It
also happens to be the picture version of a rule we already ruled in writing on 2026-08-15
(`the-reader.md`: never raise a fear without naming the beneficiary in the same piece). Two
independent routes to the same rule.

**Three weaker tests, kept for completeness:** massing into ornament (rarely applies — our shots
have one figure, not a crowd); congealed-versus-ceaseless posture (needs bodies we don't have);
and camera behaviour — a worshipful glide up a structure reads differently from a static hold
while a small human crosses the bottom (only fires on video, and half our output is stills).

### 🔴 And doing it on purpose does not protect you

Verhoeven built *Starship Troopers* quoting *Triumph of the Will* deliberately and was called
neo-fascist on release. Mary Harron has said on the record that she is mystified traders adopted
Patrick Bateman as aspirational. The same aspirational-reading pattern recurs across *Fight Club*
and *The Wolf of Wall Street*. Pair this with `story-craft` principle 18 — irony is decoded
through prior belief — and the conclusion is the same from two directions: **the undeniable
on-screen consequence is not optional.**

## 2. Awe — what the register is actually reaching for

**Awe is vastness plus a need for accommodation** — the experience does not fit existing mental
categories, so the concept of the world has to update (Keltner & Haidt). Not "big things look
impressive". **A (mixed)**

**Threat-based awe** — the same structure appraised as dangerous or uncontrollable — is a
documented distinct variant, roughly 1 in 5 naturally reported awe experiences. **This is the
variant our narrator embodies: awed by its own catastrophe, not uplifted by it.** **A (mixed)**

**The small-self effect is one of the better-replicated results in the field** (Piff et al. 2015,
five studies, N=2,078): awe shrinks the felt self relative to the world, and measurably increases
prosocial behaviour and reduces entitlement. For a collective trying to move people off
entitlement politics, that is a mechanism worth knowing. **A**

**Burke got there 250 years early.** The sublime — vastness, obscurity, darkness, power, terror
at a safe distance — is a distinct and *greater* passion than the beautiful, and Burke names
**obscurity and darkness** as its instruments: an unseen threat frightens more than a visible
one. Kant's split is worth keeping: the *mathematical* sublime is sheer magnitude (a server hall
running past the frame edge); the *dynamical* sublime is a force that could destroy you, viewed
from safety. **Our machine shots are reaching for the dynamical sublime specifically.**

**What kills awe**, following directly from the accommodation model: fast cutting (no time to
fail to assimilate before the next image), showing the whole object in one clean establishing
shot (nothing withheld), over-explaining in narration or score (the meaning is handed over),
and clutter competing with the vastness cue.

**Do not claim awe expands time.** One well-cited study found it; a later replication found no
effect. Contested. **A (mixed)**

## 3. The documentary register — for the money film

**Visible imperfection reads as truth.** Grain, unstable framing, uneven exposure, a camera
reacting a beat late, an unmotivated cut, no reframing to catch the perfect composition — all
signal *this was recorded, not staged*.

**All of which can be faked, and film theory says so out loud.** Cinéma vérité's own definition
concedes deliberate interaction and construction; "handheld and grainy is more truthful" is myth.

**🔴 The ethical limit, which is ours to hold.** The visual codes transfer to a generated clip.
Borrowing them for *invented* footage of a *real* subject manufactures the signal "this really
happened" for something that did not. The Magic Money Tree film is about money creation, which is
a true subject. Flag every use. *(ruling R6)*

**Beautifying suffering is a named trap.** A subject filmed too beautifully reads as spectacle
rather than testimony, and documentary theorists split hard on whether that is ever acceptable.

## 4. British social realism — the register we did not choose

The grammar closest to our target reader's own visual vocabulary, and we adopted its opposite.

- **Ken Loach's naturalism is a deliberate refusal of beautiful cinematography** — long lenses
  shooting from a distance so actors forget the camera, available daylight, minimal movement.
  Named on the record by two of his own DPs across different films and decades: Chris Menges
  (*Kes*) and Barry Ackroyd (twelve films). **P**
- **Alan Clarke's walking shot** — unhurried Steadicam following someone from behind through a
  real, unglamorous location — *Made in Britain* (1982), pushed to its purest in *Elephant*
  (1989). **p**, one filmmaker's signature.
- **Andrea Arnold shoots Academy 4:3 as a stated ethical choice**, in her own words: *"it's a very
  respectful frame… it's a very human frame."* She is refusing to let a landscape make a person
  small. **p** — do not generalise to "4:3 equals respect".
- **Mike Leigh's grammar comes from process** — months of improvisation before a script exists —
  not from camera technique. A rehearsal discipline, not something we can borrow.
- **"Poverty porn" is a real named critique** of exactly this tradition: that it packages
  deprivation as visual pleasure for an audience that does not live it, and that the people
  depicted often do not recognise themselves in it. **A (mixed)**
- **There is a counter-tradition.** Martin Parr's saturated-colour, on-camera-flash photography of
  British seaside working-class life (*The Last Resort*, 1983–85) was a deliberate break from
  black-and-white miserabilism — and drew its own accusation of mocking its subjects. **P**

### 🔴 The tension, unresolved

Arnold's frame refuses to make a person small. Our register's most load-bearing awe device is
**one human-scale figure, visibly small against architecture**. Both are deliberate. They cannot
coexist in one shot.

**Our ruling (R2): pair, don't choose.** Monumental stays the default awe engine. Arnold's tight
human-scale frame is a deliberate exception, spent on character beats inside the machine-scale
story.

**What is NOT resolved (R1):** whether either register actually works on this specific reader.
Nobody has evidence. The house look is not shown safe; social realism is not shown to be the
answer. See [`principles.md`](./principles.md) §R1 — a human ruling is owed, and the cheap next
step is running the visible-cost gate against three or four shipped panels.

## 5. Quick reference — other genre grammars

**Noir** — low-key, skewed angles, deep focus; descended from German Expressionism. **Horror** —
noir's underexposure plus withheld information: empty frame edges, negative space that *could*
contain something. **Sci-fi** trends the opposite way: bright, sharp edge to edge, wide lenses
making space itself feel vast and abstract.
