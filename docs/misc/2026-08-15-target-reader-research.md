# The target reader: a sourced evidence base

**Date:** 15 August 2026. **Purpose:** the evidence base for
[`../marketing/the-reader.md`](../marketing/the-reader.md) — who BadCode's work is actually aimed
at, what that person believes, and what moves them. Commissioned as the counterweight to
[`2026-08-15-anti-ai-research.md`](./2026-08-15-anti-ai-research.md), which measured the people who
are *loud* about AI; this file measures the person the work is *for*.

**Method:** four research agents (AI attitudes by class · working-class realignment · blame
attribution and persuasion · automation-vs-immigration blame). The session's WebSearch quota was
exhausted, so verification ran through direct fetches of primary documents (MAC, OBR, ONS, Ofcom,
Ipsos, Ada Lovelace/Turing, Deezer) and the OpenAlex/Crossref APIs. Claims are tagged: **[V]**
verified against a source this session; **[T]** training knowledge, unverified today. Findings the
agents could *not* establish are listed in §7 — several load-bearing questions turn out to be
unmeasured, and we say so rather than fill the gap with vibes.

---

## 1. Who the reader is

- **The mechanism of the rightward drift is status, not a spreadsheet.** Lower *subjective social
  status* predicts right-populist support across 20 democracies (Gidron & Hall, *BJS* 2017) [V].
  Voters respond **sociotropically** — to the story of their region and country, not their own
  payslip (Colantone & Stanig, *APSR* 2018; Hainmueller & Hopkins, *ARPS* 2014) [V]. You are
  competing with a story about the country, not a calculation.
- **Decline, not poverty.** Anti-system voting tracks *long-term decline of places that have seen
  better times*, far more than inequality per se (Rodríguez-Pose 2017, 2020) [V]. A place that was
  always poor doesn't revolt; a place that lost something does.
- **Austerity is causally implicated in the drift.** Exposure to post-2010 welfare cuts predicts
  UKIP support and Leave; Fetzer (*AER* 2019): "the EU referendum could have resulted in a Remain
  victory had it not been for austerity" [V]. Misdirection with receipts: the wound was policy;
  the culprit offered was the border.
- **The reader is already sold on public ownership.** Reform UK's 2024 contract pledged **50%
  public ownership of utilities and critical national infrastructure**; in 2025 it called for
  nationalising Scunthorpe steel [V]. Reform polled **20% among C2DE vs 11% among ABC1** in 2024,
  and by Aug 2026 polls at 22–26% nationally [V]. **The contest is over which structure gets
  blamed, not over whether structure matters.**
- **Attitudes move.** The share wanting immigration reduced fell 65% (2015) → 42% (2022) before
  rising again as net migration hit 866k (YE 2023) [V]. A 23-point swing in seven years is the
  empirical warrant for believing culture work can matter at all.
- **Do not import US despair statistics.** Case & Deaton's deaths-of-despair finding is explicitly
  US-only ("no other rich country saw a similar turnaround") [V]. A UK equivalent is unverified.
- Source hygiene: **Matthew Goodwin is now a GB News presenter and was a Reform UK candidate** [V]
  — cite his pre-2023 peer-reviewed work if at all, never as neutral analysis.

## 2. What the reader thinks about AI

- **AI does not exist as an issue for them.** Ipsos Issues Index June/July 2026 (unprompted):
  immigration 39–43%, economy 30–37%, NHS ~19. **AI appears nowhere.** YouGov's issue tracker
  doesn't even offer it as an option [V]. Immigration concern runs **44% among C2DE** [V].
- **Low income is the strongest demographic predictor of AI scepticism** — outperforming education
  in the Ada Lovelace/Turing regression (n=3,513, random-probability, low-income oversample) [V].
- **Their machine fear is being judged and defrauded, not art.** Concern about AI assessing
  **welfare eligibility rose 44% → 59% in two years** — the sharpest rise in the survey. Top
  self-reported harms: false information 61%, financial fraud 58%, deepfakes 58% [V]. And
  low-income respondents are *less* worried about generative AI than the national average (42% vs
  47%) [V]. The UK's flagship AI-attitudes survey doesn't ask about AI art *at all* [V].
- **Creative-AI anger is narrow and intense.** In AI-led open interviews (YouGov, Apr 2026,
  non-representative), creative work/culture/ownership was raised by just **10%** — but with the
  highest engagement and lowest sentiment of any theme [V]. Small, intense, loud.
- **But disclosure is a supermajority demand, not a niche one.** 94% say companies should disclose
  AI-generated actors; 89% want AI content labelled; 78% would trust a brand less for undisclosed
  AI actors [V]. The correct model: *almost everyone hates being deceived by AI; only a small,
  intense minority organises its politics around AI's existence.* Disclosure neutralises the
  majority objection; nothing satisfies the intensity minority.
- **Behaviour vs attitude:** AI music is >50% of daily Deezer uploads but **1–3% of listening**,
  and up to 85% of fully-AI streams were bot fraud [V]. The flood is a fraud phenomenon, not an
  audience phenomenon. The 97%-can't-tell / 80%-want-labels result verifies exactly [V].

## 3. Immigration economics, honestly (proportionality, never denial)

The reader's sense that something changed is **correct**, and copy that denies it gaslights them.

- **Wages:** MAC's replication of Dustmann/Frattini/Preston (1993–2017): EU migration's cumulative
  association is roughly **−5% at the 5th wage percentile and +4.4% at the 90th** [V]. Small at the
  bottom, positive at the top — *the same regressive tilt as everything else in the economy*. MAC's
  own caveat: short-run, likely dissipates.
- **The proportionality line, from MAC's chair:** the post-referendum fall in sterling "probably
  raised prices by 1.7 per cent — almost certainly a larger impact than the effect on wages and
  employment opportunities of residents from all the EEA migration since 2004" [V]. **One currency
  move outweighed twelve years of free movement.**
- **Housing is where the grievance is most nearly right:** ~1% house-price rise per 1pp
  migration-driven population rise, *larger where planning refuses development*; social housing is
  explicitly zero-sum for new tenants — "given there is little building of new social housing" [V].
  Real effect; the mediating villain is the building rate.
- **Fiscal:** positive in aggregate for EEA migrants (+£22bn for post-2000 arrivals) [V], but OBR
  2024: a low-wage migrant turns net-negative by their early 40s [V]. "Immigrants pay for
  themselves," flat, is overclaiming.
- **Services:** no evidence of harm to NHS waiting times (outpatient waits *fell*), school
  attainment, or crime [V]. This part of the narrative is simply wrong.

**The honest sentence:** immigration didn't take your wage — it tilted the board slightly, against
you and toward the man who owns the firm; and the tilt is dwarfed by austerity, sterling and the
planning system. That is not a denial. It is a re-aiming.

## 4. The blame machinery

- **Scapegoating has two measured motives: guilt and lost control** (Rothschild et al., *JPSP*
  2012) [V]. A story that restores explicability and agency without indicting the reader removes
  both. A story that says "you fell for it" supplies the guilt motive directly.
- **Correcting facts about immigrants barely moves attitudes** — seven experiments over 11 years
  (Hopkins, Sides & Citrin 2019); misperceptions are a *consequence* of attitudes [V]. The backfire
  effect, however, is largely a myth (Wood & Porter 2018; Swire-Thompson 2020) [V] — corrections
  work on beliefs, they just don't reach attitudes, and they decay.
- **The single most important experiment for us — anger is conserved and re-targeted.** Wu
  (*Economics & Politics* 2022): telling people about technological displacement made Republicans
  demand immigration restriction and Democrats demand tariffs; almost nobody demanded tech
  restriction, and nobody redirected at firms [V]. **Raising automation anxiety without aiming it
  feeds nativism.** The evidenced claim is not "the two blames are twins" — it is **"automation
  anxiety is feedstock for anti-immigrant politics"** (and political entrepreneurs are harvesting
  it: Borwein et al., *POQ* 2026 — "fissures in the public's attitudes toward AI are already primed
  for exploitation by political entrepreneurs" [V]).
- **Inevitability demobilises; injustice mobilises.** Automation *vulnerability* alone did not
  raise redistribution demand; framing the resulting inequality as **unjust** did (Jeffrey, *JEBO*
  2021) [V]. What anxiety produces unaimed is a *restriction* reflex — "slow the machines down"
  (Gallego et al. 2021, Spain) [V] — the same shape as the immigration reflex. And
  technology-framed worker-protection appeals mobilise *worse* than trade-framed ones (Borwein et
  al. 2023) [V].
- **AI aversion leans right, not left.** Political conservatism predicts aversion to consequential
  AI (Castelo & Ward 2021) [V] — so AI anger and immigration anger likely *compound in the same
  people* rather than living in different tribes.

## 5. What actually works (and its honest ceiling)

- **Arguments alone: zero. Narrative exchange: durable.** Kalla & Broockman (*APSR* 2020, 6,869
  voters, pre-registered): argument-only canvassing had **no effect** on exclusionary immigration
  attitudes; adding non-judgemental narrative exchange held at 4 months, **d = 0.08** [V]. The
  active ingredient is **perspective-getting** — *receiving* someone's first-person account — not
  perspective-taking prompts (Kalla & Broockman, *AJPS* 2021) [V].
- **Broadcast narrative moves norms and behaviour, not private belief** (Paluck's Rwanda radio-soap
  year-long field experiment) [V]. Design for what becomes *sayable* and for the argument after the
  song, not for conversion.
- **A four-minute music video beat the academic state of the art** at prejudice reduction, via
  identification with characters (Murrar & Brauer 2017; German replication 2020 — works *best* on
  high-authoritarian viewers) [V]. Serialised fiction changed fertility decisions of low-income
  audiences at national scale (Brazilian telenovelas; La Ferrara et al. 2012) [V]. Transportation
  suppresses counterarguing; the fact/fiction label makes no difference; **medium doesn't
  matter** (Green & Brock 2000; Braddock & Dillard 2016 meta: r ≈ .17–.23) [V].
- **Character alone can misfire.** Episodic (one-person) framing shifts blame *off* individuals but
  only **thematic** framing moves blame *onto* political actors — and that effect concentrated
  among right-leaning viewers (IJPP 2021) [V]. Keep the beneficiary on-panel.
- **Inoculation works small and generically** — teach the *technique* on third parties, never
  accuse the viewer; effects hold across the political spectrum. **Scapegoating was the hardest
  technique to inoculate against** (d = 0.28, weakest of five; Roozenbeek et al., *Sci. Adv.* 2022)
  [V]. Reactance = anger + counterarguing, triggered by controlling/identity-indicting framing
  (Rains 2012 meta) [V]. Don't repeat the false claim in order to knock it down; the best
  messenger is the one whose interests cut against what they're saying (Berinsky 2015) [V].
- **Sarcasm carries diagnosis, not re-aiming.** One-sided satire provokes counterarguing;
  Daily-Show-style exposure raised cynicism about *everything* while raising viewers' confidence
  (Becker & Anderson 2019; Baumgartner & Morris 2006) [V]. Snark for "the system is rigged"; the
  care register for "and here is who did it."
- **Ceiling:** the prejudice-reduction field is publication-biased and light-touch (Paluck et al.
  2020); campaign persuasion's base rate is zero (Kalla & Broockman 2018) [V]. d ≈ 0.04–0.08
  durable is what the *best* interventions deliver. The bet is defensible; returns are small,
  additive, cumulative — not conversional.

## 6. Where the reader is

Ofcom News Consumption 2025 + Reuters DNR 2025 (UK) [V]: online news 70%, TV 68% and falling;
**Facebook is the biggest social news source at 30%; TikTok 11% and rising from 1% in 2020**;
GB News has climbed to 4th in weekly news usage; trust in news is 35%, 15+ points below
pre-referendum. Print is at 12%. The reader is on Facebook, YouTube and TikTok, trusts almost
nobody, and is not browsing web-comic sites.

## 7. What nobody has measured (claim the gaps; don't fill them)

1. **The symmetry claim** — that "blame the AI" and "blame the immigrant" are the same manoeuvre —
   is directly tested nowhere. Every experiment runs the arrow one way: automation cues *produce*
   nativism.
2. **The name-the-decision-maker experiment** — tell people a specific firm's choice caused the
   loss, measure whether anger relocates to capital — has never been run.
3. **Inevitability-vs-choice framing** as an experiment on political engagement: never run.
4. **Who the online anti-AI activists demographically are:** no survey exists. Any claim about
   r/antiai's age or politics is unevidenced (and the one adjacent finding — conservatism predicts
   AI aversion — points the *other* way).
5. **Class-segmented attitudes to AI art:** measured nowhere, by anyone.
6. **UK automation-exposure → Leave/Reform voting:** no study; the UK evidence is about Chinese
   import competition (Colantone & Stanig) and digitalisation's *winners* (Gallego et al. 2021).
7. **"Condescension drove them right":** plausible, consistent with the status mechanism,
   never causally demonstrated. Treat as hypothesis.
8. **Grime/football/soaps/stand-up as political vehicles for this audience:** no rigorous causal
   evidence found (possibly a discovery limitation; the verified evidence base is telenovelas and
   sitcoms).
