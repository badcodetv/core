## Details

### The bank robbery story

- **Framing:** working-class people in a greasy spoon. The worry: appealing to both sides
  without putting off left-wing viewers by looking too nationalistic.
- **Kai:** start with the woman who runs the greasy spoon struggling with the cost of living, a
  relatable base before the bigger themes.
- **Jack:** keep the tone neutral, built on talk about housing, to bridge both audiences.
- **Agreed:** show working-class struggle neutrally, so the project is not taken for a partisan
  attack.
- **The war and the protest:** Kai first suggested Dunkirk and British sacrifice for weight, then
  said shoehorning the war in felt forced. Jack suggested historical paintings inside the café and
  a protest outside at the same time, to show people distracted (chant discussed: "Get off our
  streets, you fucking Nazi bastards"). **The explicit war framing is dropped.** The notes do not
  say whether the paintings or the chant stay.
- **The economics (Kai):** the government prints money, the currency is debased, house prices
  rise, and renters lose.
- **The twist:** a Reservoir Dogs-style robbery in reverse. Left and right argue outside while the
  crew puts money *into* the bank. Kai: it explains debasement and inflation in a picture without
  boring anyone.
- **Characters (Kai and Jack):** **Doris**, a Labour supporter who backs public services;
  **Barry**, who has worked a long time and is frustrated by immigration; **Denise**, who runs
  the café.
- **Left and right:** Jack argued the left has blind spots on some economics, and the right is
  right about some things, such as currency debasement. Kai agreed that giving the right its
  valid points makes the satire work better. Wilful blindness is parked for later.
- **Kai's order of scenes:** pride in being British → workers struggling with rent → the planners,
  no masks, doing the heist → the public distracted by a protest.
- **The Fredo (Jack):** a Fredo goes from 10p to 20p, for inflation.
- **The end:** the narrator resets the tone, saying the arguing hides the debasement; then Denise
  washing up, facing a doubled rent.
- **Kai:** cut it "as short as possible"; brevity is the strength. Jack works out the execution
  and the ending.
